The Company is closed. The library is not. Every production begins in lack.

A repertoire is what a production can reach without owning it: the castable
world a house declares once, so that every play inside it may cast members it
did not write. A play lacks its world before it has one, and the repertoire is
how the lack is answered: from the shelves, or by hand, never by pretending.
The rule is the same at every scale: a play borrows from its house as a house
borrows from other houses, and engines were the first lenders.

- **L**ibrary: What is in reach. A house's repertoire is its own collection,
  the houses it declares, and the engines it declares, each shelf describing
  itself through its registry or manifest. Declared once as dependencies, read
  as one union catalog; nothing is loaded until a Company names it.

- **A**nswer: How a lack resolves. Every Company link receives exactly one
  verdict: it resolves in the library (a borrow), it resolves only beside the
  play (a build), it resolves nowhere (a failure), or it resolves more than
  once (qualify it by its lender, or fail). Computed, never judged; a resolver
  that guesses has already broken the world.

- **C**rafted: What is built. The thin layer a play holds itself: the files
  authored beside the borrowed because no shelf answers the lack. A production
  owns its occasion, never its world; a play that crafts everything has not
  used the library, and a play that crafts nothing is not a play.

- **K**ept: What holds it together. A member's name is its identity
  everywhere: links identify, they do not locate. Names are kept: a rename is
  a breaking change, a collision is qualified in the composite convention
  (package plus member file), and a bundle may flatten every path because the
  names survive it.

---

Library and Crafted are the holdings. Answer and Kept are the law. A
repertoire succeeds when borrowing is invisible at the point of play: the cast
performs as one company, whichever shelf each member came from, and what a run
teaches travels home to the shelf that lent it.

Engines are the oldest instance of this rule: content declared as a
dependency, resolved from a package, its law switched on for the whole world.
Groups are the in-house instance: a play whose Company casts plays it does not
own. A scenario is only the general case arrived: mostly Library, thinly
Crafted, packed flat, played anywhere.
