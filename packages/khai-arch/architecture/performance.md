The play is what happens. A performance is what happened.

A performance is the claim ticket a run leaves riding beside the play: thin,
in band, and permanently on loan from where the evening is written down. The
full captured script is lodged with the result layer, which owns it; the
performance file is the archive's loan back to the production, carrying just
enough to be cast. For it is a member like any other: a sequel's Company may
borrow the premiere, a plot may draw on what an earlier evening settled, and
in time a kept performance ages into history the way a plot already is one.
The Director captures it and never authors it; a performance records what the
cast performed, in the key that was called, and nothing the stage did not
show.

- **L**odged: Where the written performance lives. The link to the full
  captured script at its home in the result layer; it must resolve, or this
  is not yet a performance. The ticket travels, the archive stays put.

- **O**ccasion: The evening itself. Which play was run, the key that was
  called, the Conditions the Human provided, the date, and the venue it
  played. Enough that a reader knows which of many runs this was, since the
  same play keyed otherwise is a different production.

- **A**ftermath: What the run closed. Verdict shaped, like a plan's targets:
  what was settled, what failed, what was left open when the Collaboration
  rested. The outcome a later production can stand on without reading the
  whole evening.

- **N**ote: What was sent home. The word to the lenders whose members the
  run exercised, and to the production that comes next: what the evening
  taught about the cast, the key, or the shelf it was borrowed from. The
  return leg of the tour, written down.

---

Lodged and Occasion are the ticket; Aftermath and Note are the yield. A
performance succeeds when a production that never saw the evening can cast
it: the sequel borrows the premiere by its ticket, and only the reader who
wants the whole evening follows the link home.
