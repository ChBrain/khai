# Reprisal: Reference

## Line of Work

Reprisal as **composite**: what a persona expects getting even to do, and what it does. It is not a
fourth mechanism but a wiring of three. The **forgiveness** engine supplies the stance -- vengeful
rather than avoidant or benevolent, the motivation to see the transgressor answer, one of the three
transgression-related orientations that engine already carries. The **bias** engine supplies the
forecast, through the impact bias: the settling imagined in full colour with nothing else in the
frame, and its felt duration read off the loudness of that picture. The **rumination** engine supplies
the loop the episode was already running in, and the replaying form it settles into.

The joining is Carlsmith, Wilson and Gilbert's, and it is in none of the three. Punishment research
had established that people will pay to punish and that they say it feels good; their question was
whether it does. In a public-goods game with a free rider, participants given the option to punish
took it -- and afterwards reported thinking about the free rider **more**, and feeling **worse**, than
participants who had no such option. They had confidently predicted the opposite. So had the
non-punishers, asked to imagine having punished. Nobody in the study misunderstood their own feelings;
everybody mispredicted a duration, which is a different failure and a far more common one.

That is why the composite needs all three atoms and why it reads the aftermath rather than the moment.
From forgiveness: the stance, which is a real orientation and not a lapse -- the wish exists because
acting on it works, at the level of deterrence and standing, and a persona who feels nothing when
wronged is missing a signal rather than possessing a virtue. From bias: the mechanism of the
misprediction, which is focalism -- the persona imagines the settling with the rest of the week left
out of the frame, and the rest of the week is where the cost lands. From rumination: the reason it
persists, since an unfinished episode recurs **because** it is unfinished, and the persona has just
added the one element they can never settle, which is whether they should have.

Hence the keystone. Retaliation is the one response that guarantees the matter will not be finished
with, because it converts a thing that happened to the persona into a thing they did -- and unlike
somebody else's conduct, which the mind can eventually put down, their own is theirs to defend, doubt,
refine, and rehearse indefinitely.

There is an exception, it is well evidenced, and the composite keeps it rather than tidying it away.
Gollwitzer and colleagues found that revenge satisfies where the offender's response makes it
**legible that they understood why it was done** -- comparative suffering alone does not close the
account, a delivered and received message does. Which sharpens the keystone rather than softening it:
what a persona actually wants from a reprisal is a reply, they do not control whether one comes, and
an act designed to make somebody suffer is poorly designed to produce one.

It is the second composite to wire the forgiveness engine. Moral-account runs it with guilt, repair,
and betrayal, reading one wrong across three ledgers that refuse to agree; here it is run forward from
the wronged party alone, and the subject is what their own answer costs them. It is the second to wire
bias, after intergroup, and it takes a single member from that tree rather than the tree. No composite
owns either: an atom carries a phenomenon, and a composite carries a question asked of it.

The domain does not model any side alone -- the releasing or keeping of a grievance is forgiveness's,
the circling is rumination's, the misprediction is the bias engine's. Nor is it costly third-party
sanction with nothing coming back, which is altruistic-punishment's and has no personal injury in it;
nor a spiral between two parties trading blows, which is escalation's; nor what a persona holds
another to have coming, which is deserving's. It owns the purchase: what the persona pays for the
stroke, and when the payment falls due.

**On the evidence, stated plainly.** The joining is a real and well-known result, and the composite is
an inference across three atoms that should be read as one. Carlsmith, Wilson and Gilbert's finding
has held up and the affective-forecasting literature it sits in is among the more robust in social
psychology, with focalism and the durability bias demonstrated across many domains. What is **weaker
is the reach**. The original studies are laboratory games with modest stakes among strangers, and a
free rider in a public-goods task is a long way from a betrayal by somebody a persona loves; whether
the same aftermath follows a serious injury, where the wrong is larger and the relationship real, is
not established and is not obviously the same question. The rumination link is measured as
self-reported thinking about the transgressor, which is good evidence for the loop and weaker evidence
for the causal story the composite tells about **why** the loop runs. Gollwitzer's message account is
supported and has replicated, but it also cuts against a flat reading of the paradox and is the
strongest reason not to treat "revenge never satisfies" as the finding -- the honest summary is that
it satisfies under a condition the avenger rarely secures. There is also a real cultural bound: much
of this work is on individualist Western samples, and in settings where honour is a standing
obligation the meaning and the aftermath of retaliation are likely different, which the worth-logic
engine is better placed to read than this composite is. What should not be taken from the composite is
that retaliation is always a mistake, that a persona who wants it is failing at something, or that
forgiveness is being recommended here. The claim is narrow: expect the wish to be well founded, the
moment to deliver, the forecast to be about the wrong quantity, and the account to stay open unless a
reply arrives.

## Origin

The psychology of revenge and affective forecasting, wired as a composite over the forgiveness, bias,
and rumination engines. The first row is the **joining** -- the published work that makes retaliation
the thing which keeps a grievance alive -- and the rest anchor the individual bridges and their
bounds.

## Restrictions

What the composite refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The three sides (the atoms)**: The composite wires the three but owns none. The releasing or
  keeping of a grievance, in its decisional, emotional, withheld, and self-directed forms, and the
  avoidant, vengeful, and benevolent stances, are the forgiveness engine's. The repetitive dwelling
  itself, in its brooding, replaying, and reflection forms, is rumination's. The misprediction of a
  future feeling is the bias engine's. Reprisal reads only what the second does to the first once the
  third has been spent.
- **Third-party sanction (altruistic-punishment)**: A persona paying a personal price, with nothing
  material coming back, to make a defector answer for breaking a cooperative norm is the
  altruistic-punishment engine's. That has no personal injury in it and no grievance held, which is
  the whole difference: reprisal's persona was the one wronged.
- **A spiral between two parties (escalation)**: Positions hardening, the other narrowing to an
  antagonist, and each retaliation raising the stakes is the escalation engine's. Reprisal reads one
  persona and one stroke, and says nothing about what the other does next -- a play may run both, and
  should not confuse the cost of having acted with the cost of being answered.
- **One wrong across three ledgers (moral-account)**: The transgressor's felt debt, the dyad's spoken
  account, and the wronged party's reckoning, held simultaneously and refusing to agree, are the
  moral-account composite's, which wires this same forgiveness engine. Reprisal runs it forward from
  the wronged party alone and asks what their own answer costs them.
- **What somebody has coming (deserving, condemnation)**: Moral judgment, desert, costly sanction,
  and the CAD triad are those composites'. Reprisal is indifferent to whether the retaliation was
  proportionate or justified, and its persona is generally right that they were wronged.
- **Standing and the obligation to answer (worth-logic, face, status)**: Where personal worth comes
  from and how it is defended -- across the dignity, honour, and face logics -- is the worth-logic
  engine's. In an honour setting retaliation is an obligation rather than an option and the aftermath
  is likely different; that reading belongs there, and this composite does not claim it.
- **Being unable to stop thinking about a person (rumination, transference)**: The circling is
  rumination's and a figure carried from elsewhere onto a present other is transference's. Reprisal
  reads only the loop that has the persona's own conduct in it.
- **The vengeance as authored symbol (the play's pitch)**: An act of retaliation made to **stand
  for** a theme -- the settled score that mirrors a motif of balance -- is refused: that is authored
  key, the play's pitch. Reprisal sets what the stroke cost; the meaning read into it is the play's.

## Encoding

Source to constraint. A composite root over three engine atoms it declares as dependencies, with
three bridges.

- **[reprisal](process_reprisal.md)** (the root): what a persona expects getting even to do and what
  it does, read on the aftermath and never on the moment of the stroke. Anchored by Carlsmith, Wilson
  & Gilbert (2008).
- **[the score](process_reprisal_score.md)** (bridge): the debt named and the receipt imagined --
  a well-founded wish and an estimate of the wrong quantity, since the persona forecasts the intensity
  of a moment and buys with it a change in a state. Anchored by McCullough's TRIM model and Wilson &
  Gilbert (2003).
- **[the stroke](process_reprisal_stroke.md)** (bridge): the act, which discharges something real and
  is not hollow, and which changes what the memory is made of by adding the persona's own conduct to
  it. Anchored by Carlsmith et al. (2008).
- **[the keeping](process_reprisal_keeping.md)** (bridge): the persona who acted still turning it over
  after the one who did not has stopped, and the exception they do not control -- an answer that shows
  the message was understood. Anchored by Carlsmith et al. (2008), Nolen-Hoeksema, and Gollwitzer &
  Denzler (2009); the composite's twist and keystone, that retaliation is the one response which
  guarantees the matter will not be finished with.
