# Spacing: Reference

## Line of Work

Spacing as **composite**: what a room's arrangement does to the distances the people in it can hold.
It is not a third mechanism but a wiring of two: the **space** engine supplies the setting as a party
to what happens in it -- what it affords, what can be claimed in it, and the mood it induces -- and
the **proxemics** engine supplies the portable bubble each persona carries and the equilibrium by
which closeness is held at a tolerable level.

The joining is Osmond's and Sommer's, and neither is in either atom for this scope. Osmond, designing
psychiatric wards, drew the distinction the composite runs on: an arrangement is **sociofugal** when it
holds people apart -- chairs in a row against a wall, everybody shoulder to shoulder and nobody face
to face -- and **sociopetal** when it draws them together. Sommer and Ross then tested it on a
geriatric ward whose day room had been newly furnished and was, by every account, pleasant and quiet.
They moved the chairs off the walls and into small groups around tables. Conversation roughly doubled.
Nothing about the patients had changed, and nothing about the patients was what had been wrong.

That is why the composite needs both atoms. From space: the setting as an active party, which the atom
already carries through Barker's behaviour settings -- a physical milieu pressing a standing pattern
on whoever enters, persisting across occupants. From proxemics: the reason the pressing works, which is
that a persona cannot simply accept a distance the room imposes. Argyle and Dean's equilibrium account,
carried in the atom, has closeness summed across channels and held at a tolerable level, so a distance
that is wrong gets paid for elsewhere -- in gaze withdrawn, in a topic kept shallow, in a visit cut
short. The regulation never fails; it only chooses a different channel.

And the channels are not interchangeable in what they cost. Distance is cheap to adjust and invisible
when it works. Its substitutes are not: a withdrawn gaze reads as coldness, a shallow topic reads as
having nothing to say, an early exit reads as a judgement about the company. So an arrangement that
forbids the preferred distance does not merely make people closer or further apart -- it converts a
spatial constraint into what looks exactly like a disposition.

Hence the keystone. The arrangement sets the ceiling on contact and takes none of the credit. The
people in a room are visible, mobile, and each did something specific; the arrangement is fixed,
silent, and was there before anybody arrived, having been settled by somebody who is not present and
was solving a different problem -- cleaning, capacity, sightlines, the fire regulations. So the causal
search stops at the only moving parts, and a room is praised or written off through the people in it.
The test that would settle it is moving the furniture, which is cheap and almost never tried, because
nobody proposes rearranging a room to check a claim about somebody's temperament.

The domain does not model either side alone -- affordance, territory, and atmosphere are the space
engine's, and the zone, the intrusion, and the equilibrium are proxemics'. Nor is it what a place comes
to mean to somebody who lives in it, which is dwelling's; nor how a settlement is made readable and
how its street life runs, which is peopling's; nor a crowd's own effects, which are midst's. It owns
the conversion: an arrangement, a requirement, and the misattribution between them.

**On the evidence, stated plainly.** The atoms are well founded and the joining is a real and
well-known result, but its evidential status should be stated rather than assumed. Sommer and Ross is a
single-setting field study from 1958 with a small ward population and no control ward, which is to say
it is a demonstration rather than a measured effect; its influence on design practice is much larger
than its sample. The broader claim it stands for -- that seating arrangement changes interaction rates
-- has been supported repeatedly since in classrooms, offices, and care settings, and is about as close
to settled as environmental psychology gets, but the effect sizes vary widely with the setting and the
sociofugal/sociopetal distinction is a useful description rather than a measured dimension. Barker's
behaviour-setting theory is observational and much cited but hard to test. Hall's proxemic zones, which
the proxemics atom carries, have real cross-cultural variation and the specific distances should not
be treated as universal. And the misattribution step -- that observers credit the people rather than
the arrangement -- is an application of a well-established finding about causal explanation to this
case rather than a result obtained in this case. What should not be taken from this composite is that
temperament is an illusion or that rooms determine behaviour. People do differ, and most of what
happens in a room is about them. The claim is narrow: where an arrangement makes a preferred distance
unavailable, expect the compensation to show on a channel that looks like character, and expect the
explanation to be given in those terms.

## Origin

Environmental psychology and the study of interpersonal distance, wired as a composite over the space
and proxemics engines. The first row is the **joining** -- the published work that puts an arrangement
and an interpersonal distance into one design and changes the first to move the second -- and the rest
anchor the individual bridges and their bounds.

## Restrictions

What the composite refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The two sides (the atoms)**: The composite wires the two but owns neither. Affordance, territory,
  and atmosphere are the space engine's; the zone, the intrusion, and the equilibrium are proxemics'.
  Spacing reads only what the first does to the second, and one alone is a place or a process, not
  the conversion.
- **What a place comes to mean (dwelling)**: Place attachment, the restorative environment, and the
  third place are the dwelling composite's, and they run on a persona's standing relation to a
  setting over time. Spacing runs on one occasion and needs no history with the room at all.
- **The settlement and its street (peopling)**: Legibility, social time, and street life are the
  peopling composite's, and they run at the scale of a district. Spacing runs at the scale of a room,
  where the distances are within one bubble's reach of each other.
- **A crowd's own effects (midst)**: The crowd, social facilitation, and the bystander effect are the
  midst composite's, and they turn on how many others are present. Spacing turns on where they can be
  -- and a gathering of three in a badly arranged room runs this with no crowd at all.
- **A made thing arranged so a choice goes one way (artifice)**: Choice architecture, usability, and
  guile are the artifice composite's. There the object is designed to move a decision and the maker
  chose the arrangement for that; here the arrangement was set for cleaning or capacity by somebody
  with no interest in the outcome, which is why there is nobody to accuse.
- **A room made safe to speak in (airing)**: Psychological safety, the group, and the org are the
  airing composite's, and they concern what a group has established about what may be said. Spacing
  concerns what the furniture has established about who can be near whom, and a play may run both --
  they will not agree.
- **Holding a distance from a rival (vigil)**: Guarding, vigilance, and mate-guarding are the vigil
  composite's, where the distance is chosen and defended. Spacing's distances are imposed and nobody
  chose them.
- **The room as authored symbol (the play's pitch)**: An arrangement made to **stand for** a theme --
  the long table that mirrors a motif of estrangement -- is refused: that is authored key, the play's
  pitch, not the force. Spacing sets what the room permitted; the meaning read into it is the play's.

## Encoding

Source to constraint. A composite root over two engine atoms it declares as dependencies, with three
bridges.

- **[spacing](process_spacing.md)** (the root): what a room's arrangement does to the distances its
  people can hold, read on the room and never on the temperament. Anchored by Osmond (1957) and
  Sommer & Ross (1958).
- **[the fixture](process_spacing_fixture.md)** (bridge): the arrangement as it stands, set by
  somebody absent for another reason, unremarked because it is the background, and pressing its
  program on whoever enters. Anchored by Barker (1968) and Sommer (1969).
- **[the reach](process_spacing_reach.md)** (bridge): the portable bubble and the equilibrium that
  must balance, so a distance the room fixes is paid for on a channel that is not cheap. Anchored by
  Argyle & Dean (1965) and Hall (1966).
- **[the verdict](process_spacing_verdict.md)** (bridge): the outcome attributed to the people, since
  they are the only moving parts in view. Anchored by Ross (1977) and Ross & Nisbett (1991); the
  composite's twist and keystone, that the arrangement sets the ceiling on contact and takes none of
  the credit, so a room is praised or written off through the people in it.
