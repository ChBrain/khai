# Lasting: Reference

## Line of Work

Lasting as **composite**: whether anything of a good thing survives its arrival. It is not a
fourth mechanism but a wiring of three: the **savoring** engine supplies the attention that makes
a pleasure fuller and longer -- or, thrown the other way, kills it; the **capitalization** engine
supplies the reception a persona's good news meets when it is told, on which the whole gain of
sharing turns; and the **hedonic-adaptation** engine supplies the return to the level the persona
held before, which runs whether or not anybody does anything. This composite adds no fourth
engine; it reads the seam the three make -- a good thing does not keep itself -- and stages that
seam into three bridges, closing on the discovery that the fading is not a fault in the machinery
but the machinery working.

The domain does not model any single fate -- sharpening, sharing, keeping, and dampening are the
savoring engine's; the four response styles are capitalization's; habituation, aspiration, and
setpoint are hedonic-adaptation's; and one alone is a process, not the fate of a good. Nor is it
the present feeling a persona carries beneath the moment, which is the mood engine's; nor the
resting temperament that sets the height of the baseline, which is the temperament engine's; nor
the softening of what hurts rather than the heightening of what pleases, which is the coping side
held by the resilience composite. It owns the arc: what becomes of a good between its arrival and
the persona's return to their own level.

The spine is Sheldon and Lyubomirsky's Hedonic Adaptation Prevention model, which is where the
two literatures are joined rather than merely set beside each other here. The model asks what
becomes of the well-being gained from a positive change, and names two routes by which it is
eroded: a bottom-up one, the positive emotions the change generates declining as it becomes the
constant, and a top-down one, aspirations climbing so that more positivity is now required -- the
two currents this composite's fading bridge carries as habituation and aspiration. Against them it
names two moderators that forestall the erosion, continued **appreciation** of the original change
and continued **variety** in the experiences it brings, which is the attending bridge stated as an
intervention; and Sheldon, Boehm and Lyubomirsky tested the predictions in a three-wave
longitudinal study. So the pairing of a good's fate with the work done on it is published, not
asserted here. What this composite adds on top is the third fate: the model treats the sharing of
a positive change as one appreciation practice among others, where the capitalization literature
shows the gain turning on the responder rather than the sharer -- so the telling is set apart as a
fate the persona does not control -- and the keystone, that the return the model exists to
prevent is the same return that carries a persona back from a bad change. The atoms supply the
fates. From one side, adaptation: Brickman and Campbell's hedonic treadmill, in which
satisfaction is judged against a standard that rises with circumstance, and the Brickman, Coates
and Janoff-Bulman study whose lottery winners were not much happier and whose accident victims not
much less happy than controls -- with Frederick and Loewenstein consolidating the mechanisms and
Lykken and Tellegen locating a large, heritable, stable component to which people return. From the
other side, the regulation of positive experience: Bryant established savoring as a distinct
perceived-control process, the counterpart to coping rather than a by-product of it, and Bryant and
Veroff catalogued the ways it is done and the kill-joy thinking that undoes it; Langston named
capitalization, the finding that telling someone about a positive event yields affect beyond the
event; and Gable, Reis, Impett and Asher established the responder typology, in which only the
active-constructive reply builds the bond and the other three cost the sharer something. The two
literatures set the terms of the composite: work can be done on a good, and the baseline will
reclaim it anyway. Diener, Lucas and Scollon supply the honest qualification -- baselines are often
positive, differ across persons and domains, and can themselves shift with sustained change -- so
the setpoint is real but not immovable. The three bridges are those three fates: the attending,
the telling, and the fading.

## Origin

The regulation of positive experience and the psychology of adaptation, wired as a composite over
the savoring, capitalization, and hedonic-adaptation engines. The first row is the **joining** --
the published work that sets a good's erosion and the work done against it as one model -- and the
rest anchor the individual bridges.

## Restrictions

What the composite refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The single fates (the atoms)**: The composite wires the three but owns none. Sharpening,
  sharing, keeping, and dampening are the savoring engine's; the four response styles are
  capitalization's; habituation, aspiration, and setpoint are hedonic-adaptation's. Lasting reads
  only the arc the three make; one fate alone is a process, not the fate of a good.
- **The feeling itself (mood)**: The persisting affective background a persona carries beneath the
  moment is the mood engine's. Lasting reads what becomes of a good, not the weather it lands in.
- **The height of the baseline (temperament)**: The resting disposition that sets where a persona
  returns to is the temperament engine's. Lasting reads the returning; how high the level sits is
  temperament's.
- **The softening of what hurts (resilience)**: Coping with adversity, the mirror process, is the
  resilience composite's. Lasting is savoring's side of the same distinction: the heightening of
  what pleases, not the management of what does not.
- **The valuation of an outcome (reward)**: The discount by which an outcome is weighed against
  its distance is the reward engine's. Lasting reads a good that has already arrived.
- **Needing ever more of the same (addiction)**: Tolerance, and the escalation of pursuit past its
  cost in a needed currency, is the addiction engine's. Lasting reads the ordinary weakening of a
  response to a constant, not a dependency.
- **Measuring against other people (bias)**: The comparison of one's lot with someone else's,
  rather than with one's own past, is the social-comparison territory of the bias engine. The
  aspiration form raises the persona's own standard against their own gain.
- **The windfall as authored symbol (the play's pitch)**: A good made to **stand for** a story --
  the inheritance that mirrors a theme of undeserving, the prize that signals a fall to come -- is
  refused: that is authored key, the play's pitch, not the force on the persona. Lasting sets what
  becomes of the good; the meaning read into it is the play's.

## Encoding

Source to constraint. A composite root over three engine atoms it declares as dependencies, with
three bridges, the fates a good thing can meet.

- **[lasting](process_lasting.md)** (the root): whether anything of a good survives its arrival --
  a good thing that does not keep itself. Anchored by Bryant (savoring as a distinct process) and
  Brickman & Campbell (the treadmill).
- **[the attending](process_lasting_attending.md)** (bridge): the notice paid to a good, which
  enlarges it by focus and memory or thins it by kill-joy thinking -- one lever, thrown either
  way. Anchored by Bryant & Veroff (2007) and Quoidbach et al. on dampening; the fate a good
  meets before anyone else has heard of it.
- **[the telling](process_lasting_telling.md)** (bridge): the good carried outward and the
  responder's fork it lands on, only the active-constructive reply building the bond, and no reply
  neutral. Anchored by Langston (1994) and Gable et al. (2004, 2006); the one fate the persona who
  owns the good does not control.
- **[the fading](process_lasting_fading.md)** (bridge): the response worn down, the standard
  risen, the level reasserted. Anchored by Brickman, Coates & Janoff-Bulman (1978) on the symmetry
  of the return and Diener, Lucas & Scollon (2006) on its limits; the composite's twist and
  keystone, that what returns a persona from the worst is what takes the best away.
