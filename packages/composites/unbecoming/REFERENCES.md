# Unbecoming: Reference

## Line of Work

Unbecoming as **composite**: what a room does to a persona whose role and whose read do not agree. It
is not a third mechanism but a wiring of two: the **role** engine supplies the sanctioned expectations
attached to a position -- what its occupant may do and be, and whether a persona matches, violates,
exceeds, or receives unclear ones -- and the **gender** engine supplies the read the room applies
before the persona speaks. Neither is unusual. A position that carried no expectations could not be
filled or judged, and a room reads whoever walks into it because that is what rooms do.

The joining is Eagly and Karau's, and the role atom already carries it: role congruity, where a
prejudice arises not from anything the persona does but from the relation between two pictures the
room already holds. Their account gives the composite its two working faces, and it is the separation
of the two that does the work. The **descriptive** form discounts the persona's potential for the
role, because what the room expects of somebody like them does not match what it expects of somebody
in the position -- a judgement made about capability, before there is any performance to inspect. The
**prescriptive** form penalises the persona's actual conduct in the role, because behaving as the
position requires means behaving as the read forbids.

The second of those is the composite's twist, and the work that makes it stateable is not in either
atom. Rudman found that women who promoted themselves in the way the situation rewarded were rated
more competent **and** less likeable, and were less likely to be hired for it: the same behaviour
producing both results at once. Rudman and Glick located the cause in prescriptive stereotypes rather
than descriptive ones -- not a belief about what women are like, which evidence can correct, but a
belief about what they ought to be like, which evidence cannot. Heilman's work supplies the sharpest
version: women who unambiguously succeeded in male gender-typed work were not merely doubted, they
were disliked and interpersonally derogated, and the derogation was strongest precisely where the
success was least deniable.

That is the keystone. The two forms are not one prejudice applied twice but a scissors: falling short
of the role disqualifies, meeting it penalises, and no conduct answers both. And it is invisible
because the blades are held by different hands and expressed in different registers -- the first as an
assessment of capability, the second as an impression of character -- so neither speaker is
dissembling and the two are never asked to hold their verdicts together.

Merton stands under the whole of it, since a role is a bundle of expectations attached to a position
rather than a description of anyone, and West and Zimmerman under the read, since accountability to a
frame is applied to a persona whether or not they endorse it. Both are already in the atoms, which is
where they belong.

The domain does not model either side alone -- role conflict, overload, and ambiguity in their own
right are the role engine's, and the read in its own right is gender's. Nor is it a discrediting
attribute borne by the one who has it, which is the carrying composite's and runs on a mark rather
than on a position; nor the us/them line and the catalogued slants, which are intergroup's; nor rank
and the moves that contest it, which are standing's. It owns the mismatch: what a room does when its
two expectations of the same person disagree.

**On the evidence, stated plainly.** The theory is well specified and its two forms have held up, but
the sizes matter and the composite should not be read as claiming more than they support. The
meta-analytic picture on evaluations of leaders is that the devaluation is real and, averaged across
studies, small -- and that the average is the least interesting number in it, because the effect is
strongly moderated: largest in male-dominated settings and roles, for directive and autocratic
conduct, and among male evaluators, and near zero elsewhere. The backlash effect is separately well
replicated, and its dissociation -- competence up, liking down -- is the part that has proved most
robust. The leader stereotype has become measurably less masculine over the decades the research
covers, so a play set now and a play set fifty years ago are not staging the same intensity. The
evidence base is overwhelmingly Western and organisational; role congruity is stated generally, for
any group whose stereotype mismatches a role's norms, but it has been tested far less outside the
case of women and leadership, and this composite's warrant is strongest there. What should not be
taken from it is that any dislike of a person in a mismatched role is this mechanism, or that a room
running it is a room of bad actors. The claim is structural: where a role's requirements and a read's
prescriptions conflict, expect the shortfall before the conduct and the penalty after the success.

## Origin

Role theory and the social psychology of gendered role expectations, wired as a composite over the
role and gender engines. The first row is the **joining** -- the published work that puts a role's
norms and a group's read together and derives a prejudice from the relation between them -- and the
rest anchor the individual bridges and their bounds.

## Restrictions

What the composite refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The two sides (the atoms)**: The composite wires the two but owns neither. Congruence,
  incongruence, overload, and ambiguity are the role engine's; the read and its expressions are
  gender's. Unbecoming reads only what happens when the two disagree, and one alone is a position,
  not a mismatch.
- **A role's own strain (role)**: Competing demands from different parties, too many demands from
  one, and demands that are not clear -- role conflict, role overload, role ambiguity -- are the
  role engine's, and they are real costs that need no read at all. Unbecoming requires a second
  expectation about the **person**, not a second expectation from the position.
- **The read in its own right (gender)**: What the room does with the read generally, and what it
  costs across a life, is the gender engine's. This composite takes one narrow slice of it: the slice
  where the read meets a position and the two disagree.
- **A mark carried by the one who has it (carrying)**: Stigma, secrecy, and shame are the carrying
  composite's, and they run on a discrediting attribute the bearer manages. Unbecoming's persona is
  not discredited by a mark but by a position, and the penalty arrives from the role rather than from
  the attribute; a play staging concealment, disclosure, and the cost of keeping it is in carrying's
  territory.
- **The us/them line and the standing slants (intergroup)**: Social identity, bias, and hostility
  between groups are the intergroup composite's. Unbecoming does not need two groups in contact and
  does not run on group membership as such; it runs on one persona in one position.
- **Rank and the moves that contest it (standing)**: Status, power, hierarchy, and the status move
  are the standing composite's. Unbecoming is not a contest over rank -- the persona already holds
  the position -- it is a judgement about whether they should.
- **What somebody has coming for what they did (deserving)**: Moral judgment, sanction, and the
  pleasure at a fall are the deserving composite's, and they need a transgression. Here nothing has
  been transgressed except a picture, and the persona's conduct was the role's own requirement.
- **The accounting for somebody else's misfortune (befalling)**: An observer explaining why a bad
  thing happened to a person is the befalling composite's. Unbecoming is not an explanation offered
  after the fact; it is a standard applied before it.
- **Being held to a face in front of others (presentation)**: Impression management, front and
  backstage, and emotional labour are the presentation composite's. They are what a persona under
  this pressure may do about it, and doing it well does not remove the scissors.
- **The mismatch as authored symbol (the play's pitch)**: An incongruity made to **stand for** a
  theme -- the outsider who mirrors a motif of belonging -- is refused: that is authored key, the
  play's pitch, not the force. Unbecoming sets what the room did; the meaning read into it is the
  play's.

## Encoding

Source to constraint. A composite root over two engine atoms it declares as dependencies, with three
bridges.

- **[unbecoming](process_unbecoming.md)** (the root): what a room does to a persona whose role and
  whose read do not agree, read on the room and never on the persona. Anchored by Eagly & Karau
  (2002).
- **[the read](process_unbecoming_read.md)** (bridge): two sets of expectations already standing,
  neither the persona's doing, both applied before any conduct is available to judge. Anchored by
  Merton (1968) on the role and West & Zimmerman (1987) and Ridgeway (2011) on the read.
- **[the shortfall](process_unbecoming_shortfall.md)** (bridge): the descriptive form -- less of what
  the role calls for, found in advance, evidence-resistant rather than evidence-limited. Anchored by
  Eagly & Karau (2002) and Heilman (2001).
- **[the penalty](process_unbecoming_penalty.md)** (bridge): the prescriptive form -- competence
  granted and liking withdrawn on the same conduct, triggered by success rather than by failure.
  Anchored by Rudman (1998), Rudman & Glick (2001), and Heilman et al. (2004); the composite's twist
  and keystone, that this is a scissors rather than a bias -- two blades each defensible alone, and
  no conduct that answers both.
