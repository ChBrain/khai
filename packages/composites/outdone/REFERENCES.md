# Outdone: Reference

## Line of Work

The responses to upward comparison as **composite**: one reckoning with another's superior standing, and one dial -- how the persona meets it -- that sets which of three corners the reckoning lands in. No single atom engine sees the meeting axis the corners share. The warm response that would rise toward the other is the admiration engine's; the painful but assimilative pull to gain the same is the envy engine's benign form; the painful, hostile wish to see the other lose it is the envy engine's malicious form. This package authors none of those readings again. It declares the two engines as dependencies, wires their members by hard package links, and adds only what the atoms cannot hold: the meeting axis itself, and the three motions along and across it -- the sort where deservingness and attainability fork one comparison into three, the turn where warmth sours and benign envy hardens as the path up closes, and the blend where a persona admires and envies the same rival at once.

The warrant is accordingly integrative, not primary: the sources below establish the tripartite response to upward comparison the composite reads over, not the individual emotions, which their own engines warrant. The spine is van de Ven, Zeelenberg and Pieters's account of the emotions of upward comparison -- admiration, benign envy, and malicious envy -- and the appraisals of deservingness and control that sort them: an advantage read as deserved and attainable draws admiration or the benign pull to rise, one read as undeserved or out of reach curdles toward the malicious wish to level down. It is the reckoning-with-a-superior counterpart within the comparison family: where the comparison engine measures the self against others up, down, and level, this composite reads the three ways the upward case is emotionally met.

## Restrictions

What the composite refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The two atoms**: The composite authors no emotion of its own. Admiration -- the warm upward regard that would rise toward another's excellence -- is the admiration engine's, whole; benign and malicious envy -- the painful pulls to gain the same or to level the other down -- are the envy engine's. This package reads over them and adds only the meeting axis and its motions; every corner is an atom's ground, cited by hard link, never restated.
- **Comparison**: The composite models the emotional response to being outdone, not the comparing itself. The measuring of the self against others -- upward, downward, and level -- and the selection of a comparison target are owned by the comparison engine; this composite reads only the three emotions the upward case draws, not the mechanism that produces the comparison.
- **Schadenfreude**: The composite models the wish to see the other lose their advantage, not the joy when they do. The pleasure at a rival's misfortune -- malicious envy's payout when the envied at last falls -- is owned by the schadenfreude engine; this composite owns the response up to the fall, and hands the joy at it across.
- **Status and Hierarchy**: The composite models the response to a rank difference, not the rank itself. The standing the other's advantage confers, and the order the two occupy, are owned by the status and hierarchy engines; this composite owns how the difference is emotionally met, not the difference.
- **Praise**: The composite models the reckoning with a superior from below, not the freely-given regard for another's good. The other-praising emotions -- gratitude, elevation, admiration given without the sting of comparison -- are the praise composite's; admiration sits in both, but praise reads it as freely-given warmth while this composite reads it as the benign pole of an upward comparison that could also have stung.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per file.

- **[outdone](process_outdone.md)** (the root): The upward-comparison family as one reckoning with one dial -- how another's advantage is met -- landing in admiration, benign envy, or malicious envy. Anchored by van de Ven et al. (the tripartite response).
- **[outdone, sort](process_outdone_sort.md)** (bridge): One comparison met three ways, forked by the appraisals of deservingness and attainability. Anchored by van de Ven et al. (the deservingness-and-control account).
- **[outdone, turn](process_outdone_turn.md)** (bridge): The responses tipping into one another and darkening -- admiration souring to envy, benign envy hardening to malicious as the path up closes. Anchored by the evolutionary status-gap accounts and van de Ven (the leveling functions).
- **[outdone, blend](process_outdone_blend.md)** (bridge): Admiring and envying the same rival at once -- the respected rival, the frenemy, the sustained ambivalence. Anchored by the ambivalent-relationship literature.
