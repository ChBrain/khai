# Time: Reference

## Line of Work

Time as **composite**: time whole, read over its two faces -- the measured passage a
thing accrues and the opportune moment that punctuates an unfolding -- and what their
meeting makes. It is not a new force but a wiring of two: the **chronos** engine
supplies the measured, sequential passage (how old, in what order, how long endured,
matured or not), the **kairos** engine the qualitative opportune moment (the window,
the juncture, the timing, the ripeness). This composite adds no new force; it reads
the phase relation of the two -- whether the moment lands right on the passage, falls
out of step with it, or meets it each at its fullness -- and stages that into its
shapes.

It is the repository's first **multi-cargo** composite, cast where its atoms are cast:
the measured face on an object at its Yearbook (chronos's home), the moment face on a
plot at its Cue, a plan at its Implementation, or a process at its Initiated by
(kairos's homes). The domain does not model either face alone -- each is an engine's,
and one alone is a clock, not the whole; nor the material the years work in the fabric
or substance, which is decay's and wear's; nor the object's career of status, which is
biography's; nor a persona's felt duration, which is time-perception's. It owns time
read whole: the passage and the moment, and how they fall into step or out of it.

The spine is the classical distinction between _chronos_ and _kairos_, read not as a
split but as one domain with two faces. Kermode named the pair -- _chronos_ as mere
"passing time," _kairos_ as the moment "charged with significance" -- and read their
relation as the very stuff of how a life or a fiction is felt to have shape: the
interval between "tick" and "tock," the plain passage made meaningful by the moment
that falls at the right point in it. Read together, they give three bridges: the two
clocks in tune (timeliness), out of step (untimeliness), and come together each at its
fullness (the culmination, gathering chronos's maturation and kairos's ripeness).

## Origin

The classical philosophy of time and the chronos / kairos distinction, wired as a
composite over the two time engines.

| Source            | Key Work                                 | Scope                                                                                                                                                                       |
| ----------------- | ---------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Frank Kermode** | _The Sense of an Ending_ (1967)          | _Chronos_ (passing time) and _kairos_ (the charged moment) read as one domain with two faces, and their relation as the shape a life or fiction is felt to have. The root.  |
| **Paul Tillich**  | _"Kairos"_ (1922); _Systematic Theology_ | The _pleroma tou chronou_, the fullness of time -- the passage and the moment come together to their fullness, the right thing at the right time. The fullness bridge.      |
| **Norbert Elias** | _Time: An Essay_ (1984)                  | Time as a single institution of measuring and sequencing within which moments are placed -- the passage and the occasion held as one reckoning. The whole the bridges read. |

## Restrictions

What the composite refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The single faces (the atoms)**: The composite wires the two engines but owns
  neither. What a given passage is -- its age, sequence, endurance, maturation -- is
  the chronos engine's; what a given moment is -- its window, juncture, timing,
  ripeness -- is the kairos engine's. Time reads only what the two make as a whole; one
  face alone is a clock, not the whole time.
- **The material the years work (decay, wear)**: The composite reads the measure of
  time and the moment in it, not what time does to matter. The rust, the rot, the
  abrasion the years bring are decay's and wear's; time owns the passage and the
  moment, not the fabric they spend.
- **The object's career of status (biography)**: The charted biography engine reads the
  object's movement between commodity and singular status. Time reads the measured
  passage and the opportune moment, not the status either confers; the two are parallel
  readings of a thing in time, one of standing and one of the clock.
- **A persona's felt time (time-perception)**: How long a stretch feels, how real the
  future seems, the subjective texture of duration are the time-perception engine's, on
  the persona. Time is the objective measure and the occasion, not the experience of
  them; a moment is opportune, and a passage is long, whether or not anyone feels it so.
  And the whole of a thing's time made to **stand for** a character's own life is
  refused: that is authored key, the play's pitch, not the force.

## Encoding

Source to constraint. A composite root over two engine atoms it declares as
dependencies, with three bridges.

- **[time](process_time.md)** (the root): time whole, read over its two faces -- the
  measured passage (chronos) and the opportune moment (kairos) -- and the question their
  meeting forces: in tune, out of step, or come to fullness. Anchored by Kermode (the
  chronos / kairos pair as one domain).
- **[timeliness](process_time_timeliness.md)** (bridge): the moment lands right on the
  passage -- the act in season, the ripe moment met when the thing is matured to meet
  it, both clocks agreeing.
- **[untimeliness](process_time_untimeliness.md)** (bridge): the moment falls out of the
  passage -- too early, too late, the window missed, or the thing out of its era, the
  anachronism, the two clocks at odds.
- **[fullness](process_time_fullness.md)** (bridge): the long passage come to maturation
  meets the moment come to ripeness -- the right thing at the right time. Anchored by
  Tillich (the fullness of time); the composite's twist, gathering chronos's maturation
  and kairos's ripeness into one reading.
