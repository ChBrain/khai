# Instructions: Time

How a Playwright wires the time composite into a play. You wire by linking the
play's own content to the composite's files; the files stay untouched, they are
canon. The composite installs the chronos and kairos engines as dependencies, and
their laws arrive with them. Authoring guidance, not runtime content, and it does
not go on tour.

## Human

- The human sets the whole of a thing's or an unfolding's time: the measured passage it accrues and the moments it turns on, and -- the question that governs the read -- whether its passage and its moment fall into step or out of it.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, read time whole: cast the measured face from chronos on an object at its Yearbook, and the moment face from kairos on a plot at Cue, a plan at Implementation, or a process at Initiated by; then link the bridge the two make -- timeliness while the moment lands right on the passage, untimeliness while it falls out of step, fullness while the matured thing meets the ripe moment. Read time by how its two clocks meet, never by one alone (a ripe moment on an unready thing is untimely, not full), and never as the word time alone. You link to the composite, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- What a given passage or moment is made of belongs to the two engines this composite wires -- one face alone is a clock, not the whole time. The material the years work in the fabric is decay's and wear's; the object's career of status is biography's; a persona's felt duration is time-perception's. This composite owns time read whole -- the passage and the moment, and how they meet.

## Knowledge

- Time is time whole, read as a wiring of the chronos and kairos engines: chronos the measured passage a thing accrues (how old, in what order, how long endured, matured or not) and kairos the opportune moment that punctuates an unfolding (the window, the juncture, the timing, the ripeness), and its question is how the two clocks meet (Kermode's chronos and kairos as one domain with two faces). It is the first multi-cargo composite, cast on the object's Yearbook and the unfolding's own moment-chapter alike. It runs in three shapes. Timeliness is the two clocks agreeing -- the moment landing right on the passage, the act in season, the ripe moment met when the thing is matured to meet it. Untimeliness is the two at odds -- too early, too late, the window missed, or the thing out of its era, the anachronism. Fullness is the twist -- the long passage come to maturation meeting the moment come to ripeness, the right thing at the right time, gathering chronos's maturation and kairos's ripeness into one reading. Read a thing's time by how its passage and its moment fall into step, or out of it.

## System

- Do read time as a whole of two faces, never as one clock alone: cast the passage from chronos and the moment from kairos, and name whether they fall into step (timeliness), out of it (untimeliness), or come each to its fullness (fullness).
- Do let the reading turn on the phase relation, not either clock alone: the same ripe moment is timely on a matured thing and untimely on an unready one -- which it is, is how the two meet.
- Do let fullness gather chronos's maturation and kairos's ripeness into one reading: the matured thing met by the ripe moment, the right thing at the right time -- the composite's twist, the sibling of the whole family's ambivalence.
- Do not model the single faces (those are chronos and kairos), the material the years work (decay, wear), the object's career of status (biography), or a persona's felt duration (time-perception). Do not make a thing's whole time mirror a character's life (that is the play's pitch). Do not edit the composite's files; wire only from the play's side.
