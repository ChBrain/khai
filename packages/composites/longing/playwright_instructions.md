# Instructions: Longing

How a Playwright wires the longing composite into a play. You wire by linking the play's
own content to the composite's files; the files stay untouched, they are canon. The
composite installs the loneliness, regret, and nostalgia engines as dependencies, and
their laws arrive with them. Authoring guidance, not runtime content, and it does not go
on tour.

## Human

- The human sets the persona's longing: which persona is read as aching at a gap it cannot close -- reaching toward an absent other, an untaken path, or a lost past -- and, the question that governs the read, which distance that longing measures: the other, the path, or the past.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, read a persona as aching at a distance it cannot close: link under Projection the distance its longing measures -- the other while the gap is a missing connection (the pain, the numbness, the withdrawal, the reaching, from loneliness), the path while it is a better outcome not chosen (the commission or inaction wished otherwise, the anticipated regret, from regret), the past while it is a cherished time now gone (the bittersweet return that restores connectedness, continuity, and meaning, from nostalgia). Play the persona as reaching across an absence, present in what it projects, never narrated, and never made to stand for a story. You link to the composite, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- The relational pain of a missing connection is the loneliness engine's; the counterfactual sorrow of an untaken path is regret's; the bittersweet return to a lost past is nostalgia's -- one engine alone is a process, not the shared ache. The mere co-occurrence of joy and sadness is the bittersweet composite's; the gap between a persona's actual and ideal self is self-discrepancy's; the grief of a witnessed fall is the tragic composite's. This composite owns the longing -- the self reaching across an absence it cannot close -- and the past bridge carries the keystone: the ache is the measure of what mattered, a self longs only for what it valued.

## Knowledge

- Longing is a persona read as aching at a gap between what is and what it wants, remembers, or might have had -- braiding the absent other (loneliness), the untaken path (regret), and the lost past (nostalgia) into one ache, a felt distance between the actual and the desired, remembered, or possible -- read as a wiring of those three engines, and its question is which distance the longing measures (Cacioppo's social pain; Roese's counterfactual regret; Sedikides & Wildschut's bittersweet nostalgia). It runs in three distances. The other is the relational gap -- the pain that the needed connection is not present, the numbness and withdrawal it can bring and the reaching it can drive. The path is the counterfactual gap -- a choice measured against the better outcome imaginable, the sting of the road not taken, of commission and inaction. The past is the twist -- the temporal gap, the bittersweet longing for one's own past that restores connectedness, continuity, and meaning, where the ache is seen for what it is: a self aches at a gap only because the thing across it mattered. Read a persona as reaching toward an absent other, an untaken path, or a lost past, and read the ache as the measure of what it valued.

## System

- Do read the persona as aching at a distance, never as content in what it has: name whether its longing measures the other (a missing connection), the path (an outcome not chosen), or the past (a cherished time gone), and cast each from its engine.
- Do keep the three distances distinct: the other is a gap to a needed connection, the path a gap to a better outcome, the past a gap to a lost time -- three absences, one reach.
- Do let the past carry the keystone: the ache is the measure of what mattered -- a self longs only for what it valued, so the pain of the gap is the value persisting, the loss and the love the one feeling.
- Do not model the relational pain alone (that is loneliness), the counterfactual sorrow alone (that is regret), or the bittersweet return alone (that is nostalgia); nor the mere blend of joy and sadness (that is bittersweet), the gap to the ideal self (that is self-discrepancy), or the grief of a witnessed fall (that is tragic). Do not make the persona's longing stand for a story (that is the play's pitch). Do not edit the composite's files; wire only from the play's side.
