# Love: Reference

## Line of Work

Love as **composite**: the composition of a love -- which of Sternberg's three components it is made of, and how they shift over time. It is not a new engine but a wiring of three: **self-disclosure** supplies the intimacy (the closeness of two selves opened to each other), **desire** supplies the passion (the drive and attraction that pulls them together), and **loyalty** supplies the commitment (the decision to hold the love and stay). The composite adds no fourth phenomenon; it stages the corners into the shapes a love can take -- the two-cornered partial loves, the three-cornered consummate love, and the single corners that are not yet love but its ingredients.

The domain does not model the dispositional style of wanting a persona brings to loving -- passionate, companionate, pragmatic, obsessive, selfless -- which is the desire engine's; nor the bond and its security dynamics beneath the intimacy, which is the attachment engine's; nor the loss when a love ends, which is the grief engine's. It owns the composition itself: what a given love is made of, and how its corners come and go.

The spine is the structural account of love. A love is not one feeling but a triangle of three components -- intimacy, passion, and commitment -- that grow and fade at different rates: intimacy slowly and durably, passion quickly and volatilely, commitment by decision and then maintenance. The recognizable kinds of love are the combinations of which corners are present: romantic (intimacy and passion), companionate (intimacy and commitment), fatuous (passion and commitment), consummate (all three), and the deficient forms of a single corner alone. The three bridges are the two-cornered loves: romantic, abiding, fatuous.

The social psychology of love and its structure, wired as a composite over three engines.

## Restrictions

What the composite refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **Desire**: The composite models the structure of a love -- which components are present in the relationship -- not the dispositional style of wanting a persona brings to it. The resting style of loving -- passionate, companionate, pragmatic, obsessive, selfless -- is owned by the desire engine (the colours of love); love wires desire for the passion corner but reads the relationship's composition, a different level from the individual's style. A persona of any love-style may be in a love of any shape.
- **Attachment**: The composite models the intimacy corner, not the bond and its security dynamics. How a persona seeks or gives up on closeness to a secure base under threat -- secure, anxious, avoidant -- is owned by the attachment engine; love governs the presence of intimacy in a relationship, not the attachment style that colours how that intimacy is held.
- **Grief**: The composite models the love while it holds, not the loss when it ends. The adaptation to the irreversible loss of a love -- the heartbreak, the mourning -- is owned by the grief engine; love owns the composition of a living tie, and hands its ending to grief.
- **The atoms**: The composite adds no content its atoms already own. The opening of the self and its reciprocity are the self-disclosure engine's; the drive and attraction are the desire engine's; the allegiance and the exit-cost are the loyalty engine's. Love owns only the seams -- the shapes the three corners make together that none makes alone.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per file.

- **[love](process_love.md)** (the root): A love read by which of Sternberg's three corners -- intimacy, passion, commitment -- it is made of, the corners growing and fading at different rates. Anchored by Sternberg (the triangular theory) and Rubin (loving distinct from liking).
- **[romantic](process_romantic.md)** (bridge): Intimacy and passion without commitment -- the being-in-love, close and aflame and unpledged, held by feeling with nothing beneath to catch a fall. Wires self-disclosure to desire. Anchored by Sternberg (romantic love) and Hatfield & Berscheid (passionate love).
- **[abiding](process_abiding.md)** (bridge): Intimacy and commitment without passion -- Sternberg's companionate love, the long settled tie that outlasts desire because passion was never what held it. Wires self-disclosure to loyalty. Anchored by Sternberg (companionate love) and Hatfield & Berscheid (companionate love).
- **[fatuous](process_fatuous.md)** (bridge): Passion and commitment without intimacy -- the whirlwind, bound fast on a feeling before the slow work of knowing, the pledge left holding a love that has lost its reason once passion cools. Wires desire to loyalty. Anchored by Sternberg (fatuous love) and Fisher (passion's fast fade).
