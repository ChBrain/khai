# Freehand: Reference

## Line of Work

Freehand as **composite**: what an aversive disposition can do, and the room that decides whether it
can. It is not a third mechanism but a wiring of two: the **dark-triad** engine supplies the
disposition -- cold self-advancement with the empathy that would slow it turned low, resolving into
narcissism, machiavellianism, and psychopathy -- and the **deception** engine supplies what a persona
carrying it actually does, which is to build a false belief in somebody who did not consent to hold
it.

The joining is Jones and Paulhus's, and it is in neither atom. Their paper puts the two together
directly and finds that the three shapes do not deceive more or less than one another but
**differently**: the machiavellian deceives strategically and cautiously, picking moments and hedging
exposure; the psychopathic deceives impulsively, over-reaches, and is caught; the narcissistic
deceives upward, toward a version of the self it would like confirmed rather than toward a gain. That
is the composite's first claim, and it is why there is no such thing as being good at deceiving in
the abstract -- only at a kind of it.

The second claim is the twist, and it comes from the source the dark-triad atom already carries for a
different scope. Christie and Geis built the Mach-IV, which is in the atom; what is not in the atom is
their finding about **when** the disposition converts into an advantage. In their studies the high
scorers prevailed under a specific set of conditions -- face-to-face encounters, latitude to
improvise, and situations arousing enough to distract everybody else -- and did no better than anyone
when the situation was structured and the moves were fixed. The advantage was a property of the
setting rather than of the person.

Everything else follows from putting those two claims next to each other. The easy parts of a
deception -- constructing the impression and delivering it -- are available to anybody and almost
always land, since the target has no reason to be checking. The expensive part is maintenance, and
maintenance is where a room does its work: a false belief has to survive every later thing that
touches it, and the cost of holding it rises with every party who knows, every occasion it must be
repeated, and every record it must not contradict. So a room without records, without repeat
encounters, and without fixed roles is not merely permissive; it is the only kind in which the
disposition is worth carrying.

Hence the keystone. What reads as a superior person is a superior fit. The apparent competence belongs
to the setting, the persona is the constant across scenes and the conditions are the variable, and the
remedy is structural rather than personal -- taking the latitude out disarms it without anybody having
to name who it was aimed at, and the persona cannot adapt, because the disposition is not the sort of
thing that adapts.

The domain does not model either side alone -- narcissism, machiavellianism, and psychopathy are the
dark-triad engine's, and construction, delivery, and maintenance are deception's. Nor is it the
account a persona gives themselves afterwards, which is squaring's -- and this persona often needs
less of it, which is the boundary rather than an oversight. Nor is it a favourable self-view meeting a
contradiction, which is smarting's; nor the arrangement of a made thing so a choice goes one way,
which is artifice's, where the maker is absent and here they are in the room. It owns the conversion:
a disposition, a repertoire, and the conditions that price it.

**On the evidence, stated plainly.** The atoms are well founded and the joining is a direct empirical
result, but the composite's twist is the part that needs the care, and the evidence supports it in a
way that cuts against the popular story rather than for it. The dark triad does **not**, on average,
pay. O'Boyle and colleagues' meta-analysis of work outcomes found machiavellianism and psychopathy
related negatively to job performance and positively to counterproductive behaviour, with the
relations moderated by authority and by cultural context -- so a play that uses this composite to
explain somebody's success in general is making a claim the evidence contradicts. The Christie and
Geis situational findings are from the early 1970s, in laboratory tasks, and have not been replicated
as heavily as the trait measures have; they should be treated as a well-motivated account of the
moderator rather than as a settled effect size. The triad's structure is also disputed: several
researchers argue it reduces largely to a single antagonistic core, or to low Honesty-Humility in the
six-factor model, and whether the three add anything beyond that is an open question -- which matters
here, because the composite's first claim depends on the three being genuinely distinct. What should
not be taken from this composite is that a persona with this disposition will do well, or that people
who do well have it. The claim is conditional and narrow: where the room is unrecorded, improvisational,
and one-off, expect the disposition to convert into an advantage; where it is not, expect the same
conduct to convict its carrier.

## Origin

The personality psychology of aversive normal-range traits and the study of deception, wired as a
composite over the dark-triad and deception engines. The first row is the **joining** -- the published
work that puts the disposition and the deceit into one design and finds them to differ in kind -- the
second carries the twist, and the rest anchor the bridges and their bounds.

## Restrictions

What the composite refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The two sides (the atoms)**: The composite wires the two but owns neither. Narcissism,
  machiavellianism, and psychopathy are the dark-triad engine's; construction, delivery, and
  maintenance are deception's. Freehand reads only the conversion between them and the conditions
  that price it.
- **The account given afterwards (squaring)**: The reassigned cause, the settled dissonance, and the
  loosened standard are the squaring composite's. The boundary is substantive rather than tidy: a
  persona high on this disposition typically needs **less** of that machinery, because there is less
  discomfort to resolve -- and a play that gives them an elaborate self-justification has staged an
  ordinary persona instead.
- **A favourable self-view meeting a contradiction (smarting)**: The smarting composite wires the
  narcissism family on a refusal, reading what a punctured claim does. Freehand reads the disposition
  as a working instrument rather than as something being defended, and its persona is not smarting;
  where a play wants the wound, that is smarting's scene.
- **What is kept hidden and what it costs (opacity, carrying)**: Concealment, secrecy, and the labour
  of keeping are those composites'. Freehand's persona conceals in service of a move, and the cost
  they pay is exposure rather than the strain of holding something in.
- **Arranging a made thing so a choice goes one way (artifice)**: Choice architecture, guile, and
  captology are the artifice composite's, and there the maker is absent and the arrangement does the
  work. Freehand's persona is in the room and improvising, which is why the record disarms them and
  would not disarm an interface.
- **The record that outlives the occasion (writ)**: The act inscribed and travelling is the writ
  composite's. It is the single strongest thing this composite's room can be given, and the two meet
  there -- writ asks what a record does once nobody can check it, freehand asks what its mere
  existence prevents.
- **Who pays to enforce a shared thing (upkeep)**: Costly sanctioning and the second-order problem are
  the upkeep composite's. Freehand's persona is much of what the enforcement is for, and the boundary
  is one of side rather than of subject.
- **The tilts of judgement in general (bias, estimation)**: The catalogued slants and the family of
  judgemental errors are those engines' and composites'. Freehand runs on a disposition, not on a
  reasoning error, and the target's failure to check is ordinary rather than biased.
- **The operator as authored symbol (the play's pitch)**: A manipulator made to **stand for** a theme
  -- the schemer who mirrors a motif of corruption -- is refused: that is authored key, the play's
  pitch, not the force. Freehand sets what the room permitted; the meaning read into it is the play's.

## Encoding

Source to constraint. A composite root over two engine atoms it declares as dependencies, with three
bridges.

- **[freehand](process_freehand.md)** (the root): what an aversive disposition can do and the room
  that decides whether it can, read on the room and never on the persona alone. Anchored by Jones &
  Paulhus (2017) and Christie & Geis (1970).
- **[the make](process_freehand_make.md)** (bridge): the shared core and its three shapes, continuous
  rather than categorical, carried unchanged into every room. Anchored by Paulhus & Williams (2002)
  and the Short Dark Triad (2014).
- **[the play](process_freehand_play.md)** (bridge): the deceit each shape actually runs, with
  construction and delivery cheap and maintenance the part that separates them. Anchored by Jones &
  Paulhus (2017), and by Ekman and DePaulo et al. on why the first move usually lands.
- **[the room](process_freehand_room.md)** (bridge): the conditions deciding the outcome -- latitude,
  improvisation, and above all whether anything outlasts the conversation. Anchored by Christie &
  Geis (1970) and bounded by O'Boyle et al. (2012); the composite's twist and keystone, that what
  reads as a superior person is a superior fit, so the remedy is the setting.
