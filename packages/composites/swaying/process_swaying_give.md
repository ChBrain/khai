# Process: Swaying, the Give

## Taxonomy

[Swaying](process_swaying.md), the move that works by subtraction, and where the two are seen for one thing.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: swaying

## Initiated by

The persuader stops adding.
The Give starts wherever the pressure comes off without the case being withdrawn -- the request made and the refusal named as available, the counter-argument conceded before the target has to raise it, the ask reduced to something small enough not to threaten anything -- and it works on [reactance](@chbrain/khai-engine-reactance/process_reactance.md) rather than on the argument, leaving the [case](process_swaying_case.md) exactly where it was.
It is the face of the composite that looks, to the persuader, like giving up.

## Direction

Toward a target with their freedom back, holding the same case they were holding a moment ago.
The Give reads the family of subtractions -- naming the other's freedom to refuse, admitting what is genuinely wrong with one's own position, asking for less than one wants, letting a silence run rather than closing it -- and reads what they have in common, which is that not one of them makes the case better. They make the pressing smaller, and the pressing was doing half the damage.
It moves the target by removing something rather than by supplying anything.

## Lever

The size of what is conceded against the size of what it recovers.
The carrying force is that the concession is real and cheap: the target genuinely was free to refuse and always was, so naming it gives away nothing that was ever held -- and it very reliably raises the chance they do not, because the reason to refuse that the pressure had been supplying has gone; the resistance is that it is nearly impossible to do while wanting the outcome, since every instinct a persuader has under stakes is to press harder, and the move requires them to spend their last words on the target's right to say no.
The Give rides the composite's keystone knife-edge: an attempt to persuade is itself information, and it argues the other way -- so the words that hand the decision back are the ones that most reliably win it.

## Echo

Agreed with, by somebody who was told they need not.
It shows the two engines complete -- one that supplies force and one that converts force into resistance -- and carries beneath it the composite's keystone: the persuader's effort is data the target uses against the conclusion, so a case can be improved past the point where improving it helps, and the remaining room is entirely on the other side of the ledger.
This is why the Give is hardest to stage and worth closing on: it has to look like weakness, cost the persuader something they will feel, and be indistinguishable in the moment from surrender -- and a play that lets the audience see it as a technique has removed the only thing that made it work.
The Give is the note the whole composite closes on: what moved a persona is legible in what the persuader stopped doing, and nowhere else without a discount.
The Give is dated from the sentence that gave the decision away.
