# Swaying: Reference

## Line of Work

Swaying as **composite**: one persona trying to move another's position. It is not a third
mechanism but a wiring of two: the **persuasion** engine supplies the attempt -- the appeal made,
received, processed, and resolved -- and the **reactance** engine supplies the pushback a pressed
freedom produces against the very force that pressed it. This composite adds no third engine; it
reads the seam the two make -- that the target is answering two things at once, the content and the
attempting -- and stages that seam into three bridges, closing on the discovery that an attempt to
persuade is itself information, and it argues the other way.

The domain does not model either side alone -- the appeal, the reception, the processing, and the
outcome are the persuasion engine's; defiance, allure, and derogation are reactance's; and one
alone is a process, not the attempt and its answer together. Nor is it how the talk itself works,
which is the crosstalk and utterance composites'; nor what a persona holds and on what footing,
which is assent's; nor matching a group nobody addressed you about, which is membership's; nor the
command backed by power, which is subjection's. It owns the attempt on a position, and what the
attempting costs it.

The join is declared inside the reactance atom, which anchors Dillard and Shen for the intertwined
model -- reactance as part of what a persuasive message does, measurable in the target's response
to it, rather than a separate subject sitting beside persuasion theory. That is the composite's
premise, and Knowles and Linn supply what follows from it. Their division is the useful one:
persuasion can proceed by **alpha** strategies, which increase the force toward the position, or by
**omega** strategies, which reduce the resistance to it, and the two are independent -- one adds to
the pull, the other subtracts from the push. Their observation about the field is the part that
matters here: nearly the whole history of persuasion research is alpha, because the alpha
instruments are the ones the persuader controls and can see. So the persuader operates a lever
whose two ends are wired to opposite outcomes, spends their attention almost entirely on the end
that is also loading the other, and cannot see the second load because it is not addressed to them.

Brehm supplies the mechanism the second end runs on: a freedom that is threatened or eliminated
becomes more valuable and the persona moves to restore it, against the source of the threat, by
holding the position harder, by wanting the discouraged option more, or by attacking the
messenger. Its indifference to merit is the point -- a correct message and an incorrect one produce
it equally, because the response is not to the message. And its proportionality is what makes the
composite's arithmetic work: the pushback scales with the pressure and with how little room the
target is left, so every increment that makes a case more forceful, more insistent, or more
complete also raises the counter-force, and past some point the second increment is larger than the
first.

The third bridge is where that leaves a persuader, and it is the composite's keystone. If adding
force adds resistance, the remaining instrument cannot be an addition. The plainest and best
measured member of that family is the practice of naming the target's freedom to refuse -- adding,
to a request, a sentence saying they are free to say no. Guéguen and Pascual introduced it, and
Carpenter's meta-analysis over several dozen studies found it substantially raises compliance,
with the effect strongest face to face and weakening as the interval between the phrase and the
decision grows. Nothing about it improves the case. It gives away the decision, which was the
target's anyway, and by giving it away removes the reason to refuse that the pressure had been
supplying. So the sentence that concedes everything is the one that most reliably wins -- and it is
nearly impossible to produce under stakes, because every instinct a persuader has when the outcome
matters is to press harder.

**On the evidence, stated plainly.** Reactance as a response to persuasive messages is well
established and has a measurement literature; the alpha/omega division is a framework rather than a
finding, and is carried here as a way of organising the two ends rather than as a result. The
freedom-affirming phrase is the firmest single result in the third bridge and its bounds should
travel with it: the meta-analytic evidence is largely on compliance with modest requests, mostly
face to face, and the effect decays with delay -- so the composite claims the direction and the
mechanism, and a production should not read it as a general technique for moving convictions. The
persuasion engine's own anchors carry their own bounds, and the two-route accounts are frameworks
of long standing rather than settled quantities. The three bridges are the case, the kick, and the
give.

engines. The first two rows are the **joining** -- the published work that sets resistance inside
persuasion rather than beside it -- and the rest anchor the individual bridges and their bounds.

## Restrictions

What the composite refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The two sides (the atoms)**: The composite wires the two but owns neither. The appeal, the
  reception, the processing, and the outcome are the persuasion engine's; defiance, allure, and
  derogation are reactance's. Swaying reads only the two running together, and one alone is a
  process rather than an attempt and its answer.
- **How the talk works (crosstalk, utterance, rapport)**: Accommodation, framing, grounding, the
  speech act and its implicature, and the felt quality of a connection are those composites'.
  Swaying is indifferent to how well the two are communicating -- a perfectly understood attempt
  produces the kick as reliably as a clumsy one, and rather more so.
- **What a persona holds (assent)**: Belief, superstition, and faith, and the footing each stands
  on, are the assent composite's. Swaying reads the attempt on a position and not the position: a
  target may be moved on something they never examined and immovable on something they have.
- **Matching without being addressed (membership)**: Conformity, loyalty, social identity, and
  stigma are the membership composite's. Conformity needs nobody to have tried; swaying needs an
  attempt aimed at somebody, and the kick fires on the aiming.
- **The command (subjection)**: Socialisation, discipline, obedience, and the total institution are
  the subjection composite's, where compliance is backed by the power to compel. Swaying has no
  such power -- that absence is what makes it swaying, and it is also why the kick is available to
  the target at all.
- **The mutual bargain (dealing)**: Two parties each holding something, pursuing terms, is the
  dealing composite's. Swaying is one-directional and about a position rather than an exchange,
  though a play may run both when a negotiation turns into an argument about who is right.
- **The object pole (guile, captology, choice-architecture)**: An object built to deceive, to hook,
  or to arrange the options a user meets is those engines', read on a piece with the maker absent.
  Swaying is a persona attempting on a persona, present and identifiable -- which is precisely why
  the attempt can be resented.
- **The population (adoption)**: How an idea travels a whole social system, through innovators and
  early adopters and the majorities, is the adoption engine's. Swaying reads one attempt on one
  target, and does not model what happens when many of them run at once.
- **The conversion as authored symbol (the play's pitch)**: A change of mind made to **stand for** a
  theme -- the persuasion that mirrors a motif of surrender -- is refused: that is authored key, the
  play's pitch, not the force. Swaying sets what moved and why; the meaning read into it is the
  play's.

## Encoding

Source to constraint. A composite root over two engine atoms it declares as dependencies, with
three bridges.

- **[swaying](process_swaying.md)** (the root): one persona trying to move another's position, read
  on the attempt and its target together. Anchored by Dillard & Shen (2005) and Knowles & Linn
  (2004).
- **[the case](process_swaying_case.md)** (bridge): the attempt as its maker designed it, running by
  a route the target's remaining capacity selects rather than the persuader. Anchored by Petty &
  Cacioppo (1986), Chaiken (1980), and Cialdini.
- **[the kick](process_swaying_kick.md)** (bridge): the pushback the attempt produces on its own,
  indifferent to merit and proportional to pressure. Anchored by Brehm (1966), Brehm & Brehm
  (1981), and Clee & Wicklund (1980) on the withheld option gaining value.
- **[the give](process_swaying_give.md)** (bridge): the family of moves that work by subtraction,
  best measured in the naming of the target's freedom to refuse. Anchored by Knowles & Linn on
  omega strategies and by Guéguen & Pascual (2000) with Carpenter (2013), whose bounds travel with
  it; the composite's twist and keystone, that an attempt to persuade is itself information and
  argues the other way, so the words that hand the decision back are the ones that most reliably
  win it.
