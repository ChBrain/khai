# Instructions: Neighborhood-Cycle

How a Playwright wires the neighborhood-cycle composite into a play. You wire by
linking the play's own content to the composite's files; the files stay
untouched, they are canon. The composite installs the dereliction, gentrification,
and reclamation engines as dependencies, and their laws arrive with them.
Authoring guidance, not runtime content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human sets the arc: which place turns through decline and return across the play, and -- the question that governs the read -- whom its return is made to serve.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, read the place across time: link the bridge its cycle is in under Shown -- turnover while the place returns for capital and the community is displaced, stewardship while it returns for the people who held it, attrition while no human return comes at all -- and let the decline come from dereliction, the re-entry from gentrification, and the taking-back from reclamation, all over the decay they act on. Read the cycle by whom the return serves, never by the fact of return alone, and never as the word neighborhood-cycle alone. You link to the composite, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- What a given decline, re-entry, or taking-back is made of belongs to the three engines this composite wires -- one phase alone is a moment, not the cycle. The material decay beneath all the phases is decay's; the biological reclaiming of an emptied place with no human return is succession's; the feeling of the people carried through the turn is the persona's own engines'. This composite owns the arc and the question its return forces.

## Knowledge

- Neighborhood-cycle is the whole arc a built place turns through -- decline, capital re-entry, reclamation -- read as a wiring of the dereliction, gentrification, and reclamation engines over the decay they act upon, and its question is whom the return serves. It is the produced cycle, not the fated one: decline is done, not suffered, and return is chosen, not natural (Smith's see-saw of capital; Marcuse's paired abandonment and gentrification), against the old lifecycle determinism that read decline as a place's fate and used that reading to justify redlining. It runs in three shapes. Turnover is the extractive full turn -- the place reclaimed for capital, the community displaced, the revival that completes the loss for the displaced. Stewardship is the cycle kept for its people -- reclamation that recovers without displacing, the deliberate and effortful alternative. Attrition is the cycle with no human return -- the place written off for good, or the community displaced from one disinvested place to the next; where no human return comes, the biological one is succession's. Read a place's arc by which shape its return takes.

## System

- Do read the cycle as a composition of three phases, never as one blurred story of decline-and-revival: name whether the return is a turnover, a stewardship, or an attrition, and cast each phase from its engine, all over the same place's decay.
- Do let the return carry its question: a place saved is not simply a place served -- read whom it was saved for, and let turnover's revival and its displacement be one event, not two.
- Do let stewardship be earned and effortful (the land trust, the tenant union, the protected tenancy), never a thing that happens on its own when a place improves; and do let attrition genuinely abandon -- not every fall is a prelude to a rise.
- Do not model the single phases (those are the three engines), the material decay beneath them (that is decay), the non-human return of an emptied place (that is succession), or the people's feeling (that is the persona's own engines). Do not make the cycle mirror a character's rise and fall (that is the play's pitch). Do not edit the composite's files; wire only from the play's side.
