# Neighborhood-Cycle: Reference

## Line of Work

Neighborhood-cycle as **composite**: the whole arc a built place turns through --
decline, capital re-entry, and reclamation -- and, above all, whom the return
serves. It is not a new phase but a wiring of three, the second composite in the
canon to wire engines rather than composites (after `artifice`), and the first on
the place type: the **dereliction** engine supplies the decline, the
**gentrification** engine the capital re-entry, the **reclamation** engine the
taking-back, all over the **decay** they act upon. This composite adds no new
phase; it reads what the phases make across a place's life, and stages that arc
into its shapes -- the return that keeps, the return that displaces, and the
return that never comes.

The domain does not model any single phase -- each is an engine's, and one alone
is not the cycle but a moment of it; nor the material decay beneath all the
phases, which is the decay engine's substrate; nor the biological reclaiming that
fills an emptied place no human return arrives for, which is succession's; nor the
feeling of the people carried through the turn, which is the persona's own
engines'. It owns the arc and the question its return forces.

The spine is the produced cycle, read against the fated one. The older
neighborhood-lifecycle model -- the Chicago School's invasion-succession, the
stage theories of development and decline -- treated a place's fall and turnover
as a natural life-course, and that determinism was used to justify the very
redlining that dug the decline. The composite takes the opposite frame: decline
is done, not suffered, and return is chosen, not fated. Smith's rent-gap makes
decline and re-entry one see-saw of capital rather than two accidents; Marcuse
pairs abandonment and gentrification on a single market and names the serial
displacement that rolls the loss from place to place; and Jacobs shows that a
neighborhood's keeping or unslumming is a thing a community does, not a stage it
passively reaches. So the three bridges are the shapes of the arc, told by whom
the return serves: turnover, stewardship, attrition.

## Origin

The urban ecology and political economy of neighborhood change, wired as a
composite over the three cycle engines.

| Source                            | Key Work                                                     | Scope                                                                                                                                                                 |
| :-------------------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------- | :-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Ernest W. Burgess**             | _"The Growth of the City"_ (1925), the concentric-zone model | The origin of the stage model -- neighborhoods passing through occupation and re-occupation (invasion-succession). The root, read critically against its determinism. |
| **Edgar Hoover & Raymond Vernon** | _Anatomy of a Metropolis_ (1959)                             | The neighborhood life-cycle in stages -- development, decline, thinning, renewal -- the arc the composite reads, stripped of its inevitability. The root and bridges. |
| **Neil Smith**                    | _Uneven Development_ (1984); the rent-gap                    | Capital's see-saw: decline and re-entry as one movement, not two fates, so the cycle is produced by investment and disinvestment. The turnover bridge.                |
| **Peter Marcuse**                 | _"Gentrification, Abandonment, and Displacement"_ (1985)     | The paired abandonment and gentrification on one market, and the serial displacement that moves the loss from place to place. The turnover and attrition bridges.     |

## Restrictions

What the composite refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The single phases (the atoms)**: The composite wires the three engines but
  owns none. What a given decline is -- its disrepair, abandonment, disinvestment,
  ruin -- is the dereliction engine's; what a given re-entry is -- its rent-gap,
  upgrading, eviction, commodification -- is the gentrification engine's; what a
  given taking-back is -- its conservation, reconstruction, reuse, facsimile -- is
  the reclamation engine's. Neighborhood-cycle reads only what the three make as an
  arc; a single phase alone is a moment, not the cycle.
- **The material substrate (decay)**: The composite reads the human arc -- the
  withdrawal, the return, the intervention; the rust, rot, and crumble in the
  fabric that all three phases act upon are the decay engine's, the substrate the
  cycle runs over rather than a phase of it.
- **The non-human return (succession)**: Where the attrition bridge leaves a place
  with no human return, the biological reclaiming that arrives instead -- the wild
  taking the emptied ground -- is the succession engine's. The composite marks the
  absence of the human return and hands the non-human one across; it does not model
  the ecology itself. (The neighborhood-cycle composite surfaced succession as the
  natural next place-force build: the referenced-but-unbuilt neighbor of the
  no-return path.)
- **The people's feeling (the persona's engines)**: The grief of the displaced,
  the attachment of those who stayed, the hope or the exhaustion of a community
  across the turn -- these are the persona's own engines' (grief, place-attachment,
  and their kin). The composite delivers the arc of the place and the question its
  return forces; the feeling of the people carried through it is theirs. And the
  cycle made to **stand for** a character's rise and fall is refused: that is
  authored key, the play's pitch, not the place's force.

## Encoding

Source to constraint. A composite root over three engine atoms it declares as
dependencies, with three bridges.

- **[neighborhood-cycle](process_neighborhood_cycle.md)** (the root): the whole arc
  a built place turns through, and the question its return forces -- for whom.
  Anchored by the lifecycle tradition (Burgess, Hoover & Vernon) read against its
  determinism, and by Smith and Marcuse (the produced cycle).
- **[turnover](process_neighborhood_cycle_turnover.md)** (bridge): the extractive
  full turn -- the place reclaimed for capital and the community displaced, the
  revival that completes the loss for the displaced. Anchored by Smith (the
  see-saw) and Marcuse (paired abandonment and gentrification).
- **[stewardship](process_neighborhood_cycle_stewardship.md)** (bridge): the cycle
  kept for its people -- reclamation that recovers without displacing, the value
  held for the ones who bore the fall. The deliberate, effortful alternative to
  turnover.
- **[attrition](process_neighborhood_cycle_attrition.md)** (bridge): the cycle with
  no human return -- the place written off for good, or the community displaced from
  one disinvested place to the next. Anchored by Marcuse (serial displacement);
  hands the emptied place's non-human return to succession.
