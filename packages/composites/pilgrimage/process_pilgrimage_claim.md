# Process: Pilgrimage, the Claim

## Taxonomy

[Pilgrimage](process_pilgrimage.md), whose death it is by the end, and where the composite is seen for one thing.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: pilgrimage

## Initiated by

Somebody asks what the death meant, and is answered by a sign.
The Claim starts at the interpretation: a panel, a guide's script, an anniversary with speakers, a phrase that gets repeated until it is the phrase.

## Direction

Toward a meaning settled by the people who came rather than by the people it happened to.
The account that circulates is the one the visitors carry away, and [the account home](@chbrain/khai-engine-tourism/process_the_account_home.md) is not written by the family. It is shorter, it is about what the death stands for, and it is usually generous -- a life made into an example, a loss made into a lesson somebody needed.
The family's version is longer, more specific, and about a person. It has no channel. Nobody is suppressing it; there is simply no panel for it, and no visitor has an hour for it.

## Lever

The good of being remembered by many against the loss of being remembered as yourselves.
The carrying force is that the public account is a gift and refusing it would be strange: the dead are honoured, the death is not wasted, strangers travel to stand in the cold for somebody they never met, and a family that wanted their loss to matter has got the largest possible version of that; the resistance is that the version that matters is not theirs -- the person becomes the meaning, the meaning gets fixed by whoever wrote the sign, and a relative who thinks the phrase on the panel is wrong discovers they are one opinion among the site's stakeholders.
The Claim rides the composite's keystone knife-edge: the better a death is looked after, the less of it belongs to the people it happened to.

## Echo

A family standing at a well-maintained grave, listening to a stranger explain it.
It is where the composite is seen for one thing, and it is the loop the two engines make: tourism supplies the account that travels, and grave supplies an object whose whole relationship is maintenance, so the account belongs to whoever is maintaining.
This is why the Claim is the hardest of the three to stage and the only one worth closing on: the visitors must be sincere, the trust must be competent, the family must be grateful, and the audience must be able to watch something be taken by a process in which nobody takes anything.
A play that stages the visitors as ghouls has replaced the mechanism with a mob, and one that stages the family as ungrateful has replaced it with a grievance.
The Claim is dated from the wording on the sign and is not experienced by anybody as a transfer.
