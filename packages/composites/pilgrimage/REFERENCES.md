# Pilgrimage: Reference

## Line of Work

Pilgrimage as **composite**: what happens to a death when strangers start coming to the place of it. It
is not a third mechanism but a wiring of two: the **tourism** engine supplies the marking that makes
somewhere worth going to and the account that travels home, and the **grave** engine supplies the leased
plot, the burial right, and a bond that consists entirely of maintenance. This composite adds no third
engine. It reads the one thing the two make together, which is a death looked after by people it did not
happen to.

The warrant is accordingly integrative rather than primary: the sources below are the literature of
travel to places of death, where each atom's own REFERENCES carries the primary work of its side.
**Seaton is the joining.** His argument is not that people are morbid, and not separately that
cemeteries attract visitors; it is that travel motivated wholly or partly by an encounter with death is
a continuous practice from medieval pilgrimage to the modern coach party, and that such sites can be
graded by two things at once -- how central the death is to why anybody comes, and how far the visitor
stands from the person who died. That second axis is a claim about the two engines together. A plot is
made for people with the closest possible relation to the dead, and thanatourism is what happens when
the visitors have the most distant one, and Seaton is the account of what that distance does.

Both atoms are wired here for the first time, and an engine can be in 0..n composites: an atom carries a
phenomenon, and a composite carries a question asked of it. The pairing is structural rather than
convenient, because the two meet on a single object. Tourism supplies a visitor whose relationship to a
place is an hour; grave supplies an object whose relationship to anybody is a chore. Put them in the
same square metre and the arithmetic is immediate.

**The Arrival** is where the two touch physically. The plot acquires a marker -- a guidebook line, a
documentary, a route somebody drew -- and after that the people who come arrive holding an image of it.
They are sincere, they behave well, and they are often the only people still saying the name aloud;
none of them will drive three hours to weed. So attendance rises, hands do not, and the tributes they
leave are objects somebody else will have to clear.

**The Custody** follows from the upkeep outgrowing one relative. A trust, a council, or a society takes
it on, and the transfer is a rescue: the ordinary fate of a plot is a lapse, and here the term will
never be allowed to expire and the lettering will be recut before it fades. With the money comes a
standard, written for somebody arriving with no context -- presentable rather than decent, safe for
numbers, explained on a panel -- and the small absolute powers the next of kin held become a
consultation.

**The Claim** is the loop. The version of the death that circulates is the one visitors carry home:
shorter, about what the death stands for, and usually generous. The family's version is longer, more
specific, and about a person, and it has no channel -- not because anybody is suppressing it, but
because there is no panel for it and no visitor has an hour for it.

Hence the **twist**. **The better a death is looked after, the less of it belongs to the people it
happened to.** The two run on one mechanism: maintenance is the only relationship the object supports,
and whoever pays for the maintenance sets its standard, so the moment the upkeep exceeds what a family
can do the standard stops being theirs. What arrives is real care and permanent funding. What leaves is
the authority over how a loss is presented. Nobody takes it; it transfers with the invoice.

The composite is built so that nobody has to be behaving badly for any of it to run, and a play should
keep it that way. The visitors were sincere. The trust was competent. The family was grateful.

**On the evidence, stated plainly.** A composite cannot be firmer than the atoms it stands on, and both
of these were built with their weaknesses on the page: tourism's REFERENCES records an unresolved
disagreement in its own field about whether the staged encounter is a degradation or the ordinary
condition of travel, and grave's records that it has no experimental base at all. Everything below
inherits that. **Dark tourism is a young and contested field, and it is mostly conceptual.** Seaton's
continuum, Lennon and Foley's account of the modern form, and Stone's spectrum are typologies supported
by case description; the systematic study of why visitors actually go is thin, and what exists suggests
motives are mixed, mostly ordinary, and poorly captured by the label -- many visitors reject the word
tourist for what they are doing, which is itself a finding the field has not absorbed. **The literature's
centre of gravity is a different kind of site from this composite's.** Its main cases are mass death --
camps, battlefields, disaster sites, assassinations -- where the dead are numerous, the meaning is
already public, and no single family ever held the plot; this composite reads the case where an
identifiable dead person's grave acquires visitors, which is the literature's periphery, and it borrows
the mechanism across that gap deliberately rather than accidentally. **The custody claim is drawn from
heritage practice, not measured.** That funding brings a standard written for strangers is visible in
conservation management plans, interpretation policy, and the ordinary economics of a visited site, and
nobody has studied what it does to the families. **The Claim bridge is the composite's most speculative
leg.** That the circulating account displaces the family's is an inference from tourism's account-home
member and from the way interpretation gets written; it is plausible, it is not demonstrated, and the
study that would settle it -- asking relatives at visited graves what they think the sign says -- does
not appear to have been done. What should not be taken from this composite is that visitors are ghouls,
that trusts are appropriators, or that families are victims -- most families in this position sought the
attention, and the visitors are frequently the only reason the place still exists. The claim is narrow:
attendance does not supply maintenance, maintenance is bought, and the buyer sets the standard.

## Restrictions

What the composite refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The marking, the seeking, and the staging (tourism)**: How somewhere becomes a sight, the search for
  the unstaged, the host's performance, and the souvenir are the tourism engine's own movements. This
  composite links them and reads only their arrival at a grave.
- **The object, the right, and the lapse (grave)**: The leased plot, the burial right, the visit made of
  chores, and the ending nobody decides are the grave engine's. This composite reads what happens when
  the maintenance is taken over.
- **The mourning itself (grief, memory, lasting)**: The adaptation to loss and the bond carried forward
  are those packages'. The family here is a party to a decision, not a case study in mourning.
- **The rite (ritual)**: The funeral, the committal, the anniversary observance, and what performing a
  rite does to the people in it are ritual's. The composite reads the sign, not the service.
- **The public monument (the world, and Doss's territory)**: The war memorial, the national museum, and
  the purpose-built site of conscience are addressed to a public from the start and were never anybody's
  private plot. The composite reads the case where a family's object becomes one.
- **The heritage trade (the world)**: Conservation management, interpretation policy, visitor numbers,
  ticketing, and the statutes governing listed monuments are matters of practice and law. They set the
  format; this composite reads who is left deciding.
- **Who is entitled to a say (recognition, standing, heeding)**: What institutional acknowledgement
  does, and what a fair hearing buys, are those packages'. The Custody bridge reads only the moment
  standing becomes consultation.
- **A shrine as authored symbol (the play's pitch)**: A visited grave staged in order to **stand for** a
  theme -- the site that mirrors a motif of memory -- is refused: that is authored key, the play's
  pitch, not the force.

## Encoding

Source to constraint. A composite tree: one root and three bridges over two atoms.

- **[pilgrimage](process_pilgrimage.md)** (the root): people with no relation to the dead arriving at
  the ground the dead are kept in, read on who decides, carrying the twist -- the better a death is
  looked after, the less of it belongs to the people it happened to. Anchored by Seaton (1996).
- **[the Arrival](process_pilgrimage_arrival.md)**: the visitors and what they can and cannot do, so the
  plot gains attendance and no hands. Anchored by Seaton (1996), linking tourism's marker and prior
  image against grave's visiting.
- **[the Custody](process_pilgrimage_custody.md)**: who maintains it and to whose standard, buying the
  object a future and pricing it in decisions. Anchored by Lennon & Foley (2000) and Stone (2006),
  linking grave's next of kin and lapsing.
- **[the Claim](process_pilgrimage_claim.md)**: whose death it is by the end, where the composite is
  seen for one thing. Anchored by Walter (2009), linking tourism's account home.
