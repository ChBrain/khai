# Process: Pilgrimage

## Taxonomy

What happens to a death when strangers start coming to the place of it.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: pilgrimage

## Initiated by

People with no relation to the dead begin arriving at the ground the dead are kept in.
Pilgrimage starts wherever both are in play: somewhere turned into a place worth going to, through [tourism](@chbrain/khai-engine-tourism/process_tourism.md); and the marked plot held on a term by the people the dead belonged to, through [grave](@chbrain/khai-engine-grave/piece_grave.md).
This composite adds no third engine. It reads the one thing the two make together, which is a death looked after by people it did not happen to.

## Direction

Toward custody moving away from the family while the object gets better than it has ever been.
The composite wires the two and reads their working in three shapes: [the Arrival](process_pilgrimage_arrival.md), the visitors and what they can and cannot do; [the Custody](process_pilgrimage_custody.md), who maintains it and to whose standard; and [the Claim](process_pilgrimage_claim.md), the twist -- whose death it is by the end.
The three are not a sequence of events. They are one arrangement, and a question about who is entitled to decide.

## Lever

The rescue of a place that would otherwise have lapsed against what the rescue costs the people it was for.
The carrying force is that the visitors save the thing and are usually welcome: attention brings funding, a repaired stone, a term nobody is allowed to let expire, a caretaker, and a death remembered by more people than any family could manage -- and most families in this position want the visitors, many of them campaigned for years to get them, and the alternative on offer was a green stone and a reused plot; the resistance is that the same attention moves every decision that matters -- the wording, the hours, the access, the flowers, what the death is said to have been about -- to people whose whole relation to it is that they read about it.
Pilgrimage is read on who decides, never on who comes.

## Echo

A well-kept place that the family has to ask permission to sit in.
Pilgrimage does not close; it is a private death being taken into public keeping, gathered into one reading -- an arrival, a custody, a claim.
The twist is here: **the better a death is looked after, the less of it belongs to the people it happened to.** The two run on one mechanism. Maintenance is the only relationship the object supports, and whoever pays for the maintenance sets its standard -- so the moment the upkeep exceeds what a family can do, the standard stops being theirs. What arrives is real care, permanent funding, and a stone that will still be legible in a century; what leaves is the small absolute authority over how a loss is presented that the plot used to confer on one relative. Nobody takes it. It transfers with the invoice.
It hands off what it is not: the marking of a place as worth seeing, the search for the unstaged, and the account carried home are tourism's; the leased ground, the burial right, the visit made of chores, and the lapse are grave's; the mourning itself is grief's; the rite is ritual's; the public monument addressed to a nation is the world's; and a shrine staged to **stand for** a theme is authored key, the play's pitch, not the force.
The persona is dated from the last time they were consulted.
