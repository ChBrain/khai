# Instructions: Pilgrimage

How a Playwright wires the pilgrimage composite into a play. You wire by linking the play's own content to the composite's files; the files stay untouched, they are canon. The composite declares two atoms as dependencies and links their members by name -- you never copy an atom's reading into the play, you point at it. The Roadie plumbs the composite's law into Instructions Knowledge on deploy. Authoring guidance, not runtime content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human makes the rescue real before staging any loss: the plot was heading for a green stone and a reused grave, and the visitors are the reason it is not. Give every side its case -- the trust is a position in the composite, not a villain in it. And keep everybody decent, since the composite needs no ghouls and no ungrateful relatives and stops working the moment it gets either.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, a persona links under Projection which bridge they are standing in: whether they came or were come to, whether they hold the burial right or a budget line, when they were last consulted, and what they think the sign says. Read the composite on who decides, never on who comes. Keep it the arrangement: the marking of a place as worth seeing is tourism's, the leased plot and the visit made of chores are grave's, and this reads only what the two make together. You link to the composite, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- The marking, the seeking, the staging, the souvenir, and the account home are the tourism engine's; the leased plot, the burial right, the visit made of chores, and the lapse are the grave engine's; the mourning is grief's, memory's, and the lasting composite's; the rite is ritual's; what institutional acknowledgement does and what a fair hearing buys are recognition's and the heeding composite's; the war memorial and the purpose-built site of conscience were addressed to a public from the start and are not this. Conservation management, interpretation policy, visitor numbers, and monument statute are the trade and set the format. Whether a production's trust is careful or careless, and whether a family sought the visitors or was overtaken by them, are the team's review.

## Knowledge

- Pilgrimage is what happens to a death when strangers start coming to the place of it. It is not a third mechanism but a wiring of two: the tourism engine supplies the marking that makes somewhere worth going to and the account that travels home, and the grave engine supplies the leased plot, the burial right, and a bond that consists entirely of maintenance. It adds no third engine and wires on a persona at Projection, and it reads the one thing the two make together, which is a death looked after by people it did not happen to. Three bridges. The Arrival is where the two touch physically: the plot acquires a marker, the people who come arrive holding an image of it, they are sincere and often the only ones still saying the name aloud, and none of them will drive three hours to weed -- so attendance rises, hands do not, and the tributes left behind are objects somebody else has to clear. The Custody follows from the upkeep outgrowing one relative: a trust takes it on, which is a rescue, since the ordinary fate of a plot is a lapse and here the term will never be allowed to expire; with the money comes a standard written for somebody arriving with no context, and the small absolute powers the next of kin held become a consultation. The Claim is the loop: the version that circulates is the visitors', shorter and about what the death stands for, and the family's version has no channel, not because anybody is suppressing it but because there is no panel for it. The twist: the better a death is looked after, the less of it belongs to the people it happened to -- nobody takes it, it transfers with the invoice.

## System

- Do stage the rescue before the loss. Show the green stone and the expiring term, so the trust arrives as relief rather than as an intrusion.
- Do keep the visitors sincere. They are moved, they behave well, and some of them have a grief of their own with nowhere else to put it.
- Do put the chore next to the hour. One relative with secateurs and a bottle of water, in the same square metre as people who came for forty minutes and a photograph.
- Do let the tributes accumulate. What is left at the stone is real feeling and is also a clearing job for somebody, and both facts are true at once.
- Do write the standard for strangers. The gravel, the rope, the opening hours, the panel explaining who is underneath -- each one sensible, and none of them what the family would have chosen.
- Do make the wording the fight, and keep it quiet. A relative thinks the phrase on the sign is wrong and discovers they are one opinion among the site's stakeholders.
- Do close on a family being explained to. Somebody stands at a well-maintained grave and listens to a stranger account for it, correctly and generously and not as they would have.
- Do not model the marking, the seeking, or the staging (tourism), the leased plot or the lapse (grave), the mourning (grief, memory, lasting), the rite (ritual), or the purpose-built public monument. Do not make the site stand for a theme; that is authored key, the play's pitch. Do not edit the composite's files or the atoms'; wire only from the play's side.
