# Artifice: Reference

## Line of Work

Artifice as **composite**: the designed object read whole -- the settlement a made thing strikes among the five design forces, and whose ends that settlement serves. It is not a new force but a wiring of five, the first composite in the canon to wire engines rather than composites, and the first on the piece type: the **usability** engine supplies the honest confirmation, the **agency** engine the enlistment, the **ergonomics** engine the bodily fit, the **guile** engine the deception, and the **captology** engine the hook. This composite adds no sixth force; it reads what the five make together in one object, and stages that settlement into its shapes -- held for the user, turned to take, or fallen apart.

The domain does not model any single design force -- each is an atom's, and one alone is not a settlement but an ingredient of it; nor the making process by which a designer arrives at the object, which is a method's concern, not a piece's; nor the object's aesthetic surface as such, its beauty apart from its forces, which is its own domain. It owns the composition: the five forces held in one made thing, and the settlement they reach.

The spine is the synthesis view of design -- that a made thing is never one force but the resolution of many in a context, and that the worth of the object lies in whether that resolution serves the one who uses it. Simon set design as the science of the artificial: the artifact shaped at the meeting of an inner and an outer environment to serve a goal, so an object is read by the goal it is bent toward. Alexander gave the resolution its shape and its failure: form as the settlement of forces in a context, good fit where the settlement holds and misfit where it does not -- the warrant for concord and discord. Papanek gave the settlement its ethics: design is accountable for whom it serves, and a made thing can be turned against the good of its user -- the warrant for extraction, concord's mirror. The three bridges are the shapes of the settlement: concord, extraction, discord.

## Restrictions

What the composite refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The five forces (the atoms)**: The composite wires the five engines but owns none. What a given object's usability is -- its feedback, its honest confirmation -- is the usability engine's; its enlistment is agency's; its bodily fit is ergonomics'; its deception is guile's; its hook is captology's. Artifice reads only what the five make held in one object; a single force alone is not a settlement but its ingredient.
- **The design process (methods)**: The composite models the object, not the making of it. How a designer discovers the forces, iterates the form, and arrives at the settlement is a method's concern -- a way of working, not a made thing; artifice reads the object that results, and the settlement it keeps, not the path to it.
- **The aesthetic surface (its own domain)**: The composite models the settlement of functional forces, not beauty as such. An object's aesthetic charge -- its grace, its ugliness, the pleasure of its form apart from what its forces do to the user -- is a domain of its own; artifice reads whom the forces serve, which beauty may accompany but is not.
- **The general composition of any forces (arch)**: The composite models the specific design-force settlement, not the general fact that pieces compose. That any object is a bundle of forces read together is the architecture's premise; artifice is the particular five-force reading of the designed object, the affective-functional instance, not the general one.

## Encoding

Source to constraint. A composite root over five engine atoms it declares as dependencies, with three bridges.

- **[artifice](piece_artifice.md)** (the root): The designed object read whole, as the settlement of five design forces. Anchored by Simon (the artificial) and Alexander (form as the resolution of forces).
- **[concord](piece_artifice_concord.md)** (bridge): The forces aligned for the user -- the object that fits, tells the truth, and enlists openly, neither tricking nor hooking against the one who uses it. Anchored by Alexander (good fit).
- **[extraction](piece_artifice_extraction.md)** (bridge): The forces aligned for the maker against the user -- the hook and the trick and the coerced enlistment worn under the surface of service, the object built to take. Anchored by Papanek (design turned against the user).
- **[discord](piece_artifice_discord.md)** (bridge): The forces at war with each other -- usable but unfit, honest but hooking, no settlement struck, so the object misfits itself. Anchored by Alexander (misfit).
