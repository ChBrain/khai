# Artifice: Reference

## Line of Work

Artifice as **composite**: the designed object read whole -- the settlement a made thing strikes among the six design forces, and whose ends that settlement serves. It is not a new force but a wiring of six, the first composite in the canon to wire engines rather than composites, and the first on the piece type: the **usability** engine supplies the honest confirmation, the **agency** engine the enlistment, the **ergonomics** engine the bodily fit, the **guile** engine the deception, the **captology** engine the hook, and the **choice-architecture** engine the arrangement of the options. This composite adds no seventh force; it reads what the six make together in one object, and stages that settlement into its shapes -- held for the user, turned to take, or fallen apart.

The domain does not model any single design force -- each is an atom's, and one alone is not a settlement but an ingredient of it; nor the making process by which a designer arrives at the object, which is a method's concern, not a piece's; nor the object's aesthetic surface as such, its beauty apart from its forces, which is its own domain. It owns the composition: the six forces held in one made thing, and the settlement they reach.

The spine is the synthesis view of design -- that a made thing is never one force but the resolution of many in a context, and that the worth of the object lies in whether that resolution serves the one who uses it. Simon set design as the science of the artificial: the artifact shaped at the meeting of an inner and an outer environment to serve a goal, so an object is read by the goal it is bent toward. Alexander gave the resolution its shape and its failure: form as the settlement of forces in a context, good fit where the settlement holds and misfit where it does not -- the warrant for concord and discord. Papanek gave the settlement its ethics: design is accountable for whom it serves, and a made thing can be turned against the good of its user -- the warrant for extraction, concord's mirror. The three bridges are the shapes of the settlement: concord, extraction, discord.

The sixth atom is joined to the other five in the literature rather than by assertion here, on two seams. Thaler and Sunstein made the join themselves and in these words: the choice architect is set out by direct analogy to the architect of a building, whose design cannot be neutral, and the arrangement of options is put on exactly the footing Alexander puts form on -- a resolution of forces in a context that somebody makes whether or not they notice making it. That is the same claim the root already rests on, arriving from the other side. The second seam is the dark-patterns literature, which is the intersection of guile and choice-architecture stated as such: Gray and colleagues, and Mathur and colleagues at scale, describe the deceptive interface as a choice architecture bent against the person meeting it, so the two engines were already joined in published work before this composite wired them. What the composite adds on top of that join is the asymmetry the other five do not have -- that the arrangement admits no absent setting -- and the three consequences that follow, one per bridge: concord becomes a labour rather than a restraint, extraction acquires its deniable and most durable form, and discord acquires a case with no author at all.

## Restrictions

What the composite refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The six forces (the atoms)**: The composite wires the six engines but owns none. What a given object's usability is -- its feedback, its honest confirmation -- is the usability engine's; its enlistment is agency's; its bodily fit is ergonomics'; its deception is guile's; its hook is captology's; the arrangement of the options it offers is choice-architecture's. Artifice reads only what the six make held in one object; a single force alone is not a settlement but its ingredient.
- **The magnitudes (the atoms', not the composite's)**: The six forces do not carry equally strong evidence, and the composite inherits each atom's caveats rather than smoothing them. Choice-architecture in particular carries its own: the arrangement is a structural fact and needs no experiment, but the size of each facet's pull is a separate question with four different answers, and "nudging" as a category does not survive publication-bias correction. A play that reads a settlement as though every force pulls with equal strength has overclaimed, and the atom's references are where the correction lives.
- **The design process (methods)**: The composite models the object, not the making of it. How a designer discovers the forces, iterates the form, and arrives at the settlement is a method's concern -- a way of working, not a made thing; artifice reads the object that results, and the settlement it keeps, not the path to it.
- **The aesthetic surface (its own domain)**: The composite models the settlement of functional forces, not beauty as such. An object's aesthetic charge -- its grace, its ugliness, the pleasure of its form apart from what its forces do to the user -- is a domain of its own; artifice reads whom the forces serve, which beauty may accompany but is not.
- **The general composition of any forces (arch)**: The composite models the specific design-force settlement, not the general fact that pieces compose. That any object is a bundle of forces read together is the architecture's premise; artifice is the particular six-force reading of the designed object, the affective-functional instance, not the general one.

## Encoding

Source to constraint. A composite root over six engine atoms it declares as dependencies, with three bridges.

- **[artifice](piece_artifice.md)** (the root): The designed object read whole, as the settlement of six design forces -- five of which can be absent from an object and one of which cannot. Anchored by Simon (the artificial), Alexander (form as the resolution of forces), and Thaler and Sunstein (the choice architect by analogy to the architect, no design being neutral).
- **[concord](piece_artifice_concord.md)** (bridge): The forces aligned for the user -- the object that fits, tells the truth, enlists openly, and arranges its options toward what an informed user would have chosen, neither tricking nor hooking against the one who uses it. Anchored by Alexander (good fit); the sixth force is where concord stops being restraint and becomes work, since abstaining from the arrangement is not on offer.
- **[extraction](piece_artifice_extraction.md)** (bridge): The forces aligned for the maker against the user -- the hook and the trick and the coerced enlistment worn under the surface of service, the object built to take. Anchored by Papanek (design turned against the user) and by the dark-patterns literature (Gray, Mathur) for the seam where the trick and the arrangement are the same act; the arrangement is the deniable instrument, so an extraction that sheds its nameable forces and keeps the tilt is the mature form, not a lesser one.
- **[discord](piece_artifice_discord.md)** (bridge): The forces at war with each other -- usable but unfit, honest but hooking, no settlement struck, so the object misfits itself. Anchored by Alexander (misfit); the sixth force supplies its hardest case, the object tilting hard against its users with nobody in the building who chose the tilt or could name it.
