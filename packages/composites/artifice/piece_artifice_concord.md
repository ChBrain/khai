# Piece: Artifice, Concord

## Taxonomy

[Artifice](piece_artifice.md), the bridge where the five forces align for the user. Concord is the object whose forces pull the same way and that way is the user's good: it fits the body ([ergonomics](@chbrain/khai-engine-ergonomics/piece_ergonomics.md)), tells the truth of its state ([usability](@chbrain/khai-engine-usability/piece_usability.md)), and enlists openly or not at all ([agency](@chbrain/khai-engine-agency/piece_agency.md)), while it neither tricks ([guile](@chbrain/khai-engine-guile/piece_guile.md) absent) nor hooks against the one who uses it ([captology](@chbrain/khai-engine-captology/piece_captology.md) absent, or present and transparent in the user's own service). Alexander's good fit: the form that resolves its forces so cleanly that nothing in it fights the person who holds it.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: artifice

## Place

At the object that serves without cost to the user -- the tool that fits the hand and says plainly what it did, the door that opens the way it looks, the thing whose every force is turned toward the one using it.

## Load Bearing

What concord holds up is trust that survives inspection. The extractive object works until the user sees the mechanism; the concordant one works better the more closely it is read, because there is no gap between what it appears to do and whom it serves. Remove the alignment -- let one force turn against the user -- and concord is gone, whatever the surface still says; the trust rests on the forces genuinely pulling one way. This is the rarest settlement and the hardest to fake, because faking it is precisely the extraction it is not.

## Apparent

What shows is an object that simply works, and keeps working, and asks nothing hidden. What is built is the alignment beneath -- the fit, the honesty, the openness all turned the same way -- which does not announce itself because a thing that serves cleanly has no seam to hide. Concord is the least dramatic of the three bridges and the most valuable: it looks like nothing wrong, because there is nothing wrong.

## Yearbook

The history of a concord is the record of an object that wore honestly -- used for years without a betrayal discovered, closely inspected and found clean, trusted more the longer it was known. It is dated from the absence of the reckoning the other two bridges eventually meet: no mechanism unmasked, no misfit endured, only a made thing that served the one who used it and went on serving.
