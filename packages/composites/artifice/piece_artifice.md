# Piece: Artifice

## Taxonomy

A composite reading over six engine atoms (no canon file of its own): the [usability](@chbrain/khai-engine-usability/piece_usability.md), [agency](@chbrain/khai-engine-agency/piece_agency.md), [ergonomics](@chbrain/khai-engine-ergonomics/piece_ergonomics.md), [guile](@chbrain/khai-engine-guile/piece_guile.md), [captology](@chbrain/khai-engine-captology/piece_captology.md), and [choice architecture](@chbrain/khai-engine-choice-architecture/piece_choice_architecture.md) engines this package declares as dependencies. It models the designed object whole -- not any single design force but the settlement a made thing strikes among all six: the fit to the body, the honest confirmation, the enlistment, the deception, the hook, and the arrangement of the options, read as one object rather than six separate findings. This is the synthesis view of design -- Simon's artifact shaped to serve a goal, Alexander's form as the resolution of forces in a context -- so a piece is never one force but their composition, and the question the composite asks is whom that composition serves. Five of the six can be absent from an object; the sixth cannot, which is why it sits here and not merely alongside.

## Place

At the designed object entire -- wherever a made thing meets its user across all of its forces at once: the app, the door, the machine, the form, the room built to be used. Artifice lives not in any one seam but in the whole object, where the six forces are struck into a single settlement the user lives inside.

## Load Bearing

What artifice holds up is the object read as one thing. Each atom, alone, sees its own force and cannot see the family: usability reads the honest confirmation, captology the hook, and neither sees that they run in the same object toward opposite ends. Remove the composite and the object fragments into six separate readings, none of which is the object; keep it and the six compose into a settlement that can be named -- [concord](piece_artifice_concord.md) when the forces align for the user, [extraction](piece_artifice_extraction.md) when they align for the maker against the user, [discord](piece_artifice_discord.md) when they war with each other. The made thing that hooks and fits and deceives at once is not three findings but one object, and the composite is what bears it as one.

The sixth force changes what a settlement can be. An object can be built with no deception in it, no hook, no enlistment, no bodily demand worth reading -- but not with no [arrangement](@chbrain/khai-engine-choice-architecture/piece_choice_architecture.md), since a box is ticked or it is not, the options sit in some order, and the paths cost something to take. So the settlement is never optional and never empty: a maker who resolved nothing has resolved that one, by default and in nobody's name, and the object still tilts. This is what the composite bears that the atoms cannot -- that the honest answer to "whom do the forces serve" is sometimes "nobody set them, and they serve whoever the accident favours."

## Apparent

What shows is a made thing used -- a surface, a function, a thing that works or does not. What is borne beneath is the settlement of forces that surface is: whose ends the fit serves, whether the confirmation is honest, whether the ease is a gift or a mechanism, whether the object that appears to serve is quietly built to take. The whole ethics of the artifact lives in that gap between the object apparent and the settlement borne, and the composite reads the gap rather than the surface.

## Yearbook

The history of an artifice is the record of what the object did to those who used it over its life -- whether it fit them and told them the truth and left them free, or hooked and tricked and conscripted them, or simply fought itself and served no one well. A made thing is dated not from the day it shipped but from the settlement it kept: the concord that wore honestly, the extraction that took until it was found out, the discord that never cohered. The object is judged, in the end, by whom its forces served.
