# Position: ADHD, Hyperfocus

## Taxonomy

[ADHD](process_adhd.md), the attention that will not release -- the same ungovernance running the other way.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: adhd

## Has

The fourth movement, and the one that makes the compound a single thing rather than a list of deficits. Attention here is not scarce; it is [captured](@chbrain/khai-engine-attention/process_captured.md) and cannot be recalled. The persona works for six hours without eating, misses the appointment, does not hear their name, and surfaces to find the evening gone -- absorbed in something that was interesting, novel, or urgent enough to hold them without any intention being required. It is the same failure as the other three, inverted: attention was never under intentional control, so it does not release on command any more than it arrives on command. The mechanism is that the interval which fails elsewhere is here supplied from outside -- the task itself does the holding, so no [shifting](@chbrain/khai-engine-executive-function/process_shifting.md) is available to end it and no delay needs surviving because the reward is continuous.

The evidence here is thinner than for the other three and should be held as such. Hyperfocus is reported almost universally by adults with ADHD and is well described (Hupfeld, Abagis & Shah; Ashinoff & Abu-Akel's review), but it is not a diagnostic criterion, it lacks a settled operational definition, and it is not established as specific to ADHD -- absorption of this kind occurs without it. The engine carries it because a model that omits it cannot explain the twist, not because the measurement is as good as Barkley's or Sonuga-Barke's.

## Orders

Stay, past the point of choosing to.
The hyperfocus movement orders the persona to remain in what has taken them -- through the meal, the message, the meeting, the night -- and to experience the interruption of it as genuinely painful rather than merely unwelcome. The room sees concentration of a quality it envies and cannot reconcile with the rest, and stores it. It stages a persona whose attention, once engaged, is not held by discipline and cannot be ended by it either, so that leaving requires the same absent machinery as starting.

## Loses

The exit, and the alibi. Carrying this movement, the persona forgoes the ordinary ability to stop -- to notice the time, to break cleanly, to leave something unfinished on purpose -- and pays in the obligations that ran in parallel and were not perceived. But the larger cost is evidential, and it is what the root's twist is made of: having been seen to concentrate completely, the persona can no longer claim that concentration is the thing they lack. Every witness now holds a counterexample. What it trades is the credibility of its own difficulty for a demonstration of capacity it did not choose to give and cannot repeat on request.

## Drives

On that unreleasable attention:
what caught the persona keeps them, and ending it needs the governance that was never there. Hyperfocus is one of four movements, the inverse case, set beside the [behavioral inhibition](position_behavioral_inhibition.md) and [delay aversion](position_delay_aversion.md) that fail to start and hold, and the [default intrusion](position_default_intrusion.md) that leaves mid-task. That attention is ungoverned rather than absent -- and that this movement is the proof the room turns against the persona -- is the [root's](process_adhd.md); the capture of the spotlight by a salient object is the attention engine's, where this movement owns the specifically ADHD case in which the capture is total, prolonged, and as involuntary at its end as at its beginning.
