# Estimation: Reference

## Line of Work

Estimation as **composite**: how a persona judges a quantity or a likelihood it cannot
compute. It is not a new faculty but a wiring of three: the **anchoring** engine supplies the
reference value that entered first and drags the estimate toward it; the **availability**
engine supplies frequency read off the ease of bringing instances to mind; and the
**representativeness** engine supplies probability read off resemblance to a type. This
composite adds no fourth engine; it reads what the three share -- the substitution of a
tractable question for the one actually asked, made without the persona noticing the exchange
-- and stages that into three substitutions, closing on the discovery that coherence and
likelihood run in opposite directions.

The domain does not model any single force -- the dragging reference is the anchoring
engine's, the fluency-as-frequency is availability's, the resemblance-as-probability is
representativeness's, and one alone is a process, not the whole operation. Nor is it the
named tilts a persona brings to judgment before the evidence lands, catalogued as standing
positions routed through motive families -- that is the **bias** engine, which names the lean
where this composite works the operation; nor the weighing of options toward a choice, which
is the decision engine's; nor the frame an option is presented in, which is the framing
engine's, read in the crosstalk composite; nor the belief held and defended, which is the
belief engine's. It owns the estimating itself: a mind answering the only question it could
and mistaking that for the one it was asked.

The spine is the heuristics-and-biases programme. Tversky and Kahneman set the three in a
single 1974 paper: under uncertainty people do not compute probabilities but rely on a small
number of judgmental heuristics -- representativeness, availability, and adjustment from an
anchor -- which are economical and usually effective, and which produce severe and systematic
errors. Their 1983 work on the conjunction fallacy sharpened the last into the composite's
turn: a conjunction can never be likelier than either of its parts, yet a detailed,
representative story is routinely judged likelier than the plain statement it contains, so
the qualities that make an account persuasive are the qualities that make it improbable.
Kahneman later named the underlying move -- attribute substitution, a hard question silently
exchanged for an easy one, with the answer carried over unnoticed. And the ecological
tradition supplies the counterweight the composite keeps: heuristics are not defects but the
means by which a bounded mind decides in the time it actually has. The three bridges are
those readings staged: the anchor, the ready, and the likeness.

## Origin

The heuristics-and-biases account of judgment under uncertainty, wired as a composite over
the anchoring, availability, and representativeness engines.

## Restrictions

What the composite refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The single engines (the atoms)**: The composite wires the three but owns none. The
  planting, accessibility, and insufficient adjustment of a reference value are the anchoring
  engine's; the retrievability, searchability, and imaginability behind a frequency judgment
  are availability's; the priors, randomness, and conjunction of a resemblance judgment are
  representativeness's. Estimation reads only what the three make as one operation; a single
  engine alone is a process, not the whole.
- **The named tilt as a standing position (bias)**: The resting lean a persona brings to
  judgment before the evidence lands -- the catalogue of named biases routed through their
  motive families -- is the bias engine's. The two altitudes are distinct and both are needed:
  bias names _that_ a persona tilts and which named tilt it is; estimation works _how_ the
  judgment is actually made in the moment. Bias is the position; estimation is the operation.
- **The weighing toward a choice (decision)**: How a persona compares options, trades off, and
  commits is the decision engine's. Estimation supplies the quantities and likelihoods a
  decision may then weigh; it does not do the choosing.
- **The frame an option arrives in (framing / crosstalk)**: How a prospect is presented -- as a
  gain or a loss, in one description rather than another -- is the framing engine's, read in
  the crosstalk composite. Estimation is the judgment made once a question is posed, not the
  posing of it.
- **The belief held and defended (belief)**: What a persona takes to be true and how it holds
  it under challenge is the belief engine's. Estimation is the arriving at a number or an odds,
  not the conviction that results.
- **The misjudgment as authored symbol (the play's pitch)**: A persona's error made to **stand
  for** a story -- the anchoring that mirrors a theme of manipulation, the vivid fear that
  mirrors a moral about media -- is refused: that is authored key, the play's pitch, not the
  force on the persona. Estimation delivers the judging; the meaning read into it is the play's.

## Encoding

Source to constraint. A composite root over three engine atoms it declares as dependencies,
with three bridges, the substitutions a persona makes for a question it cannot compute.

- **[estimation](process_estimation.md)** (the root): judging what cannot be computed -- an
  easier question answered in place of the one asked, the exchange unnoticed. Anchored by
  Tversky & Kahneman (1974) and Kahneman (attribute substitution), with Gigerenzer as the
  counterweight.
- **[the anchor](process_estimation_anchor.md)** (bridge): the reference value that entered
  first, made accessible by its planting and adjusted from too little. Anchored by Tversky &
  Kahneman (adjustment from an anchor).
- **[the ready](process_estimation_ready.md)** (bridge): frequency read off ease of recall --
  retrievability, searchability, imaginability -- so the vivid and recent are judged common.
  Anchored by Tversky & Kahneman (availability).
- **[the likeness](process_estimation_likeness.md)** (bridge): probability read off resemblance
  -- priors unweighted, randomness expected to look random, and the conjunction preferred for
  its detail. Anchored by Tversky & Kahneman (representativeness; the conjunction fallacy); the
  composite's twist and keystone, that the better a story hangs together the more probable it
  feels and the less probable it is.
