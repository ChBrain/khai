# Instructions: Squaring

How a Playwright wires the squaring composite into a play. You wire by linking the play's
own content to the composite's files; the files stay untouched, they are canon. The
composite installs the attribution, dissonance, and moral-disengagement engines as
dependencies, and their laws arrive with them. Authoring guidance, not runtime content,
and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human sets the persona's account: which outcome or deed is read as being squared with the self-picture -- explained by where its cause is put, covered by a belief that has moved to fit it, or exempted from a standard that stays in force everywhere else -- and, the question that governs the read, how deep the repair had to go: the cause, the revision, or the suspension.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, read a persona as already reconciled to what they did: link under Projection the depth at which the account was repaired -- the cause while the placement is doing the work (the four corners of locus by stability: the actor's nature, the actor's effort, the standing conditions, the wind, from attribution), the revision while a belief has moved to cover a deed (the counter-attitudinal act covered by coming to mean it, the alternatives spread after a hard choice, the goal prized in proportion to its cost, from dissonance), the suspension while a standard has been switched off for one act (the act relabelled, the authorship displaced onto an order or a crowd, the harm shrunk below notice, the victim placed outside the circle, from moral-disengagement). Play the persona as sincerely holding the account, present in what they explain without being asked, never narrated, and never made to stand for a story. You link to the composite, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- The causal placement is the attribution engine's; the attitude bent to the deed is dissonance's; the selective carve-out is moral-disengagement's -- one depth alone is not the account. The standing catalogue of tilts is the bias engine's; the moral self that governs in advance is the conscience composite's; the wrong and the reckoning between two people is moral-account's; the sanction that fires when a harm is owned is guilt's; restoring the self by affirming a value elsewhere instead of editing this account is the self-affirmation engine's and the selfhood composite's; persisting in a failing course is escalation's; misleading another is deception's, where this misleads the self; judging someone else's conduct is moral-judgment's; circling without resolving is rumination's, where this resolves. This composite owns the ladder -- what is done to an account so the self survives what the account is about -- and the suspension bridge carries the keystone: the account is not a lie the persona tells but what the persona now holds.

## Knowledge

- Squaring is a persona read through the account that let them go on -- braiding the cause placed where it need not disturb the self (attribution), the belief bent to fit a deed that cannot be undone (dissonance), and the standard suspended for one act alone (moral-disengagement) into one repair -- read as a wiring of those three engines, and its question is how deep the repair had to go (Aronson's self-consistency recasting of dissonance as a threat to the self-concept; Heider's naive analysis and Weiner's locus and stability; Festinger's one-dollar result, Brehm's spreading of alternatives, the severity-of-initiation result; Bandura's four loci of moral disengagement; Steele's self-affirmation as the alternative). It runs at three depths, and the order is a cost ladder rather than a schedule. The cause is the cheapest: an outcome's why placed inward or outward, lasting or passing, so that a failure laid on a night's slack costs nothing where the same failure laid on a fixed lack costs everything -- and it is the only depth an outsider can argue with, since how others fared and how this one usually fares are evidence anyone can bring. The revision is reached when the deed was freely the persona's own and no placement will cover it: the act is fixed and the attitude is not, so the attitude gives -- the dull task becoming genuinely interesting for a dollar and not for twenty, the chosen option upgraded the moment it is chosen, the group loved most by those whose initiation was worst -- and it is held in good faith, so there is no insincerity to expose. The suspension is the twist -- the morals not abandoned wholesale but switched off at one of four points, the act, the agency, the harm, or the victim -- where the ladder is seen for what it is: each repair goes deeper and shows less, so the deepest cannot even be located, because the persona will still tell you the act would be wrong for someone else and will be telling the truth. Read a persona as the last available witness to their own conduct, and read an explanation offered unprompted as the repair itself rather than a report on one.

## System

- Do read the persona as already reconciled, never as reporting neutrally on what they did: name whether the cause (the placement), the revision (the belief moved), or the suspension (the standard switched off) is at work, and cast each from its engines.
- Do keep the depths distinct: the cause moves only the explanation, the revision moves what the persona believes, the suspension moves nothing and exempts one act.
- Do let the suspension carry the keystone: the deeper the repair, the less it shows -- and the persona keeps the standard in force for everyone else.
- Do remember none of it is felt as a change: a revised belief has no date of arrival, and a play that has a persona notice themselves rationalising has flattened the engine into confession.
- Do remember the cheapest repair is tried first: a persona who reaches straight for the carve-out when a placement would have covered it is being played as a villain rather than as a self.
- Do remember there is an exit that is not on the ladder: a self threatened in one domain can be restored by affirming a value in another, which dissolves the pressure to square this account at all -- that is self-affirmation, not a fourth depth.
- Do not model the standing tilt (that is bias), the moral self that governs in advance (that is conscience), the reckoning between two people (that is moral-account), the sanction that fires on an owned harm (that is guilt), the persistence in a failing course (that is escalation), the misleading of another (that is deception), or the judging of someone else (that is moral-judgment). Do not make the account stand for a story (that is the play's pitch). Do not edit the composite's files; wire only from the play's side.
