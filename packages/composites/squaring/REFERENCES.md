# Squaring: Reference

## Line of Work

Squaring as **composite**: how a persona squares what happened with who they take themselves to
be. It is not a fourth mechanism but a wiring of three: the **attribution** engine supplies the
cause placed where it need not disturb the self-picture, the **dissonance** engine supplies the
belief bent to fit a deed that cannot be undone, and the **moral-disengagement** engine supplies
the standard switched off for one act alone. This composite adds no fourth engine; it reads the
seam the three make -- one repair reached for at three depths, each deeper one taken up when the
shallower will not hold -- and stages that seam into three bridges, closing on the discovery that
the deepest repair is the one the persona is least able to see.

The domain does not model any single depth -- disposition, effort, circumstance, and chance are
the attribution engine's; induced compliance, free choice, and earning are dissonance's;
justification, displacement, minimizing, and blaming are moral-disengagement's; and one alone is a
process, not the account. Nor is it the standing catalogue of tilts held as positions -- the
self-serving slant, the correspondence bias -- which is the bias engine's; nor the moral self that
governs conduct in advance, which is the conscience composite's; nor the wrong and the reckoning
between two people, which is the moral-account composite's. It owns the ladder: what a persona
does to an account so that the self survives what the account is about.

The spine is Aronson's recasting of dissonance, which is what makes the three one story. Festinger
stated the original theory in terms of inconsistency among any cognitions; Aronson's
self-consistency revision located the bite specifically in a threat to a consistent, competent
self-concept -- dissonance is sharpest when what the persona did is at odds with who the persona
takes themselves to be. Read back through that revision, Heider's naive analysis of action and
Weiner's causal dimensions stop being a neutral account of explanation and become the first and
cheapest place a self-picture is defended: a cause placed outside the actor, or placed on
something passing, costs the self nothing, and the placement is made fast and under the pull of
what flatters. Bandura's social-cognitive account then supplies the depth beneath revision --
moral self-sanctions selectively disengaged at one of four loci in the self-regulatory process,
with the standards left in force everywhere else, so that otherwise-decent people do harm and
still regard themselves as decent. The three literatures were built separately and describe one
escalating repair. Steele's self-affirmation theory marks the boundary rather than the sequence:
a self threatened in one domain can be restored by affirming a value in an unrelated one, which
dissolves the pressure to repair the account of the threatening act at all -- the alternative to
squaring, not a stage of it. The three bridges are those three depths: the cause, the revision,
and the suspension.

## Origin

The social psychology of causal explanation, cognitive dissonance, and moral self-regulation,
wired as a composite over the attribution, dissonance, and moral-disengagement engines.

## Restrictions

What the composite refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The single depths (the atoms)**: The composite wires the three but owns none. Disposition,
  effort, circumstance, and chance are the attribution engine's; induced compliance, free choice,
  and earning are dissonance's; justification, displacement, minimizing, and blaming are
  moral-disengagement's. Squaring reads only the ladder the three make; one depth alone is a
  process, not the account.
- **The standing tilt (bias)**: The systematic, catalogued slants -- the self-serving bias, the
  correspondence bias, the fundamental attribution error -- held as standing positions are the
  bias engine's, and the attribution engine already delegates them there. Squaring reads the
  repair as it happens in a particular case, not the tilt that is always present.
- **The moral self that governs in advance (conscience)**: How central being moral is to a
  persona, the attitudes held as absolutes, and the running moral balance drawn on are the
  conscience composite's -- prospective, and moral throughout. Squaring is the account given
  afterward, and most of it is not moral at all.
- **The wrong and the reckoning between two (moral-account)**: The transgressor's felt debt, the
  dyad's spoken account, and the wronged party's grievance are the moral-account composite's.
  Squaring is what a persona does so that no reckoning is required.
- **The sanction that fires (guilt)**: The self-condemnation that arrives when a harm is owned
  rather than exempted is the guilt engine's. Squaring reads what happens instead of it.
- **Restoring the self elsewhere (self-affirmation, selfhood)**: Repairing a threatened
  self-integrity by affirming a value in an unrelated domain is the self-affirmation engine's and
  the selfhood composite's. It is the alternative to squaring -- it dissolves the pressure rather
  than editing the account -- and the two should not be run as one move.
- **Persisting in a failing course (escalation)**: Throwing more after a losing commitment is the
  escalation engine's, and the dissonance engine already delegates it there. Squaring reads the
  account, not the continued investment.
- **Misleading another (deception)**: The management of another's belief is the deception
  engine's. Squaring is the case where the audience is the persona.
- **Judging someone else's conduct (moral-judgment)**: The lens by which a persona reads another's
  act as wrong is the moral-judgment engine's. Squaring is the first-person case.
- **Circling without resolving (rumination)**: Repetitive, passive dwelling on distress is the
  rumination engine's. Squaring resolves -- that is exactly the problem with it.
- **The account as authored symbol (the play's pitch)**: An excuse made to **stand for** a story
  -- the rationalisation that mirrors a theme of self-deceit -- is refused: that is authored key,
  the play's pitch, not the force on the persona. Squaring sets the account; the meaning read into
  it is the play's.

## Encoding

Source to constraint. A composite root over three engine atoms it declares as dependencies, with
three bridges, the depths at which an account can be edited.

- **[squaring](process_squaring.md)** (the root): how a persona squares what happened with who
  they take themselves to be -- one repair at three depths, each deeper one taken up when the
  shallower will not hold. Anchored by Aronson's self-consistency revision.
- **[the cause](process_squaring_cause.md)** (bridge): the outcome's why placed on locus and
  stability, the cheapest repair, since a cause put outside or put down as passing leaves the self
  untouched -- and the only one an outsider holding better evidence can argue with. Anchored by
  Heider (1958) and Weiner (1985).
- **[the revision](process_squaring_revision.md)** (bridge): the belief bent to fit a deed that
  cannot be undone -- the counter-attitudinal act covered by coming to mean it, the alternatives
  spread after a choice, the goal prized for its cost -- and sincerely held, so there is no
  insincerity to expose. Anchored by Festinger (1957), Festinger & Carlsmith (1959), Brehm (1956),
  and Aronson & Mills (1959).
- **[the suspension](process_squaring_suspension.md)** (bridge): the moral standard switched off
  at the act, the agency, the harm, or the victim, with the standards left in force everywhere
  else. Anchored by Bandura (1999, 2002); the composite's twist and keystone, that each repair
  goes deeper and shows less -- and the deepest cannot be located at all, because the persona will
  still say the act would be wrong for someone else and will be telling the truth.
