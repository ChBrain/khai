# Accent: Reference

## Line of Work

Accent as **composite**: what it costs a persona to be understood in a room, and who the room charges
for it. It is not a third mechanism but a wiring of two: the **language** engine supplies the first
gate -- five channels each running at one of four widths, from a tongue gripped as a foreign object up
to one that needs no grip, with the widths moving independently of each other -- and the **register**
engine supplies the second, a specialist code layered on the base tongue that binds insiders and gates
outsiders at once.

The joining is Lev-Ari and Keysar's, and it is in neither atom. Their question was why non-native
speakers are believed less, and their answer is not prejudice. Accented speech is harder to process,
listeners experience that difficulty without a label attached to it, and they resolve it the way
difficulty is usually resolved -- as a property of what is being said. Statements delivered in an
accent were judged less truthful than the same statements delivered without one, and the effect
persisted when participants were told about it, which is what makes it a mechanism rather than an
attitude. The difficulty is felt in the hearer and charged to the speaker.

That is why the composite needs both atoms and why it reads the crossing rather than the persona.
From language: the width, and the fact that widths do not move together. A persona may read a tongue
easily and speak it haltingly, catch every joke and be unable to make one, think in one language and
answer in another -- and a room takes the narrowest channel it can see as the measure of the whole. The
assembling is real work and it is invisible, so the capacity spent building each sentence is capacity
not going into timing, wit, or the graceful qualification, and the room meets only what was left.
From register: a second gate that is independent of the first. The code sits on top of the base tongue
and is acquired separately, so a persona at native width can be entirely outside it while a persona at
borrowed width may command it precisely because it is what they were taught. Its closure is usually a
side effect rather than a wall anybody built -- the compression that makes shorthand fast is the
compression that makes it opaque -- and the only remedy the atom names, translation, is a cost
somebody has to volunteer to pay.

Hence the keystone. The two gates compound and produce the same signature at the listener's end,
because what a listener registers is not which gate is in the way, only that this is hard. So a
persona speaking at a borrowed width inside a closed register is discounted twice for one crossing,
and the harder they work to be understood, the less they are believed -- the work showing up in the
room as difficulty, and the difficulty being billed to them.

This composite is the third in the canon to run on a misattributed processing experience, and the
family is worth naming because the boundaries matter. The **bias** engine owns the tilt itself, held as
a standing position under ease. **Glide** reads a persona steering their own study by that smoothness.
**Anomaly** reads a charged rejection standing in for a sorting that would not resolve. Accent reads
the case where the effort belongs to a listener and the cost lands on a speaker. Same underlying fact
about how fluency is read; four different questions asked of it, and only bias catalogues the tilt.

It is the second composite to wire the register engine. Utterance runs it with implicature, speech act,
and tone, as one of the ways a persona talks; here it is run as a gate somebody is standing outside.
Same engine, different question, and neither composite owns it.

The domain does not model either side alone -- the channels and their widths are the language engine's,
and shorthand, badge, closure, and translation are register's. It owns the crossing: two gates, the
effort of passing them, and the party who is charged.

**On the evidence, stated plainly.** The joining is a well-designed and much-replicated result and the
mechanism it identifies -- processing fluency misread as truth -- is one of the better-established
findings in the area, supported independently by the illusory-truth and fluency-and-judgement
literatures. What should be held more loosely is its reach. Lev-Ari and Keysar tested trivia
statements in a laboratory, and the size of the effect in a real room, where a listener has a great
deal of other information about a speaker, is not something that study establishes. Accent also
carries social meaning that has nothing to do with processing -- status, nationality, class -- and
disentangling the fluency mechanism from those is genuinely hard; the composite models the fluency
route and does not claim it is the only one or the largest one. The language engine's four widths are
a khai construct rather than a measured scale, though they sit on a real and well-evidenced
distinction between held and inhabited language, and the finding that the widths move independently
across channels is standard in second-language research. The register literature is
sociolinguistic and descriptive. What should not be taken from this composite is that any
under-estimation of a speaker is this mechanism, or that listeners who discount are bigots. The claim
is narrow and conditional: where a crossing costs the listener effort, expect the effort to be
resolved as a judgement about the speaker, and expect neither party to be able to see it happen.

## Origin

Second-language and psycholinguistic research, sociolinguistics, and the psychology of processing
fluency, wired as a composite over the language and register engines. The first row is the **joining**
-- the published work that traces a judgement about a speaker back to the listener's own effort -- and
the rest anchor the individual bridges and their bounds.

## Restrictions

What the composite refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The two sides (the atoms)**: The composite wires the two but owns neither. The five channels and
  their four widths are the language engine's; shorthand, badge, closure, and translation are
  register's. Accent reads only the crossing the two make and what it costs.
- **How a persona talks (utterance)**: The utterance composite wires this same register engine with
  implicature, speech act, and tone, reading a persona in conversation -- what is meant past what is
  said, and how it is pitched. Accent runs the same engine as a gate somebody is standing outside,
  and its subject is the listener's bill rather than the speaker's craft.
- **The tilt itself (bias)**: A persona who trusts what processes smoothly -- the fluency heuristic,
  the illusory-truth effect -- is the bias engine's, catalogued there as a standing position under
  ease. Accent does not restate it; it reads one case where the smoothness belongs to the crossing
  and the deduction lands on somebody.
- **Steering one's own study by smoothness (glide)**: The glide composite reads a persona whose
  judgments of their own learning track fluency and who then reallocates effort on the reading. Same
  underlying fact, and the party harmed is the persona themselves rather than somebody they are
  listening to.
- **A rejection standing in for a sorting (anomaly)**: The anomaly composite reads a charged
  rejection arriving where a classification would not resolve. Accent's listener reaches a verdict
  rather than a revulsion, and about a person rather than a thing.
- **A role and a read that disagree (unbecoming)**: The unbecoming composite's scissors close on
  conduct -- falling short disqualifies and meeting the role penalises. Accent's discount closes on
  delivery, needs no role at all, and would run on the same persona in any position.
- **Managing a face in front of others (presentation)**: Impression management, front and backstage,
  and emotional labour are the presentation composite's, and they are what a persona under this
  pressure may attempt. Doing it well does not remove the discount, because the discount is not
  applied to the impression.
- **The social meanings of accent (not modelled here)**: Status, nationality, and class read off a
  way of speaking are real, are often larger than this mechanism, and belong with standing,
  intergroup, and unbecoming. This composite models the fluency route only and should never be
  offered as an account of the rest.
- **The foreignness as authored symbol (the play's pitch)**: A way of speaking made to **stand for** a
  theme -- the outsider's voice that mirrors a motif of exclusion -- is refused: that is authored key,
  the play's pitch, not the force. Accent sets what the crossing cost; the meaning read into it is the
  play's.

## Encoding

Source to constraint. A composite root over two engine atoms it declares as dependencies, with three
bridges.

- **[accent](process_accent.md)** (the root): what it costs a persona to be understood and who the
  room charges for it, read on the room's effort and never on the persona's competence. Anchored by
  Lev-Ari & Keysar (2010).
- **[the width](process_accent_width.md)** (bridge): how tightly each channel grips the tongue, read
  per channel since the widths do not move together, with the assembling paid for before anything is
  heard. Anchored by the second-language processing literature.
- **[the code](process_accent_code.md)** (bridge): the register layered above, a second and
  independent gate whose closure is usually a side effect of an efficiency. Anchored by Halliday
  (1978), Biber (1988), and Bourdieu (1991).
- **[the discount](process_accent_discount.md)** (bridge): the room's own effort resolved as a
  judgement about the speaker. Anchored by Lev-Ari & Keysar (2010) and by Reber & Schwarz (1999) on
  the underlying mechanism; the composite's twist and keystone, that the harder a persona works to be
  understood the less they are believed, and that the two gates compound into one bill.
