# Instructions: Accent

How a Playwright wires the accent composite into a play. You wire by linking the play's own
content to the composite's files; the files stay untouched, they are canon. The composite
installs the language and register engines as dependencies, and their laws arrive with them.
Authoring guidance, not runtime content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human sets which personas are speaking through a gate, which rooms run a code, and who ends up believed.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, read a persona getting meaning across and link under Projection what is running: the width while a channel holds the tongue at some grip, borrowed, carried, worn, or native, and per channel rather than per persona (from language), the code while a register is running as shorthand, badge, closure, or translation (from register), the discount while a listener is resolving their own effort as a judgement about the speaker. Read the room's effort and never the persona's competence, since the competence is not what is being measured. You link to the composite, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- The five channels and their four widths are the language engine's; shorthand, badge, closure, and translation are register's -- one alone is a process or a position, not the crossing. How a persona talks is the utterance composite's, which wires this same register engine among the ways a persona speaks where this runs it as a gate somebody stands outside; the tilt that trusts what processes smoothly is the bias engine's, catalogued there as a standing position, and this composite does not restate it; a persona steering their own study by that same smoothness is glide's, where the party harmed is the persona themselves; a rejection standing in for a failed sorting is anomaly's, which reaches a revulsion about a thing where this reaches a verdict about a person; a role and a read that disagree is unbecoming's, whose scissors close on conduct where this closes on delivery; managing a face is presentation's, and doing it well does not remove the discount. The social meanings of a way of speaking -- status, nationality, class -- are standing's, intergroup's, and unbecoming's, are often larger than this mechanism, and are not modelled here. This composite owns the crossing -- and the discount bridge carries the keystone: the harder a persona works to be understood, the less they are believed.

## Knowledge

- Accent is a persona read while they get meaning across a gap in a room that will price the effort -- braiding the first gate (language: five channels, each running at one of four widths from a tongue gripped as a foreign object up to one that needs no grip, with the widths moving independently) with the second (register: a specialist code layered on the base tongue, binding insiders and gating outsiders at once). The join is Lev-Ari and Keysar's, and it is in neither atom. Their question was why non-native speakers are believed less, and their answer is not prejudice: accented speech is harder to process, listeners experience that difficulty without a label attached to it, and they resolve it the way difficulty is usually resolved -- as a property of what is being said. The same statements were judged less truthful in an accent, and the effect persisted when participants were told about it. It runs in three faces. The width is read per channel and not per persona, which is the thing rooms get wrong: a persona may read a tongue easily and speak it haltingly, catch every joke and be unable to make one, think in one language and answer in another, and a room takes the narrowest channel it can see as the measure of the whole. The assembling is real work and invisible, so capacity spent building each sentence is capacity not going into timing, wit, or the graceful qualification, and the room meets only what was left. The code is a second and independent gate: it sits on top of the base tongue and is acquired separately, so a native speaker can be entirely outside it while somebody at borrowed width may command it because it is what they were taught, and its closure is usually a side effect rather than a wall, since the compression that makes shorthand fast is the compression that makes it opaque. The discount is the twist: a listener does not feel effort as effort, they feel the material as less clear, and from less clear they get to less considered, less certain, and less likely to be right. Here the two are seen for one thing: what a listener registers is not which gate is in the way, only that this is hard -- so the gates compound, a persona at a borrowed width inside a closed register is discounted twice for one crossing, and each deduction is invisible to the person making it.

## System

- Do read the room's effort and never the persona's competence: name whether the width (a channel gripping the tongue), the code (a register gating), or the discount (a listener charging their effort onward) is running, and cast each from its engines.
- Do set the widths per channel and let them disagree, since a persona who follows everything and speaks haltingly is the ordinary case and the one a room most reliably misreads.
- Do let what the persona is saying be right: the composite has nothing to read where the content is weak, and the audience must be able to hold the content next to the reception.
- Do make the discounting listener decent and attentive, because the misattribution is not contempt and a play that supplies somebody sneering has staged a snub instead.
- Do let no slight be intended or perceived: neither party can see the deduction, and the persona often cannot name what happened either.
- Do use the second gate on somebody native where the play wants it, since a register shuts out a native speaker as readily as anyone and that keeps this from being a story about foreigners.
- Do not offer this as an account of what an accent means socially: status, nationality, and class are real, are often larger, and belong elsewhere.
- Do not model how a persona talks in conversation (that is utterance, wiring the same engine), the tilt itself (that is bias), a persona misjudging their own learning (that is glide), a rejection where a sorting failed (that is anomaly), a role and a read in conflict (that is unbecoming), or the management of a face (that is presentation). Do not make a way of speaking stand for a theme (that is the play's pitch). Do not edit the composite's files; wire only from the play's side.
