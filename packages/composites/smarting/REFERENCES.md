# Smarting: Reference

## Line of Work

Smarting as **composite**: what a favourable self-view does when the room declines to confirm it. It
is not a fourth mechanism but a wiring of three: the **self-esteem** engine supplies the resting
regard and whether it is held securely or staked on a domain that must keep delivering, the
**narcissism** engine supplies the inflated case and the self-regulation that defends it, and the
**aggression** engine supplies what the mobilised force becomes and where it goes.

The joining is Baumeister, Smart and Boden's, and it is a claim about the relation between the atoms
rather than about any of them. The prevailing account had it that violence comes from low
self-regard -- that people who hurt others do so because they think badly of themselves. Their review
argued the opposite direction: what precedes hostility is a **favourable** self-view meeting a
contradiction, and the operative quantity is the distance between what a persona claims for
themselves and what their situation will ratify. Bushman and Baumeister then put the three atoms of
this composite into one experiment, and the paper names them in its own title. Participants who had
written an essay and received a scathing evaluation were given a chance to blast the evaluator with
noise. Narcissism predicted how hard they blasted; self-esteem, on its own, did not. In the companion
study the aggression appeared again against an innocent third party who had done nothing at all.

That is why the composite needs all three, and why it reads a gap rather than a height. From
self-esteem: the resting regard, and the contingent form, which is what makes a self-picture depend
on outcomes the persona does not control. From narcissism: the inflated claim actively promoted, and
the injury-and-rivalry route by which a refusal is answered with antagonism rather than with revision.
From aggression: the forms the force takes, including the displaced one -- which is the composite's
twist, because it is what lets the account close on somebody who was not involved.

Kernis, Grannemann and Barclay supply the correction that keeps the composite from overclaiming, and
it is the reason the claim bridge insists on the difference and not the level. Measuring self-esteem
repeatedly rather than once, they found that hostility and anger were predicted not by how high a
persona's regard sat but by how much it moved: highest in those whose regard was both high and
unstable, and lowest in those whose high regard was steady. A secure good opinion of oneself is not
the risk. An exposed one is.

It is the third composite to wire the self-esteem engine and the second to wire aggression.
Self-relation runs self-esteem inward, as one of three modes by which a persona relates to their own
self, alongside self-compassion and authenticity; cptsd runs it as damage laid down early. Here it is
read outward, as a claim the room can refuse. Intergroup runs aggression on the us/them line, where
the target is chosen by category; here it is run on one persona's regard, where the target is chosen
by proximity. Same engines, different questions, and no composite owns them.

The domain does not model any side alone -- high, low, and contingent are the self-esteem engine's;
the four types and the admiration/rivalry/injury routes are narcissism's; the five forms are
aggression's. Nor is it the moral emotions aimed at a wrongdoer, which are condemnation's and hate's;
nor a wanting produced by somebody else's advantage, which is outdone's; nor a rank being contested,
which is standing's. It owns the path: a claim, a refusal, and a bill sent to the wrong address.

**On the evidence, stated plainly.** The parts are unequally supported and the composite should be
read accordingly. The narcissism-to-aggression link is the strongest leg: it has replicated widely
and a large meta-analysis across hundreds of studies finds it across forms of aggression and across
both the grandiose and vulnerable types, with the antagonistic component carrying most of it. The
self-esteem leg is genuinely contested. The strong version of the original claim -- that high
self-esteem is itself the hazard and low self-esteem is unrelated to aggression -- has been
challenged by longitudinal and cohort work finding low self-esteem predicting antisocial and
aggressive outcomes, and it should not be treated as settled. This composite therefore takes the
narrower and better-supported position: the level of self-regard predicts little on its own, and what
carries the risk is regard that is exposed -- inflated relative to what the room grants, contingent
on a domain that can stop delivering, or unstable. Displaced aggression is separately well
established, with its own meta-analysis, and does not depend on the self-esteem dispute. What should
not be taken from this composite is that self-regard causes violence, or that a persona who thinks
well of themselves is dangerous. The claim is conditional and directional: where a favourable view is
staked in a room that will not confirm it, expect the contradiction to be answered rather than
absorbed, and expect the answer to land somewhere cheap.

## Origin

Self-esteem research, narcissism research, and aggression research, wired as a composite over the
self-esteem, narcissism, and aggression engines. The first two rows are the **joining** -- the
published work that argues and then tests the relation between a favourable self-view, its
contradiction, and harm -- and the rest anchor the individual bridges and their bounds.

## Restrictions

What the composite refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The three sides (the atoms)**: The composite wires the three but owns none. High, low, and
  contingent are the self-esteem engine's; the four types, the three routes, and their aims are
  narcissism's; hostile, instrumental, displaced, relational, and passive are aggression's. Smarting
  reads only the path the three make, and one alone is a position or a process, not the path.
- **The persona's relation to their own self (self-relation)**: The self-relation composite wires
  this same self-esteem engine inward -- how a persona evaluates their worth, cares for themselves in
  suffering, and is true to themselves, three modes weaving into one self-stance. Smarting reads the
  same regard outward, as something claimed in a room that can refuse it. A play may run both on one
  persona, and should not confuse a self-stance with a claim.
- **Hostility between groups (intergroup)**: The intergroup composite wires this same aggression
  engine on the us/them line, where the target is selected by category and the hostility can be
  seeded by grouping alone. Smarting's target is selected by proximity and safety, and the injury is
  personal. Where a play stages a whole class of people answered this way, the machinery is
  intergroup's.
- **The emotions aimed at a wrongdoer (condemnation, hate)**: Anger, contempt, and disgust at
  somebody who broke a code are those composites'. The puncture is not indignation at a
  transgression; nothing has been transgressed except an estimate.
- **The wanting produced by another's advantage (outdone)**: Envy, admiration, and comparison are the
  outdone composite's, and they run on somebody else having something. Smarting runs on the persona's
  own claim being refused, and needs no rival.
- **The rank being contested (standing)**: Status, power, hierarchy, and the status move are the
  standing composite's. Smarting is not a fight over position; it is what a persona does when their
  own estimate of themselves is not returned, which can happen at any rank.
- **The account given afterwards (squaring)**: The excuse, the reassigned cause, and the loosened
  standard are the squaring composite's. Smarting stops at the return; whatever the persona says
  about it later is squaring's.
- **The damage laid down early (cptsd)**: Developmental injury to regulation, attachment, and
  self-esteem is the cptsd composite's. Smarting reads an episode in a persona who arrives however
  they arrive, and does not model how the regard got exposed.
- **The wound as authored symbol (the play's pitch)**: An outburst made to **stand for** a theme --
  the tantrum that mirrors a motif of pride -- is refused: that is authored key, the play's pitch,
  not the force. Smarting sets what the persona did with the refusal; the meaning read into it is the
  play's.

## Encoding

Source to constraint. A composite root over three engine atoms it declares as dependencies, with
three bridges.

- **[smarting](process_smarting.md)** (the root): what a favourable self-view does when the room
  declines to confirm it, read on the gap and never on the height. Anchored by Baumeister, Smart &
  Boden (1996).
- **[the claim](process_smarting_claim.md)** (bridge): the regard put out where the room can answer
  it -- secure, low, or contingent, and in the inflated case actively promoted. Anchored by Kernis,
  Grannemann & Barclay (1989) on instability, Crocker & Wolfe (2001) on staked domains, and Back et
  al. (2013) on the admiration route.
- **[the puncture](process_smarting_puncture.md)** (bridge): the contradiction landing in proportion
  to the distance rather than to the remark, so the trigger looks trivial from outside and is not.
  Anchored by Bushman & Baumeister (1998) and by Back et al. (2013) on the rivalry route.
- **[the return](process_smarting_return.md)** (bridge): the force mobilised and aimed, at the source
  or -- more often -- at whoever is nearest and safest. Anchored by Marcus-Newhall et al. (2000) on
  displacement; the composite's twist and keystone, that what does the damage is the distance between
  the regard and what the room will confirm, so the remedy most often prescribed widens the gap it
  was meant to close.
