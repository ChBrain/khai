# Instructions: Dealing

How a Playwright wires the dealing composite into a play. You wire by linking the play's
own content to the composite's files; the files stay untouched, they are canon. The
composite installs the negotiation, trust, and escalation engines as dependencies, and
their laws arrive with them. Authoring guidance, not runtime content, and it does not go
on tour.

## Human

- The human sets which pairs in the play need each other and can hurt each other: what is on the table, what each has exposed, and what each can do to the other -- and, the question that governs the read, which state the relation is in.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, read a pair through the state their relation is in: link under Projection the state that is running -- the table while terms are being pursued (the preparation done beforehand, the exploration of what is wanted behind what is asked for, the invention of terms neither arrived with, the resolution that closes or does not, from negotiation), the bet while exposure is being accepted or refused (extended into ground the other could take, tested by what they do with it, withheld so nothing is at risk, betrayed, or repaired at a price, from trust), the spiral while each move is answering the last (positions hardening into things that must be defended, the counterpart narrowing into an antagonist whose motives explain everything, each retaliation raising what the next must answer, from escalation). Keep both parties needing each other -- a party who can simply walk away is not dealing -- and keep every escalating move locally reasonable. You link to the composite, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- Preparation, exploration, invention, and resolution are the negotiation engine's; extending, testing, withholding, betrayal, and repair are trust's; hardening, the antagonist, and retaliation are escalation's -- one alone is a process, not the condition the pair are in. The moral wrong between two and its reckoning is the moral-account composite's, which owns the injury where this owns the price; the settlement that ends a relation is owing's; how the talk works is crosstalk's and utterance's; what is withheld as truth-status is opacity's; the verdict on what the other has coming is deserving's and the rank either holds is standing's, where this reads only the leverage in the deal at hand; what happens between groups is intergroup's. This composite owns the mixed-motive relation -- and the spiral bridge carries the keystone: everything each side does to make itself safe is what makes the agreement unreachable.

## Knowledge

- Dealing is a pair read through the state their mixed-motive relation is in -- braiding the pursuit of terms (negotiation), the exposure accepted on conduct that cannot be controlled (trust), and the loop neither party is steering (escalation) -- read as a wiring of those three engines, and its claim is that these are not three relationships but three states of one, so a pair who need each other and can hurt each other is always in exactly one of them. The join is Deutsch's, and half of it already sits inside the trust engine, which anchors his account of trust as a choice to expose oneself under uncertainty in a mixed-motive game rather than a sentiment about somebody: cooperative and competitive processes are self-reinforcing, each eliciting more of itself, so the state a pair falls into does more to set their outcome than the substance they are dividing -- and with Krauss he showed it, in a bargaining game where the means to threaten made both parties worse off and both holding it was worst of all, the players with nothing to threaten with earning the most. Pruitt, Rubin and Kim supply the other flank, escalation, stalemate, and settlement as one arc rather than separate subjects. It runs in three states, ordered by what each side has put at risk. The table is where an agreement is being looked for and where the substance still decides: the ground a deal could stand on widens whenever an interest is found under a position and narrows every time a party commits publicly to a number they must now defend, and the standing dilemma is that the candour which finds those terms is the candour a counterpart can use to take a larger share, so creating value and claiming it cannot both be done wholeheartedly. The bet is the exposure one party accepts on the other's conduct, never spoken of at the table and pricing everything on it: what a bet supplies has to be bought some other way when it is refused -- in verification, drafting, escrow, and the third party paid to stand between two people who could have said yes -- so a party who protects themselves thoroughly has not made the deal safer but dearer, and past a point has priced it out while doing nothing anyone could call unreasonable. The spiral is the twist, where each answer is locally correct -- an unanswered move is a loss, and a party who absorbs everything will be taken from -- and the sequence is lose-lose, because the capacity to hurt the other, acquired precisely to strengthen a hand, is what puts the agreement out of reach. Here the three are seen for one thing: at the table a firm position protects a share and narrows the ground, in the bet a refused exposure removes a risk and prices the terms, in the spiral a threat strengthens a hand and destroys the deal -- three instruments of self-protection, three mechanisms, one result, each paid for out of the agreement itself. Read a pair by the state they are in, and never by what is on the table.

## System

- Do read the pair, not either persona alone: name whether the table (terms sought), the bet (exposure accepted or refused), or the spiral (each move answering the last) is running, and cast each from its engines.
- Do keep both parties needing each other: a party who can walk away costlessly is not dealing, and a scene that gives one side a free exit has left the composite.
- Do keep every escalating move locally reasonable: responding really is the better move each time it is made, so an escalation that requires somebody to be foolish or hot-tempered has misread the mechanism as a character flaw.
- Do let the bet be arithmetic, not sentiment: stage a refused exposure as a cost -- the clause, the escrow, the lawyer -- and let a deal die of being too expensive to write rather than of anybody's bad faith.
- Do let the spiral carry the keystone: the leverage each side builds is what removes the agreement, so the strongest position at a table is the one with the least to threaten with, which reads as weakness and is not.
- Do let both accounts be true: each party will name the other as the reason it failed, and each will be right about every step they cite -- a play that assigns the fault to one side has flattened the composite.
- Do remember a good agreement and a surviving relation are different results: a table can close well and leave a pair who will not do this again.
- Do not model the moral wrong and its reckoning (that is moral-account, which owns the injury where this owns the price), the settlement that ends a relation (that is owing), how the talk works (that is crosstalk and utterance), what is withheld as truth (that is opacity), the verdict on what the other has coming (that is deserving), the rank either holds (that is standing), or what happens between groups (that is intergroup). Do not make a breakdown stand for a story (that is the play's pitch). Do not edit the composite's files; wire only from the play's side.
