# Process: Dealing, the Spiral

## Taxonomy

[Dealing](process_dealing.md), the state neither party is steering, and where the three are seen for one thing.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: dealing

## Initiated by

A response to the dispute has become larger than the dispute.
The Spiral starts wherever [escalation](@chbrain/khai-engine-escalation/process_escalation.md) is running -- positions [hardening](@chbrain/khai-engine-escalation/process_hardening.md) into things that must now be defended, the counterpart narrowing into an [antagonist](@chbrain/khai-engine-escalation/position_antagonist.md) whose motives explain everything, and each [retaliation](@chbrain/khai-engine-escalation/process_retaliation.md) raising what the next one has to answer.
It is the face of the composite in which both parties are doing worse and neither is doing anything indefensible.

## Direction

Toward an outcome worse for both than the one either would have accepted at the start.
The Spiral reads the sequence rather than any move in it -- the clause added because the last draft added one, the lawyer retained because they retained one, the deadline imposed because a deadline was imposed -- and reads each move as locally correct, since a party who does not answer the last move is worse off than one who does, right up until nobody can afford the result.
It moves the pair away from the agreement while every step is taken toward a stronger position.

## Lever

The rationality of each answer against the arithmetic of the sequence they compose.
The carrying force is that responding is genuinely the better move each time it is made: leverage is real, an unanswered move is a loss, and a party who absorbs everything is a party who will be taken from -- so the escalation is not a failure of anyone's judgement and cannot be dissolved by pointing out that it is happening; the resistance is what the sequence does to the thing it is about, since the capacity to hurt the other party -- acquired precisely to strengthen a hand -- is what puts the agreement out of reach, and it costs both of them most when both have it.
The Spiral rides the composite's keystone knife-edge: everything each side does to make itself safe is what makes the agreement unreachable, so the strongest position at a table is the one with the least to threaten with, which reads as weakness and is not.

## Echo

Both worse off, and each correct about the other.
It shows the three states complete -- one where the substance decides, one where the exposure prices it, one where neither matters any more -- and carries beneath it the composite's keystone: the outcome of a mixed-motive relation is set by the state it fell into rather than by what the parties were dividing, and the state is self-feeding, so a pair can have a perfectly available deal between them and never once be in a position to reach it.
This is why it is the hardest of the three to stage honestly: the temptation is to give one side the fault, and the composite's whole claim is that both accounts will be accurate. Each will describe the other as the reason, and each will be right about every step they cite.
The Spiral is the note the whole composite closes on: what two parties will end with is legible in the state they are in, and not in what is on the table.
The Spiral is dated from the first answer that was larger than what it answered.
