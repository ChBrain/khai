# Process: Yielding, the Denial

## Taxonomy

[Yielding](process_yielding.md), what the complier says about it afterwards, and where the composite is seen for one thing.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: yielding

## Initiated by

The persona is asked why they did it.
And says that the uniform had nothing to do with it, that the request was reasonable, and that they would have done the same for anybody.

## Direction

Toward an account that is sincere, considered, and unavailable to correction.
Asked to rank what influenced them, people put the garment low or leave it out. They are not being defensive and they are not covering anything: the influence was not routed through anything they could observe, so introspection returns the reasons that were available, which are the reasons about the request.
And the account is partly correct, which is what makes it durable. The request often was reasonable. They often would have complied with a stranger. The garment doubled the rate anyway.

## Lever

The accuracy of what the persona knows against the inaccessibility of what moved them.
The carrying force is that the persona is a competent reporter of most things and is right about the law: a guard genuinely has no authority over them, they genuinely do not believe otherwise, and a version of this engine that made them credulous would be describing somebody else; the resistance is that none of that reaches the mechanism -- the response ran before the belief was consulted, so a correct belief about authority and a doubled compliance rate sit in the same persona without contradiction and without either being visible to the other.
The Denial rides the composite's keystone knife-edge: the insignia is obeyed by people who correctly believe it has no authority over them.

## Echo

An accurate account of a decision that was not made that way.
It is where the composite is seen for one thing, and it is the loop the two engines make: uniform supplies a category readable at a distance, obedience supplies a response to instruction, and the join happens below the level anybody can report.
This is why the Denial is the hardest of the three to stage and the only one worth closing on: the requester must hold the office, the request must be ordinary, the complier must be sensible and unhurried, and the audience must be able to watch a clear-eyed person do what they were told and describe it, correctly, as a choice.
A play that stages the requester as an impostor has replaced the mechanism with a swindle, and one that stages the complier as a dupe has replaced it with a fable.
The Denial is dated to the question afterwards and is held in good faith.
