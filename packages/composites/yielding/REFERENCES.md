# Yielding: Reference

## Line of Work

Yielding as **composite**: what a stranger gives an insignia, and how fast. It is not a third mechanism
but a wiring of two: the **uniform** engine supplies a category readable at a distance and a wearer
holding an authority nobody granted them personally, and the **obedience** engine supplies compliance
with the instruction of an authority. This composite adds no third engine. It reads the one thing the
two make together, which is compliance collected by an insignia from people it has no claim on.

The warrant is accordingly integrative rather than primary: the sources below are the field
experiments on authority cues, where each atom's own REFERENCES carries the primary work of its side.
**Bickman is the joining.** His experiments are not about what a uniform means to its wearer, and not
separately about obedience inside a hierarchy; they put a confederate on a street in either civilian
clothes or a guard's uniform, had them make small arbitrary requests of passers-by, and measured what
happened. Compliance roughly doubled with the garment. The decisive detail is that it held when the
requester delivered the instruction and walked away before it could be carried out -- nobody was being
watched, and they complied anyway. That is a claim about the two engines together: the insignia is
doing the work of an authority in a setting where no authority relationship exists.

Uniform is wired here for the first time and obedience for the second -- the **subjection** composite
already wires it, over socialization, discipline, and total-institution, for the person made by
institutional power. An engine can be in 0..n composites: an atom carries a phenomenon, and a composite
carries a question asked of it, and the two questions asked of obedience here are far apart. Subjection
asks what a lifetime inside institutions makes of a self; this composite asks what four hundred
milliseconds on a pavement makes of a stranger. The pairing is structural rather than convenient,
because the two engines look at opposite sides of the same encounter: uniform reads what the garment
does to the person wearing it, obedience reads what an instruction does to the person receiving one, and
this composite reads the half-second in which they meet.

**The Glance** grants the office before establishing there is one. The cue is manufacturable -- it is a
set of clothes, obtainable by anybody, carrying no information about whether the wearer holds the office
at all -- and the shortcut that responds to it is nonetheless a good one, because the base rate of
uniformed instructions being legitimate is extremely high and verifying each would make an ordinary
street unworkable.

**The Reach** is granted widest where the warrant is narrowest. A security guard has authority over a
car park and none over a passer-by's wallet; the request is complied with anyway. Nobody can be
expected to know the exact powers of a hospital porter, a rail employee, or a private contractor, and
the boundary a persona declines to litigate is precisely where the office's actual authority ends.

**The Denial** is where the composite is seen for one thing. Asked what influenced them, people rank
the garment low or leave it out, and they are not being defensive: the influence was not routed through
anything observable, so introspection returns the reasons that were available, which are the reasons
about the request. The account is also partly correct, which is what makes it durable.

Hence the **twist**. **The insignia is obeyed by people who correctly believe it has no authority over
them.** Both halves hold at once. The compliance is real, measured, and large. And the complier is not
confused about the law: a security guard cannot require a stranger to hand over money, everybody knows
this, and they hand it over. What the garment collects is not a belief about authority but a response
that runs before any belief about authority is consulted.

The composite is built so that nobody has to be deceiving anybody for it to run, and a play should keep
it that way. The requester held the office. The request was ordinary. The complier was sensible.

**On the evidence, stated plainly.** This composite rests on an unusually good kind of study and a
small quantity of it, and both halves of that should be visible. **The core finding is behavioural,
in the field, on real strangers.** Bickman's and Bushman's designs measure what people did rather than
what they said they would do, with unwitting participants in ordinary settings, which is the strongest
available evidence for a claim of this shape and is much better than the laboratory analogue would be.
**There is not very much of it and it is old.** The key experiments date from the 1970s and 1980s, the
samples are small by current standards, the settings are American streets and campuses, and nothing
here has been through the kind of large-scale re-estimation that other classic social effects have
lately received; a reader should hold the doubling as an order of magnitude rather than a coefficient.
**The Denial bridge borrows a general claim.** That people give sincere and inaccurate accounts of what
moved them is well established far beyond this literature, and this composite uses only the narrow
version the authority-cue studies themselves measured, which is that participants rank the uniform low
among the influences on their own compliance. **One famous uniform-adjacent study is deliberately left
out.** The 1966 nurses experiment, in which nurses followed a telephoned drug order from an unknown
doctor, is the demonstration most readers will expect here; its design has been criticised heavily and a
better-controlled replication did not reproduce it, so no bridge depends on it and its absence is a
choice rather than an oversight. **The composite does not adjudicate the Milgram debate.** Whether
laboratory obedience is better described as blind compliance or as engaged followership is a live
argument that the obedience atom carries; nothing here turns on it, because this composite's claim
concerns a stranger in a street rather than a subject in a hierarchy. What should not be taken from
this composite is that people are dupes, that uniformed staff are exploiting anybody, or that the
deference is irrational -- the shortcut is correct almost every time, and that is exactly why it is
worth so much to whoever is wearing the clothes. The claim is narrow: the response precedes the
assessment, it extends past the warrant, and the person giving it reports otherwise in good faith.

## Restrictions

What the composite refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **What the garment does to its wearer (uniform)**: The licence, the constraint, the issuing, and the
  standard that does not come off are the uniform engine's own members. This composite links them and
  reads only the person in front of the wearer.
- **The command inside a hierarchy (obedience, power, hierarchy)**: Milgram's destructive obedience, the
  instrument, the legitimacy, and the binding are the obedience engine's; where authority comes from and
  how it is arranged are power's and hierarchy's. This composite reads a stranger in a street.
- **Compliance bought by fair treatment (heeding, recognition, law)**: What a persona gives a rule they
  did not agree with, and what a fair hearing buys, are the heeding composite's and those engines'. That
  arrangement earns compliance; this one collects it without earning anything.
- **The deliberate attempt to move a mind (persuasion, swaying, reactance)**: Influence attempts, the
  cues that carry them, and what pushing produces are those packages'. Nobody in this composite is
  trying to persuade anybody; a request is made and it is followed.
- **Impersonation (deception, guile, law)**: Wearing an office one does not hold, and the offence of
  doing so, are deception's and the trade's. The composite requires a genuine wearer, and its finding is
  interesting precisely because the requester is legitimate.
- **What a group does to restraint (crowd, conformity, moral-disengagement)**: How conduct changes in a
  mass, and how it gets released from a persona's own standards, are those engines'. This is one person
  responding to one garment.
- **The law of the encounter (the world)**: What a given office may actually require, in which
  jurisdiction, and what the citizen's rights are, are matters of statute. They set the format; this
  composite reads the compliance that happens before anybody consults them.
- **An insignia as authored symbol (the play's pitch)**: A uniform staged in order to **stand for** a
  theme -- the badge that mirrors a motif of power -- is refused: that is authored key, the play's pitch,
  not the force.

## Encoding

Source to constraint. A composite tree: one root and three bridges over two atoms.

- **[yielding](process_yielding.md)** (the root): compliance collected by an insignia from people it has
  no claim on, read on what was asked, carrying the twist -- the insignia is obeyed by people who
  correctly believe it has no authority over them. Anchored by Bickman (1974).
- **[the Glance](process_yielding_glance.md)**: the recognition that arrives ahead of the reasoning,
  granting the office before establishing there is one. Anchored by Bickman (1974), linking uniform's
  anchor.
- **[the Reach](process_yielding_reach.md)**: how far past the office's warrant the compliance extends,
  widest where the warrant is narrowest. Anchored by Bushman (1984, 1988), linking uniform's wearer.
- **[the Denial](process_yielding_denial.md)**: what the complier says afterwards, sincere and partly
  correct and not reaching the mechanism, where the composite is seen for one thing. Anchored by Bushman
  (1988).
