# Process: Yielding

## Taxonomy

What a stranger gives an insignia, and how fast.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: yielding

## Initiated by

Somebody in a uniform asks a stranger to do something, and the stranger does it.
Yielding starts wherever both are in play: the garment that replaces a person with an office, through [uniform](@chbrain/khai-engine-uniform/piece_uniform.md); and compliance with the instruction of an authority, through [obedience](@chbrain/khai-engine-obedience/process_obedience.md).
This composite adds no third engine. It reads the one thing the two make together, which is compliance collected by an insignia from people it has no claim on.

## Direction

Toward an instruction followed before anybody has decided whether it should be.
The composite wires the two and reads their working in three shapes: [the Glance](process_yielding_glance.md), the recognition that arrives ahead of the reasoning; [the Reach](process_yielding_reach.md), how far past the office's warrant the compliance extends; and [the Denial](process_yielding_denial.md), the twist -- what the complier says about it afterwards.
The three are not a sequence of events. They are one arrangement, and a question about what a garment is doing that a person could not.

## Lever

The sensibleness of deferring to a uniform against how little it is being checked.
The carrying force is that the reflex is correct almost every time and life would be impossible without it: the overwhelming majority of instructions given by people in uniform are legitimate, the cost of complying is usually seconds, verifying each one would make an ordinary street unworkable, and a persona who demanded a warrant card from every marshal at every event would be both exhausting and worse off; the resistance is that the reflex responds to the garment rather than to the office behind it, which means it is available to anybody who buys one, works on requests the office could not lawfully make, and is granted at full strength by people who would deny granting it at all.
Yielding is read on what was asked, never on who asked.

## Echo

Somebody who did what they were told and does not think they were told anything.
Yielding does not close; it is authority collected by an insignia, gathered into one reading -- a glance, a reach, a denial.
The twist is here: **the insignia is obeyed by people who correctly believe it has no authority over them.** Both halves of that hold at once. The compliance is real, measured, and large -- an arbitrary request roughly doubles its success rate when the person making it is in uniform, and it keeps working after the requester has walked away. And the complier is not confused about the law: a security guard cannot require a passer-by to give a stranger money, everybody knows this, and they hand it over anyway. What the garment gets is not a belief about authority. It is a response that runs before any belief about authority is consulted.
It hands off what it is not: the licence and the constraint the garment puts on its wearer are uniform's; the command of a legitimate authority inside a hierarchy is obedience's; the compliance bought by a fair procedure is the heeding composite's; the deliberate attempt to move a mind is persuasion's; the impersonation of an office is deception's; and an insignia staged to **stand for** a theme is authored key, the play's pitch, not the force.
The persona is dated from the last thing they were asked and did.
