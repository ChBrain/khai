# Instructions: Yielding

How a Playwright wires the yielding composite into a play. You wire by linking the play's own content to the composite's files; the files stay untouched, they are canon. The composite declares two atoms as dependencies and links their members by name -- you never copy an atom's reading into the play, you point at it. The Roadie plumbs the composite's law into Instructions Knowledge on deploy. Authoring guidance, not runtime content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human keeps the requester legitimate and the complier sensible: the composite needs no impostor and no dupe, and stops working the moment it gets either. Make the request small and ordinary, and make it one the office has no actual power to make, because that gap is the whole engine. And decide what the persona says about it afterwards, since that sentence is what the composite is aimed at.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, a persona links under Projection which bridge they are standing in: whether they gave the instruction or received it, how quickly they complied, whether the request was inside the office's warrant, and what account they give of why they did it. Read the composite on what was asked, never on who asked. Keep it the arrangement: the licence the garment puts on its wearer is uniform's, the command inside a hierarchy is obedience's, and the compliance bought by fair treatment is the heeding composite's. You link to the composite, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- The licence, the constraint, and the standard that does not come off are the uniform engine's; the command inside a hierarchy and where authority comes from are obedience's, power's, and hierarchy's; the compliance bought by fair treatment is the heeding composite's, recognition's, and law's; the deliberate attempt to move a mind is persuasion's, the swaying composite's, and reactance's; wearing an office one does not hold is deception's and guile's; what a mass does to restraint is crowd's and conformity's. What a given office may lawfully require is statute and sets the format. Whether a production's request was inside the warrant, and whether a complier was deferring or agreeing, are the team's review.

## Knowledge

- Yielding is what a stranger gives an insignia, and how fast. It is not a third mechanism but a wiring of two: the uniform engine supplies a category readable at a distance and a wearer holding an authority nobody granted them personally, and the obedience engine supplies compliance with the instruction of an authority. It adds no third engine and wires on a persona at Projection, and it reads the one thing the two make together, which is compliance collected by an insignia from people it has no claim on. Three bridges. The Glance grants the office before establishing there is one: in the field experiments, small arbitrary requests roughly doubled their success rate when the requester was dressed as a guard rather than as a civilian, and the effect held when the requester gave the instruction and walked away before it could be carried out, so nobody was being watched. The shortcut is a good one, because the base rate of uniformed instructions being legitimate is extremely high and checking each would make an ordinary street unworkable -- and the cue it responds to is a set of clothes obtainable by anybody. The Reach is granted widest where the warrant is narrowest: a guard has authority over a car park and none over a passer-by's wallet, nobody can be expected to know the exact powers of a porter or a rail employee, and the boundary a persona declines to litigate is precisely where the office's authority ends. The Denial is sincere: asked what influenced them, people rank the garment low or leave it out, because the influence was not routed through anything observable, and their account is partly correct, which is what makes it durable. The twist: the insignia is obeyed by people who correctly believe it has no authority over them.

## System

- Do keep the requester legitimate. They hold the office, they are not impersonating anybody, and the composite is more interesting because of it.
- Do make the request small, ordinary, and outside the warrant. Move away from there, pick that up, give this man a coin -- nothing the office could actually require.
- Do let the compliance happen before the thinking. The persona is already moving while the sentence is finishing, and nobody in the scene finds that odd.
- Do have the requester leave. The instruction is followed after they have gone, with nobody watching, which is the sharpest form of the finding.
- Do keep the complier competent. They know a guard has no authority over them, they would say so if asked, and they comply anyway.
- Do give the account afterwards in good faith. It was a reasonable request, they would have done it for anybody, the uniform had nothing to do with it -- and let all three be things a reasonable person would say.
- Do not model what the garment does to its wearer (uniform), the command inside a hierarchy (obedience, power, hierarchy), compliance bought by fair treatment (heeding, recognition, law), the attempt to persuade (persuasion, swaying, reactance), impersonation (deception, guile), or restraint in a mass (crowd, conformity). Do not make the insignia stand for a theme; that is authored key, the play's pitch. Do not edit the composite's files or the atoms'; wire only from the play's side.
