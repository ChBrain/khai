# Instructions: Sensorium

How a Playwright wires the sensorium composite into a play. You wire by linking the play's
own content to the composite's files; the files stay untouched, they are canon. The
composite installs the smellscape, soundscape, and touch engines as dependencies, and their
laws arrive with them. Authoring guidance, not runtime content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human sets the place's sensory reach: which place is read as meeting the body apart from the eye -- holding a constant field, raising a figure from it, or withholding a sense altogether -- and, the question that governs the read, which shape it is in: the ground, the figure, or the absence.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, read a place as reaching the body before it is looked at: link under Shown the shape its sensory field is in -- the ground while the constant layer works below notice (the base smell it sits in, the keynote heard beneath everything, the texture and thermal the skin reads, from smellscape, soundscape, and touch), the figure while something rises from that field or marks the place (the drifting plume, the foreground signal, the give and resistance the body meets, and the redolence and soundmark that bind the place to memory), the absence while a sense is withheld (the scentless room, the silence, the surface that must not be touched). Play the place as a surround already acting on whoever entered, present in what it puts before the body, never narrated, and never made to stand for a story. You link to the composite, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- The olfactory field is the smellscape engine's; the acoustic field is soundscape's; the haptic field is touch's -- one field alone is not the shared shape. The setting as a party to events, its affordances and territory, is the space engine's; the place as a social stage is street-life's; the distance a persona keeps from others is proxemics'; the body's own hunger, fatigue, and pain are the body engine's; how a place sustains and roots a person is the dwelling composite's. This composite owns the non-visual reach of a place -- what the body met before the eye reported -- and the absence bridge carries the keystone: a place is felt hardest where a sense has been taken away.

## Knowledge

- Sensorium is a place read as the body meets it apart from the eye -- braiding the olfactory field (smellscape), the acoustic field (soundscape), and the haptic field (touch) into the shape all three independently take -- read as a wiring of those three engines, and its question is which shape the field is in (Pallasmaa's body-met place against the eye-addressed one; Schafer's keynote, signal, soundmark, and silence; Porteous's smellscape). It runs in three shapes. The ground is the constant field -- the base smell a place sits in, the keynote heard beneath everything else, the surface grain and the warmth or chill the skin reads -- unremarked precisely because it does not change, so the body acclimatises within moments and can no longer name the very layer that colours everything else. The figure is what rises from that field and is registered -- the drifting plume, the foreground signal, the give and resistance the body meets -- and what rises and stays, binding the place to memory and naming it: the signature smell and the sound no other place makes; it owes its force to the ground it contrasts with, so a place recorded by figures alone reads as a list of incidents rather than a surround. The absence is the twist -- the scentless room, the silence, the surface withheld from the hand -- where the shape is seen for what it is: the senses are built for a world that always supplies something, so an absence is a violated expectation and the body treats it as information, searching where it found nothing; the withheld field registers more strongly than any field supplied. Read a place as having already worked on the body before it was seen, and read its emptiest sense as often its loudest.

## System

- Do read the place as reaching the body unseen, never as a view for an eye: name whether the ground (the constant field), the figure (what rises from it), or the absence (the sense withheld) is at work, and cast each from its engines.
- Do keep the shapes distinct: the ground is what the place continuously is, the figure is what the body picks out or will remember it by, the absence is what the place refuses to give.
- Do let the absence carry the keystone: a withheld sense is not the field's zero but its most forceful state -- the emptiest place is often the most felt.
- Do remember the ground cannot be reported by the persona who adapted to it: a place described only by its figures has lost what made them mean anything.
- Do not model the setting as a party to events (that is space), the place as a social stage (that is street-life), the distance between persons (that is proxemics), the body's own demands (that is body), or how a place sustains a person (that is dwelling). Do not make the sensory field stand for a story (that is the play's pitch). Do not edit the composite's files; wire only from the play's side.
