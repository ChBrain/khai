# Sensorium: Reference

## Line of Work

Sensorium as **composite**: the place as the body meets it, apart from the eye. It is not a
new sense but a wiring of three: the **smellscape** engine supplies the olfactory field, the
**soundscape** engine the acoustic field, and the **touch** engine the haptic field. This
composite adds no fourth engine; it reads the shape all three independently take -- a ground
that works below notice, a figure that rises from it, and an absence read as a presence --
and stages that shape into three bridges, closing on the discovery that the withheld sense
registers hardest of all.

The domain does not model any single field -- the base smell, plume, redolence, and
scentlessness are the smellscape engine's; the keynote, signal, soundmark, and silence are
soundscape's; the texture, thermal, press, and untouchable are touch's; and one alone is a
process, not the shared shape. Nor is it the physical setting as a party to what happens --
its affordances, territory, and standing atmosphere -- which is the space engine's; nor the
place as a social stage, its presence, mingling, and lingering, which is the street-life
engine's; nor the distance a persona keeps from other persons, which is the proxemics
engine's; nor the persona's own bodily demands, which is the body engine's; nor how a place
holds and sustains a person, which is the dwelling composite's. It owns the non-visual reach
of a place: the surround already acting on a body before anything has been looked at.

The spine is the correction of a bias in how places are recorded. Pallasmaa named it directly
for architecture: a discipline and a literature that render place as a view, addressed to an
eye, while the body actually meets a place through the skin, the ear, and the nose --
peripheral, unfocused, and continuous senses that shape the experience of a room far more
than its appearance does. Schafer built the vocabulary for one of those fields: the soundscape
as a composition with a keynote heard beneath everything, signals that must be attended to,
and soundmarks that belong to one place alone -- and silence as an element in its own right,
not an empty slot. Porteous carried the same move to the nose, coining the smellscape and
insisting the olfactory field is spatially and temporally structured rather than incidental.
The sensory-anthropology tradition after them read the ranking of the senses as cultural
rather than natural, which is why the non-visual fields go unrecorded and not unfelt. The
three bridges are that shared structure staged: the ground, the figure, and the absence.

The sensory geography and phenomenology of place, wired as a composite over the smellscape,
soundscape, and touch engines.

## Restrictions

What the composite refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The single fields (the atoms)**: The composite wires the three but owns none. The ground,
  plume, redolence, and scentless are the smellscape engine's; the keynote, signal, soundmark,
  and silence are soundscape's; the texture, thermal, press, and untouchable are touch's.
  Sensorium reads only the shape the three share; a single field alone is a process, not the
  whole surround.
- **The setting as a party to events (space)**: The affordances a setting offers, the territory
  it is claimed as, and the standing atmosphere it holds are the space engine's. Sensorium is
  what the place delivers to the body's non-visual senses, not the setting's role in what
  happens in it.
- **The place as a social stage (street-life)**: The people out and using a place, the casual
  exchange between them, the lingering and the watching are the street-life engine's.
  Sensorium is the field a place gives a body, with or without anyone else in it.
- **The distance between persons (proxemics)**: The bubble a persona keeps, its zones, its
  intrusions, and the equilibrium it restores are the proxemics engine's -- a persona's
  regulation of others, not a place's field.
- **The body's own demands (body)**: Hunger, fatigue, pain, arousal, and sickness are the body
  engine's. Sensorium is what the place presses on the body, not what the body presses on
  itself.
- **How a place sustains a person (dwelling)**: The belonging, restoration, and rootedness a
  place gives a self are the dwelling composite's. Sensorium is the immediate sensory reach,
  prior to any bond.
- **The sensory field as authored symbol (the play's pitch)**: A place's smell, sound, or
  silence made to **stand for** a story -- the antiseptic corridor that mirrors a theme of
  sterility, the silence that mirrors a dread -- is refused: that is authored key, the play's
  pitch, not the force on the place. Sensorium delivers what the place gives the body; the
  meaning read into it is the play's.

## Encoding

Source to constraint. A composite root over three engine atoms it declares as dependencies,
with three bridges, the shape every non-visual field of a place takes.

- **[sensorium](process_sensorium.md)** (the root): the place as the body meets it apart from
  the eye -- a surround already acting before anything is looked at. Anchored by Pallasmaa,
  with Classen & Howes on the visual bias in the record.
- **[the ground](process_sensorium_ground.md)** (bridge): the constant field -- base smell,
  keynote, texture and thermal -- working below notice precisely because it never stops.
  Anchored by Schafer (keynote) and Porteous (the olfactory ground).
- **[the figure](process_sensorium_figure.md)** (bridge): what rises from the field and what
  rises and stays -- plume, signal, press, and the redolence and soundmark that bind a place to
  memory. Anchored by Schafer (signal, soundmark) and Porteous.
- **[the absence](process_sensorium_absence.md)** (bridge): the withheld field -- scentless,
  silent, untouchable -- read by the body as an act rather than a zero. Anchored by Schafer
  (silence as an element); the composite's twist and keystone, that a place is felt hardest
  where a sense has been taken away.
