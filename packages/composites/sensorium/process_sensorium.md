# Process: Sensorium

## Taxonomy

The place as the body meets it, apart from the eye.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: sensorium

## Initiated by

A place is read not as a view but as a surround the body is already inside before anything has been looked at.
Sensorium starts wherever a place gives the body its non-visual fields -- the [smellscape](@chbrain/khai-engine-smellscape/process_smellscape.md) it sits in, the [soundscape](@chbrain/khai-engine-soundscape/process_soundscape.md) it is heard against, and the [touch](@chbrain/khai-engine-touch/process_touch.md) it presses on the skin.
This composite adds no fourth engine; it reads the shape the three share: each field has a ground that works below notice, a figure that rises from it, and an absence that is read as a presence.

## Direction

Toward the whole of what a place gives the body unseen, read through the shape its fields take.
The composite wires the three engines and reads their working in three shapes: [the Ground](process_sensorium_ground.md), the constant field; [the Figure](process_sensorium_figure.md), what rises from it and marks the place; and [the Absence](process_sensorium_absence.md), the twist -- the sense withheld.
A place does not wait to be looked at: it has already reached the body through the nose, the ear, and the skin, and has been working on whoever entered before a single thing was seen.

## Lever

The fields that reach the body first against the eye that takes all the credit.
The carrying force is that smell, sound, and touch arrive without being sought -- they cannot be closed off the way a gaze can be turned, they work continuously, and they set how a place feels long before it is described; the resistance is the habit of rendering a place as a view, so that what is written down is what could be seen and everything the body actually met goes unrecorded.
Sensorium is read wherever a place is granted its non-visual reach: not a scene presented to an eye but a surround already acting on a body.

## Echo

The place revealed as something the body was inside before it was seen.
Sensorium does not close; it is the place going on reaching the body, gathering the three engines into one reading -- a ground, a figure, and an absence.
It hands off what it is not: the physical setting as a party to what happens -- its affordances, territory, and standing atmosphere -- is the space engine's; the place as a social stage, its presence, mingling, and lingering, is the street-life engine's; the distance a persona keeps from other persons is the proxemics engine's; the persona's own bodily demands, its hunger, fatigue, and pain, are the body engine's; how a place holds and sustains a person is the dwelling composite's; and the sensory field made to stand for a story is authored key, the play's pitch, not the force.
The place is dated from what it gave the body, and read as a surround that had already worked before it was ever looked at.
