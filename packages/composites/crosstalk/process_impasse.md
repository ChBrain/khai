# Process: Impasse

## Taxonomy

[Crosstalk](process_crosstalk.md), the bridge where the loop is caught or hardens.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: crosstalk

## Initiated by

The loop reaches a break-point: someone flags the trouble, or both harden.
A [clarification](@chbrain/khai-engine-grounding/process_clarification.md) that reaches the frame itself -- "wait, are we even talking about the same thing?" -- can catch it, re-grounding the two on a shared frame at last.
Absent that, each concludes the other is obtuse or willful, and the exchange locks.

## Direction

Toward one of two ends.
Caught: the mismatch is named, the frames align, and the accumulated misgroundings clear at once -- the loop was breakable all along, needing only a clarification aimed at the frame rather than the words.
Uncaught: the loop hardens into impasse -- the two disengage or dig in, each holding the other at fault, the understanding never reached and now not sought.

## Lever

The clarification that reaches the frame -- or its absence.
Ordinary clarification repairs a word; crosstalk's catch has to repair a whole frame, which is rarer and harder: you must first suspect that the trouble is a mismatch and not the other's failure, and from inside the loop it reads as the other's failure. That gap between what would fix it and what the loop makes you feel is why crosstalk persists where a plain misunderstanding would have resolved itself in a turn.
The bridge is the seam where grounding's repair either rises to the level of the frame or never does.

## Echo

The two are re-grounded, or estranged over a gap neither could see.
What hardens here may hand off to the escalation engine as conflict, or to the repair engine as a relationship now needing mending; what catches returns them to ordinary [grounding](@chbrain/khai-engine-grounding/process_grounding.md), the frame finally shared.
The talking-past itself ends at this bridge, one way or the other -- either dissolved by a single question that named the frame, or set, a distance kept by two people each sure they were the one making sense.
