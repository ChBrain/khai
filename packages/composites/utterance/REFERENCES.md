# Utterance: Reference

## Line of Work

Utterance as **composite**: what a saying carries beyond its words. It is not a new faculty
but a wiring of four: the **speech-act** engine supplies the deed, the force by which an
utterance performs rather than describes; the **implicature** engine supplies the unsaid, the
meaning a hearer works out that was never stated; and the **register** and **tone** engines
supply the manner, the specialized variety and the affective key the saying arrives in. This
composite adds no fifth engine; it reads what the four make together -- an utterance as
freight rather than content -- and stages that into three parts of the cargo, closing on the
discovery that the heaviest part of the load is the part with no words to point at.

The domain does not model any single force -- the illocutionary force is the speech-act
engine's, the inference from cooperative talk is implicature's, the specialized variety is
register's, the affective key is tone's, and one alone is a process or a position, not the
whole cargo. Nor is it the moving of meaning across the gap between speaking and hearing, or
the mother tongue it moves in, which is the language engine's; nor two personas grounding
against different frames until they talk past each other, which is the crosstalk composite's;
nor the deliberate creation of a false belief, which is the deception engine's, read in the
opacity composite; nor the labour of performing a self before an audience, which is the
presentation composite's. It owns the freight of a saying: an utterance read not as a content
delivered but as a cargo, most of which was never in the sentence.

The spine is the twentieth-century discovery that meaning is not contained in wording. Austin
broke the assumption that utterances describe: some of them _do_ -- to say the words is to
have promised, named, or married, and the question is not whether they are true but whether
they came off. Searle sorted the forces into kinds, giving the assertive, directive,
commissive, expressive, and declaration. Grice showed that even plain description overruns
itself: hearers assume cooperative talk and reason from it, so a speaker can convey what they
never said, and can mean most precisely by openly breaking the very maxims the inference runs
on. And the sociolinguistic tradition -- register as a variety tied to a domain and a
community, tone as the key a telling is tuned to -- gives the last and heaviest freight, the
pitch that settles a hearing before the content is weighed. The three bridges are those
readings staged: the deed, the unsaid, and the manner.

## Origin

The philosophy of language and sociolinguistics of what an utterance carries, wired as a
composite over the speech-act, implicature, register, and tone engines.

| Source                | Key Work                                       | Scope                                                                                                                                       |
| :-------------------- | :--------------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **J. L. Austin**      | _How to Do Things with Words_ (1962)           | The utterance that performs rather than describes, and the conditions under which the act comes off. The root and the deed bridge.          |
| **John R. Searle**    | _Speech Acts_ (1969); _Expression and Meaning_ | The kinds of illocutionary force -- assertive, directive, commissive, expressive, declaration. The deed bridge.                             |
| **H. P. Grice**       | _"Logic and Conversation"_ (1975)              | The cooperative principle and its maxims -- meaning conveyed without being stated, and meant precisely by being flouted. The unsaid bridge. |
| **M. A. K. Halliday** | _Language as Social Semiotic_ (1978); register | Language varying by domain and community -- the variety that binds insiders and gates outsiders. The manner bridge, the keystone.           |

## Restrictions

What the composite refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The single engines (the atoms)**: The composite wires the four but owns none. The
  assertive, directive, commissive, expressive, and declaration forces are the speech-act
  engine's; the maxims, flouting, and scalar readings are implicature's; the badge, shorthand,
  closure, and translation of a variety are register's; the cue, key, and attitude of a telling
  are tone's. Utterance reads only what the four make as one cargo; a single engine alone is a
  process or a position, not the whole.
- **The moving of meaning across the gap (language)**: The speaking, hearing, reading, writing,
  and thinking by which a persona moves meaning, and the mother tongue or borrowed tongue it
  moves in, are the language engine's. Utterance is what a given saying carries; language is
  the channel it crosses and the medium it is made of.
- **The dyadic misfire (crosstalk)**: Two personas each grounding against their own frame, so
  every attempt to understand misgrounds and the gap widens, is the crosstalk composite's.
  Utterance is one saying's freight, not the loop two speakers fall into.
- **The built false belief (deception / opacity)**: The deliberate creation of a false belief
  in another is the deception engine's, read in the opacity composite. Utterance reads what a
  saying carries whether or not it is honest -- a wholly truthful saying still arrives with
  implication and pitch.
- **The labour of being seen (presentation)**: The face claimed, the region held, the display
  tuned, and the feeling managed before an audience are the presentation composite's. Utterance
  is the cargo of the words, not the work of holding up a self while saying them.
- **The saying as authored symbol (the play's pitch)**: An utterance made to **stand for** a
  story -- the euphemism that mirrors a theme of evasion, the register that mirrors a moral
  about class -- is refused: that is authored key, the play's pitch, not the force. Utterance
  delivers what the saying carried; the meaning read into it is the play's.

## Encoding

Source to constraint. A composite root over four engine atoms it declares as dependencies,
with three bridges, the parts of an utterance's freight.

- **[utterance](process_utterance.md)** (the root): what a saying carries beyond its words --
  the act performed, the meaning implied, the variety and key it arrives in. Anchored by Austin
  and Grice (saying as doing, meaning as outrunning wording).
- **[the deed](process_utterance_deed.md)** (bridge): the utterance that acts rather than
  describes -- committing, obliging, binding, discharging, or altering the world by being
  competently said. Anchored by Austin and Searle.
- **[the unsaid](process_utterance_unsaid.md)** (bridge): the meaning a hearer works out from
  cooperative talk -- the maxims, the flouting, the scalar limit -- delivered in full and never
  stated. Anchored by Grice.
- **[the manner](process_utterance_manner.md)** (bridge): the variety and affective key that
  settle a hearing before the content is weighed -- admitting, excluding, elevating, mocking.
  Anchored by Halliday and the tone tradition; the composite's twist and keystone, that what a
  persona cannot be quoted as saying is what it most surely communicated.
