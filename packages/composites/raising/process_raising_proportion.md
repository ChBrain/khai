# Process: Raising, the Proportion

## Taxonomy

[Raising](process_raising.md), what decides how hard the swap is worked, and where the composite is seen for one thing.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: raising

## Initiated by

The scrutiny scales with the implication.
The Proportion starts at an arithmetic nobody performs deliberately: what it would cost to act if the warning is right, set against what it costs to examine the person who raised it.

## Direction

Toward an inverse relation between a warning's importance and its examination.
A small warning is cheap to act on, so it is acted on and the speaker is never looked at. A large one -- stop the line, ground the fleet, withdraw the product, reopen the finished report -- has no cheap response, and the only available surface is the person.
So the scrutiny lands on the warner in proportion to how much would follow from their being right, which means the correct warnings about serious things attract the most of it, and attract it earliest.

## Lever

The rationality of looking for a reason not to act against what looking does to whoever raised it.
The carrying force is that the receiver is behaving reasonably and would be reckless not to: a decision that expensive should be examined hard, most such claims are wrong, the cost of a false alarm falls on real people, and wanting a good reason to stand down is not corruption; the resistance is that the examination has nowhere to go except the speaker, so the harder the decision the more comprehensively the speaker is worked over -- and by the time the harm arrives or does not, there is a file about them and none about the hazard.
The Proportion rides the composite's keystone knife-edge: the more expensive a warning would be to act on, the more thoroughly the warner is examined instead.

## Echo

A record of a person where a record of a risk should be.
It is where the composite is seen for one thing, and it is the loop the two engines make: warning's cost of acting sets how much doubt is needed, and credibility supplies the only place to put it.
This is why the Proportion is the hardest of the three to stage and the only one worth closing on: the receiver must be conscientious, the warning must be right, nobody must be retaliating, and the audience must be able to watch a careful process examine everything except the thing that matters.
A play that stages the receiver as covering something up has replaced the mechanism with a conspiracy, and one that stages the warner as vindicated at the end has replaced it with a courtroom.
The Proportion is dated from the size of the implication and does not depend on the speaker at all.
