# Process: Raising, the Substitution

## Taxonomy

[Raising](process_raising.md), the question swapped for one that can be answered.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: raising

## Initiated by

The conversation stops being about the hazard.
The Substitution starts when the claim turns out to have no [corroboration](@chbrain/khai-engine-credibility/process_the_corroboration.md) available, because the event has not happened -- and the receiver, needing something to examine, turns to the [manner](@chbrain/khai-engine-credibility/process_the_manner.md), the history, and the motive of whoever raised it.

## Direction

Toward due diligence performed on the wrong object.
Nobody decides to investigate the person. What happens is that the claim offers no purchase -- nothing to test, no second source, no event yet -- while the speaker offers a great deal: a record, a manner, a relationship with a manager, a reason they might want this to be true.
So the inquiry goes where the material is, and every step of it is defensible on its own.

## Lever

The legitimacy of examining a source against what examining a source produces here.
The carrying force is that source examination is proper practice and frequently decisive: people do raise things for reasons, a history is information, motive matters, and a receiver who never asked why this person is saying this would be neglecting the job; the resistance is that the substitution is undetectable to everybody inside it, because a question about the speaker looks exactly like rigour -- so the hazard goes un-examined by a process every participant experiences as careful.
The Substitution replaces an unanswerable question with an answerable one.

## Echo

A thorough process with the hazard outside it.
It shows why people who raise things so consistently report that nobody ever engaged with the substance.
What it hands upward is a finding about a person, which is now on a file, and which the next warning from that person will have to be weighed against.
The Substitution is dated from the first question about the speaker.
