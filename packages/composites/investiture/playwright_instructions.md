# Instructions: Investiture

How a Playwright wires the investiture composite into a play. You wire by linking the play's
own content to the composite's files; the files stay untouched, they are canon. The
composite installs the extended-self, heirloom, and totem engines as dependencies, and
their laws arrive with them. Authoring guidance, not runtime content, and it does not go
on tour.

## Human

- The human sets the charged object: which thing is read as carrying an identity that is not in it -- a possession held as part of one self, a possession withheld from sale and passed along a line, or an emblem set apart by a gathered collective -- and, the question that governs the read, whose identity is in it: the held, the handed, or the hallowed.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, read a persona through what they cannot be reasonable about: link under Projection the width at which the object is charged -- the held while one persona's own self is in it (the taking-in through mastery, making, knowing, gift, or the sediment of use; the possessor who holds it as self; the tearing-out that makes the person smaller, from extended-self), the handed while a line's claim is in it (the heir who holds custody and not ownership, the bequest that must carry its account or it is only a handover of goods, the alienation that begins the moment a price attaches, from heirloom), the hallowed while a collective's we is in it (the assembly whose heat charges it, the adherent for whom it is not available as material, the violation that converts a thing into an injury, from totem). Play the object as ordinary to everyone not invested in it, present in the disproportion between what it is and what it commands, never narrated, and never made to stand for a story. You link to the composite, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- The object taken into one self is the extended-self engine's; the object kept out of exchange for a line is heirloom's; the emblem set apart by a collective is totem's -- one width alone is not the shape. The object's material life, worn and decayed and mended, is the object-cycle composite's, and its marks prized as worth are that composite's treasure; the inscription whose authority is that it says the same thing everywhere is the document engine's; the obligation a transfer leaves between two persons is the owing composite's; the formation a persona arrives already carrying is heritage's; the acute mourning of a loss is grief's. This composite owns the charging -- what happens when an identity is lodged in a thing -- and the hallowed bridge carries the keystone: what the object carries is not in the object.

## Knowledge

- Investiture is an object read for the identity lodged in it -- braiding the thing taken into one self (extended-self), the thing withheld from exchange and passed down a line (heirloom), and the thing set apart by an assembled collective (totem) into one shape -- read as a wiring of those three engines, and its question is whose identity is in it (Kopytoff's singularization; James's material self; Belk on possessions and the extended self; Weiner's keeping-while-giving; Durkheim's totemic principle and collective effervescence; Douglas on prohibition and violation). It runs at three widths, and the mechanism does not change. The held is one persona's own self put into a thing through use, mastery, making, or gift, so that having is a way of being and the loss is grieved past the cost of replacement -- the extension invisible in the object and visible only in the having, which is why a cheap thing fiercely kept betrays more self than a costly one held lightly, and why the one exit that does not tear is a deliberate divestment, the meaning drawn back out before the thing is let go. The handed is a line's claim put into one possession set outside the circuit that moves everything else it owns -- held in custody rather than ownership, brought out at successions and funerals, plain enough that a stranger would not price it, and moving to the next heir only if the account moves with it -- because an account recited without an object is contestable and one recited over an object is much less so; under strain the pull to convert it into the cash it could plainly become is the sharpest test the position faces. The hallowed is the twist -- a collective's we put into an emblem charged by nothing but the heat of assembly, cooling without renewal, defended as though the group itself were being struck -- where the shape is seen for what it is: the object is sacred by no property of its own, so remove the investment and it is only a broken object, and the disproportion between what a thing is and what it commands is the whole tell. Read the object as ordinary to whoever is not invested in it, and read that as the truth about it rather than the outsider's mistake.

## System

- Do read the object through whose identity is in it, never through what it is worth: name whether the held (one self), the handed (a line), or the hallowed (a collective) is at work, and cast each from its engines.
- Do keep the widths distinct: the held needs no witness, the handed needs a line and an account, the hallowed needs an assembly and cannot outlive one that stops gathering.
- Do let the hallowed carry the keystone: what the object carries is not in the object -- so no identical thing substitutes, and a scratch on the right one is a wound.
- Do remember the outsider is not wrong about the material: the scrap of cloth really is a scrap of cloth, and the adherent really cannot see it that way, and both hold at once.
- Do remember the charge can be withdrawn as well as broken: a divestment leaves the thing intact and empty, and a totem left un-gathered cools to a relic -- a play that can only take a charged object by force has missed half the engine.
- Do not model the object's material life (that is object-cycle), the record that says the same thing everywhere (that is document), the obligation a transfer leaves (that is owing), the formation a persona arrives with (that is heritage), or the mourning of a loss (that is grief). Do not make the object stand for a story (that is the play's pitch). Do not edit the composite's files; wire only from the play's side.
