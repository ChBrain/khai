# Investiture: Reference

## Line of Work

Investiture as **composite**: how an object comes to carry an identity that is not in it. It is
not a fourth mechanism but a wiring of three: the **extended-self** engine supplies the object
taken into a single persona's own self, the **heirloom** engine supplies the object withheld from
exchange and passed down a line, and the **totem** engine supplies the object set apart by an
assembled collective. This composite adds no fourth engine; it reads the seam the three share --
the same move made at three widths, an identity lodged in a thing that has no property justifying
it -- and stages that seam into three bridges, closing on the discovery that this is precisely why
nothing identical will do.

The domain does not model any single width -- appropriation, the possessor, and dispossession are
the extended-self engine's; the heir, the bequest, and alienation are heirloom's; consecration,
the adherent, and desecration are totem's; and one alone is a piece, not the shape. Nor is it the
object's material life -- worn by use, decayed by time, met or not by mending -- which is the
object-cycle composite's; nor a claim fixed into durable form that travels unchanged and says the
same thing everywhere, which is the document engine's; nor the obligation a transfer leaves
between two persons, which is the owing composite's; nor the formation a persona arrives already
carrying, which is the heritage engine's. It owns the charging: what happens when an identity is
put into a thing, and what it costs when the thing is reached.

The spine is one mechanism described three times by literatures that were not talking to each
other. Kopytoff named it most generally as singularization -- how an object is drawn out of the
commodity sphere and rendered unexchangeable, and what re-commodifying it costs -- and the three
bridges are three authorities doing the singularizing. James located the narrowest: the material
self, the person as the sum of what they can call their own, so that a loss of possessions is a
shrinkage of the person; Belk built the modern account of appropriation and of loss as
self-diminishment, and Csikszentmihalyi and Rochberg-Halton showed household objects storing
memory, relationship, and identity through invested psychic energy, with McCracken supplying the
divestment ritual that lets a thing go without tearing. Weiner supplied the middle width: the
paradox of keeping-while-giving, possessions deliberately kept out of exchange to reproduce a
group's identity and rank across time, the point of the giving being what is withheld from it.
Durkheim supplied the widest and, with it, the keystone: the totem is charged not by any property
of its own but by collective effervescence, the assembled group's force lodged in an emblem, so
the worshipper is not wrong to feel the power and is wrong only about where it lives -- with
Douglas on what a boundary violation releases and Robertson Smith on the object set apart by
prohibition. The three bridges are those three widths: the held, the handed, and the hallowed.

sacred, wired as a composite over the extended-self, heirloom, and totem engines.

## Restrictions

What the composite refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The single widths (the atoms)**: The composite wires the three but owns none. Appropriation,
  the possessor, and dispossession are the extended-self engine's; the heir, the bequest, and
  alienation are heirloom's; consecration, the adherent, and desecration are totem's. Investiture
  reads only the shape the three share; one width alone is a piece, not the charging.
- **The object's material life (object-cycle)**: The wear of use, the decay of time, and the
  mending that meets or fails to meet them are the object-cycle composite's, and the marks
  themselves prized as worth are its treasure bridge. Investiture reads what was put into a thing,
  not what time does to it; a patina prized is object-cycle's, an identity lodged is this one's.
- **The claim fixed into durable form (document)**: An inscription whose authority is that it says
  the same thing everywhere it goes is the document engine's. Investiture is the opposite case: an
  object whose worth does not travel with it, and which becomes ordinary goods the moment it
  leaves the people invested in it.
- **The obligation a transfer leaves (owing)**: The claim one persona carries because another
  handed something over is the owing composite's. Investiture reads the thing withheld from that
  circuit entirely -- the one possession that is not moving.
- **The formation a persona arrives carrying (heritage)**: The habitus absorbed before choice, the
  family cells it formed in, and the capital transmitted are the heritage engine's. Investiture
  reads a specific object charged with a claim, not the whole inheritance of a position.
- **The grief of a loss (grief)**: The acute mourning that follows a loss is the grief engine's.
  Investiture owns the diminishment specifically -- a self, a line, or a people made smaller --
  which is why the feeling runs past the cost of replacement.
- **The shared resource and its governance (commons)**: A common-pool resource governed by its own
  users through crafted institutions is the commons engine's. Investiture is about a thing set
  apart from use, not a thing used in common.
- **The object as authored symbol (the play's pitch)**: A charged object made to **stand for** a
  story -- the ring that mirrors a theme of fidelity, the burned flag that signals a fall -- is
  refused: that is authored key, the play's pitch, not the force on the persona. Investiture sets
  what the object carries; the meaning read into it is the play's.

## Encoding

Source to constraint. A composite root over three engine atoms it declares as dependencies, with
three bridges, the widths at which a thing can be invested.

- **[investiture](process_investiture.md)** (the root): how an object comes to carry an identity
  that is not in it -- the durability an identity gains by being lodged in a thing, against the
  exposure it takes on there. Anchored by Kopytoff (1986).
- **[the held](process_investiture_held.md)** (bridge): the object taken into one persona's own
  self, enlarging it in the having and shrinking it in the loss, with divestment the one exit that
  does not tear. Anchored by James (1890), Belk (1988), Csikszentmihalyi & Rochberg-Halton (1981),
  and McCracken on the divestment ritual; the narrowest width.
- **[the handed](process_investiture_handed.md)** (bridge): the object withheld from the circuit
  that moves everything else the line owns, held in custody rather than ownership, moving only
  when its account moves with it. Anchored by Weiner (1992) and Kopytoff on what re-commodifying
  costs; a claim made hard to dispute by being made pointable.
- **[the hallowed](process_investiture_hallowed.md)** (bridge): the object charged by an assembled
  collective, sacred by no property of its own, cooling without renewal, and answering for the
  group when it is attacked. Anchored by Durkheim (1912) and Douglas (1966); the composite's twist
  and keystone, that what the object carries is not in the object -- so no identical thing can
  substitute, and no damage to it can be answered as damage to a thing.
