# Instructions: Object-Cycle

How a Playwright wires the object-cycle composite into a play. You wire by linking
the play's own content to the composite's files; the files stay untouched, they
are canon. The composite installs the wear, decay, and restoration engines as
dependencies, and their laws arrive with them. Authoring guidance, not runtime
content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human sets the arc: which object turns through a life of use and age across the play, and -- the question that governs the read -- what its keeper's care makes of it.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, read the object across its life: link the bridge its life is in under Apparent -- upkeep while care mends it and keeps it in service, discard while it is used up and let go, treasure while its very marks of wear and age are prized as worth -- and let the use-wear come from wear, the time-entropy from decay, and the mending from restoration. Read the object by what care makes of its wearing, never by its condition alone (the same worn thing is a candidate for the bin, the workbench, or the vitrine), and never as the word object-cycle alone. You link to the composite, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- What a given wearing, decay, or mend is made of belongs to the three engines this composite wires -- one phase alone is a moment, not the life. The object's standing worth or belonging to a persona is the heirloom, extended-self, and sign-value engines'; its career of status is biography's; the keeper's feeling for it is the persona's own. This composite owns the material arc and the fate its care decides.

## Knowledge

- Object-cycle is the whole material life an object turns through -- worn by use, decayed by time, mended or not -- read as a wiring of the wear, decay, and restoration engines, and its question is what the keeper's care makes of it (Kopytoff's cultural biography of things; Appadurai's social life of things, read as a material arc). It runs in three shapes. Upkeep is care meeting the degradation -- the object mended against wear and decay and kept in service, its life bought by maintenance (restoration's continuation stance as the standing answer). Discard is care withdrawing -- the object used up and let go, the disposable and the throwaway, most objects' plain fate. Treasure is the twist -- the wear and decay themselves become the object's worth, its seasoning and patina prized, the thing made singular and more, not less, by its life. Read an object's life by what its care makes of it.

## System

- Do read the life as a composition of three phases, never as one blurred decline: name whether the object is kept (upkeep), let go (discard), or prized for its marks (treasure), and cast each phase from its engine.
- Do let the object's fate turn on care, not condition: the same worn and aged thing can be maintained, discarded, or treasured -- which it becomes is the keeper's answer, not the wear's.
- Do let treasure gather wear's seasoning and decay's patina into one reading: the marks of a life prized as worth, the object made singular -- the composite's twist, the sibling of the whole family's ambivalence.
- Do not model the single phases (those are wear, decay, restoration), the object's standing worth to a persona (heirloom, extended-self, sign-value), its career of status (biography), or the keeper's feeling (the persona's own engines). Do not make the object's life mirror a character's life (that is the play's pitch). Do not edit the composite's files; wire only from the play's side.
