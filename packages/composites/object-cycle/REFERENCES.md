# Object-Cycle: Reference

## Line of Work

Object-cycle as **composite**: the whole material life an object turns through --
worn by use, decayed by time, and met or not by mending -- and what its keeper's
care makes of it. It is not a new force but a wiring of three, the object parallel
to the neighborhood-cycle composite (which wires the place-decline family) on the
piece type: the **wear** engine supplies the degradation of use, the **decay**
engine the entropy of time, the **restoration** engine the mending. This composite
adds no new force; it reads what the three make across a thing's lifespan, and
stages that arc into its shapes -- the life kept, the life let go, and the life
prized for its marks.

The domain does not model any single phase -- each is an engine's, and one alone
is a moment, not the life; nor the object's standing worth or belonging to a
persona, which the heirloom, extended-self, and sign-value engines own; nor the
object's career between commodity and singular status, which is biography's (this
composite reads the _material_ arc, not the status one); nor the keeper's feeling
for the thing, which is the persona's own engines'. It owns the material life and
the fate its care decides.

The spine is the biography-of-things view, read materially. Kopytoff argued that
things have cultural biographies -- careers, life-stages, moments of singularity --
just as persons do; Appadurai gathered the trajectories objects trace through
regimes of value. Read as a _material_ arc rather than a career of status, that
biography is the interplay of degradation (wear and decay) and care (restoration)
over a finite life, and its drama is what care makes of the wearing: whether the
object is maintained (Denis & Pontille's care of things), used up and discarded
(Chapman's disposability), or prized for the very marks its life left
(Csikszentmihalyi & Rochberg-Halton's cherished thing). The three bridges are those
fates: upkeep, discard, treasure.

## Origin

The material culture and biography of the object's life over time, wired as a
composite over the three object engines.

| Source                                               | Key Work                                    | Scope                                                                                                                                          |
| :--------------------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------ | :--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Igor Kopytoff**                                    | _"The Cultural Biography of Things"_ (1986) | Things have biographies -- careers, life-stages, moments of singularity; the object read as living a life. The root.                           |
| **Arjun Appadurai**                                  | _The Social Life of Things_ (1986)          | The trajectories objects trace through regimes of value; the object's social life over time. The root.                                         |
| **Jerome Denis & David Pontille**                    | _The Care of Things_ (2022)                 | Maintenance as the discreet, continuous care that keeps things in being -- upkeep as an ethic of attention, not an event. The upkeep bridge.   |
| **Jonathan Chapman**                                 | _Emotionally Durable Design_ (2005)         | Why objects are kept or thrown away -- disposability, the throwaway economy, and the attachment that resists it. The discard bridge.           |
| **Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi & Eugene Rochberg-Halton** | _The Meaning of Things_ (1981)              | The cherished object, prized and individualized by a life of use and age -- the thing made more, not less, by its living. The treasure bridge. |

## Restrictions

What the composite refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The single phases (the atoms)**: The composite wires the three engines but
  owns none. What a given wearing is -- its abrasion, flexure, depletion, seasoning
  -- is the wear engine's; what a given decay is -- its rust, rot, crumble, patina
  -- is the decay engine's; what a given mend is -- its reversion, testimony,
  stabilization, continuation, fidelity -- is the restoration engine's. Object-cycle
  reads only what the three make as a life; a single phase alone is a moment, not
  the arc.
- **The object's standing worth and belonging (heirloom, extended-self,
  sign-value)**: The composite models the material life and its fate, not what the
  object _means_ to a persona. Whether it is an heirloom, an extension of a self, or
  a coded status-sign is those engines'; object-cycle reads the wear, the decay, the
  mending, and what care makes of them. The treasure bridge touches worth, but as
  the _marks made value_ -- not the persona's attachment, which is elsewhere.
- **The object's career of status (biography)**: The charted biography engine reads
  the object's movement between commodity and singular status -- an economic and
  social career. Object-cycle reads the _material_ arc: the wearing, the ageing, the
  mending. The two are parallel readings of "the life of a thing," one of status and
  one of matter; treasure is where they touch (the marked thing become singular).
- **The keeper's feeling (the persona's engines)**: The attachment to a kept thing,
  the pang at a discarded one, the reverence for a treasured one are the persona's
  own engines' (nostalgia, and their kin). The composite delivers the object's arc
  and the fate its care decides; the feeling in the keeper is theirs. And the
  object's life made to **stand for** a character's own life is refused: that is
  authored key, the play's pitch, not the object's force.

## Encoding

Source to constraint. A composite root over three engine atoms it declares as
dependencies, with three bridges.

- **[object-cycle](process_object_cycle.md)** (the root): the whole material life
  an object turns through, and the question its care forces -- kept, let go, or
  prized. Anchored by Kopytoff and Appadurai (the biography of things), read as a
  material arc.
- **[upkeep](process_object_cycle_upkeep.md)** (bridge): care meets the degradation
  -- the object mended and maintained, its service-life extended. Anchored by Denis
  & Pontille (the care of things); restoration's continuation stance as the standing
  answer.
- **[discard](process_object_cycle_discard.md)** (bridge): care withdraws -- the
  object used up and let go, the disposable and the throwaway. Anchored by Chapman
  (emotionally durable design and its absence).
- **[treasure](process_object_cycle_treasure.md)** (bridge): the wear and decay
  become worth -- the seasoning and patina prized, the object made singular by its
  life. Anchored by Csikszentmihalyi & Rochberg-Halton; the composite's twist,
  gathering wear's seasoning and decay's patina into one reading.
