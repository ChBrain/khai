# Process: Object-Cycle, Upkeep

## Taxonomy

[Object-Cycle](process_object_cycle.md), the bridge where care meets the degradation and keeps the thing in service.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: object-cycle

## Initiated by

The object wears and ages, and its keeper answers by mending it, over and over, to keep it working.
[Wear](@chbrain/khai-engine-wear/process_wear.md) dulls the edge and [decay](@chbrain/khai-engine-decay/process_decay.md) rusts the fitting; [restoration](@chbrain/khai-engine-restoration/process_restoration.md) re-grinds, re-oils, replaces the worn part -- its continuation stance, the thing kept alive through the turnover of its matter.
Upkeep starts wherever the response to degradation is maintenance rather than replacement or reverence.

## Direction

Toward a serviceable object, its life extended by care.
The tool is sharpened, the machine serviced, the boot re-soled, the fitting de-rusted and painted; the object is held at working order against the wear and decay that would end it, its span stretched well past what neglect would have allowed.
Upkeep moves the object through many small mends, each buying more life, the thing kept fit for its work rather than let fall or set aside.

## Lever

The cost of the mending against the value of the continued service.
The carrying force is that a kept thing outlasts a spent one -- maintenance buys life more cheaply than replacement, and a well-kept object serves for generations; the resistance is the labour and the parts each mend costs, and the point where the mending costs more than the object is worth.
A production holds this lever by making the keeping deliberate and recurring -- the serviced watch, the maintained blade, the re-soled boot -- never a thing that stays whole on its own.

## Echo

The object still in service, its life bought by care.
It shows a working thing kept working -- the re-ground edge, the fresh sole, the serviced mechanism -- and carries beneath it the standing answer to wear and decay: that a thing need not be spent when it wears, that maintenance is the quiet labour that keeps the object-world in being.
Upkeep is dated not from one mend but from the long succession of them, and it is the bridge where the object's finite life is met, again and again, by the hand that will not let it go.
