# Process: Object-Cycle, Discard

## Taxonomy

[Object-Cycle](process_object_cycle.md), the bridge where care withdraws and the thing is used up and let go.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: object-cycle

## Initiated by

The object wears and ages, and no mending comes -- it is used until spent, then let go.
[Wear](@chbrain/khai-engine-wear/process_wear.md) spends it and [decay](@chbrain/khai-engine-decay/process_decay.md) takes it, and [restoration](@chbrain/khai-engine-restoration/process_restoration.md) never reaches in; the thing runs down to the end of its use and is thrown out or replaced.
Discard starts wherever the object is not worth mending -- the consumable meant to be spent, the cheap thing cheaper to replace than repair, the possession simply outgrown or tired of.

## Direction

Toward the object let go, its life ended at its wearing-out.
The disposable is used and binned, the depleted thing not refilled, the broken cheap thing replaced rather than fixed, the tired possession given away or dumped; the object runs its natural span and no care extends it.
Discard moves the object out of the keeper's world -- to the bin, the tip, the charity bag, the back of a drawer -- its life closed at the point wear and decay would close it.

## Lever

The cost of keeping against the ease of replacing.
The carrying force is that replacement can be cheaper, easier, or more wanted than repair -- the economy of the disposable, the pull of the new, the design that makes mending hard; the resistance is whatever would hold the keeper to the thing, a value, a habit, a scarcity that made mending worth it.
When letting go pencils out over keeping, the object is spent and released -- and at scale this is the throwaway world, the thing built to be discarded and the waste it leaves.

## Echo

The object gone, its life closed at its wearing-out.
It shows an absence where the thing was -- the replaced tool, the emptied and binned consumable, the possession passed on or thrown away -- and carries beneath it the plainest fate of a made thing: that most objects are used up and let go, and only some are kept.
Discard is dated from the release, and it is the bridge that keeps the cycle honest: not every worn thing is mended or treasured; most are simply spent.
