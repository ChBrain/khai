# Process: Object-Cycle, Treasure

## Taxonomy

[Object-Cycle](process_object_cycle.md), the bridge where the wear and decay themselves become the object's worth.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: object-cycle

## Initiated by

The object wears and ages, and the marks its life left are prized rather than mended away or discarded.
Here the [wear](@chbrain/khai-engine-wear/process_wear.md) is read as its seasoning and the [decay](@chbrain/khai-engine-decay/process_decay.md) as its patina, and the [restoration](@chbrain/khai-engine-restoration/process_restoration.md) that touches it, if any, is the stance that keeps those marks -- stabilizing, or gilding them as testimony, never wiping them to new.
Treasure starts wherever an object's age and use make it worth more, not less.

## Direction

Toward the object revered for the life it has visibly lived.
The broken-in handle, the patinated bronze, the faded and fitted cloth, the mended-in-gold bowl, the antique whose wear is its provenance -- these are kept and prized precisely for what wear and time did to them; the marks are the value, and to erase them would be to destroy it.
Treasure moves the object from serviceable, or spent, to singular -- the one worn by this life to this state, no longer interchangeable with its unused twin.

## Lever

The worth the marks confer against the impulse to renew or replace.
The carrying force is that age and use are scarce and unfakeable -- seasoning and patina are earned only by a life, and the eye that prizes them (Riegl's age-value) values the earning; the resistance is the pull to clean back to new, or to trade the marked old thing for an unmarked one.
The stance rides a knife-edge the atoms already named: this is wear's seasoning and decay's patina gathered into one reading -- the same wear and decay that spend one object here ennoble another, and the composite is what reads which.

## Echo

The object made more, not less, by its life.
It shows a thing prized for its marks -- the worn, the aged, the mended-visibly, the loved -- and carries beneath it the object-cycle's own twist, the sibling of the whole family's ambivalence: that a made thing's wear and age are not only its ruin but, kept and read the other way, its worth.
Treasure is dated from the life it took to earn its marks, and it is the bridge where an object outlives mere service and becomes an heirloom, a relic, a thing kept for having lived.
