# Relative Deprivation: Reference

## Line of Work

Relative deprivation as a **composite**: the grievance that arises when an unfavorable comparison is appraised as unjust and ignites resentment, read whole across the atoms it spans. Runciman and the modern literature define it as more than a comparison and more than a want: it is the felt injustice of a disadvantage judged undeserved. This composite reads the two its atoms hold -- the comparison engine's upward comparison (the persona or its group measured against a better-off referent) and the anger engine's grievance and indignation (the resentment the gap ignites) -- and adds only what no atom holds: the appraisal of the disadvantage as unjust, undeserved, and wrongful, the affective-injustice layer that turns a neutral difference into a felt deprivation. It declares the two engines as dependencies, wires their members by hard package links, and reads the compound and its two levels.

The warrant is accordingly integrative, not primary. Relative deprivation is not a third atom beside comparison and anger; it is what those two compose when a comparison is appraised through a sense of injustice. Runciman drew the crucial distinction of level: egoistic relative deprivation, the individual measured against other individuals, and fraternal (group-based) relative deprivation, one's group measured against other groups -- and argued it is the fraternal kind that underlies collective grievance. Smith and Pettigrew's meta-analysis confirmed and specified the model: relative deprivation requires a cognitive comparison, a cognitive appraisal of disadvantage, and -- decisively -- that the disadvantage be seen as unfair and felt with resentment; and it is group-based, not individual, relative deprivation that predicts collective action, protest, and intergroup prejudice. The sources below are the literature of that compound and its levels; each atom's own REFERENCES carries the primary science of its part.

## Restrictions

What the composite refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The neutral comparison (comparison)**: The evaluation of one's standing against a referent -- upward, downward, or lateral -- is the comparison engine's; relative deprivation is what that comparison becomes when its shortfall is appraised as unjust. The composite restates none of the comparison mechanics; a bridge links, it never re-authors. Comparison notes the gap; this composite resents it as a wrong.
- **The resentment itself (anger)**: The grievance and indignation the deprivation ignites are the anger engine's; relative deprivation supplies what those feelings are about -- a wrongful disadvantage -- but the affective heat is anger's. The composite reads the injustice appraisal that directs the anger, not the anger's own machinery.
- **Envy**: The pain at another's specific advantage, and the hostility toward the advantaged other, is the envy engine's; relative deprivation overlaps it but is distinguished by its injustice-and-entitlement core and, in the fraternal form, its group level and collective-action drive. Envy fixes on the rival who has more; relative deprivation fixes on the unfairness of having less, and can move a whole group. The composite reads the deprivation, not the envy it can accompany.
- **The group identification (social-identity)**: The fraternal form presupposes that the persona identifies with the group whose deprivation it feels; that identification -- the drawing of the self-concept from group membership -- is the social-identity engine's. The composite reads the group grievance and its collective-action drive, not the mechanics of identifying with the group in the first place.

## Encoding

Source to constraint. A root (the deprivation compound) with two forms, each a level the grievance is pitched at, wired by hard package links into the atoms it spans.

- **[relative-deprivation](process_relative_deprivation.md)** (the root): an upward comparison (comparison) appraised as unjust and igniting resentment (anger), the affective-injustice layer that makes a difference a deprivation. Anchored by Runciman (the definition) and Smith & Pettigrew (the three-requirement model).
- **[relative deprivation, egoistic](position_deprivation_egoistic.md)** (form, the individual level): the persona deprived as an individual against other individuals, driving personal resentment and self-focused response. Anchored by Runciman and Crosby (egoistic relative deprivation) over the comparison and anger members it links.
- **[relative deprivation, fraternal](position_deprivation_fraternal.md)** (form, the group level): the persona's group deprived against other groups, driving collective action, protest, and intergroup hostility. Anchored by Runciman and Smith & Pettigrew (fraternal deprivation as the driver of collective action) over the comparison and anger members it links.
