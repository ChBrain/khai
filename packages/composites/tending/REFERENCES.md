# Tending: Reference

## Line of Work

Tending as **composite**: what happens in a persona who meets another's need. It is not a third
mechanism but a wiring of two: the **compassion** engine supplies the other-focused state and the
two responses that shadow it, and the **caregiving** engine supplies the behavioural system that
acts on a felt need and what running it costs. This composite adds no third engine; it reads the
seam the two make -- one system met at its feeling end and its acting end -- and stages that seam
into three bridges sorted by where the relief is aimed, closing on the discovery that the two ways
tending fails are one mechanism at two speeds.

The domain does not model either side alone -- tenderness, succor, distress, pity, and the
tenderhearted trait are the compassion engine's; sensitivity, overprotection, and burnout are
caregiving's; and one alone is a process, not the question of where the relief went. Nor is it
what the suffering is like for the sufferer, which is the tragic composite's; nor the bond a
persona has to somebody, which is love's, since this fires for strangers and needs no bond; nor
the standing capacity to absorb stress, which is resilience's. It owns the aim: whom the movement
is actually for.

The join is declared inside both atoms and runs in both directions. The compassion engine anchors
Goetz, Keltner and Simon-Thomas, whose evolutionary review sets compassion as a distinct affective
state built on the caregiving system rather than as a general good feeling -- the feeling end
pointing at the acting end. The caregiving engine anchors Figley on the erosion of the carer,
which names compassion itself as the thing that wears -- the acting end pointing back at the
feeling end. What neither atom can say alone is the thing this composite is for: that the
movement's occurrence carries no information, because the system fires whether or not the relief
is pointed at the other person, and the aim is the whole question.

Batson supplies the first half of the answer and the sharpest test in the literature. He
separated empathic concern -- an other-focused state -- from personal distress, the observer's own
aversive arousal at another's suffering, and found the two dissociate under one condition: make
escape easy, and personal distress predicts leaving while empathic concern predicts helping
anyway. That is the flinch bridge's mechanism and its tell, and it is why intensity of feeling
cannot be used as the measure -- distress is frequently the stronger experience. Nussbaum supplies
the second exit, pity as the response that relieves by placing the sufferer below, and the
caregiving engine's own overprotection supplies the third, a protection sized to the carer's
anxiety rather than the other's need. Ainsworth's sensitivity is the turn's positive criterion:
reading the need that is there rather than the one expected.

Singer and Klimecki supply the step that makes the third bridge more than an account of tiredness,
and it is the composite's keystone. Empathic resonance with suffering -- feeling **with** -- and
compassion -- feeling **for**, with warmth and a motivation to help -- are separable states with
different signatures and different consequences: resonance with pain is aversive and leads toward
withdrawal, while compassion is affiliative and does not. In their training work, cultivating
empathy raised negative affect and cultivating compassion afterwards reversed it. Read against
Figley, that reframes the carer's collapse: what is spent by years of taking each suffering on is
not a stock of caring but the cost of running one's own alarm several times a day, so the flinch
and the toll are the same failure at two timescales -- fast where an exit exists, slow where it
does not. Which means the persona instructed to feel what the sufferer feels has been aimed at
the exit, and the quiet carer who is accused of detachment may be in the only state that can be
kept up.

**On the evidence, stated plainly.** The parts are not equally firm. Batson's dissociation under
easy versus difficult escape is old, replicated, and the strongest single result the composite
rests on. The compassion-as-caregiving-substrate account is a well-motivated synthesis rather than
a single decisive finding. The empathy/compassion separation is well argued and supported by
imaging and by training studies, but the training evidence is a modest base from a small number of
programmes, so the direction should be taken from it and not a magnitude. Compassion fatigue as a
measured construct overlaps heavily with burnout and secondary traumatic stress and its
discriminant validity is disputed -- which is a reason to state the mechanism, as this composite
does, rather than to lean on the label. The three bridges are the turn, the flinch, and the toll.

## Origin

The compassion literature and attachment-theoretic caregiving research, wired as a composite over
the compassion and caregiving engines. The first two rows are the **joining** -- the published work
that sets the feeling and the acting as one system -- and the rest anchor the individual bridges
and their bounds.

## Restrictions

What the composite refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The two sides (the atoms)**: The composite wires the two but owns neither. Tenderness, succor,
  distress, pity, and the tenderhearted trait are the compassion engine's; sensitivity,
  overprotection, and burnout are caregiving's. Tending reads only where the relief is aimed, and
  one side alone is a process rather than an answer to that question.
- **The sufferer's end (tragic)**: Empathy, grief, and reversal are the tragic composite's, which
  reads what the suffering is like and what it is like to be with it. Tending is read strictly on
  the one who meets the need, and is indifferent to whether the sufferer is comforted -- a turn
  that fails to help is still a turn, and a flinch that accidentally helps is still a flinch.
- **The bond (love, moral-account)**: Desire, loyalty, and self-disclosure are the love
  composite's, and the wrong between two and its repair is moral-account's. Tending needs no
  relationship at all: the system fires for strangers, and a play that requires an attachment
  before it can run has confused this with love.
- **The capacity to absorb (resilience, slack)**: Coping and stress under load are the resilience
  composite's, which owns the absorbing and the recovering; the room a persona has before their own
  shortfall does their thinking is slack's. Tending owns the aiming, and the toll bridge is where
  all three meet -- a carer with no slack burns out sooner, and that is slack's reading of the same
  scene.
- **Kindness to oneself (self-relation)**: Self-compassion, self-esteem, and authenticity are the
  self-relation composite's. This composite is outward-facing throughout, and its inward-facing
  failure -- the flinch -- is still about somebody else's suffering, not about the persona's
  standing with themselves.
- **The response to the fortunate (praise, outdone)**: Admiration, elevation, gratitude, and envy
  run toward people who are doing well. Tending runs toward the badly-off, and the two families
  should not be collapsed into a general warmth toward others.
- **The response to the guilty (deserving)**: The costly sanction paid to make a wrongdoer answer
  is the altruistic-punishment engine's, inside deserving. It also moves a persona to act at their
  own expense on somebody else's behalf, and it points at a transgressor where this points at a
  sufferer.
- **The suffering as authored symbol (the play's pitch)**: A misfortune made to **stand for** a
  theme -- the illness that mirrors a motif of decay -- is refused: that is authored key, the play's
  pitch, not the force on the persona. Tending sets where the relief went; the meaning read into
  the suffering is the play's.

## Encoding

Source to constraint. A composite root over two engine atoms it declares as dependencies, with
three bridges, sorted by where the relief is aimed.

- **[tending](process_tending.md)** (the root): what happens in a persona who meets another's need,
  read on the one meeting it and never on the one who has it. Anchored by Goetz, Keltner &
  Simon-Thomas (2010) and by Bowlby and George & Solomon on the caregiving system.
- **[the turn](process_tending_turn.md)** (bridge): the relief aimed at the other, tracking their
  state rather than the persona's, and undemonstrative by construction. Anchored by Ainsworth on
  sensitive responsiveness and Collins & Feeney on support that does not take over.
- **[the flinch](process_tending_flinch.md)** (bridge): the relief aimed at the persona's own state
  by three routes -- distress that departs, pity that places the other below, protection sized to
  the carer's anxiety. Anchored by Batson on the easy-escape dissociation and Nussbaum on pity.
- **[the toll](process_tending_toll.md)** (bridge): what sustained tending costs, and what is
  actually spent. Anchored by Figley on the carer's erosion and Singer & Klimecki on resonance
  against compassion; the composite's twist and keystone, that the flinch and the toll are one
  failure at two speeds, so what ends the caring is not a shortage of it but an excess of
  resonance.
