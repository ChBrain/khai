# Selfhood: Reference

## Line of Work

Selfhood as **composite**: the self as an object to itself -- held against the selves it is
not, repaired when threatened, and anchored by the confirmation it seeks. It is not a new
self but a wiring of four: the **self-discrepancy** engine supplies the guides a persona
falls short of, ideal and ought; the **possible-selves** engine supplies the futures it
carries, hoped, feared, expected, and lost; the **self-affirmation** engine supplies the
restoration of integrity from an untouched domain when the self is struck; and the
**self-verification** engine supplies the drive to have feedback match the self-view already
held. This composite adds no fifth engine; it reads what the four make together -- a
self-concept that compares, defends, and holds itself in place -- and stages that into three
workings, closing on the discovery that a self's need for coherence outruns its wish to be
flattered.

The domain does not model any single force -- the guides and their distinct emotions are the
self-discrepancy engine's, the future selves are possible-selves', the restoration is
self-affirmation's, the confirmation-seeking is self-verification's, and one alone is a
process or a position, not the whole structure. Nor is it the evaluation of the self's worth,
the care it gives itself in suffering, or its fidelity to itself, which are the self-relation
composite's; nor the self-story a persona tells about who they are and defends under
challenge, which is the identity engine's; nor the belief about what the self can do, which is
the capability composite's; nor the labour of showing a self to others, which is the
presentation composite's. It owns the self-concept as a working structure: the self taken as
an object by the persona that holds it, and kept coherent because a self that shifted with
every appraisal could not act at all.

The spine is the reading of the self-concept as an active structure rather than a stored
description -- what Markus and Wurf called the dynamic self-concept, a working set of
self-representations that regulate what a persona attempts, expects, and takes personally.
Higgins gave the comparison its precision: the actual self measured against an ideal guide,
whose shortfall yields dejection, and against an ought guide, whose shortfall yields
agitation -- two gaps with two distinct emotional signatures. Markus and Nurius carried the
self into time: possible selves, the hoped-for, feared, and expected versions that pull and
warn. Steele read the defence: self-affirmation, integrity restored from a valued domain
unrelated to the threat, which lets a hard truth be absorbed instead of fought. And Swann read
the anchor and the composite's turn: self-verification, the drive to be seen as one already
sees oneself -- so robust that people with negative self-views seek and hold onto the feedback
that confirms them, preferring to be known accurately over being esteemed wrongly. The three
bridges are those readings staged: the measure, the repair, and the anchor.

## Origin

The social psychology of the self-concept as a working structure, wired as a composite over
the self-discrepancy, possible-selves, self-affirmation, and self-verification engines.

| :------------------------------ | :------------------------------------------------------------- | :---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Hazel Markus & Elissa Wurf**  | _"The dynamic self-concept"_ (1987)                            | The self-concept as an active, working structure that regulates behaviour rather than a stored description. The root.                                 |
| **E. Tory Higgins**             | _"Self-discrepancy: A theory relating self and affect"_ (1987) | The actual self against ideal and ought guides -- two gaps, dejection and agitation. The measure bridge.                                              |

## Restrictions

What the composite refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The single engines (the atoms)**: The composite wires the four but owns none. The gaps from
  the ideal and ought guides and their distinct emotions are the self-discrepancy engine's; the
  hoped, feared, expected, and lost futures are possible-selves'; the affirming, adequacy, and
  buffering are self-affirmation's; the soliciting, signaling, and resistance are
  self-verification's. Selfhood reads only what the four make as one structure; a single engine
  alone is a process or a position, not the whole.
- **The self's evaluation of itself (self-relation)**: How a persona values its own worth,
  cares for itself in suffering, and stays true to itself is the self-relation composite's.
  Selfhood is the structure of the self-concept and its maintenance, not the persona's
  evaluative or caring stance toward it.
- **The self-story (identity)**: The account a persona holds of who they are, how firmly, and
  on what basis it is defended under challenge is the identity engine's. Selfhood is the
  comparison and confirmation machinery around a self-view, not the narrative content of it.
- **The belief about what the self can do (capability)**: The theory of ability, the task
  confidence, and the handicapping that guards them are the capability composite's. Selfhood is
  what a persona holds itself to be, not what it believes it can do.
- **The labour of being seen (presentation)**: The face claimed, the region held, the display
  tuned, and the feeling managed before an audience are the presentation composite's. Selfhood
  is the self-concept the performance may or may not be faithful to, not the performing.
- **The self-concept as authored symbol (the play's pitch)**: A persona's self-concept made to
  **stand for** a story -- the ideal-self shortfall that mirrors a theme of failure, the
  verification that mirrors a moral about self-knowledge -- is refused: that is authored key,
  the play's pitch, not the force on the persona. Selfhood delivers the persona's own
  self-holding; the meaning read into it is the play's.

## Encoding

Source to constraint. A composite root over four engine atoms it declares as dependencies,
with three bridges, the workings of a self-concept.

- **[selfhood](process_selfhood.md)** (the root): the self as an object to itself -- measured,
  repaired, and anchored, held coherent because a self that shifted with every appraisal could
  not act. Anchored by Markus & Wurf (the dynamic self-concept).
- **[the measure](process_selfhood_measure.md)** (bridge): the other selves a persona is held
  against -- the ideal and ought guides, and the hoped, feared, expected, and lost futures.
  Anchored by Higgins (self-discrepancy) and Markus & Nurius (possible selves).
- **[the repair](process_selfhood_repair.md)** (bridge): integrity restored from an untouched
  valued domain, buffering a threat so it can be admitted rather than denied. Anchored by
  Steele (self-affirmation).
- **[the anchor](process_selfhood_anchor.md)** (bridge): the drive to have the world confirm the
  self already held -- soliciting, signaling, resisting, even against a kinder view. Anchored by
  Swann; the composite's twist and keystone, that a self needs to be known more than it needs to
  be thought well of.
