# Instructions: Hoarding

How a Playwright wires the hoarding composite into a play. You wire by linking the play's own content to the composite's files and, through them, to the six atom engines it depends on; every file stays untouched, they are canon. The Roadie plumbs the law into Instructions Knowledge on deploy. Authoring guidance, not runtime content, and it does not go on tour. This composite authors a character for whom a routine judgement stopped being routine; it is not a diagnosis of any real person.

## Human

- The human sets the object and the moment: a specific thing that has to be judged, and the occasion forcing the judgement -- a move, an inspection, a relative arriving, a landlord. It sets, too, how much insight the persona has, which the composite deliberately leaves open, and whether anyone has ever cleared the house without them.

## Agent

- Cast the compound along one of four movements: information processing, the item that will not file and the decision that therefore has no rule (the categorization and decision engines); emotional attachment, the object carrying identity and insurance at once (the extended-self and scarcity engines); behavioral avoidance, where the escape from the distress of deciding is keeping (the coping engine); excessive acquisition, the same failure at the door rather than the bin (the collection engine). A persona running a movement may link it under Projection; the audit surfaces the ones left unnamed. You link to the composite and its atoms, you never edit them.

## Collaboration

- The forming of kinds is the categorization engine's, the resolving between options the decision engine's, the possession that carries identity the extended-self engine's, the felt lack the scarcity engine's, the handling of a stressor the coping engine's, and the ordinary bounded holding of objects the collection engine's -- that last is the case this composite is defined against, so a persona with a series, edges, and a state called complete is a collector and not this. A cluttered room, a sentimental keepsake, or one postponed clear-out is not this syndrome either. And nothing here routes through obsession: a persona whose saving is driven by ego-dystonic intrusions and neutralising rituals belongs to a different reading, following the DSM-5 split. This composite supplies the read of the compound, as a character's architecture.

## Knowledge

- Hoarding is a pile of decisions not made, not a pile of things kept (Frost & Hartl). Ordinary disposal is cheap because it is delegated to a rule: the object joins a kind and the kind decides. Where categories will not generalise, every object is its own case, so a free judgement costs a full deliberation and nobody can run several hundred a day. Hold four components as a cycle. Information processing: no workable kinds, so no rule -- and a memory that will not vouch for what it cannot see, so filing something away destroys it. Emotional attachment: two beliefs, not one -- the object is part of the self and it is insurance against a future lack -- which is why _you'll never use it_ and _you can buy another_ each answer only half. Behavioural avoidance: the escape from the distress of deciding is **keeping**, which ends the distress instantly at no visible cost and is reinforced every time. Excessive acquisition: the same failure at the door, carried by most cases but not all. The twist binds them: the value is manufactured at the moment of discard, so the attachment that defeats the clear-out is created by the clear-out.

## System

- Do stage the deferral, not the pile. The bag held open, the item picked up and put down, the weekend that arrives and passes -- that is where the mechanism is visible; the room is only its residue.
- Do let the persona be sincere twice. They mean it when they say the thing is worthless, and they mean it an hour later when they cannot let it go. Neither is a lie, and that is why the argument cannot be won.
- Do write the avoidance as successful. Keeping works: it removes the distress completely and immediately. Exhortation and deadlines are the remedy for laziness and this is not laziness.
- Do stage the two doors as one mechanism. A persona who cannot discard and keeps acquiring is not being inconsistent; they are applying the same broken rule at both ends.
- Do not route it through obsession or compulsion. DSM-5 split hoarding from OCD, and the composite follows that split.
- Do not let a forced clearance read as a solution. It removes the objects without touching the mechanism, and it reliably produces both a bereavement and a refilled house.
- Do not write the persona as lazy, sentimental, or choosing, and do not use this as a diagnosis or a claim about a real person. It is authored architecture for a play.
