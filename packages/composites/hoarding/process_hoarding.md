# Process: Hoarding

## Taxonomy

A composite reading over six atom engines (no canon file of its own): the categorization, decision, extended-self, scarcity, coping, and collection engines this package declares as dependencies.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: hoarding

## Initiated by

A persona meets an object whose disposal requires a decision, and has no procedure for making it.
The compound begins at the point of discard, not at the point of acquisition, which is where the ordinary account of hoarding goes wrong. What enters is a single unremarkable object -- a newspaper, a container, a cable for a device long gone -- presented for a judgement the persona cannot perform: is this one of a kind, or one of a class? For most personas the question is answered before it is asked, because the object files itself into a category whose members are interchangeable and disposable. Here it does not file. Each item arrives as [an exemplar of nothing](@chbrain/khai-engine-categorization/process_exemplars.md), particular and unrepeatable, so a judgement that is normally free costs a full deliberation -- and the deliberation is what the persona cannot afford to run several hundred times a day.

## Direction

From an object that will not file, through a decision that is deferred rather than made, toward a room organised by everything that was never resolved.
The four components are Frost & Hartl's and they run as a cycle rather than a list. The [categorization](@chbrain/khai-engine-categorization/process_categorization.md) deficit makes each item singular, so [deciding](@chbrain/khai-engine-decision/process_decision.md) about it cannot be delegated to a rule and the persona [stalls](@chbrain/khai-engine-decision/process_paralysed.md). Stalling is unpleasant, so the [escape](@chbrain/khai-engine-coping/process_escape.md) is taken: not discarding, which resolves nothing and costs distress now, but _keeping_, which resolves nothing and costs nothing now. The object stays, and while it stays it is [appropriated](@chbrain/khai-engine-extended-self/process_appropriation.md) -- lived beside, handled, made part of the persona's extended reach -- so the next time it comes up for judgement it is worth more than it was. Meanwhile the same failure runs at the door: an object encountered in the world is also singular, also unrepeatable, also cheaper to take than to reason about, and [acquisition](@chbrain/khai-engine-collection/process_hunting.md) proceeds on the same mechanism that prevents disposal.

## Lever

Whether the object is being judged, or merely postponed. Everything the compound stages turns on this, and it is what separates hoarding from an untidy house. The clutter is not an accumulation of things the persona decided to keep; it is an accumulation of decisions the persona did not make, and each object in it is a deferral wearing the shape of a possession. Stage the deferral rather than the pile -- the bag held open, the item picked up and put down, the _I'll deal with it properly at the weekend_ -- because that is where the mechanism is visible and the pile is only its residue.

**The twist is that the value is manufactured at the moment of discard.** While an object sits unregarded it is worth nothing and the persona will say so freely. Open the bag, and the same object becomes unbearable to lose: the [dispossession](@chbrain/khai-engine-extended-self/process_dispossession.md) is felt as a loss of self rather than of property, and reasons arrive to justify what the feeling already decided. So the attachment that defeats the clear-out is _created by the clear-out_, which means every attempt to solve the problem generates the resistance that beats it, and the persona is at their most sincere both when they agree the thing is worthless and when, an hour later, they cannot let it go. Neither statement is a lie, which is why the argument with them cannot be won and why help offered as force -- clearing the house while the persona is out -- reliably produces both a bereavement and a refilled house.

## Echo

The syndrome surfaces through four movements, Frost & Hartl's components: [information processing](position_information_processing.md), the item that will not file and the decision that therefore cannot be made; [emotional attachment](position_emotional_attachment.md), the object carrying identity and insurance, so that losing it is a loss of self and of safety at once; [behavioral avoidance](position_behavioral_avoidance.md), the saving chosen because it is the move that costs nothing _now_; and [excessive acquisition](position_excessive_acquisition.md), the same mechanism running at the door rather than at the bin. The forming of kinds is the categorization engine's; the resolving between options is the decision engine's; the possession that carries identity is the extended-self engine's and the felt lack that makes it insurance the scarcity engine's; the effortful handling of a stressor is the coping engine's; and the ordinary, curated, bounded holding of objects is the collection engine's, against which this composite is the unbounded case. Hoarding is what those six look like when a routine judgement stops being routine -- read across the compound, never in the pile alone, and authored as a character's architecture, never diagnosed onto a real person.
