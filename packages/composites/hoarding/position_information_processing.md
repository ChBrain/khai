# Position: Hoarding, Information Processing

## Taxonomy

[Hoarding](process_hoarding.md), the item that will not file -- and the decision that therefore cannot be made.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: hoarding

## Has

The first component, and the one that makes the others necessary rather than merely present. Ordinary disposal is cheap because it is delegated: an object joins a kind, the kind carries a rule, and the rule decides without the persona attending. Here the delegation fails. [Categorization](@chbrain/khai-engine-categorization/process_categorization.md) does not produce workable kinds -- categories come out narrow to the point of uselessness, so each object is [its own exemplar](@chbrain/khai-engine-categorization/process_exemplars.md) rather than an instance of a [prototype](@chbrain/khai-engine-categorization/process_prototype.md), and there is no class of _interchangeable containers_ to which this container belongs. What should have been a rule application becomes a fresh [decision](@chbrain/khai-engine-decision/process_decision.md) about a unique case, and because the case is unique there is no [heuristic](@chbrain/khai-engine-decision/process_heuristic.md) that fits it and no threshold at which [satisficing](@chbrain/khai-engine-decision/process_satisficing.md) is permitted. The persona [stalls](@chbrain/khai-engine-decision/process_paralysed.md). Frost & Hartl add the aggravating half: confidence in memory is low, so the object must stay _visible_ to remain known, which rules out the ordinary remedy of putting it away.

## Orders

Treat every object as a case, and decide none of them.
The information-processing movement orders the persona to attend fully to things that were supposed to be beneath attention -- to pick up the envelope and genuinely consider it, to be unable to say which of two identical jars is the spare, to lose an afternoon to a drawer. The room sees someone being absurdly precious about rubbish and reads it as sentimentality or as stalling. It stages a persona drowning not in objects but in _open questions_, each one small, none of them closable, and the fact that they are visibly working -- sorting, handling, considering -- while nothing leaves is the part that reads worst from outside.

## Loses

The delegation that makes ordinary life affordable. Carrying this movement, the persona forgoes the enormous unnoticed economy of the rule -- the ability to throw away a hundred things without thinking about any of them -- and pays for it in attention, which is finite and now spent on jars. It loses the use of storage, since anything filed is effectively destroyed for a memory that will not vouch for what it cannot see. And it loses the credibility of its own effort: having worked all day on the spare room and cleared nothing, it cannot demonstrate the labour, so the labour is assumed not to have happened. What it trades is the cheap general rule for an unpayable bill of particular judgements.

## Drives

On that failed filing:
each object arrives as a case rather than an instance, so the judgement that should be free costs a deliberation and the deliberation is not completed. Information processing is one of four movements, the one that makes disposal expensive in the first place, set beside the [emotional attachment](position_emotional_attachment.md) that raises the price of losing, the [behavioral avoidance](position_behavioral_avoidance.md) that takes the cheap way out of the stall, and the [excessive acquisition](position_excessive_acquisition.md) where the same failure runs at the door. That the pile is deferred decisions rather than chosen possessions is the [root's](process_hoarding.md); the forming of kinds is the categorization engine's and the resolving between options the decision engine's, where this movement owns the specifically hoarding case in which categories will not generalise and the decision therefore has no rule to stand on.
