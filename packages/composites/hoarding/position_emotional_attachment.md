# Position: Hoarding, Emotional Attachment

## Taxonomy

[Hoarding](process_hoarding.md), the object carrying identity and insurance -- so that losing it is a loss of self and of safety at once.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: hoarding

## Has

The second component, and it is two beliefs rather than one, which is why the attachment is so hard to argue with. The first is identity: the object has been [appropriated](@chbrain/khai-engine-extended-self/process_appropriation.md) -- lived beside, handled, incorporated into the persona's reach -- so it is not property but [an extension of the self](@chbrain/khai-engine-extended-self/piece_extended_self.md), and its removal is a [dispossession](@chbrain/khai-engine-extended-self/process_dispossession.md) felt where a bereavement is felt rather than where a loss of value is. The second is insurance: the object is held against a future need, and the [scarcity](@chbrain/khai-engine-scarcity/process_scarcity.md) engine's machinery is running -- the felt lack [tunnels](@chbrain/khai-engine-scarcity/process_tunneling.md) attention onto what might one day be missing, and every discarded thing is a hole in a defence. The two beliefs support each other and defeat different arguments: _you'll never use it_ is answered by the first, _you can buy another_ by neither.

## Orders

Hold it, because letting it go costs more than keeping it.
The emotional-attachment movement orders the persona to price an object by what its absence would mean rather than by what its presence is worth -- so a broken thing is retained, a duplicate is retained, and a gift from someone now dead is not a candidate for discussion. The room sees the valuation and calls it irrational, because by the room's accounting it is. It stages a persona doing an entirely coherent calculation in a currency the room does not use, and being told that the arithmetic is wrong when what is actually in dispute is the unit.

## Loses

The ability to be reassured about anything it owns. Carrying this movement, the persona forgoes the ordinary separation between what it is and what it has, and pays in a grief that is available at any moment, from any bin bag, over anything. It loses the protection that replaceability offers -- if the thing were a member of a class, another could stand in for it, and the class is precisely what the first movement failed to build. And it loses the argument in advance: having felt the loss as real, it cannot accept a valuation that treats the object as a commodity, so every negotiation begins with the other party denying the persona's own experience of what is at stake. What it trades is the lightness of holding things loosely for a set of possessions it cannot survive parting with individually.

## Drives

On that double claim:
the object is both a part of the self and a hedge against want, so its removal is a bereavement and an exposure. Emotional attachment is one of four movements, the one that sets the price of losing, set beside the [information processing](position_information_processing.md) that made the judgement expensive, the [behavioral avoidance](position_behavioral_avoidance.md) that spares the persona from paying it, and the [excessive acquisition](position_excessive_acquisition.md) that adds to the estate. That the price is manufactured at the moment of discard rather than held all along is the [root's](process_hoarding.md); the possession that carries identity is the extended-self engine's and the felt lack that makes it insurance the scarcity engine's, where this movement owns the specifically hoarding case in which both claims attach to ordinary objects in quantity.
