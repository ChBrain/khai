# Instructions: Peopling

How a Playwright wires the peopling composite into a play. You wire by linking the play's
own content to the composite's files; the files stay untouched, they are canon. The
composite installs the legibility, social-time, and street-life engines as dependencies,
and their laws arrive with them. Authoring guidance, not runtime content, and it does not
go on tour.

## Human

- The human sets which places in the play are being read by who is in them: who crosses because they must, who comes back on a rhythm, and who stays for nothing -- and, the question that governs the read, which grade of presence the place actually holds.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, read a place by its population and link under Shown the grade it holds: the errand while people are crossing under obligation (moved along the paths and steered by the landmarks, timed by the schedule that put them there, counted in the presence of eight in the morning, from legibility and social-time), the round while the same people are coming back at the hour they came last time (the calendar and the tempo gathered into a shared moment, the mingling of half-familiars and the watch that comes free with being regularly seen, from social-time and street-life), the loitering while somebody is staying who does not have to be (the sitting-on, the standing-about at a node worth standing about at, from street-life). Play the presence as evidence about the place rather than as scene-dressing, and grade it by what would have kept those people away. You link to the composite, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- The path, edge, district, node, and landmark are the legibility engine's; the calendar, schedule, tempo, and synchrony are social-time's; presence, mingling, watch, and lingering are street-life's -- one alone is a property of a place, not a judgement of it. A persona's own bond with a place is the dwelling composite's, read on the person where this is read on the place; a persona altered by the presence of others is midst's, also on the person; the distance one keeps from another is proxemics'; the built form itself is the place's own chapters and the design engines'; the order kept in an institution's shared quarters is subjection's. This composite owns the population -- and the loitering bridge carries the keystone: the only presence that reports on a place is the presence the place could not compel.

## Knowledge

- Peopling is a place read by the presence in it -- braiding the image it can be held as and moved through (legibility), the rhythm it keeps and gathers on (social-time), and the social occupation it hosts (street-life) into one reading -- and its question is not how many are there but what would have kept them away. The join is Gehl's, and it is diagnostic rather than descriptive: necessary activity happens under nearly all conditions because it must, optional activity happens only where conditions invite it, and social activity is resultant, arising wherever the other two put people in the same space, so it cannot be produced directly at all -- from which follows his order of operations, life first, then space, then buildings, and never the reverse. What the three atoms share is that none of them is in the buildings: an image is held in a head, a rhythm is kept by people, an occupation happens or does not, and a maker can build only the conditions. Lynch established that a place exists for its users as an image built from nameable elements, so its readability is measurable from outside; Jacobs supplied the sidewalk mechanism, the informal watching that comes free with being regularly seen and cannot be installed; Whyte supplied the method and the banal correction, that people sit where there is somewhere to sit and that what most attracts people is other people; Lefebvre supplied the temporal half, a place constituted by rhythms that are spatial and temporal at once. It runs in three faces, graded by how much the place had to compel them. The errand is the crossing that would have happened whatever the place were like: it can show the place works as a route, since a person who gets lost is a person the image failed, and it cannot show the place is any good, since nobody chose it -- so the busiest streets include some of the worst, and the count alone cannot tell them apart. The round is the return kept on a rhythm, half-obliged and half-chosen, where recurrence accumulates recognition and strangers become faces, and where the shared hour that gathers is also the schedule that constrains, so the one who keeps the place's time without being gathered by it is more alone in the full square than in the empty one. The loitering is the twist -- the staying done by people with no reason to be there, the bench occupied all afternoon, the wall sat on though it was not built to be sat on -- and here the faces are seen for one thing: presence is informative in inverse proportion to how much it was compelled, so the loudest reading is the emptiest and the idler on the step is the only one that counts. And because optional presence is indistinguishable from waste to anyone not treating it as an instrument, the hostile bench and the swept plaza and the moving-on remove the sole measurement by which the place could have been known to work. Read a place by who stays for no reason.

## System

- Do read the place, not the person in it: name whether the errand (compelled crossing), the round (kept rhythm), or the loitering (free staying) is what the place holds, and cast each from its engines.
- Do grade presence rather than count it: a market-day crowd and a rush-hour crowd may be the same number and are not the same evidence, and the difference is only the share that would have come anyway.
- Do let the errand prove little: a place can be full and bad, so a play that establishes a place's quality by showing it busy has proved nothing but that people had somewhere to be.
- Do let the round carry its own cost: the rhythm that gathers is the rhythm that constrains, and the persona kept to the place's time without being gathered by it is the loneliest figure in a crowded scene.
- Do let the loitering carry the keystone: it is the instrument and not the ornament, no budget produces it, and the case for it has to be made to people who can see with their own eyes that these persons are doing nothing.
- Do stage the removal where it belongs: the angled seat, the swept plaza, the group moved along -- read that as a place destroying its own measurement, not as tidiness and not as villainy.
- Do not model a persona's bond with a place (that is dwelling), a persona altered by others' presence (that is midst), the distance between two (that is proxemics), the built form itself (that is the place's chapters and the design engines), or presence compelled by an authority (that is subjection, where this grading does not apply). Do not empty a square to stand for a story (that is the play's pitch). Do not edit the composite's files; wire only from the play's side.
