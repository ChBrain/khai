# Peopling: Reference

## Line of Work

Peopling as **composite**: a place read by the presence in it. It is not a fourth mechanism but a
wiring of three: the **legibility** engine supplies the image a place can be held as and moved
through, the **social-time** engine supplies the rhythm it keeps and gathers on, and the
**street-life** engine supplies the social occupation it hosts. This composite adds no fourth
engine; it reads the seam the three make -- that none of the three is in the buildings, since an
image is held in a head, a rhythm is kept by people, and an occupation happens or does not -- and
stages that seam into three bridges graded by how much the place had to compel the presence,
closing on the discovery that the only presence which reports on a place is the presence it could
not have compelled.

The domain does not model any single reading -- the path, edge, district, node, and landmark are
the legibility engine's; the calendar, schedule, tempo, and synchrony are social-time's; presence,
mingling, watch, and lingering are street-life's; and one alone is a property of a place, not a
judgement of it. Nor is it a persona's own bond with a place, which is the dwelling composite's;
nor a persona altered by the presence of others, which is midst's; nor the built form itself,
which belongs to the place's own chapters and the design engines. It owns the population: who is
in a place, what would have kept them away, and what their being there is evidence of.

The spine is Gehl, and the join he made is the one this composite wires. Gehl divides outdoor
activity into three kinds and the division is not descriptive but diagnostic: **necessary**
activity -- going to work, waiting for a bus -- happens under nearly all conditions, because it
must; **optional** activity -- a walk taken for its own sake, standing about to look at something
-- happens only where the conditions invite it; and **social** activity is resultant, arising
spontaneously wherever the other two put people in the same space, so it cannot be produced
directly at all. From which follows the programme he stated as an order of operations: life first,
then space, then buildings, and never the reverse. That is exactly the claim the three atoms share
and none of them can make alone -- a maker can build the conditions and cannot build the life --
and it is why the composite grades presence rather than counting it.

The flanks are the observational tradition that made the claim checkable. Lynch established that a
place exists for its users as an image rather than a plan, built from five elements they can name
when asked to draw the city from memory, so a place's readability is measurable from the outside.
Jacobs supplied the social mechanism on the sidewalk -- the informal watching that comes free with
being regularly seen, and the intricate unplanned order she called a ballet, which is produced by
mixed use and short blocks and destroyed by the planning that tries to produce order directly.
Whyte supplied the method and the correction: filming small urban spaces over years, he found the
determinants of use to be far more banal and far more physical than theory expected -- above all
that people sit where there is somewhere to sit, and that what most attracts people is other
people. Lefebvre supplied the temporal half, arguing that a place is constituted by rhythms which
are spatial and temporal at once and cannot be understood apart, which is why the social-time
engine belongs in this wiring rather than beside it.

What the composite adds on top of that join is the grading and the inversion that follows from it.
Ranked by volume, necessary presence dominates: a street between a station and an office is full
whatever it is like, so the loudest reading is the least informative one, and the worst places on
earth include some of the busiest. Ranked by what the presence is evidence **of**, the order
reverses exactly, because informativeness runs inverse to compulsion. The consequence is the
keystone, and it is a practical one: optional presence is indistinguishable from waste to anyone
who is not treating it as an instrument, so the hostile bench, the swept plaza, the moving-on of
people who are doing nothing all remove the sole measurement by which the place could have been
known to work. The three bridges are the errand, the round, and the loitering.

## Origin

Public-life studies and urban observation, wired as a composite over the legibility, social-time,
and street-life engines. The first two rows are the **joining** -- the published work that sets
these as one graded reading of a place -- and the rest anchor the individual bridges.

## Restrictions

What the composite refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The three readings (the atoms)**: The composite wires the three but owns none. The path, edge,
  district, node, and landmark are the legibility engine's; the calendar, schedule, tempo, and
  synchrony are social-time's; presence, mingling, watch, and lingering are street-life's. Peopling
  reads only what the three make together -- a population graded by what would have kept it away --
  and one alone is a property of a place rather than a judgement of it.
- **The person's bond with a place (dwelling)**: The place a persona belongs at, the place that
  restores them, the place they are rooted in -- read on the **person** -- is the dwelling
  composite's. Peopling is read on the **place**, and is indifferent to what any one occupant feels
  about it: a square full of people who are each privately unmoved is still a well-peopled square,
  and the two composites answer different questions about the same afternoon.
- **The person among others (midst)**: A persona whose performance alters because they are seen,
  whose responsibility divides among bystanders, or who is massed into a crowd with no single
  author, is the midst composite's -- again on the person. Peopling counts and grades the presence;
  midst reads what being in it does to somebody.
- **The distance between two (proxemics)**: The bubble a persona keeps and what its size encodes is
  the proxemics engine's, read between people. Peopling reads the population of a place, not the
  spacing within it.
- **The built form (the place's own chapters, the design engines)**: What a place is made of, what
  it affords, what it conceals, and how its arrangement was set are the place's own chapters and the
  design engines' -- artifice's six forces among them. This composite reads only what happened in
  front of the form, and its whole claim is that the form permits rather than produces.
- **The order kept in shared quarters (subjection)**: The discipline of an institution's common
  spaces -- who may be where, doing what -- is the subjection composite's. Peopling reads presence
  that was permitted, and where presence is compelled by an authority rather than by an errand, the
  reading is subjection's and the grading here does not apply.
- **The emptied square as authored symbol (the play's pitch)**: A place cleared to **stand for** a
  theme -- the plaza deserted to mirror a motif of loss -- is refused: that is authored key, the
  play's pitch, not the force on the place. Peopling sets who is there; the meaning read into an
  absence is the play's.

## Encoding

Source to constraint. A composite root over three engine atoms it declares as dependencies, with
three bridges, graded by how much the place had to compel the presence.

- **[peopling](process_peopling.md)** (the root): a place read by the presence in it, on the place
  and never on the person. Anchored by Gehl (1971) on the three kinds of activity and Lefebvre
  (1992) on rhythm as constitutive of place.
- **[the errand](process_peopling_errand.md)** (bridge): the crossing that would have happened
  whatever the place were like -- highest volume, lowest information, capable of showing
  navigability and nothing else. Anchored by Lynch (1960) and by Gehl's necessary activity.
- **[the round](process_peopling_round.md)** (bridge): the return kept on a rhythm, half-obliged and
  half-chosen, where strangers become familiars and the informal watch appears. Anchored by Jacobs
  (1961) and by the social-time engine's synchrony, whose own twist -- the shared hour that binds
  and constrains at once -- this bridge carries into the grading.
- **[the loitering](process_peopling_loitering.md)** (bridge): the staying done for no reason at
  all, which no design or budget produces directly. Anchored by Gehl's optional and social activity,
  Whyte (1980) on what actually draws people, and Oldenburg (1989) on the loss of settings that
  permit it; the composite's twist and keystone, that presence is informative in inverse proportion
  to how much it was compelled, so a place made efficient destroys the sole instrument by which it
  could have been known to work.
