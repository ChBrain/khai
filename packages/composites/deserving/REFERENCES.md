# Deserving: Reference

## Line of Work

Deserving as **composite**: what a persona holds another to have coming. It is not a fourth
mechanism but a wiring of three: the **moral-judgment** engine supplies the foundation that fires
first and names the conduct, the **altruistic-punishment** engine supplies the price the persona
will absorb to make a breaker answer, and the **schadenfreude** engine supplies the lift felt when
the fall lands. This composite adds no fourth engine; it reads the seam the three make -- one
judgment of desert surfacing as a reading, a payment, and a pleasure -- and stages that seam into
three bridges, closing on the discovery that the face the persona will not avow is the one that
reports the verdict most exactly.

The domain does not model any single face -- the six foundations are the moral-judgment engine's;
the second-party and third-party forms are altruistic-punishment's; comeuppance, leveling, and
grudge are schadenfreude's; and one alone is a position or a process, not the whole of what a
persona holds another to have coming. Nor is it the other-condemning emotions themselves, which
are the condemnation composite's; nor the wrong between two people and its repair, which is the
moral-account composite's; nor the moral self governing a persona's own conduct, which is the
conscience composite's. It owns the desert: the figure a persona puts on someone else's conduct,
and the three places it shows.

The spine is deservingness, and both flanks are joined in the literature. Feather's deservingness
framework is the first: perceived deservingness -- built from the relation a person is judged to
bear to their own outcome -- drives a family of reactions that had been studied apart, so
schadenfreude rises and sympathy falls as a misfortune is judged deserved, resentment attaches to
an undeserved gain, and approval of a penalty rises with the judgement that the person is
responsible and has it coming. One appraisal, several outputs; and this composite's lens and lift
are two of them. Fehr and Fischbacher supply the other flank, and it is the one that turns a
judgement into an act with a price on it: in third-party punishment, an uninvolved observer who
loses nothing by the violation will pay out of pocket to sanction it, which is a moral verdict
about a stranger converted into a personal cost with no material return -- the lens spent as a
price. Haidt's social intuitionist account explains why the first face is the most articulate and
the least explanatory: the verdict lands fast and the reasons are gathered afterward, so what a
persona says about their judgement is a reconstruction offered in good faith. What this composite
adds on top is the ordering by avowal -- stated, paid for, disowned -- and the reading that
follows from it: that the cheapest and least admissible face is the least filtered, so the
gladness is not a lapse in the moral sense but a reading of it. The three bridges are those three
faces: the lens, the price, and the lift.

## Origin

Moral foundations theory, the strong-reciprocity literature, and the social psychology of
schadenfreude, wired as a composite over the moral-judgment, altruistic-punishment, and
schadenfreude engines. The first two rows are the **joining** -- the published work that sets these
as one appraisal -- and the rest anchor the individual bridges.

## Restrictions

What the composite refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The single faces (the atoms)**: The composite wires the three but owns none. The six
  foundations are the moral-judgment engine's; the second- and third-party forms are
  altruistic-punishment's; comeuppance, leveling, and grudge are schadenfreude's. Deserving reads
  only the appraisal the three share; one face alone is a position or a process, not the desert.
- **The condemning emotions (condemnation)**: Contempt, anger, and disgust, split by which moral
  code was broken, are the condemnation composite's. That owns what a persona **feels toward the
  wrongdoer**; this owns what they hold the wrongdoer **to have coming**, and what they will pay
  and enjoy when it arrives. A persona can feel all three condemning emotions and pay nothing.
- **The wrong between two, and its repair (moral-account)**: The transgressor's felt debt, the
  dyad's spoken account, the grievance and the forgiveness are the moral-account composite's --
  dyadic and aimed at restoration. Deserving is third-party and aimed at desert; where the persona
  is the wronged party, the second-party form of the price is theirs and the reckoning is
  moral-account's.
- **The persona's own conduct (conscience, squaring)**: The moral self that governs conduct in
  advance is the conscience composite's, and the repair of one's own account afterward is the
  squaring composite's. Deserving looks outward at somebody else.
- **The ache at another's good (envy, outdone)**: The upward pain at what another has is the envy
  engine's, inside the outdone composite. This composite's pleasure is envy's mirror and not its
  equal: the leveling form draws on it, the comeuppance form does not need it.
- **The standing worldview (belief-in-a-just-world)**: The conviction that the world is fair and
  people get what they deserve is the belief-in-a-just-world engine's -- a standing position where
  this is the operation in a particular case, the same split the bias engine keeps from estimation.
- **The group's grievance (relative-deprivation, intergroup)**: The anger of a group at a gap it
  did not earn is the relative-deprivation composite's, and what happens between groups is the
  intergroup composite's. Deserving reads one persona's verdict, though its grudge form is where
  the two meet.
- **The wish to do the harm (aggression)**: The active intent to injure is the aggression engine's.
  The lift is pleasure at a fall the persona did not cause, and the price is a sanction rather than
  an attack.
- **The fall as authored symbol (the play's pitch)**: A comeuppance made to **stand for** a story
  -- the downfall that mirrors a theme of hubris -- is refused: that is authored key, the play's
  pitch, not the force on the persona. Deserving sets the verdict; the meaning read into it is the
  play's.

## Encoding

Source to constraint. A composite root over three engine atoms it declares as dependencies, with
three bridges, ordered by what the persona will avow.

- **[deserving](process_deserving.md)** (the root): what a persona holds another to have coming --
  the order a shared sense of desert keeps, against the fact that it arrives ahead of its argument.
  Anchored by Feather and Fehr & Fischbacher.
- **[the lens](process_deserving_lens.md)** (bridge): the foundation that fires first and names the
  conduct, with the case assembled afterward and sincerely offered. Anchored by Haidt (2012),
  Graham, Haidt & Nosek (2009), and Iyer et al. (2012).
- **[the price](process_deserving_price.md)** (bridge): the sanction imposed at a cost the persona
  absorbs, second-party where they were wronged and third-party where they were not -- talk being
  free and this not, so what a persona will pay marks the edge of what they hold. Anchored by Fehr
  & Gächter (2002) and Fehr & Fischbacher (2004).
- **[the lift](process_deserving_lift.md)** (bridge): the fall enjoyed as justice, as relief, or
  for itself, licensed by the target's standing and hidden by the persona. Anchored by Feather &
  Sherman (2002), Smith (2013), van Dijk & Ouwerkerk (2014), and Wang et al. (2019) on the three
  routes; the composite's twist and keystone, that the response a persona will not avow is the one
  that reports their verdict most exactly.
