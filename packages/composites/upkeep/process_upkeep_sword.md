# Process: Upkeep, the Sword

## Taxonomy

[Upkeep](process_upkeep.md), who pays to make the rule real, and where the three are seen for one thing.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: upkeep

## Initiated by

Somebody has taken more than their share, in front of people.
The Sword starts wherever [altruistic punishment](@chbrain/khai-engine-altruistic-punishment/process_altruistic_punishment.md) is being paid for -- by a [second party](@chbrain/khai-engine-altruistic-punishment/process_second_party.md) who was defected on and gets nothing back by answering it, or by a [third party](@chbrain/khai-engine-altruistic-punishment/process_third_party.md) who lost nothing and answers it anyway -- carrying out the [sanction](@chbrain/khai-engine-commons/place_sanction.md) the pool's own rules describe.
It is the face of the composite where the arrangement stops being a document.

## Direction

Toward a rule that has been made true at somebody's expense.
The Sword reads the transaction and reads who is on which side of it: the punisher spends time, standing, goodwill, or worse, and receives no share of what the punishing preserves -- the restored contribution goes to everybody equally, including to the personas who watched and did nothing, and including to the defector once they resume.
It moves the estimate back up for everybody, and moves the punisher's own position down.

## Lever

What the enforcement is worth to the group against what it costs the one who carries it out.
The carrying force is that personas do this, reliably and at real expense, and the arrangements that survive are the ones where they do -- which is why a shared thing is not a matter of designing good rules but of there being somebody who will act on them; the resistance is that the enforcement is itself a shared good with the same structure as the first one, since its benefit falls on all and its cost on one, so a persona can free-ride on the punishing exactly as they could on the pool -- and nothing in the arrangement obliges anybody to pick it up, which means the whole thing rests on a step that is never anyone's turn.
The Sword rides the composite's keystone knife-edge: because the cost is private and the benefit is general, a commons that is working looks like one that needs no keeping.

## Echo

A rule made real, and one persona out of pocket for everybody.
It shows the three engines complete -- a resource with rules, an estimate holding it up, and a private payment underwriting the estimate -- and carries beneath it the composite's keystone: the evidence of success is the argument for dismantling what produced it, since an arrangement running smoothly presents as one where the sanctions were never needed, and the people best placed to see the cost are the ones paying it.
This is why the Sword is the hardest of the three to stage and the only one worth closing on: the punisher must gain nothing, must be able to be talked out of it, must have somebody else present who could have done it instead, and must be thanked by nobody -- and a play that lets the punishing pay has staged a bounty rather than this.
The Sword is the note the whole composite closes on: what a shared thing was actually costing is legible only in who stops paying.
The Sword is dated from the defection, and it does not appear in the accounts.
