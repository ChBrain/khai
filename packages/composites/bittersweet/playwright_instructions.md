# Instructions: Bittersweet

How a Playwright wires the bittersweet composite into a play. You wire by
linking the play's own content to the composite's files and, through them,
to the two atom engines it depends on; every file stays untouched, they are
canon. The Roadie plumbs the law into Instructions Knowledge on deploy.
Authoring guidance, not runtime content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human sets the mixed occasion: a moment that is genuinely glad and
  genuinely sorrowful at once -- an achievement that is also a parting, a good
  thing that is ending, a dread that lifts -- so both signs have a real cause
  and neither is a decoration on the other.

## Agent

- Cast both corners from the atoms as usual: the uplift, gladness, and
  savouring from the joy engine; the sinking, loss, and mourning from the
  sadness engine. Then stage the mix itself, the thing neither atom can hold:
  which moment holds joy and sadness at full at once, which feeling is
  crossing from one sign to the other, and where a bounded, meaningful ending
  draws the two together. Stage the bridges as their own shapes -- a mix is
  the two held concurrent (the poignant, read as one dense feeling, not happy
  then sad), a turn is one sign crossing into the other across a scene (relief
  as a dread lifts, the letdown as a joy closes), a meaning is an ending that
  amplifies both because time is bounded and the moment matters. A scene that
  turns on a poignant moment may link the bridge under its Tension; the audit
  surfaces the ones staged as a single valence when both were live. You link
  to the composite and its atoms, you never edit them.

## Collaboration

- Each corner's science is its atom's: joy and sadness each carry their own
  warrant and their own instructions. The sentimental longing for one's own
  past is the nostalgia engine's; the mix as a persisting background tone,
  rather than a moment, is the mood engine's; the taxonomy of the emotions and
  the structure of the affective space are the emotion engine's. Whether the
  personas genuinely feel both signs at once, rather than one named and the
  other implied, is the team's review.

## Knowledge

- The bittersweet is the mixed emotion of joy and sadness felt together -- the
  two the folk mind calls opposites, running at once. It is warranted by the
  evaluative-space model (Cacioppo & Berntson): positivity and negativity are
  separable systems, not the ends of one dial, so both can be live at full,
  and objectively mixed situations reliably evoke both (Larsen, McGraw &
  Cacioppo). It runs in shapes. Mix is the simultaneous compound -- joy and
  sadness at full at once, the poignant, the tears of joy, the smile that is
  also a goodbye. Turn is the valence-crossing -- one sign becoming the other,
  relief as a dread lifts, the letdown as a good thing closes, the oscillation
  that will not settle. Meaning is the poignancy of endings -- a bounded,
  meaningful moment drawing both signs to a joint peak, the last of a good
  thing sweetest and saddest at once (Ersner-Hershfield, Mikels et al.). Read
  the production as both-at-once, never netted to a single valence.

## System

- Do cast both corners from their atoms before staging a mix; do let joy and
  sadness run live from the same object at the same time, so the poignancy is
  earned by two real feelings, not one feeling with a wistful gloss.
- Do keep the shapes legible: a mix is the two held concurrent, a turn is one
  crossing into the other across a scene, a meaning is an ending that amplifies
  both because the moment is bounded -- and let a turn carry the sign it came
  from, so relief remembers its dread and the letdown its peak.
- Do let a meaningful ending deepen rather than only sadden: the bounded good
  is prized because it is closing, so the poignancy reads as the moment
  mattering, not merely its loss.
- Do not net the mix to a single valence -- all celebration or all grief reads
  as thinner than the truth; do not stage the sentimental longing for the past
  here -- that is nostalgia's; do not carry the lingering background tone here
  -- that is mood's; do not edit the composite's or the atoms' files; wire only
  from the play's side.
