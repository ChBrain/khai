# Bittersweet: Reference

## Line of Work

The mixed emotion as **composite**: joy and sadness felt together, the two the folk mind calls opposites running at once. No single atom engine sees the compound, because each authors one valence and the mixed state is a property of their co-activation. The uplift is the joy engine's; the sinking is the sadness engine's. This package authors neither again. It declares the two engines as dependencies, wires their members by hard package links, and adds only what the atoms cannot hold: the co-activation itself and the shapes it takes -- the mix where the two fire at full at once, the turn where one sign crosses into the other, and the meaning where a bounded, meaningful ending draws both to a joint peak.

The warrant is integrative, not primary: the sources below establish that positivity and negativity are separable, co-activable systems -- so that "happy and sad at the same time" is a real state, not a confusion -- and that the mixed state has a characteristic structure and a characteristic occasion. The spine is the evaluative-space model (Cacioppo & Berntson) and its empirical test in the felt simultaneity of happiness and sadness (Larsen, McGraw & Cacioppo): the two valences are not the ends of one bipolar dial but partly independent channels, and objectively mixed situations reliably evoke both at once. The occasion is warranted by the poignancy work of Ersner-Hershfield, Mikels and colleagues within Carstensen's socioemotional-selectivity theory: meaningful endings, and felt-limited time, amplify the mix rather than resolving it. It is the mixed-emotion counterpart within the affect family: where the joy and sadness engines each own one sign, this composite reads the states in which both are true.

## Restrictions

What the composite refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The two atoms**: The composite authors no emotion of its own. Joy -- the uplift, the gladness, the savouring -- is the joy engine's, whole; sadness -- the sinking, the loss, the mourning -- is the sadness engine's, whole. This package reads over them and adds only the co-activation and its shapes; each sign is an atom's ground, cited by hard link, never restated.
- **Nostalgia**: The composite models the mixed emotion itself, not the sentimental longing for one's own past. The bittersweet reach back toward an ended good -- the fond, wistful recollection of what was -- is owned by the nostalgia engine; this composite owns the joy-and-sadness compound at the moment it is felt, including at an ending, and hands the later longing for that ending across. Nostalgia is one occasion of the mix; the mix is more general.
- **Mood**: The composite models the mixed emotion as a state bound to an object or a moment, not as a persisting background tone. The diffuse, objectless colouring of experience over time is owned by the mood engine; this composite owns the poignant moment -- the graduation, the last day, the tears of joy -- not the lingering wistfulness it may leave, which is the mood engine's to carry.
- **Emotion (the family node)**: The composite models one specific mixture -- joy with sadness -- not the general fact of mixed or blended affect. The taxonomy of the emotions and the structure of the affective space are the emotion engine's; this composite reads only the joy-and-sadness compound, the most-studied mixed case, not every co-activation the space permits.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per file.

- **[bittersweet](process_bittersweet.md)** (the root): The mixed emotion as one state -- joy and sadness co-activated, held together, the folk "opposites" running at once. Anchored by Cacioppo & Berntson (the evaluative space) and Larsen, McGraw & Cacioppo (the simultaneity).
- **[bittersweet, mix](process_bittersweet_mix.md)** (bridge): Joy and sadness at full at the same instant -- the poignant compound, the tears of joy, the smile that is also a goodbye. Anchored by Larsen, McGraw & Cacioppo and Larsen & McGraw (mixed emotions as simultaneous).
- **[bittersweet, turn](process_bittersweet_turn.md)** (bridge): One sign crossing into the other -- relief as dread lifts, the letdown as a joy closes, the oscillation that will not settle. Anchored by the appraisal-transition accounts of relief and disappointment.
- **[bittersweet, meaning](process_bittersweet_meaning.md)** (bridge): A bounded, meaningful ending drawing both signs to a joint peak -- the last of a good thing sweetest and saddest at once. Anchored by Ersner-Hershfield, Mikels et al. (poignancy) within Carstensen's socioemotional selectivity.
