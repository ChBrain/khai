# Resilience: Reference

## Line of Work

Resilience as a **composite**: the maintenance of stable, healthy functioning across a potentially traumatic event, read whole across the atoms it spans. Bonanno defined resilience not as a rare heroic trait but as a common outcome trajectory -- the persona's functioning holding, or briefly dipping and returning, where a susceptible reading would expect collapse. This composite reads the two its atoms hold -- the stress engine's acute response to the adversity, and the coping engine's appraisal and coping through which the persona meets it -- and adds only what no atom holds: the trajectory of functioning across the event, and the finding that stable functioning is the ordinary outcome built from ordinary adaptive systems. It declares the two engines as dependencies, wires their members by hard package links, and reads the compound and its two healthy trajectories.

The warrant is accordingly integrative, not primary. Resilience is not a third atom beside stress and coping; it is the outcome those two compose when an adversity is met so that functioning holds. Bonanno's contribution was the multiple-trajectories model: across a potentially traumatic event, functioning follows one of several curves -- resilience (a stable, near-flat trajectory with only mild transient perturbation), recovery (a marked drop followed by return to baseline), chronic dysfunction, or delayed reactions -- and resilience, the stable curve, is the modal response, distinct from recovery in that the person is scarcely felled rather than felled-and-restored. Masten's ordinary magic locates its source in everyday adaptive systems rather than extraordinary traits, and Kobasa's hardiness -- commitment, control, and challenge -- names one of the personality resources, alongside flexible coping, positive emotion, and supportive bonds, that keep the curve from collapsing. The sources below are the literature of that trajectory and its resources; each atom's own REFERENCES carries the primary science of its part.

## Restrictions

What the composite refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The acute stress response (stress)**: The mobilization to a threat -- the fight, flight, freeze, or fawn of the body under adversity -- is the stress engine's; resilience is what the trajectory of functioning does across and after that response, not the response itself. The composite restates none of the stress mechanics; a bridge links, it never re-authors. Stress is the blow landing; resilience is the functioning that holds through it.
- **The coping strategies (coping)**: The appraisal of a stressor and the problem-focused and emotion-focused strategies brought to it are the coping engine's; resilience reads the outcome those strategies produce -- functioning maintained or restored -- not the strategies in themselves. Coping is the work; resilience is the result the work secures.
- **Goal stamina (grit)**: The perseverance and passion to pursue a long-term goal through effort and setback is the grit engine's; resilience is the maintenance of functioning across a potentially traumatic event, a different thing. Grit is directed at an ambition and measured by attainment; resilience is directed at an adversity and measured by stability. A persona can be gritty without being resilient to trauma, and resilient without being gritty about goals.
- **Hedonic drift (hedonic-adaptation)**: The return of pleasure and satisfaction to a baseline setpoint after a good or bad change is the hedonic-adaptation engine's; resilience is the maintenance of functioning across trauma, not the fading of an affect toward its setpoint. Hedonic adaptation is about how feeling drifts back; resilience is about how functioning holds. Both involve a return to baseline, but of different quantities and by different means.
- **Growth beyond baseline (post-traumatic growth)**: The positive transformation by which a persona is remade beyond its prior self through the struggle with trauma is post-traumatic growth's; resilience is the maintenance of the prior baseline, not transcendence of it. Resilience keeps the self intact through the storm; growth builds a changed self out of it. The two are distinct outcomes of adversity, and post-traumatic growth delegates the mere-maintenance case to this composite.

## Encoding

Source to constraint. A root (the resilience trajectory) with two forms, each a healthy outcome curve, wired by hard package links into the atoms it spans.

- **[resilience](process_resilience.md)** (the root): stable functioning maintained across a potentially traumatic event (stress) through adaptive appraisal and coping (coping), the common outcome built from ordinary systems. Anchored by Bonanno (the trajectory definition) and Masten (ordinary magic).
- **[resilience, stable](position_resilience_stable.md)** (form, resilience proper): functioning barely dips and holds near baseline throughout; the persona shaken but not felled. Anchored by Bonanno (the resilience trajectory) and Kobasa (hardiness) over the stress and coping members it links.
- **[resilience, recovery](position_resilience_recovery.md)** (form, the bounce-back): functioning drops markedly, then returns to baseline over time. Anchored by Bonanno (the recovery trajectory) and Bonanno & Burton (regulatory flexibility) over the stress and coping members it links.
