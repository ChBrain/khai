# Vigil: Reference

## Line of Work

Vigil as **composite**: what a persona does to a bond once a third party is in the room. It is not a
fourth mechanism but a wiring of three: the **jealousy** engine supplies the threat and what is done
about it, the **attachment** engine supplies the working model that decides how much of a threat it
takes, and the **trust** engine supplies the bet the whole episode is trying to settle.

The joining is Sharpsteen and Kirkpatrick's. Romantic jealousy had been treated largely as a rivalry
phenomenon -- a response to a competitor -- and they read it instead as an attachment phenomenon: what
the emotion is about is the threatened bond, and the rival is the occasion. Their evidence is that the
experience of jealousy varies systematically with attachment style rather than with the rival,
so two personas presented with the same scene are not disagreeing about the facts but appraising from
different histories. That is the composite's first claim and the reason the threshold bridge is where
it begins: the amount of rival it takes is set before the rival arrives.

The trust leg is what turns the composite from a description into a trap, and it comes from a
different literature. Rempel, Holmes and Zanna's account of trust in close relationships makes it a
bet rather than a finding -- predictability, then dependability, then faith -- and Holmes and Rempel's
diagnostic-situation argument supplies the sharp edge: trust is built only in situations where the
partner could have acted selfishly and did not, with the option genuinely open. A persona who removes
the option removes the evidence. That is the keystone, and it is why the guarding cannot work: the
vigilance, the confrontation, and the mate-guarding all operate on the circumstances of the answer,
and the answer only counts when the circumstances were free.

Bringle's distinction between reactive and suspicious jealousy is what keeps the composite honest
about which case it models. Reactive jealousy is a response to an actual threat and is normative,
widely reported, and not a marker of anything wrong; suspicious jealousy runs in the absence of
evidence and is the one associated with low trust and with insecure attachment. The composite reads
the second, and the threshold bridge is careful to say that the persona's reading is defensible from
the inside -- because in the suspicious case there is always something to point at, which is exactly
why it does not settle.

It is the second composite to wire the trust engine and the third to wire attachment. Dealing runs
trust on a bargain between parties who need not like each other, where the vulnerability is
contractual; here it runs on a bond, where the vulnerability is the whole of the relationship. Cptsd
runs attachment as damage laid down early and freud as a drive-level structure; here it runs on
tonight, as the setting that decides what tonight means. Same engines, different questions, and no
composite owns them.

The domain does not model any side alone -- threat appraisal, the jealous position, and guarding are
the jealousy engine's; the four strategies are attachment's; extend, test, withhold, betrayed, and
repair are trust's. Nor is it a wanting produced by somebody else's advantage, which is outdone's and
needs no bond of one's own; nor a wrong actually done and reckoned between two, which is
moral-account's. It owns the episode: a bond, a third party, and a persona trying to verify what can
only be risked.

**On the evidence, stated plainly.** The atoms are each well founded and the joining is a
well-supported reframing rather than a novel claim: the association between attachment insecurity --
anxious attachment especially -- and jealousy is robust and has been found across many samples, and
the reactive/suspicious distinction is standard in the measurement literature. The trust leg is
theoretically strong and empirically supported for the general proposition that partner surveillance
and control behaviours predict lower relationship satisfaction for both parties, but the causal
direction there is genuinely hard: insecurity produces surveillance and surveillance produces
insecurity, and the longitudinal work that separates them is thinner than the cross-sectional work
that shows the association. The diagnostic-situation argument is a theoretical account with good
support rather than a single decisive experiment. What should not be taken from this composite is that
jealousy is pathological, that a persona who checks is thereby insecurely attached, or that a rival is
never real. Reactive jealousy in the face of an actual threat is not this mechanism and needs none of
it. The claim is conditional: where the threshold was set by a history rather than by the evidence,
expect the guarding to operate on circumstances, and expect every reassurance obtained to expire.

## Origin

Jealousy research, attachment theory, and the study of trust in close relationships, wired as a
composite over the jealousy, attachment, and trust engines. The first row is the **joining** -- the
published work that reads jealousy as an attachment phenomenon rather than a rivalry one -- the second
carries the keystone, and the rest anchor the individual bridges and their bounds.

## Restrictions

What the composite refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The three sides (the atoms)**: The composite wires the three but owns none. Threat appraisal, the
  jealous position, and guarding are the jealousy engine's; secure, anxious, avoidant, and
  disorganized are attachment's; extend, test, withhold, betrayed, and repair are trust's. Vigil reads
  only the episode the three make, and one alone is a process, not the episode.
- **The bargain between parties (dealing)**: The dealing composite wires this same trust engine on a
  negotiation, where the vulnerability is contractual and both sides are protecting a position. Here
  the vulnerability is the relationship itself, and there is nothing to trade. A play may run both on
  one persona, and should not confuse a partner being checked with a counterparty being verified.
- **The history that set the strategy (cptsd, freud)**: The cptsd composite wires this same attachment
  engine as damage laid down early, and freud as a drive-level structure. Vigil takes the strategy as
  it arrives and reads what it does tonight; how it came to be what it is belongs to those.
- **Wanting what somebody else has (outdone)**: Envy, comparison, and admiration are the outdone
  composite's, and they need no bond of one's own. The difference is the whole boundary: outdone's
  persona wants what another has, vigil's fears losing what they hold, and a play should not run one
  as the other.
- **A wrong actually done and reckoned (moral-account, forgiveness)**: The transgression, the felt
  debt, the account demanded, and the repair are the moral-account composite's. Vigil's premise is
  that nothing has been established; where the betrayal has actually happened, the scene has moved on
  and is theirs.
- **The bond being made (love)**: Desire, loyalty, and self-disclosure are the love composite's, and
  they build what this one defends. Vigil says nothing about how the bond was formed or what it is
  worth.
- **What is being hidden and at what cost (opacity, carrying)**: Concealment, secrecy, and the labour
  of keeping are those composites'. Where the play's interest is in the other party's hidden thing
  rather than in the guarding persona, it is theirs and not this.
- **The suspicion as authored symbol (the play's pitch)**: A jealousy made to **stand for** a theme --
  the rival who mirrors a motif of doubt -- is refused: that is authored key, the play's pitch, not
  the force. Vigil sets what the persona did to the bond; the meaning read into it is the play's.

## Encoding

Source to constraint. A composite root over three engine atoms it declares as dependencies, with
three bridges.

- **[vigil](process_vigil.md)** (the root): what a persona does to a bond once a third party is in
  the room, read on the bond and never on the rival. Anchored by Sharpsteen & Kirkpatrick (1997).
- **[the threshold](process_vigil_threshold.md)** (bridge): the rival's claim appraised through a
  strategy already set, so the same scene is different evidence to two personas and neither is
  inventing what they see. Anchored by Bowlby and Hazan & Shaver on the working model, and by
  Bringle on which jealousy this is.
- **[the guard](process_vigil_guard.md)** (bridge): the watch mounted over the bond -- vigilance,
  confrontation, mate-guarding -- operating on the circumstances of the answer because the answer is
  somebody else's to give. Anchored by Buss (2000) on the repertoire and Guerrero & Andersen on what
  it communicates.
- **[the proof](process_vigil_proof.md)** (bridge): the reassurance sought, obtained, and worth
  nothing. Anchored by Rempel, Holmes & Zanna (1985) and Holmes & Rempel (1989); the composite's twist
  and keystone, that the checking cannot produce what it is looking for, because what would settle the
  question is precisely the willingness not to ask it.
