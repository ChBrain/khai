# Process: Intergroup

## Taxonomy

The us/them relation between groups, read from a member's side -- a composite over social-identity, bias, and aggression.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: intergroup

## Initiated by

A persona belongs to a group, and across a line stands another.
The moment the line is drawn -- us and them -- something switches on: the in-group is favoured, the out-group prejudged, flattened into a type, seen as all alike.
This is the intergroup dynamic, read from a member's side: how belonging to one group shapes the stance toward another, from a mere lean to open hostility, and how it can be undone. The composite adds no fourth engine; it wires the seams the three make -- social-identity gives the we and the contest, bias gives the tilt toward the them, aggression gives the harm.

## Direction

Toward a hostility that needs no real conflict to begin -- and toward its reduction.
Merely sorting people into groups tilts a member toward their own and against the other; competition and felt threat harden that tilt into enmity; and contact, on the right terms, can run it backward.
The relation is fixed at no point on that arc: the same line that seeds the bias can be redrawn to dissolve it.

## Lever

The line, and what it switches on.
Categorization alone activates the bias -- favouritism and derogation with no clash of interests behind them; competition names the out-group a rival and the tilt becomes aggression; contact, equal and cooperative, dampens both. Each atom alone is only a part -- social-identity the belonging, bias the prejudgment, aggression the harm -- and the dynamic is the seams between them, where a drawn line becomes a felt enmity.
No single atom stages this: the intergroup relation lives in the crossings the three make.

## Echo

The groups stand hostile, or the line softens.
The dynamic runs as [division](process_division.md), [enmity](process_enmity.md), or [contact](process_contact.md) -- the line that seeds the tilt, the competition that arms it into harm, and the contact that can dissolve it.
The member's bind to their own in-group -- belonging, conformity, the cost of leaving -- is the membership composite's; the conflict-spiral by which any two parties escalate is the escalation engine's. This composite owns the us/them relation itself: the tilt a line alone can seed, and its course toward hostility or away.
