# Intergroup: Reference

## Line of Work

Intergroup as **composite**: the us/them relation between groups, read from a member's side -- how belonging to one group shapes the stance toward another, from a mere lean to open hostility, and how it can be undone. It is not a new engine but a wiring of three: **social-identity** supplies the in-group belonging and the contest for standing; **bias** supplies the tilt toward the out-group -- favouritism, prejudice, stereotyping, out-group homogeneity; and **aggression** supplies the hostile harm. The composite adds no fourth phenomenon; it stages the seams the three make -- a line becoming a bias, a bias becoming enmity, and the contact that can undo both.

The domain does not model the member's bind to their own in-group -- the belonging, conformity, mark, and allegiance of being one of them -- which is the membership composite's; nor the conflict-spiral by which any two parties escalate, which is the escalation engine's. It owns the us/them relation itself: the tilt a line alone can seed, and its course toward hostility or away.

The spine is the social psychology of intergroup relations. Mere categorization into groups -- even arbitrary, even without any conflict of interest -- produces in-group favouritism and out-group derogation; competition over resources, standing, or threatened values hardens that tilt into hostility; and contact between the groups, under conditions of equal status and cooperation toward a superordinate goal, reduces it by recategorizing them-and-us into a larger we. The three bridges are the dynamic's course: division, enmity, contact.

## Origin

The social psychology of categorization, intergroup conflict, and prejudice reduction, wired as a composite over three engines.

## Restrictions

What the composite refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **Membership**: The composite models the stance toward the out-group, not the bind to the in-group. The member's tie to their own group -- the belonging drawn from it, the standard it bends them to, the mark it would discredit, the allegiance that raises the cost of leaving -- is owned by the membership composite; intergroup reads outward, toward the them, where membership reads inward, toward the us.
- **Escalation**: The composite models the us/them relation, not the general spiral of conflict. The self-amplifying stages by which any two parties -- groups or not -- raise the stakes toward lose-lose are owned by the escalation engine; intergroup owns the group-specific tilt and its crossing into harm, and hands the spiraling of that harm to escalation.
- **The atoms**: The composite adds no content its atoms already own. The in-group belonging and the contest for standing are the social-identity engine's; the prejudice, stereotyping, in-group favouritism, and out-group homogeneity are the bias engine's; the hostile, instrumental, and displaced harm are the aggression engine's. Intergroup owns only the seams -- the dynamic the three make together that none makes alone.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per file.

- **[intergroup](process_intergroup.md)** (the root): The us/them relation read from a member's side, a drawn line switching on a tilt that can harden into hostility or be reduced. Anchored by Tajfel & Turner (social identity and categorization) and Allport (the nature of prejudice).
- **[division](process_division.md)** (bridge): The line drawn -- even arbitrary -- activates the tilt, the in-group favoured and the out-group prejudged, stereotyped, and homogenized before any interaction. Wires social-identity to bias. Anchored by Tajfel & Turner (the minimal group paradigm).
- **[enmity](process_enmity.md)** (bridge): Under competition or felt threat, the tilt hardens into hostile, instrumental, or displaced aggression -- the out-group cast rival, opposed, harmed, or scapegoated. Wires bias and social-identity's competition to aggression. Anchored by Sherif (realistic conflict) and Stephan & Stephan (integrated threat).
- **[contact](process_contact.md)** (bridge): Contact under equal, cooperative terms toward a superordinate goal reduces the tilt, recategorizing them-and-us into a larger we -- or, on the wrong terms, confirms it. Wires the reduction back through social-identity's identification. Anchored by Allport (the contact hypothesis), Sherif (superordinate goals), and Pettigrew & Tropp (the meta-analytic confirmation).
