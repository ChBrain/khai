# Process: Contact

## Taxonomy

[Intergroup](process_intergroup.md), the bridge where the dynamic runs backward.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: intergroup

## Initiated by

The two groups meet -- not across the line but alongside it -- under conditions that let the tilt loosen: equal standing, a shared aim, cooperation toward a goal neither can reach alone.
This is the bridge that makes the intergroup dynamic a dynamic and not a doom: the point where the line can be redrawn.
It is not mere proximity -- meeting on the wrong terms can harden the tilt instead -- but contact of a particular, demanding kind.

## Direction

Toward reduction.
Contact under the right conditions -- equal status, common goals, genuine cooperation, the sanction of authority -- dampens the prejudice and the hostility both: the out-group, met as individuals rather than as a type, stops being all alike, and a goal that needs both groups dissolves the competition that armed the tilt.
The direction is a widening: them-and-us recategorized into a larger we.

## Lever

The conditions that turn contact from friction to thaw.
Mere proximity is not the lever -- contact on unequal or competitive terms can worsen the tilt; the bridge is the specific coupling Allport named and Sherif showed, that cooperation toward a shared, superordinate end is what reduces enmity, because it redraws the [identification](@chbrain/khai-engine-social-identity/position_identification.md) that drew the first line, pulling the boundary wider to enclose both groups.
The same categorization mechanism that seeded the bias, run at a larger scale, is what dissolves it.

## Echo

The line softens, the out-group re-seen, the hostility stood down -- or, contact botched on the wrong terms, the tilt confirmed instead and the front hardened.
This is intergroup's one bridge that runs the arc backward: the proof that the tilt a line seeds is not fixed, only defaulted-to, and answerable to the conditions of meeting.
The lasting bond a reconciled contact can grow into is beyond this composite; it owns the reduction of the tilt, not the tie that may replace it.
