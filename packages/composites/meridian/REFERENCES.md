# Meridian: Reference

## Line of Work

Meridian as **composite**: what a place gave up when it stopped taking its time from its own sky. It
is not a third mechanism but a wiring of two: the **celestial** engine supplies the first clock -- the
sky's bodies as the place's own Shown, the sun governing the day's arc and the season's swing, the
starfield as its fixed ceiling -- and the **social-time** engine supplies the second, the collective
structuring of time a place keeps across its calendar, its schedule, its tempo, and its synchrony.

The joining is the history of their severance, and it is in neither atom. The social-time engine
already carries Zerubavel on the calendar as convention and Thompson on clock-discipline; what neither
atom carries is the specific event that separated the two clocks. Through the nineteenth century a
place's noon was its own: the sun was highest over that place at that moment, and every town's clock
differed from its neighbour's by the minutes of longitude between them. Bartky traces how the railways
made this untenable and how American timekeeping was consolidated onto a handful of standard meridians,
and Ogle shows the same movement worldwide as a contested, uneven, and deeply political project rather
than a technical inevitability. Galison reads the coordination of clocks as a material undertaking --
cables, signals, and conventions -- rather than an abstraction. The outcome is the composite's subject:
a place's official noon is now set by a meridian it does not stand on, and almost nowhere on earth is
the clock's noon the sun's.

That is why the composite needs both atoms. From celestial: a measurement that cannot be disputed,
corrupted, or taken away, readable from inside any place with nothing in the hands -- and useless for
agreement, since every place has its own noon and no two are the same. From social time: a beat made
entirely of agreement, whose arbitrariness is the point rather than a defect, since a cycle answerable
to nothing outside itself binds everybody equally and travels to any place that adopts it. The week
went everywhere; the local sunrise could not.

Hence the keystone. The reference had to be surrendered to buy the coordination, so the better a place
keeps time the less it can tell what time it is. And the offset is not felt, because an error shared by
everybody present is not experienced as an error -- it is experienced as the time. The severance was
worth making: places that kept their own noon could not run a railway between them or agree on when
anything had happened. What makes it a twist rather than a trade-off is that the price is
unrecoverable. A place that wanted to check now finds the check has gone -- the clock is authoritative,
the sundial is a decoration, and, where the place's own lamps are bright enough, the starfield that
would have oriented anybody has been blinded out. Falchi and colleagues' atlas of artificial night sky
brightness puts numbers on the second severance: most of the world's population, and nearly all of
Europe's and North America's, lives under a sky in which the Milky Way cannot be seen. The same works
that bought the coordination erased the ceiling that would have shown its cost.

It is the second composite to wire the social-time engine. Peopling runs it on who is about -- the
errand, the round, the loitering -- reading a place by the presence in it. Here it is run against the
sun. Same engine, different question, and neither composite owns it.

The domain does not model either side alone -- the sun, the eclipse, the comet, the meteor, the
aurora, and the starfield are the celestial engine's, and the calendar, the schedule, the tempo, and
the synchrony are social time's. Nor is it the long-run regime a place stands in and the day it is
having, which is clime's; nor a persona's read of duration from inside, which is absorption's; nor a
moment being right rather than merely arriving, which is kairos's. It owns the gap: two clocks, both
correct, no longer measuring the same thing.

**On the evidence, stated plainly.** This composite rests on history and on measurement rather than on
experiment, and it should be read that way. The severance itself is a matter of record: the adoption of
standard time is documented in detail, and the astronomical facts on the other side -- the solar arc
from latitude and date, the equation of time putting apparent and mean solar noon up to about a quarter
of an hour apart even before zones -- are not in dispute. The light-pollution figures are measured and
recent. What is interpretation rather than record is the framing: reading the severance as a purchase,
with coordination bought and reference spent, is this composite's reading of a well-documented history
and not a finding anybody has reported. The claim that the offset goes unfelt is a plain observation
about a shared constant rather than a measured psychological effect, and where a play wants somebody to
feel it, that is a choice the composite permits but does not warrant. What should not be taken from
this composite is that standard time was a loss, or that a place ought to keep its own noon: the
composite's own reading is that the trade bought something real and irreplaceable, and a play that
stages the severance only as a wound has taken half of it. The claim is narrow: where a place keeps
time, expect its hour to be somebody else's, expect nobody in it to notice, and expect the means of
noticing to have gone too.

## Restrictions

What the composite refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The two sides (the atoms)**: The composite wires the two but owns neither. The sun, the eclipse,
  the comet, the meteor, the aurora, and the starfield are the celestial engine's; the calendar, the
  schedule, the tempo, and the synchrony are social time's. Meridian reads only the gap between them.
- **Who is about in a place (peopling)**: The peopling composite wires this same social-time engine
  with legibility and street life, reading a place by the presence in it -- the errand, the round, the
  loitering. Meridian runs the same engine against the sun, and its subject is a measurement rather
  than a population.
- **The regime and the day (clime)**: The long-run climate a place stands in and the weather it is
  actually having are the clime composite's. Meridian is not about what the sky does to a place; it is
  about what the place stopped reading off it.
- **The sky read for what it shows (celestial)**: The apparitions in their own right -- an eclipse as
  the day broken, a comet on its generational clock, an aurora, a bolide -- are the celestial engine's,
  and a play that wants the sky as an event wants the atom rather than this.
- **Duration from inside (absorption)**: Flow, attention, and time perception are the absorption
  composite's, and they run on a persona's felt hour. Meridian runs on a place's official one and
  makes no claim about how long anything seemed.
- **A moment being right (kairos)**: The opportune moment, as against the measured one, is the kairos
  engine's. Meridian is entirely about the measured one.
- **The wearing of a made thing (object-cycle)**: Wear, decay, and restoration are the object-cycle
  composite's. Time passing over an object is theirs; time being kept by a place is this.
- **The sky as authored symbol (the play's pitch)**: A sun or an hour made to **stand for** a theme --
  the eclipse that mirrors a motif of severance -- is refused: that is authored key, the play's pitch,
  not the force. Meridian sets what the place's clocks say; the meaning read into the gap is the
  play's.

## Encoding

Source to constraint. A composite root over two engine atoms it declares as dependencies, with three
bridges.

- **[meridian](process_meridian.md)** (the root): what a place gave up when it stopped taking its time
  from its own sky, read on the gap and never on either clock. Anchored by Bartky (2000, 2007) and
  Ogle (2015).
- **[the ceiling](process_meridian_ceiling.md)** (bridge): the sky the place actually stands under, a
  measurement anybody there could take and no two places could share. Anchored by the
  positional-astronomy tradition and by the celestial atom's own sources.
- **[the beat](process_meridian_beat.md)** (bridge): the time the place actually keeps, made entirely
  of agreement, whose arbitrariness is what lets it travel. Anchored by Zerubavel (1981, 1985),
  Thompson (1967), and Durkheim (1912) on what synchrony buys.
- **[the offset](process_meridian_offset.md)** (bridge): the distance between the two, unfelt because
  it is shared, and no longer checkable. Anchored by Bartky, Ogle, Galison (2003), and Falchi et al.
  (2016) on the second severance; the composite's twist and keystone, that the reference had to be
  surrendered to buy the coordination, so the better a place keeps time the less it can tell what time
  it is.
