# Instructions: Meridian

How a Playwright wires the meridian composite into a play. You wire by linking the play's own
content to the composite's files; the files stay untouched, they are canon. The composite
installs the celestial and social-time engines as dependencies, and their laws arrive with
them. Authoring guidance, not runtime content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human sets where the play's places are, what time they keep, and whether the sun is ever put next to the clock.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, read a place whose time is doing work and link under Shown what is running: the ceiling while the sky is over it, the sun setting the day's arc and the season's swing and the starfield standing as the fixed navigation (from celestial), the beat while the place keeps its calendar, its schedule, its tempo, and its synchrony (from social-time), the offset while the two no longer agree. Read the gap and never either clock, since each is correct about what it measures. You link to the composite, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- The sun, the eclipse, the comet, the meteor, the aurora, and the starfield are the celestial engine's; the calendar, the schedule, the tempo, and the synchrony are social-time's -- one alone is a process, not the gap. Who is about in a place is the peopling composite's, which wires this same social-time engine on presence where this runs it against the sun; the long-run regime a place stands in and the day it is having are clime's, and this composite is not about what the sky does to a place but about what the place stopped reading off it; the sky read as an event in its own right -- an eclipse, a comet, a bolide -- is the celestial engine's, and a play wanting the sky as spectacle wants the atom; a persona's felt duration is absorption's; a moment being right rather than merely arriving is kairos's; time passing over a made thing is object-cycle's. This composite owns the gap -- and the offset bridge carries the keystone: the better a place keeps time the less it can tell what time it is.

## Knowledge

- Meridian is a place read while its two clocks disagree -- braiding the sky (celestial: the sun as governor of the day's arc and the season's swing, the starfield as the fixed ceiling and the oldest navigation) with the time it keeps (social time: the calendar as the shared cycle, the schedule as the imposed timetable, the tempo it runs at, the synchrony in which the whole place moves as one). The join is the history of their severance. Through the nineteenth century a place's noon was its own -- the sun highest over that place at that moment, every town's clock differing from its neighbour's by the minutes of longitude between them. The railways made that untenable, and timekeeping was consolidated onto a handful of standard meridians, worldwide and unevenly and against real resistance. A place's official noon is now set by a line it does not stand on. It runs in three faces. The ceiling is the sky, which cannot be wrong: there is no version of that measurement a place could dispute, corrupt, or have taken from it, and for almost all of history it was the only clock there was -- which is exactly why it could not survive contact, since every place has its own noon and the moment two places had to keep an appointment one of the two suns had to go. The beat is the time actually kept, and none of it is read off the sky: the week has no celestial referent at all, the working day is a convention with a history, the festival falls where the calendar says. Its arbitrariness is the point rather than a defect, since a cycle answerable to nothing outside itself binds everybody equally and travels anywhere that adopts it -- but the same property removes the appeal, because a beat answerable to nothing can be lengthened or accelerated with no measurement to object. The offset is the twist: a place set to a zone runs an hour or a fraction of one from its own sun, everybody in it is offset by the same amount, and an error shared by everybody present is not experienced as an error at all -- it is experienced as the time. Here the two are seen for one thing: the reference had to be surrendered to buy the coordination, and the price is unrecoverable rather than merely paid, since the check itself has gone -- the clock is authoritative, the sundial is a decoration, and where the place's own lamps are bright enough the starfield that would have oriented anybody has been blinded out.

## System

- Do read the gap and never either clock: name whether the ceiling (a sky standing over), the beat (a calendar and a schedule running), or the offset (the two disagreeing) is running, and cast each from its engines.
- Do give the place a latitude and a longitude and let the sky follow from them, since the ceiling is a measurement rather than scenery and the play should be able to say what the sun is actually doing.
- Do show the audience the sun once, so they have something to hold the hour against.
- Do keep the offset unremarked: nobody in the place experiences it, everybody is off by the same amount, and a play that has somebody explain the time zones has replaced the mechanism with a lecture.
- Do let the severance have bought something real -- the railway, the appointment, the shared record of when a thing happened -- because a play that stages it only as a wound has taken half the composite.
- Do use the second severance where the play has a night: a place bright enough to have lost its own starfield has erased the instrument that would have shown it the first.
- Do not model the sky as an event (that is celestial, and an eclipse or a comet in its own right belongs to the atom), what the weather and the climate do to a place (that is clime), who is about in it (that is peopling, wiring the same engine on presence), a persona's felt duration (that is absorption), the opportune moment (that is kairos), or time passing over an object (that is object-cycle). Do not make a sky stand for a theme (that is the play's pitch). Do not edit the composite's files; wire only from the play's side.
