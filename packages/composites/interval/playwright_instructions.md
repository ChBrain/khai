# Instructions: Interval

How a Playwright wires the interval composite into a play. You wire by linking the play's own content to the composite's files; the files stay untouched, they are canon. The composite declares two atoms as dependencies and links their members by name -- you never copy an atom's reading into the play, you point at it. The Roadie plumbs the composite's law into Instructions Knowledge on deploy. Authoring guidance, not runtime content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human makes the furnishing genuinely good, because the composite needs no self-deception and stops working the moment the persona is staged as fooling themselves. Show what they carry in, and let it be worth carrying. Then let somebody offer to remove the journey, and let the answer be true, since the whole composite is in a sentence nobody in the room can fault.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, a persona links under Projection which bridge they are standing in: whether the hour is unclaimed, what they have put into it, and what they said when it was threatened. Read the composite on what the hour is for, never on what it costs. Keep it the arrangement: the trade struck once and the adaptation that erased one side of it are commute's, and the contractual entry and the crowd in which nobody is anybody are non-place's. You link to the composite, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- The calculation made once, the buffer paid every morning, and the ledger that cannot be audited are the commute engine's; the contractual entry, the crowd in which nobody is anybody, and the room that holds nothing are the non-place engine's; the return of feeling to a baseline is hedonic-adaptation's and the lasting composite's; what refills depleted attention is restorative-environment's and restoration's; the stretch of a duration, the unoccupied mind, and absorption are time-perception's, boredom's, mind-wandering's, and flow's; the job and the household at either end are employment's, the dwelling composite's, and family-system's. Timetables, fares, seat availability, and transport policy are the trade and set the format. Whether a production's persona is furnishing the hour or enduring it, and whether a defence was sincere, are the team's review.

## Knowledge

- Interval is the daily stretch that belongs to no part of a persona's life. It is not a third mechanism but a wiring of two: the commute engine supplies a cost agreed once and incurred every morning, including an hour that is not paid, not chosen, and not rest, and the non-place engine supplies a space entered by contract that verifies a persona once and has no further use for them. It adds no third engine and wires on a persona at Projection, and it reads the one thing the two make together, which is a stretch of time nobody owns, in a room that holds nothing, recurring twice a day for years. Three bridges. The Vacancy is unowned because it was left over: the employer does not own it since it is unpaid, the household does not since nobody in it is present, and the persona does not quite since it was never chosen and cannot be moved -- so what looks like freedom is a residue, removable by a timetable change or an office move. The Furnishing is entirely brought by its occupant: the space supplies a seat and nothing else, and travel time turns out on measurement not to be empty at all, since people work, read, sleep, plan, and above all routinise the hour, with a substantial proportion describing it as usefully spent -- and every item of it is portable, so nothing in the furnishing argues for the room. The Defence is true: a persona offered the removal of their journey will often say they would miss it, and they are right, because the transition between two roles has to happen somewhere. The twist: the hour a persona never chose becomes the only one nobody can claim, and defending it is what keeps them making the journey -- a genuine good, correctly identified, becoming the last argument standing for a trade whose other side went quiet years ago.

## System

- Do give the hour no owner. Not paid, not chosen, nobody at either end entitled to it, and the room indifferent to whether they were ever in it.
- Do let the persona furnish it well. A series they listen to nowhere else, a book that only exists on this train, a thought they have on the way in and finish on the way back.
- Do keep the furnishing portable. Everything they value about the hour would survive being moved to a chair at home, and nobody in the scene notices that.
- Do stage the offer to remove it. A move closer, a job nearer, a change that would end the crossing.
- Do make the answer true. They would miss it, they are not rationalising, and an outsider who told them otherwise would be wrong as well as rude.
- Do let the defence do its work quietly. It arrives on the same side of the ledger as the extra room, and unlike the extra room it is still being felt.
- Do close on the arrangement surviving. Nothing was settled, nobody was fooled, and the journey continues.
- Do not model the trade or the adaptation (commute), the room itself (non-place), the return to a baseline (hedonic-adaptation, lasting), attention restored (restorative-environment), how the time feels (time-perception, boredom, mind-wandering, flow), or the job and household at either end (employment, dwelling, family-system). Do not make the journey stand for a theme; that is authored key, the play's pitch. Do not edit the composite's files or the atoms'; wire only from the play's side.
