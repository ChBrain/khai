# Process: Interval, the Defence

## Taxonomy

[Interval](process_interval.md), what happens when somebody offers to take it away, and where the composite is seen for one thing.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: interval

## Initiated by

The journey is threatened with removal.
A move closer, a job nearer, a change that would end the daily crossing -- and the persona hears themselves say that they would miss it.

## Direction

Toward a defence that is sincere, partly correct, and load-bearing for the wrong thing.
What the persona says is true. They would lose the reading, the decompression, the hour in which nobody wants anything. Anybody who has worked from a bedroom for a year can confirm that removing the transition between two roles is not straightforwardly a gain.
What the defence does is different from what it says. It converts an unchosen residue into a benefit of the arrangement, and once it is a benefit it appears on the same side of [the ledger](@chbrain/khai-engine-commute/process_ledger_commute.md) as the extra room -- except that unlike the extra room, it is still being felt.

## Lever

The truth of the defence against the work it performs.
The carrying force is that the persona is not rationalising and should not be told they are: the loss would be real, the practices are valuable, the research on travel time is on their side, and an outsider who informed them that they only think they enjoy it would be both rude and wrong; the resistance is that the hour is being defended at the price of the whole arrangement, since the only way to keep it is to keep making the journey -- so a genuine good, correctly identified, becomes the last argument standing for a trade whose other side went quiet years ago.
The Defence rides the composite's keystone knife-edge: the hour a persona never chose becomes the only one nobody can claim, and defending it is what keeps them making the journey.

## Echo

Somebody defending the emptiest hour of their day, accurately.
It is where the composite is seen for one thing, and it is the loop the two engines make: commute supplies a cost whose justification has stopped registering, and non-place supplies an hour that asks nothing, and the second becomes the surviving reason for the first.
This is why the Defence is the hardest of the three to stage and the only one worth closing on: the persona must be right about what they would lose, the alternative must be genuinely worse in the way they say, nobody must be deceiving anybody, and the audience must be able to watch a true statement hold a bad arrangement in place.
A play that stages the defence as self-deception has replaced the mechanism with a delusion, and one that stages the persona as trapped has replaced it with a complaint.
The Defence is dated to the offer and is repeated whenever the question comes up.
