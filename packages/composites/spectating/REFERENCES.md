# Spectating: Reference

## Line of Work

Spectating as **composite**: what a story does to the one receiving it. It is not a fourth
mechanism but a wiring of three: the **dramatic-irony** engine supplies the gap of discrepant
awareness in which an audience holds knowledge a figure lacks, the **allegiance** engine supplies
the moral bond they form to the figures, and the **transportation** engine supplies the carrying-in
that takes them out of the room. This composite adds no fourth engine; it stages the dependency the
allegiance atom already declares and follows it to what it leaves the receiving persona holding.

It reads the one persona a play does not cast. Every other composite in the canon reads somebody
inside the production; this one reads whoever is taking it in, modelled as a persona like any
other -- which is what makes it wirable at all, since an audience monitors, sorts, and judges with
the same apparatus it uses on people.

It is also the second composite to wire the transportation engine. The elsewhere composite reads it
as one of the ways a mind leaves the room it is in, beside imagination and memory, and asks what a
persona does when they go. Here it is read as what a made story does once the audience is inside
it, and the question is what the going costs them. Same engine, two questions, and neither
composite owns it.

The domain does not model any single face -- foreknowledge and double meaning are the
dramatic-irony engine's; the verdict, the rooting, and the payoff are allegiance's; entry, imagery,
resonance, and transportability are transportation's; and one alone is a process, not the
arrangement an audience is in. Nor is it the affective shape an audience is taken through, which is
the tragic, comic, knowing, and jeopardy composites'; nor attention held as such, which is
absorption's; nor the authored key a production sets over its own events, which is the play's pitch
and is the instrument this composite reads the effect of. It owns the position: where the audience
was put, what it produced, and whether they could tell.

The spine is Murray Smith, and the allegiance atom already carries him. Smith's structure of
sympathy runs in three levels, and the order is the composite's claim: **recognition** of a figure
as an agent, **alignment** with them through access to what they know and feel, and **allegiance**,
the moral evaluation, which rests on the alignment beneath it. That dependency is the whole point.
Allegiance is not assigned by a teller and cannot be -- an audience holds a figure to what they do
rather than to what they are called, and refuses a verdict a story has not earned. But the
apparatus is fed. What it is fed is who the audience was let into and in what order, and that is
authored. So the same conduct produces opposite verdicts depending on the seat, with nothing in the
conduct changed.

Zillmann and Raney supply what the verdict then does. In affective disposition theory the audience
is a moral monitor whose sorting of the figures into liked and disliked produces a stake, so
enjoyment becomes contingent on fortunes matching the disposition -- which is why an audience is
not watching events by the second act but waiting for outcomes. Pfister, Sternberg, and Evans
supply the access bridge from the teller's side: discrepant awareness as a resource, the
distribution and withholding of knowledge as a compositional instrument, and the dramatist's
calibrated control of who knows what and when. Nothing in that literature is coy about the
asymmetry -- the calibration is the teller's, and the audience has no vantage from which to feel it.

Green and Brock supply the last bridge and the composite's keystone. Transportation into a
narrative world reduces counterarguing: a persona meeting a claim in an argument answers it with
objections, and the same persona meeting it inside a story does not, because objecting requires
standing outside a thing and they are not outside it. Belief shifts in the story's direction, and
the shift is larger the further the reader was carried. Read against the first two bridges, that
closes the circuit: the state that makes a story work is the state that suspends the faculty which
would notice the seat and the inputs. The check is unavailable in exact proportion to how well the
telling is going.

**On the evidence, stated plainly.** Smith's structure is a theoretical framework of long standing
in film studies rather than an experimental result, and it is carried here as the composite's
architecture, which is what it is good for. Disposition theory has a substantial empirical
literature and is well supported for the enjoyment relation. The transportation findings are the
firmest thing in the composite -- there is a validated scale and a meta-analysis behind the
narrative-persuasion effect -- though the usual bounds apply: effect sizes vary with medium,
message, and audience, and this composite claims the mechanism and its direction rather than a
magnitude. The three bridges are the access, the siding, and the carry.

## Origin

Film theory, media psychology, and narrative persuasion research, wired as a composite over the
dramatic-irony, allegiance, and transportation engines. The first two rows are the **joining** --
the published work that makes the audience's verdict depend on their position -- and the rest
anchor the individual bridges and their bounds.

## Restrictions

What the composite refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The three faces (the atoms)**: The composite wires the three but owns none. Foreknowledge and
  double meaning are the dramatic-irony engine's; the verdict, the rooting, and the payoff are
  allegiance's; entry, imagery, resonance, and transportability are transportation's. Spectating
  reads only the arrangement they make together.
- **The mind that leaves on its own (elsewhere)**: The elsewhere composite wires this same
  transportation engine alongside imagination and memory, reading it as one of the ways a mind
  leaves the room it is in. This reads it as what a made story does once the audience is inside.
  Both are true of it; the difference is whether anybody authored the world being entered.
- **What is felt while watching (tragic, comic, knowing, jeopardy)**: Pity and grief, amusement,
  wonder and confusion, and the dread of the not-yet are those composites'. They own the affective
  shape an audience is taken through; this owns the position it is taken from, and the two run
  together on the same evening.
- **Attention held as such (absorption)**: Attention, flow, and time-perception are the absorption
  composite's. The carry is the narrative case of being held, and where a persona is absorbed by
  something nobody composed, that is absorption's reading and not this one.
- **Working on a position by argument (swaying)**: The swaying composite reads one persona trying
  to move another's, where the attempt is itself information and produces resistance in proportion
  to its force. This is the other route, and the contrast is the point: a story is not received as
  an attempt on anybody, so it never raises what an attempt raises, and it reaches a position by
  going around the defences rather than through them. A production should not read the two as
  interchangeable instruments.
- **The authored key (the play's pitch)**: What a production sets over its own events -- the tone
  in which they are to be taken -- is the play's pitch. That is the instrument whose effect this
  composite reads, and the composite never authors it: spectating says what a seat produced, and
  the choosing of the seat is the play's.
- **The figures themselves**: The composite models the audience and not the story. What a figure
  is, does, or deserves belongs to the production and to the engines cast on it; spectating is
  read on the persona in the seat, and would run identically on two audiences given the same story
  from different seats.

## Encoding

Source to constraint. A composite root over three engine atoms it declares as dependencies, with
three bridges, in the order the teller sets them.

- **[spectating](process_spectating.md)** (the root): what a story does to the one receiving it,
  read on the audience and never on the story. Anchored by Smith (1995) on the structure of
  sympathy.
- **[the access](process_spectating_access.md)** (bridge): what the audience is told and whose
  knowledge they hold, calibrated entirely from outside them. Anchored by Pfister, Sternberg, and
  Evans on discrepant awareness as a distributed resource, and by Smith on alignment.
- **[the siding](process_spectating_siding.md)** (bridge): the moral monitoring, the rooting, and
  the enjoyment riding on fortunes matching it -- genuine, revisable, and fed. Anchored by Zillmann
  & Cantor (1977) and Raney (2004), and bounded by Cohen (2001) on identification against liking.
- **[the carry](process_spectating_carry.md)** (bridge): the absorption that stills counterarguing.
  Anchored by Green & Brock (2000), with Gerrig (1993) and van Laer et al. (2014) for the account
  and its bounds; the composite's twist and keystone, that the state in which a story works best is
  the state in which its audience can least tell what it is doing to them.
