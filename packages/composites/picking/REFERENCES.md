# Picking: Reference

## Line of Work

Picking as **composite**: what a genuinely two-sided evaluation does when a choice is required of it. It
is not a fourth mechanism but a wiring of three: the **ambivalence** engine supplies the starting
state -- a strong positive and a strong negative held toward one object, on separable dimensions,
which is neither a lukewarm midpoint nor indifference -- the **decision** engine supplies the fork
and the modes a persona runs at it, and the **dissonance** engine supplies what happens to an
evaluation once a commitment has been made against half of it.

The joining is van Harreveld, van der Pligt and de Liver's, and it is a claim none of the three atoms
makes on its own. Ambivalence, on the theory's own showing, is not inherently aversive: a persona can
be strongly divided for years, and the division does not hurt because the two sides are rarely brought
to mind together. What makes it hurt is a choice -- imminent, unavoidable, and admitting one answer.
Their model puts the discomfort at exactly that conjunction, and locates its source in anticipated
loss rather than in confusion: whichever way the persona goes, something they actually valued is given
up. The companion experiments are what make the claim testable rather than plausible; measuring
arousal directly, van Harreveld and colleagues found it in ambivalent participants only when a choice
was imminent, and not otherwise.

That is why the composite needs all three. From ambivalence: the potential/felt distinction, which is
what allows a division to sit latent and then flare, and which the decision is the device for
triggering. From decision: the fork itself, and the modes -- because a divided persona under a call
does not run their usual mode but reaches for whichever one ends the pressure fastest, so deferral,
reopened questions, borrowed rules, and satisficing all appear here as symptoms rather than styles,
and the paralysed mode is the fence made visible. From dissonance: the disposal of the forgone side
afterwards.

The last is the composite's twist, and it is Brehm's. Having rated a set of items and then chosen
between two of them rated close together, participants re-rated the chosen one higher and the rejected
one lower: the alternatives spread apart after the decision, not before it. Run that on a persona who
was genuinely ambivalent and the result is the keystone -- the persona comes out of the episode holding
a position more one-sided than any they went in with, reports it as what they always thought, and the
width of the spread tracks how hard the call was rather than how good the answer is.

It is the second composite to wire the dissonance engine and the second to wire decision. Squaring
runs dissonance on a persona's own **conduct**, where the act was wrong and the account is repaired
afterwards; here the act is a choice between two goods and nobody has done anything wrong, which is
what makes the mechanism visible without a moral reading on top of it. Slack runs decision under
scarcity, asking how much room a persona has to decide with at all; here the room is not the question
and the division is. Same engines, different questions, and no composite owns them.

The domain does not model any side alone -- the potential and felt forms are the ambivalence engine's;
the five modes are decision's; induced compliance, free choice, and earning are dissonance's. Nor is
it what a persona holds and on what footing, which is the assent composite's; nor the standing
catalogue of judgemental errors, which is estimation's; nor circling that never reaches a fork, which
is rumination's under unmoored. It owns the episode: a division, a call, and what the call leaves.

**On the evidence, stated plainly.** The three atoms are each well founded and the composite adds no
new mechanism to any of them. The joining claim -- that ambivalence becomes aversive specifically
under an imminent, unavoidable choice -- is supported by a focused body of work including direct
physiological measurement, and is a narrower and better-tested claim than the general proposition that
ambivalence is unpleasant. The spread of alternatives is one of the oldest results in the dissonance
literature and has been replicated many times, though the free-choice paradigm has a known
methodological objection: choice can partly reveal a preference the ratings did not capture rather
than create one, and the strongest designs are those that control for it. What should not be taken
from this composite is that every firm conviction is a manufactured one. The claim is directional and
conditional -- where a persona was genuinely divided and was made to choose, expect the confidence
that follows to overstate the case -- and it says nothing about a persona who was never on the fence.

## Origin

Attitudinal ambivalence, decision research, and dissonance theory, wired as a composite over the
ambivalence, decision, and dissonance engines. The first two rows are the **joining** -- the published
work that puts a divided evaluation under a required choice and measures what comes out -- and the
rest anchor the individual bridges and their bounds.

## Restrictions

What the composite refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The three sides (the atoms)**: The composite wires the three but owns none. The potential and
  felt forms are the ambivalence engine's; the intuitive, deliberate, heuristic, satisficing, and
  paralysed modes are decision's; induced compliance, free choice, and earning are dissonance's.
  Picking reads only the episode the three make, and one alone is a position or a process, not the
  episode.
- **The account after conduct (squaring)**: The squaring composite wires this same dissonance engine
  on a persona repairing their own account after their own behaviour -- the cause reassigned, the
  dissonance settled, the standard loosened. This runs it on a choice between two goods, where
  nothing wrong has been done and there is nothing to excuse. A play may run both on one persona,
  and should not confuse a conviction manufactured by a fork with a justification built over a
  fault.
- **The room to decide at all (slack)**: The slack composite wires this same decision engine under
  scarcity, reading bandwidth -- how much a persona has left to decide with. Picking takes the room
  as given and reads the division instead. The two meet where a pressed persona reaches for a
  heuristic, and the reasons differ: slack's persona has no capacity, picking's has no way to keep
  both sides.
- **What a persona holds and on what footing (assent)**: Belief, superstition, and faith are the
  assent composite's. Picking does not model conviction in general; it models one route by which a
  particular conviction gets its firmness.
- **The catalogued errors (estimation)**: Anchoring, availability, and representativeness are the
  estimation composite's. The biased search in the call bridge is a motivated search under a
  specific pressure, not a standing heuristic, and the two should not be run as one.
- **Circling without a fork (rumination, unmoored)**: Repetitive passive dwelling on a distress is
  the rumination engine's under the unmoored composite. Picking requires a choice that will be
  answered; where nothing is ever decided and the dwelling is the point, it is rumination's and not
  this.
- **What is mourned afterwards (longing)**: Regret, nostalgia, and loneliness are the longing
  composite's. The spread is the opposite move -- it is what a persona does so that there is nothing
  to regret -- and where a play wants the forgone side to come back and be felt, that is longing's
  scene and the spread has failed.
- **Two bad options with no exit (double-bind)**: A bind in which every move is punished is the
  double-bind plot engine's, under family-system. Picking's fork has a live answer on both sides,
  both of them genuinely wanted, and the difficulty is the wanting rather than the trap.
- **Throwing more after a commitment (escalation)**: Persisting in a failing course is the
  escalation engine's under dealing, and the dissonance engine already delegates it there. Picking
  reads the revaluation, not the continued investment.
- **The division as authored symbol (the play's pitch)**: A persona's dividedness made to **stand
  for** a theme -- the wavering that mirrors a motif of doubt -- is refused: that is authored key,
  the play's pitch, not the force. Picking sets what the fork did to the persona; the meaning read
  into it is the play's.

## Encoding

Source to constraint. A composite root over three engine atoms it declares as dependencies, with
three bridges.

- **[picking](process_picking.md)** (the root): what a genuinely two-sided evaluation does when a
  choice is required of it, read across the whole episode and never at one point. Anchored by van
  Harreveld, van der Pligt & de Liver (2009).
- **[the fence](process_picking_fence.md)** (bridge): both charges held toward one object, the
  conflict present whether or not it is felt, costing nothing until something brings the two sides
  to mind at once. Anchored by Cacioppo & Berntson (1994) and Thompson, Zanna & Griffin (1995) on
  the state, and Newby-Clark, McGregor & Zanna (2002) on when it is felt.
- **[the call](process_picking_call.md)** (bridge): the fork that makes both sides accessible at
  once and will take only one answer, with the mode read as a symptom of the pressure rather than a
  style. Anchored by van Harreveld et al. (2009, both papers) on where the discomfort comes from,
  and by Simon (1955) and Schwartz (2004) on what a pressed persona reaches for.
- **[the spread](process_picking_spread.md)** (bridge): the alternatives moving apart after the
  commitment, the chosen upgraded and the rejected downgraded, until the persona holds a position
  more one-sided than any they arrived with. Anchored by Brehm (1956) and Festinger (1957); the
  composite's twist and keystone, that the conviction is manufactured by the choice rather than the
  choice by the conviction, and that the width of the spread tracks the difficulty of the call.
