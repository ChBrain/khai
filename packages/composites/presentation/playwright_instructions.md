# Instructions: Presentation

How a Playwright wires the presentation composite into a play. You wire by linking the
play's own content to the composite's files; the files stay untouched, they are canon. The
composite installs the face, backstage, self-monitoring, and emotional-labor engines as
dependencies, and their laws arrive with them. Authoring guidance, not runtime content,
and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human sets the persona's performance: which persona is read as at work before an audience -- claiming a face, holding a region, tuning to the room, paying in managed feeling -- and, the question that governs the read, which part of the work is in play: the front, the tuning, or the cost.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, read a persona as at work before an audience: link under Projection the part of its performance in play -- the front while it claims and defends an image (the face wanted approved or unimpeded, the redress of deference, solidarity, hint, forbearance, or the bald act, held on the frontstage, from face and backstage), the tuning while it fits the display to the room (the scanning and tuning of a high self-monitor against the consistency of a low one, from self-monitoring), the cost while it manages feeling to sustain the display (the masking, the method that induces the feeling until it is real, the genuine moment, and the backstage where it can be set down, from emotional-labor and backstage). Play the persona as working to be seen as it means to be, present in what it projects, never narrated, and never made to stand for a story. You link to the composite, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- The claimed image and its redress are the face engine's; the frontstage and backstage regions are backstage's; the calibration of the display is self-monitoring's; the managed feeling is emotional-labor's -- one engine alone is not the whole labour. The truth-status of what a self lets through is the opacity composite's; whether the self is true to itself is authenticity's, in the self-relation composite; the rank the performance competes for is standing's; the felt quality of a connection working is rapport's. This composite owns the performance itself -- the persona at work being seen -- and the cost bridge carries the keystone: the mask worn deeply enough stops being a mask.

## Knowledge

- Presentation is a persona read as performing a self before an audience -- braiding the face claimed in interaction (face), the frontstage where it is held and the backstage where it is dropped (backstage), the watching and tuning that fits it to the room (self-monitoring), and the feeling managed to sustain it (emotional-labor) into one labour of being seen -- read as a wiring of those four engines, and its question is which part of the work is in play (Goffman's presentation of self; Brown & Levinson's face; Snyder's self-monitoring; Hochschild's managed heart). It runs in three parts. The front is the image claimed and defended -- the want to be approved of and the want to be unimpeded, every face-threatening act softened by deference, solidarity, or a hint, forborne, or gone bald, and the claim standing only while it is held where the audience can see. The tuning is the fit -- the high self-monitor scanning the room and becoming what it rewards, fluent and different in every company, against the low one letting the same self show regardless, legible and sometimes wrong for the room. The cost is the twist -- the feeling worked into place, masked on the surface where face and feeling disagree or induced by method until the feeling is really there, where the labour is seen for what it is: surface acting strains and shows, deep acting succeeds by making the feeling real, so the mask that fits best is the one that stops being a mask, and the backstage that would give the self back has less and less self waiting in it. Read a persona as at work before others, and read the performance's best success as its deepest cost.

## System

- Do read the persona as at work before an audience, never as merely on display: name whether the front (the image claimed), the tuning (the fit to the room), or the cost (the feeling managed) is in play, and cast each from its engine.
- Do keep the three parts distinct: the front is the claim and the region it must hold in, the tuning is how much the room decides what is shown, the cost is what sustaining it takes out of the performer.
- Do let the cost carry the keystone: the performance that succeeds best is the one that reaches deepest -- the mask worn deeply enough stops being a mask, and what makes the display convincing is what makes it no longer a display.
- Do not model the truth-status of what is let through (that is opacity), whether the self is true to itself (that is authenticity, in self-relation), the rank the performance competes for (that is standing), or the felt quality of a connection working (that is rapport). Do not make the persona's performance stand for a story (that is the play's pitch). Do not edit the composite's files; wire only from the play's side.
