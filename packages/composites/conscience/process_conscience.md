# Process: Conscience

## Taxonomy

The moral self that governs conduct.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: conscience

## Initiated by

A persona is read not only by what it does but by the moral self that governs the doing -- how much being moral is who it is, what it holds as moral, and how it keeps its own moral account.
Conscience starts wherever morality lives in the self and rules its acts -- the [moral identity](@chbrain/khai-engine-moral-identity/position_moral_identity.md) at the self's core, the [moral conviction](@chbrain/khai-engine-moral-conviction/position_moral_conviction.md) that stamps an attitude as a mandate, and the [moral licensing](@chbrain/khai-engine-moral-licensing/process_moral_licensing.md) that regulates conduct against a running balance.
This composite adds no new force; it reads what the three make together: the moral self as governor of conduct, guardian and alibi at once.

## Direction

Toward the whole self-government of conduct, read through its parts.
The composite wires the three engines and reads their working in three shapes: [the Core](process_conscience_core.md), how central being moral is to the self; [the Mandate](process_conscience_mandate.md), the attitude held as a moral absolute; and [the Ledger](process_conscience_ledger.md), the twist -- the running moral balance that licenses the slip.
A self does not merely act rightly or wrongly: it anchors its conduct in how moral it takes itself to be, mandates the positions it moralizes, and audits itself against a moral set-point -- and the same self-regard that governs the conduct is what excuses its lapse.

## Lever

The moral self that governs conduct against the assumption that morality lives only in the act.
The carrying force is that a persona's conduct is ruled by a moral self-regard -- how much it is moral, what it moralizes, how it keeps its score; the resistance is the reflex to read morality act by act, judging each deed alone and missing the self that anchors, mandates, and audits the whole.
Conscience is read wherever a persona is granted its moral self-government: not a ledger of separate deeds but a self that takes itself to be moral, holds its convictions as mandates, and regulates its conduct against its own image.

## Echo

The persona revealed as a self governed by its own morality.
Conscience does not close; it is the persona going on governing its conduct by a moral self, gathering the three engines into one reading -- a self anchored in its morality, mandating what it moralizes, and auditing itself against a balance.
It hands off what it is not: the felt emotions of transgression -- guilt, shame, pride -- are the self-conscious composite's; the reckoning of a specific wrong between parties is the moral-account composite's; the cognitive deactivation of moral self-sanction is the moral-disengagement engine's; the judgment of an act's rightness is the moral-judgment engine's; and the moral self made to stand for a story is authored key, the play's pitch, not the force.
The persona is dated from its own self-government, and read as a self whose conduct is ruled by the morality it takes to be its own.
