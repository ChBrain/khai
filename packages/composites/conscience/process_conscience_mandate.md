# Process: Conscience, the Mandate

## Taxonomy

[Conscience](process_conscience.md), the part where an attitude is held as a moral absolute.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: conscience

## Initiated by

The persona holds a position not as a preference but as a moral fact, binding and beyond argument.
The Mandate starts wherever an attitude carries the stamp of [moral conviction](@chbrain/khai-engine-moral-conviction/position_moral_conviction.md) -- held as objective and universal, independent of authority or consensus, and so it both [mobilizes](@chbrain/khai-engine-moral-conviction/position_mobilization.md) the persona to act on it and licenses an [intolerance](@chbrain/khai-engine-moral-conviction/position_intolerance.md) of those who dissent.
It is the moralized position: not how central morality is to the self nor how the self keeps its balance, but the mandate a specific attitude becomes when it is felt as moral truth.

## Direction

Toward the position that admits no compromise, mobilizing and excluding at once.
The Mandate reads the persona holding a stand as a moral absolute -- and what that stamp does: it steels the will to act, resist, and sacrifice for the cause, and in the same stroke it closes the mind to the other side, whose dissent is not a difference of view but a moral failing.
It moves the persona from an opinion open to trade to a mandate closed to it: a conviction that both drives the good it names and hardens against anyone outside it.

## Lever

The moral certainty that mobilizes against the tolerance it dissolves.
The carrying force is that an attitude felt as a moral mandate commands the self absolutely -- it will act, hold, and sacrifice for it beyond mere preference; the resistance is what that certainty costs: the room for the other view, the benefit of the doubt, the fellow-feeling for the one who disagrees, all dissolved because to differ on a mandate is to be, not mistaken, but wrong.
The Mandate is the moral self at its most forceful and its most closed: the same certainty that steels a persona to do right is the certainty that will not abide those who see it otherwise.

## Echo

The position held as moral truth, driving the persona and dividing it from dissent.
The Mandate leaves the persona bound to a moralized attitude -- mobilized to act on it, intolerant of those against it -- and carries the conscience's shared shape: a moral self that both governs the persona's conduct and, here, hardens it against the world outside its certainty.
It is the moralized position: the mandate that turns an opinion into a duty and a disagreement into a fault.
The Mandate is dated from the stamping, the moment an attitude became, for the persona, a moral fact beyond argument.
