# Instructions: Conscience

How a Playwright wires the conscience composite into a play. You wire by linking the play's
own content to the composite's files; the files stay untouched, they are canon. The
composite installs the moral-identity, moral-conviction, and moral-licensing engines as
dependencies, and their laws arrive with them. Authoring guidance, not runtime content,
and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human sets the persona's moral self-government: which persona is read as governed by a moral self -- anchored in how central morality is to who it is, mandating what it moralizes, and auditing itself against a moral balance -- and, the question that governs the read, which part is in play: the core, the mandate, or the ledger.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, read a persona as governed by a moral self: link under Projection the part of its self-government in play -- the core while morality is central to who it is (internalized as the true self or worn as a symbol, a custodian of standards or peripheral, from moral-identity), the mandate while an attitude is held as a moral absolute (objective, universal, authority-independent, mobilizing action and licensing intolerance, from moral-conviction), the ledger while conduct is regulated against a moral balance (a cleansing after a wrong, an indulgence after a good, from moral-licensing). Play the persona as ruled by its own morality, present in what it projects, never narrated, and never made to stand for a story. You link to the composite, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- The centrality of morality to the self is the moral-identity engine's; the moralized attitude and its intolerance is moral-conviction's; the running moral balance is moral-licensing's -- one engine alone is a position or a process, not the whole government. The felt guilt, shame, and pride of a moral act are the self-conscious composite's; the reckoning of a wrong between parties is moral-account's; the deactivation of moral self-sanction is the moral-disengagement engine's; the judgment of an act's rightness is moral-judgment's. This composite owns the moral self-government -- the persona whose conduct is ruled by the morality it takes to be its own -- and the ledger bridge carries the keystone: the conscience that keeps the score is the conscience that licenses the slip.

## Knowledge

- Conscience is a persona read as governed by a moral self -- braiding how central being moral is to who it is (moral-identity), the attitudes it stamps as moral absolutes (moral-conviction), and the running moral balance it keeps (moral-licensing) into one self-government of conduct -- read as a wiring of those three engines, and its question is which part of the government is in play (Blasi's moral self and self-consistency; Aquino & Reed's moral identity; Skitka's moral conviction; Monin, Miller & Sachdeva's moral licensing). It runs in three parts. The core is how central being moral is to the self -- morality internalized as the true self or worn as a symbolic image, the custodian of standards or held at the periphery, the anchor whose depth sets its pull on conduct. The mandate is the attitude stamped as a moral absolute -- held as objective, universal, and authority-independent, which mobilizes the persona to act and licenses intolerance of those who dissent. The ledger is the twist -- the moral self kept as a running balance, past wrong driving a compensatory cleansing and past good buying the indulgence of a later lapse, where the government is seen for what it is: the conscience that keeps the score is the conscience that licenses the slip. Read a persona as ruled by its own morality, and read the moral self as guardian of conduct and alibi for its lapse at once.

## System

- Do read the persona as governed by a moral self, never by act-by-act judgment alone: name whether the core (its moral centrality), the mandate (a moralized absolute), or the ledger (its moral balance) is in play, and cast each from its engine.
- Do keep the three parts distinct: the core is how moral the self takes itself to be, the mandate is a specific attitude held as moral truth, the ledger is the running balance of conduct against a set-point.
- Do let the ledger carry the keystone: the moral self is guardian and alibi at once -- the credit of past virtue spent as licence for present lapse, the self surest of its goodness the readiest to let it slip.
- Do not model the moral emotions of transgression (that is self-conscious), the reckoning of a wrong between parties (that is moral-account), the deactivation of moral self-sanction (that is moral-disengagement), or the judgment of an act's rightness (that is moral-judgment). Do not make the persona's moral self-government stand for a story (that is the play's pitch). Do not edit the composite's files; wire only from the play's side.
