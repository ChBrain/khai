# Conscience: Reference

## Line of Work

Conscience as **composite**: the moral self that governs a persona's conduct. It is not a
new force but a wiring of three: the **moral-identity** engine supplies the **core** -- how
central being moral is to who a person is, the self-regulatory anchor whose pull on conduct
follows its depth; the **moral-conviction** engine supplies the **mandate** -- the attitude
stamped as a moral absolute, mobilizing and intolerant; and the **moral-licensing** engine
supplies the **ledger** -- the running moral balance a self keeps and spends. This composite
adds no fourth engine; it reads what the three make together -- the moral self as governor
of conduct -- and stages that into three parts of a self's moral government, closing on the
discovery that the same self-regard that anchors good conduct is what licenses its lapse.

The domain does not model any single force -- the centrality of morality to the self is the
moral-identity engine's, the moralized attitude is moral-conviction's, the moral balance is
moral-licensing's, and one alone is a position or a process, not the whole government. Nor
is it the felt emotions of transgression -- guilt, shame, pride -- which are the
self-conscious composite's; nor the reckoning of a specific wrong between parties, which is
the moral-account composite's; nor the cognitive deactivation of moral self-sanction, which
is the moral-disengagement engine's; nor the judgment of an act's rightness, which is the
moral-judgment engine's. It owns the moral self-government: the persona whose conduct is
ruled by the morality it takes to be its own, guardian of that conduct and alibi for its
lapse at once.

The spine is the reading of morality as lodged in the self and regulating conduct, not as a
verdict on a deed. Blasi set the moral self at the centre -- moral identity as the bridge
from moral judgment to moral action, conduct governed by a demand for self-consistency.
Aquino and Reed made the anchor measurable -- moral identity as internalized in the self or
symbolized to others. Skitka read the mandate -- moral conviction as a metacognitive stamp
that turns an attitude into an objective, authority-independent truth, one that mobilizes
action and licenses intolerance. And Monin, Miller, and Sachdeva read the ledger and the
composite's turn: moral licensing, the self keeping a moral balance, past credit spent as
permission to lapse and past deficit driving a cleansing. The three bridges are those
readings staged: the core, the mandate, and the ledger.

## Restrictions

What the composite refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The single engines (the atoms)**: The composite wires the three but owns none. The
  centrality of being moral to the self -- internalized, symbolic, custodian, peripheral --
  is the moral-identity engine's; the attitude stamped as a moral mandate, with its
  mobilization and intolerance, is moral-conviction's; the running moral balance, its
  cleansing and indulgence, is moral-licensing's. Conscience reads only what the three make
  as one self-government; a single engine alone is a position or a process, not the whole.
- **The emotions of transgression (self-conscious)**: The felt guilt, shame, and pride that
  follow a moral act are the self-conscious composite's and their own engines'. Conscience is
  the moral self-concept and its regulation of conduct, not the emotion the conduct provokes.
- **The reckoning of a wrong (moral-account)**: The transgression opened between parties and
  its ledgers of guilt, repair, and forgiveness is the moral-account composite's. Conscience
  is the self's government of its own conduct, not the account settled between a wrongdoer and
  the wronged.
- **The deactivation of self-sanction (moral-disengagement)**: The cognitive manoeuvres that
  switch off moral self-sanction -- euphemism, diffusion, dehumanization -- are the
  moral-disengagement engine's. Conscience's ledger keeps the moral balance and spends it;
  disengagement removes the balance from the reckoning altogether.
- **The judgment of an act (moral-judgment)**: How a persona judges an act right or wrong is
  the moral-judgment engine's. Conscience is the moral self that governs the persona's own
  conduct, not the verdict it passes on a deed.
- **The moral self as authored symbol (the play's pitch)**: A persona's moral self-government
  made to **stand for** a story -- the licensing that mirrors a theme of hypocrisy, the
  conviction that mirrors a fanaticism -- is refused: that is authored key, the play's pitch,
  not the force on the persona. Conscience delivers the persona's own self-government; the
  meaning read into it is the play's.

## Encoding

Source to constraint. A composite root over three engine atoms it declares as dependencies,
with three bridges, the parts of a self's moral government.

- **[conscience](process_conscience.md)** (the root): the moral self that governs conduct --
  how central morality is to the self, what it moralizes, and how it keeps its balance.
  Anchored by Blasi (the moral self, self-consistency).
- **[the core](process_conscience_core.md)** (bridge): how central being moral is to the self --
  internalized as the true self or worn as a symbol, custodian of standards or peripheral.
  Anchored by Aquino & Reed (moral identity).
- **[the mandate](process_conscience_mandate.md)** (bridge): the attitude held as a moral
  absolute -- objective, universal, authority-independent, mobilizing and intolerant. Anchored
  by Skitka (moral conviction).
- **[the ledger](process_conscience_ledger.md)** (bridge): the running moral balance -- past
  wrong driving a cleansing, past good buying a licensed lapse. Anchored by Monin, Miller &
  Sachdeva; the composite's twist and keystone, that the conscience which keeps the score is
  the conscience that licenses the slip.
