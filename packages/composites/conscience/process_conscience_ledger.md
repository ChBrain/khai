# Process: Conscience, the Ledger

## Taxonomy

[Conscience](process_conscience.md), the part where the moral self keeps a running balance, and the government licenses the slip.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: conscience

## Initiated by

The persona keeps a moral account of itself over time, and the balance it carries buys the conduct it allows next.
The Ledger starts wherever a persona regulates its conduct against a moral set-point -- the [moral licensing](@chbrain/khai-engine-moral-licensing/process_moral_licensing.md) by which a past wrong drives a compensatory [cleansing](@chbrain/khai-engine-moral-licensing/process_cleansing.md) and a past good buys the [indulgence](@chbrain/khai-engine-moral-licensing/process_indulgence.md) of a later lapse.
It is the moral thermostat: not how central morality is to the self nor what it moralizes, but the running balance the self keeps and spends.

## Direction

Toward the conduct bought by the balance already banked.
The Ledger reads the persona auditing itself against its moral set-point -- a felt deficit after a wrong pressing it toward a cleansing good, a felt surplus after a good releasing it toward a licensed lapse -- the conduct swinging back toward the balance rather than climbing steadily toward the ideal.
It moves the persona from the deed to the account: not each act judged alone but each measured against the running total, so that having been good becomes a reason to allow being less so.

## Lever

The moral self-image that governs conduct against the very licence that image grants.
The carrying force is that a persona keeps a moral balance and steers by it -- cleansing a deficit, spending a surplus; the resistance is that this same self-regulation undoes the good it guards, because a credit in the ledger reads as permission: the clearer the conscience, the freer the slip, and the surer the self of its goodness, the readier its lapse.
The Ledger rides the composite's keystone knife-edge: the moral self is guardian and alibi at once -- the conscience that keeps the score is the conscience that licenses it to fall short, the same self-regard that anchors good conduct buying the right to abandon it.

## Echo

The self audited against its own balance, and licensed by it.
It shows a persona regulating its conduct against a moral set-point -- cleansing after a wrong, indulging after a good -- and carries beneath it the composite's keystone: that a moral self is not only a guardian of conduct but its alibi, the credit of past virtue spent as licence for present lapse.
The Ledger is the note the whole composite closes on: conscience is not a steady climb toward the good but a balance kept and traded, and the self surest of its own morality is the self most licensed to let it slip.
The Ledger is dated from the reckoning, the moment the persona measured its next conduct against the moral balance it already carried.
