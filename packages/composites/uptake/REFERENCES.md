# Uptake: Reference

## Line of Work

Uptake as **composite**: how far a thing travels through a population, and what stops it. It is not a
third mechanism but a wiring of two: the **adoption** engine supplies the travel -- the five adopter
positions and the four places an idea passes through, from the outside across the chasm to the old
ground -- and the **social-identity** engine supplies what taking it up means, since a persona draws
part of the self from the groups they belong to and an adoption is a claim about which those are.

The joining is Berger and Heath's, and it is in neither atom. Diffusion research had explained the
spread of a thing by the thing: relative advantage, compatibility, complexity, trialability,
observability. Berger and Heath showed that in domains where a possession or practice signals identity
-- and only in those domains -- people **abandon** what they hold when the wrong others take it up.
Their studies track the mechanism directly rather than inferring it: tastes are dropped as
dissimilar outsiders adopt them, and the dropping does not occur in domains that carry no signal, so
the effect is not a general preference for novelty or a wearing-out of the object. The population is
not a medium the thing moves through; it is an audience reading everybody in it.

That is why the composite needs both atoms and why it reads the population rather than the object.
From adoption: the curve, and the fact that a persona's position on it is relative to the thing rather
than a type -- the same persona is an innovator for one and a laggard for another. From social
identity: what an adoption says, and, crucially, the two moves available when what it says goes wrong.
The atom already carries them. **Mobility** is the exit taken as an individual where the boundary
reads as permeable -- the taste simply dropped. **Creativity** is the reframe that keeps the thing and
changes the terms of comparison -- the earlier version, the harder practice, the part the newcomers do
not do -- restoring the distinction without surrendering the object. Both are ordinary responses to a
group whose standing has come into question, and here the group is the set of people who have the
thing.

Hence the keystone. A thing travels until it starts saying the wrong thing about the people who hold
it, so the ceiling is set by who else arrived rather than by the merits, and the evidence of success
is the mechanism of decline. The same fact drives both halves: being taken up by others is what
spreads a thing, and being taken up by the wrong others is what stops it, and there is no version of
the first that does not eventually produce the second.

It is the third composite to wire the social-identity engine. Membership runs it on one persona's
standing bind to a group -- the self drawn from it, the standard it bends them to, the cost of
leaving -- and intergroup runs it on the us/them line and what that switches on. Here it is run on a
**thing**, where the group is constituted by whoever currently holds it and has no membership roll, no
entry ritual, and no way to keep anybody out. Same engine, different question, and no composite owns
it.

The domain does not model either side alone -- the five adopter positions and the four places are the
adoption engine's, and the four stances are social identity's. Nor is it talk moving through a
population with no object attached, which is grapevine's; nor a level crossing a threshold and not
returning, which is margin's and is this shape without the audience. It owns the ceiling: how far a
thing gets, and why it stops there.

**On the evidence, stated plainly.** The two legs are not equally supported and the composite should
be read accordingly. Berger and Heath's identity-signalling work is the strong leg: it is
experimental, it includes the field observation that makes it credible, and its scope condition --
that divergence occurs in identity-relevant domains and not otherwise -- is part of the finding rather
than a later rescue, which is unusual and worth crediting. The adoption curve is the weaker leg. Rogers'
categories are a descriptive scheme fitted to diffusion histories after the fact, and the proportions
attached to them are conventions rather than measurements; the model predicts poorly in advance and
its critics have long noted that it selects on innovations that spread. The chasm the atom carries is
weaker still -- a consultant's model from technology marketing, widely used and never seriously
tested -- and a play should treat it as a useful shape rather than a finding. What should not be
taken from this composite is that popularity destroys value in general, or that early adopters are
snobs. Most things that spread simply keep spreading, most adoptions carry no signal at all, and the
mechanism only runs where the thing is identity-bearing. The claim is conditional: where holding a
thing says something about the holder, expect its meaning to be revised by every arrival, and expect
the people who arrived first to be the ones who notice.

## Origin

Diffusion research and social identity theory, wired as a composite over the adoption and
social-identity engines. The first row is the **joining** -- the published work that makes the
composition of the adopters a cause of the diffusion rather than a result of it -- and the rest anchor
the individual bridges and their bounds.

## Restrictions

What the composite refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The two sides (the atoms)**: The composite wires the two but owns neither. The five adopter
  positions and the four places are the adoption engine's; identification, mobility, creativity, and
  competition are social identity's. Uptake reads only what the second does to the first.
- **A persona's bind to a group (membership)**: The membership composite wires this same
  social-identity engine on a standing bond -- the self drawn from a group, the standard it bends the
  persona to, a mark it would discredit, and the cost of leaving. Uptake's group is constituted by
  whoever currently holds a thing: it has no roll, no entry ritual, and no way to keep anybody out,
  which is precisely why it cannot defend its boundary and why the turn happens.
- **The us/them line (intergroup)**: Social identity, bias, and hostility between groups are the
  intergroup composite's. Uptake needs no outgroup hostility and no contest; the newcomers are
  sympathetic and are doing nothing wrong, and a play that makes them contemptible has staged
  something else.
- **An attempt to persuade (swaying)**: Persuasion and the reactance an attempt raises are the
  swaying composite's. Nobody in uptake is being persuaded -- each adopter has their own ordinary
  reason -- and where a campaign is pushing the thing, that is swaying's scene running alongside.
- **Talk moving through a population (grapevine)**: Gossip and rumour are the grapevine composite's,
  and they travel with no object attached. Uptake requires a thing that can be held and read.
- **A level crossing a threshold (margin)**: Drift and the tipping point are the margin composite's,
  and they are this composite's shape without an audience -- a quantity passing a point of no return
  for physical rather than social reasons. Where nobody is reading anybody, it is margin's.
- **Wanting what somebody else has (outdone)**: Envy, comparison, and admiration are the outdone
  composite's. Uptake's persona is not coveting a possession; they are noticing what theirs has come
  to mean.
- **The fashion as authored symbol (the play's pitch)**: A thing rising and falling in order to
  **stand for** a theme -- the craze that mirrors a motif of hollowness -- is refused: that is
  authored key, the play's pitch, not the force. Uptake sets where the thing got to; the meaning read
  into it is the play's.

## Encoding

Source to constraint. A composite root over two engine atoms it declares as dependencies, with three
bridges.

- **[uptake](process_uptake.md)** (the root): how far a thing travels and what stops it, read on the
  population and never on the thing. Anchored by Berger & Heath (2007, 2008) and, before its evidence,
  by Simmel (1904).
- **[the curve](process_uptake_curve.md)** (bridge): the sequence and its places, with position
  relative to the thing rather than a type, and the merits accounting for less of the travel than
  they appear to. Anchored by Rogers (1962--2003).
- **[the signal](process_uptake_signal.md)** (bridge): what having it says, assembled out of whoever
  currently holds it and revised by every arrival. Anchored by Tajfel & Turner and by Bourdieu (1979).
- **[the turn](process_uptake_turn.md)** (bridge): the signal inverting, with the early holders taking
  the exit or keeping the thing and moving the terms. Anchored by Berger & Heath (2008) and by the
  mobility and creativity stances the social-identity atom carries; the composite's twist and
  keystone, that a thing travels until it says the wrong thing about its holders, so the evidence of
  success is the mechanism of decline.
