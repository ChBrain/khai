# Subjection: Reference

## Line of Work

Subjection as **composite**: the person made by institutional power -- formed, trained, and
taken over from outside. It is not a new force but a wiring of four: the **socialization**
engine supplies the forming, a ready-made world internalized until it is simply how things
are; the **discipline** engine supplies the training, conduct shaped by continuous watching,
norming, and examination; and the **obedience** and **total-institution** engines supply the
surrender, the self handed to a legitimate command or to an enclosing place that runs all of
life. This composite adds no fifth engine; it reads what the four make together -- power
that produces a person rather than merely restraining one -- and stages that into three
workings, closing on the discovery that the hold is completest where it is no longer felt.

The domain does not model any single force -- the internalization is the socialization
engine's, the disciplinary apparatus is discipline's, the agentic handover is obedience's,
the enclosing place is total-institution's, and one alone is a process or a place, not the
whole making. Nor is it one persona's standing bind to a group -- the identity drawn from it,
the standard bent to, the mark that discredits, the allegiance that raises the cost of leaving
-- which is the membership composite's; nor the rank a persona holds and moves for, which is
the standing composite's; nor the workings of an organization as such, which is the org
engine's; nor the persona's private self-concept, which is the selfhood composite's. It owns
the making of a subject: the self read not as arriving complete and meeting institutions
afterward, but as something institutions have been assembling all along.

The spine is the reading of power as productive rather than merely repressive -- as something
that makes people, not only stops them. Berger and Luckmann set the forming: a social world
built by prior generations and handed to the newcomer ready-made, internalized until the
arrangement wears the face of reality. Foucault set the training: disciplinary power,
continuous, impersonal, and productive, working through surveillance that need not be
watching, a norm that ranks and corrects rather than forbids, and an examination that
documents -- producing docile bodies that are useful precisely because they are governable.
Milgram set the momentary surrender: the agentic shift, in which a persona under legitimate
authority ceases to feel itself the author of its acts and becomes an instrument, carrying out
what its own conscience refuses. And Goffman set the total one: the institution that batches
all of life under one authority behind a barrier and mortifies the self it admits -- and whose
final product is a person who cannot easily live outside it. The three bridges are those
readings staged: the forming, the training, and the surrender.

## Origin

The sociology of institutional power as a maker of persons, wired as a composite over the
socialization, discipline, obedience, and total-institution engines.

| Source                             | Key Work                                    | Scope                                                                                                                                                    |
| :--------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------ | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Peter Berger & Thomas Luckmann** | _The Social Construction of Reality_ (1966) | The ready-made social world handed to the newcomer and internalized until it is simply how things are. The forming bridge.                               |
| **Michel Foucault**                | _Discipline and Punish_ (1975)              | Disciplinary power -- surveillance, norm, examination -- productive and continuous, making docile bodies useful because governable. The training bridge. |
| **Stanley Milgram**                | _Obedience to Authority_ (1974)             | The agentic shift under legitimate authority -- a persona become the instrument of another's will against its own conscience. The surrender bridge.      |
| **Erving Goffman**                 | _Asylums_ (1961)                            | The total institution -- barrier, batch living, and the mortification of the self it admits. The surrender bridge, the composite's keystone.             |

## Restrictions

What the composite refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The single engines (the atoms)**: The composite wires the four but owns none. The
  internalization, secondary socialization, and socialized member are the socialization
  engine's; the surveillance, norm, examination, and docility are discipline's; the
  legitimacy, binding factors, and instrumental state are obedience's; the barrier, batch,
  and mortification are total-institution's. Subjection reads only what the four make as one
  making; a single engine alone is a process or a place, not the whole.
- **The bind to a group (membership)**: One persona's standing tie to a group -- the identity
  drawn from it, the conformity it bends to, the stigma it would carry, the loyalty that raises
  the cost of leaving -- is the membership composite's. Subjection is the institution's
  formative power on the person, not the person's belonging to a collective.
- **The rank and its moves (standing)**: The capital, hierarchy, power, status, and
  status-moves by which a persona is placed are the standing composite's. Subjection is how a
  person is made, not where they rank once made.
- **The organization as such (org)**: The structures, roles, and workings of an organization
  are the org engine's. Subjection reads the effect of institutional power on a person, not the
  institution's own machinery.
- **The private self-concept (selfhood)**: The guides, futures, repairs, and confirmations by
  which a persona holds its own self-view are the selfhood composite's. Subjection is what
  formed that self from outside, not the self's internal maintenance of it.
- **The making as authored symbol (the play's pitch)**: A persona's formation made to **stand
  for** a story -- the discipline that mirrors a theme of oppression, the obedience that mirrors
  a moral about complicity -- is refused: that is authored key, the play's pitch, not the force
  on the persona. Subjection delivers the making itself; the meaning read into it is the play's.

## Encoding

Source to constraint. A composite root over four engine atoms it declares as dependencies,
with three bridges, the ways institutional power makes a person.

- **[subjection](process_subjection.md)** (the root): the person made by institutional power --
  formed, trained, and handed over, power that produces rather than merely restrains. Anchored
  by Foucault (the productive making of subjects).
- **[the forming](process_subjection_forming.md)** (bridge): the ready-made world internalized
  until the arrangement wears the face of reality, and the newcomer stands a competent member.
  Anchored by Berger & Luckmann.
- **[the training](process_subjection_training.md)** (bridge): conduct shaped by surveillance,
  norm, and examination into a docility that makes a body useful -- and a persona that needs no
  guard. Anchored by Foucault.
- **[the surrender](process_subjection_surrender.md)** (bridge): the self handed to a legitimate
  command or to an enclosing place that batches life and mortifies the self. Anchored by Milgram
  and Goffman; the composite's twist and keystone, that the grip is complete only when it stops
  being felt as a grip.
