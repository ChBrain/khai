# Complex PTSD: Reference

## Line of Work

Complex PTSD as a **composite**: the syndrome of prolonged, repeated, and inescapable trauma, read whole across the atoms it spans. It carries the whole of core PTSD -- the ptsd engine's re-experiencing, evasion, estrangement, and hyperarousal -- and adds to it what sustained, entrapping trauma does that a single incident does not: it disturbs the organization of the self. Three disturbances follow, ICD-11's disturbances in self-organization: affect that will no longer regulate (the regulation engine), a self-concept collapsed into worthlessness and shame (the self-esteem and shame engines), and relationships severed or unreachable (the attachment engine). This composite reads what those atoms hold and adds only what none of them holds alone: the read of the syndrome as a single compound, a self reorganized by a trauma it could not escape. It declares the five engines as dependencies, wires their members by hard package links, and reads the compound and its three movements. It is authored persona-architecture -- the structure of a complexly traumatized character, for a play -- and never a diagnosis of a real person.

The warrant is integrative, and a lineage rather than solo work. Judith Herman named the syndrome: observing that survivors of prolonged, repeated trauma -- captivity, chronic childhood abuse, domestic violence, trafficking -- suffer more than the classic PTSD triad, a characteristic disturbance of affect regulation, identity, and relationships that single-incident models missed. The distinction is one of duration and entrapment, not degree: a trauma sustained long enough, and inescapable enough, becomes formative rather than merely injurious, especially when it begins in the years and relationships that build a person. The World Health Organization codified this in ICD-11, making complex PTSD a distinct diagnosis: the full PTSD criteria plus the three disturbances in self-organization. And the distinction was validated empirically by Cloitre and colleagues, whose latent-class and factor-analytic work with the International Trauma Questionnaire showed complex PTSD to be separable from PTSD along exactly those three self-organization factors, and whose phase-based treatment addresses the disturbed capacities before the trauma memory itself. Van der Kolk's work on developmental trauma anchors the childhood-onset case, where the trauma deforms a self still forming. The composite groups the three disturbances into its three movements -- dysregulation, diminishment, severance -- over the core PTSD the ptsd atom carries. The sources below are the literature of the syndrome; each atom's own REFERENCES carries the primary science of its part.

## Restrictions

What the composite refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **Single-incident PTSD and its clusters (ptsd)**: The core disorder -- the unprocessed traumatic memory that fires as present and its four clusters (re-experiencing, evasion, estrangement, hyperarousal) -- is the ptsd engine's. Complex PTSD contains all of it and delegates it there; the composite adds only the self-organization disturbance that prolonged, inescapable trauma layers on top. Single-incident PTSD is a self haunted; complex PTSD is a self reorganized -- and the difference is what this composite reads.
- **Each disturbed capacity (regulation, self-esteem, shame, attachment)**: The machinery of emotion regulation -- situation, reappraisal, suppression -- is the regulation engine's; the resting global attitude toward the self is the self-esteem engine's; the self-directed appraisal of exposure is the shame engine's; the strategies for closeness under threat are the attachment engine's. The composite restates none of them; a bridge links, it never re-authors. It reads only how sustained trauma disturbs the four, delegating the capacities themselves to their engines.
- **Ordinary low self-worth and ordinary regulation failure (out of the complex case)**: Low self-esteem, a bout of shame, a failed reappraisal, or an insecure attachment on its own is not complex PTSD -- these are the everyday workings of their engines, present in the untraumatized. The composite reads them only as the trauma-wrought disturbances they become under prolonged, inescapable trauma, atop full core PTSD; absent that, the reading does not apply. The syndrome is the compound, not any one disturbance alone.
- **Clinical assessment and adjacent syndromes (out of domain)**: The composite is character architecture for authoring a persona, not a diagnostic instrument. It does not set thresholds or differentials for a real person, and it does not diagnose the syndromes complex PTSD is discussed alongside -- borderline personality organization, the dissociative disorders -- which are their own clinical matters outside this composite. The ICD-11 criteria are used as the structure of a truthful character, never as a test to apply to anyone; diagnosis and treatment belong to clinicians.

## Encoding

Source to constraint. A root (complex PTSD as a self reorganized by sustained trauma) with three movements, each a disturbance of self-organization, wired by hard package links into the five atoms it spans -- core PTSD carried by the ptsd atom, the three disturbances over the others.

- **[cptsd](process_cptsd.md)** (the root): the syndrome of prolonged, inescapable trauma -- the whole of core PTSD (ptsd) plus the three disturbances of self-organization. Anchored by Herman (the naming), ICD-11 (the codification), and Cloitre (the validation).
- **[complex PTSD, dysregulation](position_dysregulation.md)** (movement, the affect disturbance): affect that will not settle -- flooding reactivity or numbed shutdown, the regulation engine's levers overrun. Anchored by Herman, ICD-11, and Cloitre over the regulation members it links.
- **[complex PTSD, diminishment](position_diminishment.md)** (movement, the self disturbance): a self-concept collapsed into worthlessness and pervasive shame -- the trauma's degradation taken as identity. Anchored by Herman, ICD-11, and Cloitre over the self-esteem and shame members it links.
- **[complex PTSD, severance](position_severance.md)** (movement, the relationship disturbance): bonds severed or unreachable, trust broken at the root by usually-interpersonal trauma. Anchored by Herman, ICD-11, and Cloitre over the attachment members it links.
