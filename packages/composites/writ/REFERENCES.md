# Writ: Reference

## Line of Work

Writ as **composite**: what a saying becomes once it is written down and sent on. It is not a third
mechanism but a wiring of two: the **speech-act** engine supplies the act -- the illocutionary force
that makes an utterance a deed rather than a description, and above all the declaration, which
changes the world by being uttered where the speaker has the standing -- and the **document** engine
supplies the fixing and the travelling, an inscription that says the same thing everywhere it goes
and arrives in contexts its author never occupies.

The joining is Searle's, and it is in neither atom. Austin gave the performative and Searle sorted the
illocutionary points, which is what the speech-act engine carries. But Searle's later account of
institutional reality is a claim about the relation this composite wires: a status function is created
by a declaration -- X counts as Y in context C -- and, once created, it has to be **maintained**,
because it exists only in continued collective acceptance. What maintains it, in any society past the
scale of a village, is representation: the marriage certificate, the deed, the licence, the entry in
the register. Searle's own examples are documents, and his point is that the paper is not evidence of
the status function but a constituent of how it persists. That is the seam. The act is the moment; the
document is the persistence; and neither engine, alone, says what the second does to the first.

What it does is a trade, and the trade is the composite's subject. From the speech act: the conditions
that make a declaration valid -- the speaker's standing, the setting that recognises it, the form said
properly -- all of which are features of the occasion and are checkable there and nowhere else. A
defect in any of them produces what Austin called an infelicity: not a lie, a technical failure, and
one that leaves the act looking exactly like a sound one. From the document: durability and reach,
bought by carrying the outcome and shedding the circumstances. Latour's immutable mobile travels
without distortion precisely because it is not the occasion, and Goody's fixing effect turns an
utterance into an artifact that can be filed and compared. Gitelman adds the part that makes it work
socially: the form itself -- letterhead, seal, signature -- is a claim to have been made properly, and
it is a claim the form makes equally whether or not it is true.

That is the keystone. A record's authority comes from its being uncheckable at the point of use: the
room that must act on it cannot re-run the examination, cannot summon the witnesses, and cannot assess
the standing of a speaker it never met. So it enforces the record in place of the fact -- correctly,
since it has no better option -- and a record that was wrong when it was made is from that point
indistinguishable from one that was right. The error is not corrected by scrutiny but confirmed by it,
because every subsequent check consults the same paper.

Bowker and Star supply the standing consequence and the ethical weight. A classification once
inscribed becomes infrastructure: it is consulted rather than examined, its residual categories quietly
decide who is legible, and the labour of correcting an entry falls on whoever the entry is wrong
about, who is the party with the least standing to correct it.

It is the second composite to wire the speech-act engine. Utterance runs it with implicature, register,
and tone, as one of the ways a persona talks; here it is run on what a persona's saying became after
it left the room. Same engine, different question, and neither composite owns it.

The domain does not model either side alone -- the five illocutionary points are the speech-act
engine's, and the bearer, the inscription, and the circulation are the document engine's. Nor is it the
management of another's belief, which is deception's, since a forged record is a different and smaller
story. It owns the passage: an act, its inscription, and a room with nothing else to go on.

**On the evidence, stated plainly.** This composite rests on theory and on historical and
ethnographic scholarship rather than on experiment, and it should be read that way. Austin's and
Searle's accounts of performatives and status functions are foundational and widely taught, and they
are also philosophy: the infelicity taxonomy is an analysis rather than a finding, and Searle's
collective-acceptance account has real critics, chiefly over whether it explains institutional
persistence or restates it. Latour's immutable mobile and Goody's literacy thesis are influential
historical arguments, and Goody's strong version -- that literacy itself restructures thought -- has
been substantially qualified since, most sharply by Scribner and Cole's finding that the effects
track schooling rather than script. Bowker and Star is case-based. None of this makes the composite
unsound; it makes it a well-argued account of a mechanism rather than a measured effect, and the
mechanism's core -- that records are acted on where their occasions cannot be -- is closer to an
observation about how institutions work than to a contested empirical claim. What should not be taken
from it is that records are generally unreliable, or that reliance on them is a failure of diligence.
The claim is narrow: where an act's validity depended on conditions that did not travel, expect the
record to be enforced in their place, and expect the error to become harder to reach with every use.

## Origin

Speech-act theory, the philosophy of institutional facts, and the sociology of inscription, wired as a
composite over the speech-act and document engines. The first row is the **joining** -- the published
work that ties an act done by saying to the representation that keeps it in force -- and the rest
anchor the individual bridges and their bounds.

## Restrictions

What the composite refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The two sides (the atoms)**: The composite wires the two but owns neither. The assertive,
  directive, commissive, expressive, and declaration acts are the speech-act engine's; the bearer,
  the inscription, and the circulation are the document engine's. Writ reads only what the second does
  to the first, and one alone is a process or a piece, not the passage.
- **How a persona talks (utterance)**: The utterance composite wires this same speech-act engine with
  implicature, register, and tone, reading a persona in conversation -- what is meant past what is
  said, and how it is pitched. Writ runs the same engine on a deed that has left the conversation
  entirely. A play may run both, and should not confuse a persona being understood with a persona
  being on file.
- **Managing another's belief (deception)**: Lying, concealment, and the deliberate false impression
  are the deception engine's, under opacity. A forged document is that composite's scene and a
  smaller story than this one: writ's difficulty is that the record may be wrong with nobody at fault,
  and a play that supplies a forger has replaced a structure with a culprit.
- **Relying on a person (dealing, vigil)**: Trust extended, tested, withheld, and repaired between
  parties is those composites'. Writ's room relies on a **thing**, which cannot be tested, cannot be
  asked, and does not know anything -- and that is the difference rather than a special case of it.
- **The room's two expectations of a person (unbecoming)**: A role and a read that disagree is the
  unbecoming composite's. Writ's room is not judging the bearer at all; it is reading a paper.
- **Who may draw on a shared thing (upkeep)**: Boundaries, contribution, and costly enforcement are
  the upkeep composite's. A membership roll is a document and a commons boundary is a rule, and the
  two meet, but upkeep asks who pays and writ asks what the paper can be made to say.
- **Making a place readable from above (legibility)**: The cadastral survey, the standard name, and
  the grid are the legibility engine's, under peopling, and they run on a place. Writ runs on an act
  and the record of it.
- **The document as authored symbol (the play's pitch)**: A record made to **stand for** a theme --
  the lost certificate that mirrors a motif of erasure -- is refused: that is authored key, the play's
  pitch, not the force. Writ sets what the paper did; the meaning read into it is the play's.

## Encoding

Source to constraint. A composite root over two engine atoms it declares as dependencies, with three
bridges.

- **[writ](process_writ.md)** (the root): what a saying becomes once it is written down and sent on,
  read on the record and never on the person holding it. Anchored by Searle (1995, 2010).
- **[the saying](process_writ_saying.md)** (bridge): the act done by being said, valid only if the
  speaker's standing, the setting, and the form held -- all features of the occasion and checkable
  there alone. Anchored by Austin (1962) and Searle on declarations.
- **[the fixing](process_writ_fixing.md)** (bridge): the act inscribed and set travelling, gaining
  durability and reach by carrying the outcome and shedding the circumstances. Anchored by Latour
  (1986), Goody (1977), and Gitelman (2014) on the form as a claim.
- **[the reliance](process_writ_reliance.md)** (bridge): the room acting on the paper because the act
  is no longer available to inspect. Anchored by Bowker & Star (1999); the composite's twist and
  keystone, that the record's authority comes from its being uncheckable at the point of use, so an
  error in it is not corrected by scrutiny but confirmed by it.
