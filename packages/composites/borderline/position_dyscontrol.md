# Position: Borderline, Dyscontrol

## Taxonomy

[Borderline](process_borderline.md), the impulsivity and self-harm that interrupt a state which cannot be regulated.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: borderline

## Has

The fourth movement, and the one most often misread as a separate problem. The behaviours -- the spending, the substance use, the sudden leaving, the cutting, the overdose -- are not additional recklessness sitting alongside the affect. They are the affect problem solved by other means: a state that cannot be brought down can still be **interrupted**, and each of these interrupts it, immediately and reliably. That reliability is the difficulty. [Self-control](@chbrain/khai-engine-self-control/process_self_control.md) here is not simply weak; the [lapse](@chbrain/khai-engine-self-control/process_lapse.md) is functional, because it works on the thing the persona actually needs to end, and works faster than anything it has been taught. Linehan's reading is the one to stage: the behaviour is a solution, and treating it purely as pathology to be removed leaves the persona holding the original state with nothing. Self-harm in particular is most often affect regulation rather than communication and least often what the room reads it as, which is a demand aimed at them.

## Orders

End the state now, by whatever ends it.
The dyscontrol movement orders the persona to reach for the interruption when the feeling passes what it can hold -- and to reach for the one that has worked before, which is rarely the one that costs least. The room sees the act and reasons from its shape: the spending is irresponsibility, the leaving is cruelty, the cutting is a message addressed to whoever finds it. It stages a persona whose most alarming behaviour is instrumental rather than expressive, so the ordinary response -- treating it as a demand and negotiating with it -- addresses something that was never being asked.

## Loses

The cost of the solution, paid at the far end. Carrying this movement, the persona forgoes the outcomes the interruption destroys -- the money, the relationship, the body, sometimes the life -- and pays them for a few hours of relief it could not obtain any other way. It loses, too, the last of its credibility: an act read as a demand is answered with either capitulation or refusal, and both confirm the room's reading, so the persona is now managed rather than heard. What it trades is a state it could not survive for consequences it will survive, which is a rational exchange made with the only currency available and is the reason the behaviour does not stop when it is explained.

## Drives

On that interruption:
the unbearable state is ended by an act, because ending it is available and regulating it is not. Dyscontrol is one of four movements, the behavioural one, set beside the [emotional vulnerability](position_emotional_vulnerability.md) whose states it interrupts, the [interpersonal hypersensitivity](position_interpersonal_hypersensitivity.md) that most often produces them, and the [identity diffusion](position_identity_diffusion.md) that leaves no stable self to weigh the cost against. That the act is a solution rather than a symptom is the [root's](process_borderline.md); the effortful override of an impulse, and the lapse, are the self-control engine's, where this movement owns the specifically borderline case in which the lapse is functional -- it does what the persona needs -- and is therefore not corrected by knowing what it costs.
