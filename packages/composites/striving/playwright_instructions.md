# Instructions: Striving

How a Playwright wires the striving composite into a play. You wire by linking the play's
own content to the composite's files; the files stay untouched, they are canon. The
composite installs the achievement-motive, regulatory-focus, and grit engines as
dependencies, and their laws arrive with them. Authoring guidance, not runtime content,
and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human sets the persona's striving character: which persona is read as bringing a standing manner to its pursuits -- drawn or held back by its motive, chasing in eagerness or in guard, holding on briefly or for years -- and, the question that governs the read, which disposition is in play: the pull, the stance, or the stay.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, read a persona as bringing a standing character to any pursuit: link under Projection the disposition its striving shows -- the pull while the question is why it strives (the hope of success drawing it toward the attempt or the fear of failure bending it away, from achievement-motive), the stance while the question is how (promotion's eagerness for gains or prevention's vigilance against losses, from regulatory-focus), the stay while the question is how long (passion held steady and perseverance held through setback and plateau, from grit). Play the persona as pursuing in its own manner, present in what it projects, never narrated, and never made to stand for a story. You link to the composite, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- The resting motive in achievement situations is the achievement-motive engine's; the orientation toward pursuit is regulatory-focus's; the stamina toward a distant goal is grit's -- one engine alone is a position, not the whole character. The crossing from wanting to doing -- the goal, the if-then plan, the executive control -- is the volition composite's; the moment-to-moment override of an impulse is the self-control engine's; the belief that ability can grow is mindset's; the particular goal held is the goal engine's. This composite owns the striving character -- the persona read by how it wants, not what -- and the stay bridge carries the keystone: the stamina that carries a persona through setback is the stamina that holds it to a path it should have left.

## Knowledge

- Striving is a persona read by the standing character it brings to any pursuit -- braiding why it strives (achievement-motive), how it strives (regulatory-focus), and how long it stays (grit) into one dispositional shape, prior to any particular goal -- read as a wiring of those three engines, and its question is which disposition is in play (Atkinson & McClelland's achievement motive; Higgins's regulatory focus; Duckworth's grit). It runs in three dispositions. The pull is why a persona strives -- the resting motive that energizes which goals it adopts at all, the hope of success that draws it toward the attempt or the fear of failure that bends it away, into tasks too easy to fail or too hard to be blamed for. The stance is how a persona strives -- promotion's eagerness for gains to reach or prevention's vigilance against losses to guard, the same goal chased two entirely different ways, fitting the task or fighting it. The stay is the twist -- how long a persona strives, passion held steady across years and perseverance held through setback and plateau, where the disposition is seen for what it is: the stamina that makes achievement possible is the stamina that cannot quit, faithful to the dead path exactly as faithfully as to the live one. Read a persona by how it wants rather than what it wants, and read each disposition as a character that would show on any goal it took up.

## System

- Do read the persona as bringing a standing character to pursuit, never as explained by its goal alone: name whether the pull (why), the stance (how), or the stay (how long) is in play, and cast each from its engine.
- Do keep the three dispositions distinct: the pull is the motive that decides whether the attempt is made, the stance is the orientation that shapes the chase, the stay is the stamina that decides how long it lasts.
- Do let the stay carry the keystone: the stamina that carries a persona through setback is the stamina that holds it to a dead path -- the virtue and the trap one disposition, a self that cannot be discouraged unable to be informed.
- Do not model the crossing from wanting to doing (that is volition), the moment-to-moment override of an impulse (that is self-control), the belief about whether ability can grow (that is mindset), or the particular goal held (that is the goal engine). Do not make the persona's striving stand for a story (that is the play's pitch). Do not edit the composite's files; wire only from the play's side.
