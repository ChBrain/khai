# Striving: Reference

## Line of Work

Striving as **composite**: the standing character a persona brings to any pursuit -- why it
strives, how it strives, and how long it stays. It is not a new drive but a wiring of
three: the **achievement-motive** engine supplies the **pull** -- the resting motive that
energizes which goals a persona adopts at all, hope of success or fear of failure; the
**regulatory-focus** engine supplies the **stance** -- the resting orientation by which a
goal is pursued, promotion's eagerness or prevention's vigilance; and the **grit** engine
supplies the **stay** -- the stamina by which a persona holds a distant aim across setback
and years. This composite adds no fourth engine; it reads what the three make together --
the dispositional character of striving, prior to any particular goal -- and stages that
into three standing dispositions, closing on the discovery that the stamina which carries a
persona through setback is what holds it to a path it should have left.

The domain does not model any single force -- the motive is the achievement-motive engine's,
the orientation is regulatory-focus's, the stamina is grit's, and one alone is a position,
not the whole character. Nor is it the crossing from wanting to doing -- the goal
deliberated, the if-then plan that carries it into action, the executive control that holds
the pursuit -- which is the volition composite's; nor the moment-to-moment override of an
impulse, which is the self-control engine's; nor the belief that ability can grow, which is
the mindset engine's; nor the particular goal a persona holds, which is the goal engine's.
It owns the striving character: the persona read not by what it wants but by how it wants,
the pull, stance, and stay it would bring to anything it took up.

The spine is the motive tradition's reading of striving as dispositional -- a standing shape
that precedes the goal. Atkinson and McClelland set the pull: achievement motivation as a
resting disposition, the tendency to approach success weighed against the tendency to avoid
failure, deciding not only how hard a person tries but which goals they will adopt at all.
Higgins set the stance: regulatory focus as two independent systems of self-regulation,
promotion working by eagerness toward gains and prevention by vigilance against losses, so
that the same goal is pursued two different ways and fits a person's manner or fights it.
Duckworth set the stay: grit as passion and perseverance for long-term goals, the endurance
that carries distant achievement more reliably than talent. And the composite's own turn
follows from the research that tested the edge of that endurance -- perseverance that is
costly when the signal to leave is real, the grit that will not quit a lost cause. The three
bridges are those readings staged: the pull, the stance, and the stay.

## Origin

The dispositional psychology of striving -- the standing character prior to any goal --
wired as a composite over the achievement-motive, regulatory-focus, and grit engines.

| Source                                  | Key Work                                                                     | Scope                                                                                                                                                                  |
| :-------------------------------------- | :--------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **John W. Atkinson & David McClelland** | _An Introduction to Motivation_ (1964); _The Achieving Society_ (1961)       | Achievement motivation as a resting disposition -- approach to success against avoidance of failure, energizing which goals are adopted. The root and the pull bridge. |
| **E. Tory Higgins**                     | _"Beyond pleasure and pain"_ (1997); regulatory focus theory                 | Promotion and prevention as two systems of self-regulation -- eagerness toward gains against vigilance against losses. The stance bridge.                              |
| **Angela Duckworth**                    | _Grit_ (2016); _"Grit: Perseverance and passion for long-term goals"_ (2007) | Grit as passion and perseverance for long-term goals -- the stamina that carries distant achievement. The stay bridge.                                                 |
| **Gale Lucas, Jonathan Gratch et al.**  | _"When the going gets tough: Grit predicts costly perseverance"_ (2015)      | The edge of endurance -- perseverance held past the point where leaving would serve. The stay bridge's twist, the composite's keystone.                                |

## Restrictions

What the composite refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The single engines (the atoms)**: The composite wires the three but owns none. The
  resting motive in achievement situations -- hope of success, fear of failure -- is the
  achievement-motive engine's; the resting orientation toward pursuit -- promotion,
  prevention -- is regulatory-focus's; the stamina toward a distant goal -- passion,
  perseverance -- is grit's. Striving reads only what the three make as one character; a
  single engine alone is a position, not the whole.
- **The crossing from wanting to doing (volition)**: The goal deliberated, the if-then plan
  that carries it across into action, and the executive control that holds the pursuit against
  interference are the volition composite's. Striving is the standing character brought to any
  pursuit; volition is the machinery by which a particular wish becomes a deed.
- **The override of an impulse (self-control)**: The moment-to-moment resistance of a
  temptation against a standing intention is the self-control engine's. Striving is
  dispositional and long-run -- the motive, the orientation, the stamina -- not the act of
  restraint in the moment.
- **The belief about ability (mindset)**: Whether a persona holds ability as fixed or growable
  is the mindset engine's. Striving reads the character of the pursuit itself, not the theory
  of ability behind it, though the two often move together.
- **The particular goal (goal)**: The specific aim a persona holds, its content and structure,
  is the goal engine's. Striving is prior to the goal: the pull, stance, and stay that would
  show on whatever aim were adopted.
- **The striving as authored symbol (the play's pitch)**: A persona's striving character made
  to **stand for** a story -- the grit that mirrors a theme of endurance, the fear of failure
  that mirrors a moral -- is refused: that is authored key, the play's pitch, not the force on
  the persona. Striving delivers the persona's own manner of pursuit; the meaning read into it
  is the play's.

## Encoding

Source to constraint. A composite root over three engine atoms it declares as dependencies,
with three bridges, the standing dispositions of a striving self.

- **[striving](process_striving.md)** (the root): the standing character a persona brings to
  any pursuit -- why, how, and how long it strives. Anchored by Atkinson & McClelland (striving
  as dispositional).
- **[the pull](process_striving_pull.md)** (bridge): why a persona strives -- the resting
  motive, hope of success drawing it toward the attempt or fear of failure bending it away.
  Anchored by Atkinson & McClelland.
- **[the stance](process_striving_stance.md)** (bridge): how a persona strives -- promotion's
  eagerness for gains or prevention's vigilance against losses, the same goal chased two ways.
  Anchored by Higgins (regulatory focus).
- **[the stay](process_striving_stay.md)** (bridge): how long a persona strives -- passion held
  steady and perseverance held through setback and plateau. Anchored by Duckworth, with the
  costly-perseverance finding from Lucas & Gratch; the composite's twist and keystone, that the
  stamina which carries a persona through setback holds it to a path it should have left.
