# Process: Wildland, Wearing

## Taxonomy

[Wildland](process_wildland.md), the bridge where the land shapes itself slowly, over time.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: wildland

## Initiated by

The land's forces work not in a stroke but over the long run, and the place is shaped grain by grain and year by year.
Wearing starts wherever the land does its patient work -- the surface [worn](@chbrain/khai-engine-erosion/process_erosion.md) by [scour](@chbrain/khai-engine-erosion/process_scour.md), wind, and frost; the long [drought](@chbrain/khai-engine-hydrology/process_drought.md) drawing the place down -- all at the pace the climatic regime sets.
It is the land's patience: not the catastrophe but the slow shaping that, given only time, is the strongest force there is.

## Direction

Toward the place lowered, dried, and worn to its long form.
Wearing reads the land at its slowest -- the mountain worn to stumps, the canyon cut grain by grain, the plain dried to dust over generations, the coast retreating a foot a year -- the setting shaped by forces too slow to watch and too strong to stop.
It moves the place toward its worn shape: the softened hills, the graded slopes, the dry basin, the land brought by patience to the form the eye takes for permanent, though it is only slow.

## Lever

The relentlessness of the slow forces against the shortness of the human eye.
The carrying force is that the wearing never stops -- the water always cutting, the wind always stripping, the frost always prying, the dry always drawing, under a climate that sets the rate; the resistance is only time and the land's own hardness, and against enough time neither holds.
The human sees the worn land as a fixed backdrop because the wearing is slower than a life; the land knows it as a work still in progress, the mountain on its way to a plain.

## Echo

The place shaped slowly to its long form.
Wearing leaves the setting graded and worn -- the old hills, the cut valley, the dried basin -- and hands the lowered ground and the carried material on, to be laid down elsewhere and reclaimed by the returning life.
It is the land's life at its most patient and least noticed: the slow shaping that, unlike the upheaval, no one witnesses, yet does the most of all to make the land the shape it is.
Wearing is dated from the long run, the deep time in which the land quietly became its present form.
