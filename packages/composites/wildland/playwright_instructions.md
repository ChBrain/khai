# Instructions: Wildland

How a Playwright wires the wildland composite into a play. You wire by linking the
play's own content to the composite's files; the files stay untouched, they are canon.
The composite installs the geomorphology, erosion, hydrology, fire, and succession
engines as dependencies, and their laws arrive with them. Authoring guidance, not
runtime content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human sets the land's own life: which setting is granted agency -- a place that makes and breaks, wears, floods, burns, and reclaims itself -- and, the question that governs the read, what shape that life is in: sudden upheaval, slow wearing, or the wild's reclaiming.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, read the setting as a living, self-shaping agent: link under Shown the shape its land's life is in -- upheaval while the land makes and breaks in a stroke (from geomorphology, fire's conflagration, hydrology's flood), wearing while it is shaped down slowly (from erosion and hydrology's drought, at the climate's pace), reclaiming while the wild takes it back (from succession, fire's renewal, erosion's deposition). Let the climate engine be the frame you read within, not a force you cast. Play the land as an agent with its own vast time, present in what the place shows, never narrated, and never made to mirror a human story. You link to the composite, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- What a given making, wearing, water, burn, or reclaiming is made of belongs to the five engines this composite wires -- one force alone is a process, not the life. The climate that governs the odds is the referenced frame; the built fabric on the ground is space's; the persona's feeling for the land is the persona's own. This composite owns the land's own life -- the setting as a living, self-shaping agent -- and the reclaiming bridge carries the family's keystone: the wild's return that is the human's loss.

## Knowledge

- Wildland is the setting read as a living, self-shaping agent -- the land that makes and breaks (geomorphology), wears (erosion), floods and parches (hydrology), burns (fire), and reclaims itself (succession), all within the regime the climate engine sets -- read as a wiring of those five forces, and its question is what shape the land's life is in (Marsh's and Lovelock & Margulis's land as an acting, living whole; Weisman's world without us). It runs in three shapes. Upheaval is the land's sudden making and breaking -- the ground raised or ruptured or poured new, the conflagration, the flood, the place remade in a stroke. Wearing is the land's slow shaping -- the surface worn grain by grain, the long drought, the mountain worn to a plain over deep time at the climate's pace. Reclaiming is the twist -- the wild taking itself back, the succession greening the ground, the rewilding of what was human, the world without us. Read a setting as a living thing that made itself before us, shapes itself around us, and will reclaim the ground after us.

## System

- Do read the land as an agent with its own life, never as a fixed backdrop: name whether its life is in upheaval (the sudden making and breaking), wearing (the slow shaping), or reclaiming (the wild taking itself back), and cast each from its engines.
- Do let the climate be the frame, not a force you cast: it governs the odds -- the wet or dry, the fire season, the freeze-thaw -- that the five forces play out within.
- Do let reclaiming carry the family's keystone: the wild retaking the human place is the human's loss and the land's return at once, the world going on without us.
- Do not model the single forces (those are geomorphology, erosion, hydrology, fire, succession), the climate that governs them (that is the frame), the built fabric on the ground (that is space), or the persona's feeling for the land (the persona's own engines). Do not make the land's life mirror a human story (that is the play's pitch). Do not edit the composite's files; wire only from the play's side.
