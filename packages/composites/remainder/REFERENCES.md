# Remainder: Reference

## Line of Work

Remainder as **composite**: what a persona's sense of the time they have left does to what they did
with the time they had. It is not a third mechanism but a wiring of two: the **time-horizon** engine
supplies the term -- an open-ended future that tilts a persona toward knowledge, novelty, and
expansion, or a foreshortened one that tilts them toward emotional meaning and the present -- and the
**regret** engine supplies what is being scored, a choice measured against the better outcome the
persona can imagine, across its commission, inaction, and anticipation forms.

The joining is Wrosch, Bauer and Scheier's, and it is in neither atom. Regret research had established
what people regret and when; their question was what it costs them, and the answer turns on something
outside the regret entirely. Across the adult life span, the intensity of a regret and the damage it
does to wellbeing are moderated by whether future goals in that domain remain available. Where they
do, the regret bites and predicts poorer wellbeing. Where they do not, personas who disengage from the
unattainable goal fare better -- and older adults, who have fewer available goals, show less regret
intensity and less of its cost. The same entry in the same tally is a different weight depending on
whether anything can still be done about it.

That is why the composite needs both atoms and why it reads the horizon rather than the life. From
time-horizon: Carstensen's finding that the reweighting tracks perceived time-left rather than age, so
an ending or a reprieve moves it in the young exactly as in the old -- which is what makes it a term a
play can turn rather than a stage of life. From regret: the tally, and Roese and Summerville's
opportunity principle, which the atom already carries -- regret concentrates where opportunity was
greatest, so a persona's account of their own life maps what was available to them rather than what
went wrong. Gilovich and Medvec's reversal is in the atom too, and it is the reason the entries that
survive into a short horizon are the inactions: what was done is regretted sharply and briefly, what
was not done mildly and for the rest of a life.

Hence the keystone. What makes a regret bearable is the impossibility of acting on it, so the same
life costs more to a persona who still could. The peace of a foreshortened horizon is not a wisdom
arrived at but a subtraction performed: the tally does not change, every entry stays accurate, and
what goes is the possibility that made it a demand. It cannot be had earlier and cannot be
recommended, and a persona given their future back -- by a reprieve, a remission, a door reopening --
gets the regrets back with it at full weight, which is the test that shows what was doing the work.

It is the second composite to wire the regret engine. Longing runs it with nostalgia and loneliness,
as one of the ways a persona reaches backward toward what is gone; here it is run forward, against the
time left, and the subject is the price rather than the reaching. Same engine, different question, and
neither composite owns it.

The domain does not model either side alone -- the expansive and limited poles are the time-horizon
engine's, and commission, inaction, and anticipation are regret's. Nor is it a confrontation with
mortality and what a persona builds over it, which is post-traumatic-growth's and runs on a threat
rather than a clock; nor the savouring and fading of what is good, which is lasting's; nor what a
persona might yet become, which is selfhood's. It owns the pricing: a past scored against a future.

**On the evidence, stated plainly.** Both atoms rest on substantial programmes and the joining is a
real result, but the composite is an inference across them and should be read as one. Socioemotional
selectivity theory is well supported and its central move -- that the shift tracks perceived time
rather than age, demonstrated by experimentally foreshortening and reopening the horizon -- is what
makes it more than a description of ageing. The regret findings are robust: the action/inaction
reversal and the opportunity principle have both replicated. Wrosch and colleagues' moderation result
is a genuine bridge between them and is supported by the wider goal-disengagement literature. What is
weaker is the direction of travel. Most of this evidence is cross-sectional or short-term, so the
claim that the closing of opportunity **causes** the easing rather than merely accompanying it is
better supported by theory than by longitudinal data, and the alternative -- that personas at short
horizons also reappraise, reframe, and remember more positively, which the positivity effect
independently predicts -- is live and probably operating alongside. The composite does not need to
choose, since both routes run through the horizon, but it should not be read as having established
one. What should not be taken from it is that a short horizon is good for a persona, or that regret is
something to be waited out. The claim is narrow: where a regret still has a door open, expect it to
cost more, and expect the closing of that door to relieve it without improving anything.

## Origin

Socioemotional selectivity theory and the psychology of regret, wired as a composite over the
time-horizon and regret engines. The first row is the **joining** -- the published work that makes the
availability of future goals the term that prices a regret -- and the rest anchor the individual
bridges and their bounds.

## Restrictions

What the composite refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The two sides (the atoms)**: The composite wires the two but owns neither. The expansive and
  limited poles are the time-horizon engine's; commission, inaction, and anticipation are regret's.
  Remainder reads only what the first does to the second.
- **Reaching backward for what is gone (longing)**: The longing composite wires this same regret
  engine with nostalgia and loneliness, reading a persona turned toward what is absent. Remainder
  runs the same engine forward, against the time left, and its subject is what a regret costs rather
  than what it reaches for. A play may run both on one persona, and should not confuse missing
  something with being unable to do anything about it.
- **A confrontation with mortality (post-traumatic-growth)**: Mortality, meaning, identity, and
  narrative are the post-traumatic-growth composite's, and they run on a threat to existence and what
  a persona builds over it. Remainder's horizon is a clock rather than a confrontation, and it moves
  on ordinary news -- a leaving date, a birthday, a reprieve -- with no crisis required.
- **The savouring and fading of what is good (lasting)**: Savoring, hedonic adaptation, and
  capitalization are the lasting composite's, and they run on what a persona has. Remainder runs on
  what they did not take.
- **What a persona might yet become (selfhood)**: Possible selves, self-affirmation, and
  self-verification are the selfhood composite's. Remainder does not model the future self; it models
  the past being scored against however much future there is.
- **The confidence made by a hard choice (picking)**: A conviction manufactured by having had to
  commit is the picking composite's. Remainder's persona is not defending a choice, they are pricing
  one.
- **What somebody has coming (deserving)**: Moral judgment, sanction, and desert are the deserving
  composite's. Nothing in remainder is owed to anybody, and the tally has no defendant.
- **The shortening life as authored symbol (the play's pitch)**: A closing horizon made to **stand
  for** a theme -- the illness that mirrors a motif of lateness -- is refused: that is authored key,
  the play's pitch, not the force. Remainder sets what the reckoning cost; the meaning read into it is
  the play's.

## Encoding

Source to constraint. A composite root over two engine atoms it declares as dependencies, with three
bridges.

- **[remainder](process_remainder.md)** (the root): what a persona's sense of time left does to what
  they did with the time they had, read on the horizon and never on the life. Anchored by Wrosch,
  Bauer & Scheier (2005).
- **[the horizon](process_remainder_horizon.md)** (bridge): how much time the persona takes
  themselves to have, tracking perception rather than age and movable inside a scene. Anchored by
  Carstensen et al. (1999) and the horizon-manipulation studies.
- **[the tally](process_remainder_tally.md)** (bridge): the roads not taken, clustering where
  opportunity was greatest, with the entries that survive being the inactions. Anchored by Roese &
  Summerville (2005) and Gilovich & Medvec (1995).
- **[the closing](process_remainder_closing.md)** (bridge): the opportunity shutting, and the tally
  going quiet with it. Anchored by Wrosch, Bauer & Scheier (2005) and the goal-disengagement
  literature; the composite's twist and keystone, that what makes a regret bearable is the
  impossibility of acting on it, so the same life costs more to a persona who still could.
