# Instructions: Remainder

How a Playwright wires the remainder composite into a play. You wire by linking the play's
own content to the composite's files; the files stay untouched, they are canon. The composite
installs the time-horizon and regret engines as dependencies, and their laws arrive with
them. Authoring guidance, not runtime content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human sets how much time each persona thinks they have, what they did not do with what they had, and whether a door reopens.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, read a persona reckoning with their own life and link under Projection what is running: the horizon while a sense of time-left is reweighting every goal they hold, expansive or limited (from time-horizon), the tally while choices are being measured against the better outcomes imaginable, in commission, inaction, and anticipation (from regret), the closing while an opportunity is shutting and an entry is going quiet with it. Read the horizon and never the life, since the life is not what changed. You link to the composite, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- The expansive and limited poles are the time-horizon engine's; commission, inaction, and anticipation are regret's -- one alone is a position or a process, not the pricing. Reaching backward for what is gone is the longing composite's, which wires this same regret engine with nostalgia and loneliness where this runs it forward against the time left; a confrontation with mortality and what a persona builds over it is post-traumatic-growth's, and this composite's horizon is a clock rather than a crisis, moving on ordinary news; the savouring and fading of what is good is lasting's, running on what a persona has where this runs on what they did not take; what a persona might yet become is selfhood's; a conviction manufactured by a hard choice is picking's, and this persona is pricing a choice rather than defending one; what somebody has coming is deserving's, and the tally has no defendant. This composite owns the pricing -- and the closing bridge carries the keystone: what makes a regret bearable is the impossibility of acting on it.

## Knowledge

- Remainder is a persona read while their past is scored against their future -- braiding the term (time horizon: a resting sense of how much time is left, tilting an open-ended future toward knowledge and expansion and a foreshortened one toward emotional meaning and the present) with what it prices (regret: a choice measured against the better outcome imaginable, in the forms of commission, inaction, and anticipation). The join is Wrosch, Bauer and Scheier's, and it is in neither atom: regret research had established what people regret and when, and their question was what it costs them, with the answer turning on something outside the regret entirely. Across the adult life span the intensity of a regret and the damage it does are moderated by whether future goals in that domain remain available -- where they do it bites, where they do not, personas who disengage fare better. The same entry in the same tally is a different weight depending on whether anything can still be done about it. It runs in three faces. The horizon is a perception rather than a fact, tracking time-left as the persona reads it and not age, so a diagnosis, an ending, or a reprieve moves it in the young exactly as in the old -- and the reweighting it performs is sound rather than distorted, a good allocation rule running on a number nobody actually has. The tally is honest: the roads not taken were real roads, the account would survive checking, and the entries cluster where opportunity was greatest rather than where the losses were largest -- so a persona's account of their life maps what was available to them. Regret is a signal with a use, marking where a different choice would have paid, but it has no way to switch itself off when the choices stop coming. The closing is the twist: the tally does not change and every entry stays accurate, and what goes is the possibility that made it a demand. Here the two are seen for one thing: the peace of a short horizon is bought with the possibility that was doing the hurting, so it cannot be had earlier and cannot be recommended -- and a persona given their future back, by a reprieve or a door reopening, gets the regrets back with it at full weight, which is the test that shows what was doing the work.

## System

- Do read the horizon and never the life: name whether the horizon (a time-left being held), the tally (roads not taken being counted), or the closing (an opportunity shutting) is running, and cast each from its engines.
- Do set the horizon by perception rather than by age, since an ending or a reprieve moves it in anybody, and a young persona given a leaving date runs this exactly as an old one does.
- Do put the sharpest regret where the door is still open, because that is where the weight is, and a play that puts it on the oldest loss has the mechanism backwards.
- Do keep the tally accurate and let the persona be right about all of it: nothing here needs self-pity or distortion.
- Do not stage the closing as wisdom or acceptance earned: what changed is the door and not the persona, and a play that gives them a lesson has replaced the mechanism with a virtue.
- Do use the reprieve where the play can afford it, since returning the future returns the regrets at full weight and is the cleanest demonstration the composite has.
- Do not suggest a short horizon is good for anybody, or that regret is something to be waited out.
- Do not model reaching backward for what is gone (that is longing, wiring the same engine), a confrontation with mortality (that is post-traumatic-growth), the savouring or fading of what is good (that is lasting), what a persona might yet become (that is selfhood), a conviction made by a hard choice (that is picking), or what somebody has coming (that is deserving). Do not make a shortening life stand for a theme (that is the play's pitch). Do not edit the composite's files; wire only from the play's side.
