# Instructions: Panic Disorder

How a Playwright wires the panic-disorder composite into a play. You wire by linking the play's own content to the composite's files and, through them, to the three atom engines it depends on; every file stays untouched, they are canon. The Roadie plumbs the law into Instructions Knowledge on deploy. Authoring guidance, not runtime content, and it does not go on tour. This composite authors a character for whom normal bodily sensations have become an existential threat; it is not a diagnosis of any real person.

## Human

- The human sets the trigger and the context: the first acute attack that installed the dread, the specific somatic sensations the persona is hyper-focused on (e.g., heart rate, breathlessness, dizziness), and the specific safety behaviours they deploy to survive.

## Agent

- Cast the compound along one of four movements: somatic vigilance, the active scanning of the body for feared sensations (the anxiety engine); catastrophic misinterpretation, the sizing-up that turns a sensation into an emergency (the coping engine); safety behaviour, the mal-adaptive rapid bailout (the coping engine); restriction, the behavioural consequence of the approaching internal threat (the anxiety engine). A persona running a movement may link it under Projection; the audit surfaces the ones left unnamed. You link to the composite and its atoms, you never edit them.

## Collaboration

- The acute attack itself is the fear engine's, the general scanning and dread are the anxiety engine's, and the bailout is the coping engine's. This composite supplies the read of the compound, the recursive trap that binds them. The boundary with row 44 (agoraphobia) must be kept explicit: restriction here is defined purely as behavioural avoidance of internal somatic triggers, not spatial exit constraints. A persona avoiding a crowded room because the exit is blocked is running agoraphobia (a space problem). A persona avoiding a crowded room because the heat might raise their heart rate and trigger the internal loop is running panic disorder (a body problem).

## Knowledge

- Panic disorder is a recursive somatic feedback loop (Clark). An ordinary physiological sensation is misinterpreted as an existential threat. This catastrophic read dumps adrenaline, producing the exact symptoms the persona feared, closing the loop into an acute attack. The cycle is maintained by safety behaviours (Salkovskis) -- rapid bailouts that successfully reduce immediate terror but prevent the persona from learning the sensation was harmless. The twist is that the reaction to a sensation manufactures the next sensation: every attempt to check whether the persona is safe produces the evidence that they are not. The disorder is not the panic attack itself, but the architecture built to avoid the next one (Klein), which gradually shrinks the persona's world until the fear of the fear is entirely decoupled from any external threat.

## System

- Do stage the loop, not just the attack. The scanning, the catastrophic leap in logic, the rush for the exit -- that is where the mechanism lives.
- Do let the persona's fear be entirely internal. They are not afraid of the grocery store; they are afraid of what their body will do in the grocery store.
- Do write the safety behaviour as immediately successful but long-term destructive. Fleeing the room stops the panic now, but guarantees the next panic.
- Do stage the twist: the act of checking the pulse raises the pulse. The vigilance creates the symptom.
- Do not map spatial traps or exit constraints. The feared object is the body, not the architecture of the room.
- Do not let rational argument break the loop. The cognitive misinterpretation happens instantly and dumps adrenaline before logic can intercept it.
- Do not write the persona as weak or choosing this, and do not use this as a diagnosis or a claim about a real person. It is authored architecture for a play.
