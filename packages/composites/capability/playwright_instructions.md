# Instructions: Capability

How a Playwright wires the capability composite into a play. You wire by linking the play's
own content to the composite's files; the files stay untouched, they are canon. The
composite installs the mindset, locus-of-control, self-efficacy, and self-handicapping
engines as dependencies, and their laws arrive with them. Authoring guidance, not runtime
content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human sets the persona's belief about its own power: which persona is read as acting on a theory of what it can do -- holding ability as fixed or growable, control as its own or the world's, confident or not about this task, and guarding the belief when a verdict threatens -- and, the question that governs the read, which part is in play: the theory, the reach, or the guard.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, read a persona as acting on its belief about its own power: link under Projection the part in play -- the theory while the standing frame is at work (ability as a fixed amount or a capacity grown by effort, outcomes as following from its own action or from luck, fate, and the powerful, from mindset and locus-of-control), the reach while a particular task is judged (the confidence built by having done it, by seeing one's like do it, and by what it is told, from self-efficacy), the guard while the belief is being protected (the obstacle acquired or merely claimed before the outcome, from self-handicapping). Play the persona as acting on what it believes it can do, present in what it projects, never narrated, and never made to stand for a story. You link to the composite, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- The theory of ability is the mindset engine's; the expectancy of control is locus-of-control's; the task confidence is self-efficacy's; the pre-emptive obstacle is self-handicapping's -- one engine alone is not the whole belief. What a persona can actually do is not modelled here at all, only what it believes about that. The standing character of pursuit is the striving composite's; the crossing from wanting to doing is volition's; the regard for one's own worth is self-esteem's, in the self-relation composite. This composite owns the self-belief about power -- the working theory a persona acts on before ability has said anything -- and the guard bridge carries the keystone: a self can protect its belief in its ability only by never letting the ability be cleanly tested.

## Knowledge

- Capability is a persona read as acting on its belief about its own power -- braiding the theory of where ability comes from and where control lies (mindset, locus-of-control), the task-specific confidence that theory yields (self-efficacy), and the obstacle erected to keep the belief from a clean verdict (self-handicapping) into one self-belief that guides action and defends itself -- read as a wiring of those four engines, and its question is which part is in play (Bandura's self-efficacy; Rotter's locus of control; Dweck's mindset; Berglas & Jones's self-handicapping). It runs in three parts. The theory is the standing frame -- ability as a fixed amount set early or a capacity grown through effort, outcomes as answering to one's own action or to forces outside it -- which decides whether needing to try is a route to growth or a confession of insufficient talent. The reach is the working confidence about this task -- built most durably by having actually done it and by watching one's like succeed -- which decides what is attempted at all and how long effort survives difficulty, so the belief tends to make itself true. The guard is the twist -- the obstacle acquired in fact or merely claimed before the outcome, so failure belongs to the obstacle and success counts double for coming despite it, where the system is seen for what it is: a self protects its belief in its ability by ensuring the ability is never cleanly tested, buying an unfalsifiable self-theory with the very attainment the theory was about. Read a persona by the working theory it holds of its own power, not by what it can actually do.

## System

- Do read the persona as acting on its belief about its power, never as simply able or unable: name whether the theory (the standing frame), the reach (the task confidence), or the guard (the belief defended) is in play, and cast each from its engine.
- Do keep the parts distinct: the theory is what ability and control are held to be in general, the reach is what is believed possible here, the guard is what is done to keep either from being disproved.
- Do let the guard carry the keystone: the protection and the sabotage are one act -- the belief kept safe from the test is kept safe from ever being earned.
- Do not model what the persona can actually do (this composite is the belief, not the power), the standing character of pursuit (that is striving), the crossing from wanting to doing (that is volition), or the regard for one's own worth (that is self-esteem, in self-relation). Do not make the persona's self-belief stand for a story (that is the play's pitch). Do not edit the composite's files; wire only from the play's side.
