# Post-Traumatic Growth: Reference

## Line of Work

Post-traumatic growth as a **composite**: positive psychological transformation beyond the prior baseline, wrought through the struggle with a trauma-grade event, read whole across the atoms it spans. Tedeschi and Calhoun defined it as growth that comes not despite the struggle but through it -- distinct from resilience, which maintains the prior baseline, in that the persona is remade to a higher level in some domains. This composite reads the four its atoms hold -- the meaning engine's reconstruction of meaning, the narrative engine's revision of the life story, the mortality engine's confrontation with death and the existential, and the identity engine's remaking of the self -- and adds only what no atom holds: the read of the transformation itself, growth through the rebuilding of a shattered assumptive world. It declares the four engines as dependencies, wires their members by hard package links, and reads the compound and its two movements.

The warrant is accordingly integrative, not primary. Post-traumatic growth is not a fifth atom beside meaning, narrative, mortality, and identity; it is the transformation those four compose when a shattered assumptive world is rebuilt through struggle. Janoff-Bulman named the shattering: trauma breaks the core assumptions -- that the world is benevolent and meaningful and the self worthy -- that ordinary life rests on, and the assumptive world must be rebuilt rather than restored. Tedeschi and Calhoun's model traces the rebuilding: an initial intrusive rumination gives way to deliberate, reflective processing, meaning-making, and narrative revision, and through that effortful work growth emerges across five domains -- personal strength, new possibilities, appreciation of life, relating to others, and spiritual or existential change. Crucially, growth can coexist with continuing post-traumatic distress; it is a separate axis from the disorder, not its opposite. The composite groups the five domains into two movements -- strength, the self transformed, and wisdom, the philosophy of life transformed. The sources below are the literature of that transformation; each atom's own REFERENCES carries the primary science of its part.

## Restrictions

What the composite refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The maintenance of baseline (resilience)**: The holding of stable, healthy functioning across a potentially traumatic event -- coming through unbroken rather than remade -- is the resilience composite's; post-traumatic growth is the distinct outcome of transformation beyond the prior baseline. Resilience keeps the assumptive world largely intact; growth requires it to be shattered and rebuilt. The two are different responses to adversity, and this composite reads the growth, delegating the mere-maintenance case to resilience.
- **Each part of the rebuilding**: The reconstruction of meaning is the meaning engine's; the revision of the life story is the narrative engine's; the confrontation with mortality and the existential is the mortality engine's; the remaking of the self is the identity engine's. The composite restates none of them; a bridge links, it never re-authors. It reads only the transformation those four compose.
- **Post-traumatic distress (stress and the disorder trajectories)**: The acute and chronic stress response to trauma is the stress engine's, and the chronic-dysfunction trajectory is the one the resilience composite marks and excludes; post-traumatic growth is neither the distress nor its absence. Growth can and often does coexist with continuing post-traumatic symptoms -- it is a separate axis, the positive transformation alongside, not instead of, the wound. The composite reads the growth, not the distress it can accompany.
- **Hedonic drift (hedonic-adaptation)**: The return of pleasure and satisfaction to a baseline setpoint after a change is the hedonic-adaptation engine's; post-traumatic growth is a remaking of values and self, not the fading of an affect toward its setpoint. A persona whose mood merely returns to normal has adapted, not grown; growth is a changed baseline, not a recovered one.

## Encoding

Source to constraint. A root (the growth transformation) with two movements, each a cluster of growth domains, wired by hard package links into the four atoms it spans.

- **[post-traumatic-growth](process_post_traumatic_growth.md)** (the root): positive transformation beyond baseline wrought through the struggle to rebuild a shattered assumptive world (meaning), revise the life story (narrative), confront mortality (mortality), and remake the self (identity). Anchored by Tedeschi & Calhoun (the model) and Janoff-Bulman (the shattered assumptions).
- **[post-traumatic growth, strength](position_growth_strength.md)** (movement, the self transformed): personal strength and new possibilities, the remade, more-capable self and its agentic redemptive story. Anchored by Tedeschi & Calhoun over the identity and narrative members it links.
- **[post-traumatic growth, wisdom](position_growth_wisdom.md)** (movement, the philosophy of life transformed): appreciation of life, existential and spiritual change, and a richer valuing of others, born of facing mortality and rebuilding meaning. Anchored by Tedeschi & Calhoun and Park over the mortality and meaning members it links.
