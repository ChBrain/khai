# Love-Hate: Reference

## Line of Work

Love-hate as **composite**: the ambivalence of loving and hating the same object at once -- which shape the coexistence takes, and how it moves. It is not a new feeling but a wiring of two, and the two it wires are themselves composites, the first such second-order composite in the canon: the **love** composite supplies the love, the **hate** composite supplies the hate, and this composite reads what they make when they run from the same object. It adds no third phenomenon; it stages the coexistence into its shapes -- the ambivalence held, the crossing of one sign into the other, and the bind where neither can win.

The domain does not model the general capacity to hold any opposed attitudes toward one object -- attitudinal ambivalence beyond love and hate -- which is a wider construct of its own; nor the fuller reaches of Freud's account of the psyche, which a dedicated cluster will take up; nor the approach-avoidance of an insecure bond, which is the attachment engine's; nor the acute grief of finally releasing a tie, which is the grief engine's. It owns the love and hate of one object held together, and the shapes that coexistence takes.

The spine is Freud's ambivalence joined to the neuroscience of the shared circuit. Bleuler coined ambivalence; Freud made it central, finding the coexistence of love and hate at the heart of the closest ties -- in mourning (where the survivor both longs for and reproaches the lost), in the child's bond to the parent, and in the obsessional's tormented love. The evaluative-space insight that positivity and negativity are separable systems that can co-activate -- the same fact that warrants the bittersweet mix of joy and sadness -- extends to the sentiment level here, so love and hate need not trade clean but can run at full together. And the coexistence is not merely metaphorical: Zeki and Romaya's imaging finds love and hate share a common subcortical substrate, so they are near neighbours a single bond can hold together, cross between, or be caught between -- which is why the fiercest hate is reserved for a former intimate, the negation of intimacy being love's own intimacy inverted. The three bridges are the shapes of that coexistence: holding, turn, bind.

## Origin

Freud's ambivalence and the psychology of coexisting love and hate, wired as a composite over the love and hate composites.

## Restrictions

What the composite refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **Love and hate (the atoms)**: The composite wires the two composites but owns neither. What a given love is made of -- its intimacy, passion, and commitment -- is the love composite's; what a given hate is made of -- its negation of intimacy, passion, and devaluation -- is the hate composite's. Love-hate reads only what the two make held toward one object; a single one alone is not ambivalence but its ingredient.
- **Attitudinal ambivalence (general)**: The composite models the specific love-hate case, not the general one. The capacity to hold any opposed evaluations toward a single object -- an attitude both positive and negative, in any domain -- is a broader construct that would be its own engine; love-hate is the affective, relational instance Freud named, not the general attitudinal one.
- **Freud (the wider account)**: The composite models ambivalence, not the whole of psychoanalytic theory. Freud's fuller account -- the structural model, the drives, the unconscious, transference, the defenses (which khai already holds as the defense engine) -- is a larger domain for a dedicated cluster; love-hate takes only the ambivalence of love and hate toward one object.
- **Attachment**: The composite models the coexistence of the two feelings, not the security dynamics of the bond beneath them. The approach-avoidance of an insecure or disorganized attachment -- the wanting and fearing of closeness driven by the bond's history -- is the attachment engine's; love-hate reads the love and hate held toward the object, which an insecure attachment may underlie but is not.
- **Grief**: The composite models the bind, not the loss that finally releases it. The acute grief when an ambivalent tie is at last mourned and let go is the grief engine's -- and Freud noted the ambivalent bond makes for the hardest mourning; love-hate owns the holding, crossing, and locking, and hands the loss across.

## Encoding

Source to constraint. A composite root over two atom composites it declares as dependencies, with three bridges.

- **[love-hate](process_love_hate.md)** (the root): The ambivalence of loving and hating one object at once. Anchored by Freud (ambivalence) and Zeki & Romaya (the shared circuit).
- **[holding](process_love_hate_holding.md)** (bridge): Love and hate at full toward one object at once -- the ambivalent bond, cherished and resented in one motion, neither cancelling the other. Wires the love and hate composites together.
- **[turn](process_love_hate_turn.md)** (bridge): The crossing -- love curdling to hate (or hate thawing to love), sharpest at a betrayal, the negation of intimacy being love's intimacy inverted. Anchored by Sternberg (the structural mirror).
- **[bind](process_love_hate_bind.md)** (bridge): The lock -- the object mattering too much to leave and hurting too much to hold, approach and avoidance cancelling into paralysis in the closest ties. Anchored by Freud (ambivalence in the inescapable bond).
