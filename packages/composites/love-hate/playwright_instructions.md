# Instructions: Love-Hate

How a Playwright wires the love-hate composite into a play. You wire by linking the play's own content to the composite's files; the files stay untouched, they are canon. The composite installs the love and hate composites as dependencies -- and, through them, their atoms -- and their laws arrive with them. Authoring guidance, not runtime content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human sets the ambivalence terrain: whom the persona both loves and hates, and what stakes hold the two together -- so a moment can be read for whether love and hate are borne together, crossing, or locking the persona in place.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, place the ambivalence: link the bridge a plot stages under its Tension -- holding while love and hate run at full toward one object, turn while one crosses into the other, bind while the persona is locked between them -- and let the love come from the love composite and the hate from the hate composite, both from the same object. Read the bond by whether the two are held, crossing, or locked, never in the word love-hate alone. You link to the composite, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- What a given love is made of is the love composite's; what a given hate is made of is the hate composite's -- these are the atoms this composite wires, and one alone is not ambivalence. The general capacity to hold any opposed attitudes toward one object, and the wider reaches of Freud's account, are their own domains; the approach-avoidance of an insecure bond is the attachment engine's; the acute grief of releasing the tie is the grief engine's. This composite owns the love and hate of one object held together.

## Knowledge

- Love-hate is the ambivalence Freud named: love and hate held toward the same object at once, neither cancelling the other (Freud; Bleuler coined the word). It rests on the shared substrate love and hate run on (Zeki & Romaya), which is why the two can co-fire and why a love betrayed converts to its sharpest hate rather than fading -- the negation of intimacy that hate is built on is love's own intimacy inverted (Sternberg's mirror). It runs in three shapes. Holding is love and hate at full toward one object at once -- the ambivalent bond, cherished and resented in one motion. Turn is the crossing -- love curdling to hate (or hate thawing to love), fiercest at a betrayal. Bind is the lock -- the object mattering too much to leave and hurting too much to hold, approach and avoidance cancelling into paralysis. Read a bond by which shape its ambivalence takes.

## System

- Do read the ambivalence as a composition of two composites, never as one muddled feeling: name whether love and hate are held together, crossing, or locked, and cast each from its composite, both aimed at one object.
- Do let holding keep both at full: the object cherished and resented at once, every gesture toward it carrying its opposite -- and let the fiercest bonds be the most tormented, not the calmest.
- Do stage the turn as a conversion, not a fresh feeling: a love betrayed arming the hate that follows with its own intimacy and wound, so the ex-lover's cruelty is specific and the disciple's hatred of the mentor is intimate -- the heat drawn from the love it was.
- Do let the bind genuinely trap: the love forbidding the leaving the hate demands and the hate forbidding the peace the love wants, so the persona is stuck and spends itself holding the two apart -- and let the way out be a loosening or a mourning, delegated to the grief engine, not a clean choice. Do not edit the composite's files; wire only from the play's side.
