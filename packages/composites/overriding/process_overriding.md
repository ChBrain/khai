# Process: Overriding

## Taxonomy

What a practitioner does when the rule and their judgement disagree.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: overriding

## Initiated by

A number says one thing and somebody who knows the work says another.
Overriding starts wherever both are in play: a standardised instrument producing an answer, through [measure](@chbrain/khai-engine-measure/process_measure.md); and a practitioner whose competence has moved out of deliberation and into perception, through [expertise](@chbrain/khai-engine-expertise/process_expertise.md).
This composite adds no third engine. It reads the one thing the two make together, which is that the practitioner is right often enough to be dangerous.

## Direction

Toward a decision made by whichever of the two was allowed to make it.
The composite wires the two and reads their working in three shapes: [the Rule](process_overriding_rule.md), the instrument and why it wins on average; [the Call](process_overriding_call.md), the moment the practitioner sees that this case is different; and [the Reckoning](process_overriding_reckoning.md), the twist -- what the overrides come to when they are added up.
The three are not a sequence of events. They are one arrangement, and a question about who is permitted the last word.

## Lever

The rule's average against the practitioner's particular.
The carrying force is that the practitioner's objection is not vanity and is frequently correct in substance: they hold information the instrument was never given -- the thing they saw in the room, the fact that arrived after the form was filled, the way this presentation does not fit the category -- and a system that could never act on that would be worse, not purer; the resistance is that being right in a given case and being right about which cases to intervene in are different competences, and the evidence separates them cleanly: the second is where practitioners fail, and it fails hardest in the ones with the most experience.
Overriding is read on the tally of the overrides, never on the merits of any one of them, because any one of them can be defended.

## Echo

A practitioner who was right, in a run of cases where the rule would have done better.
Overriding does not close; it is an authority being settled case by case, gathered into one reading -- a rule, a call, a reckoning.
The engine's twist is here: **the exception is real and cannot be identified in advance**. There genuinely are cases the instrument cannot see, Meehl called one of them the broken leg, and a persona who spots one has done something the rule could not -- but the signal that fires on the broken leg is the same signal that fires on ordinary cases, it fires more confidently the more expert the persona is, and it cannot be told apart from inside. So the value of the exception is destroyed by the frequency with which it is taken, and the practitioner best placed to find the one case is the one most likely to find fifty.
It hands off what it is not: the operation of turning a judgement into a number is measure's; the climb from rule-following to perception is expertise's; how a persona resolves between competing options is decision's; the tilts in handling a figure are bias's; being answerable to a procedure and what that buys is heeding's; and a contest between a person and a machine made to **stand for** a theme is authored key, the play's pitch, not the force.
The persona is dated from the last case they were allowed to decide.
