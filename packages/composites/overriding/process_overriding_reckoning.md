# Process: Overriding, the Reckoning

## Taxonomy

[Overriding](process_overriding.md), the overrides added up, and where the two are seen for one thing.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: overriding

## Initiated by

Somebody has counted what the departures came to.
The Reckoning starts wherever the tally exists: the cases where the instrument was overruled, set beside what the instrument would have done, across enough decisions for the answer to mean something.
It is the face of the composite that no individual case can show.

## Direction

Toward a finding that is unanswerable in aggregate and impossible to feel.
Practitioners given the instrument and the freedom to depart from it do worse than the instrument alone -- not because their exceptions are all wrong, but because the good ones are outnumbered.
And the asymmetry runs deeper than arithmetic: an instrument that errs once is abandoned, while a colleague who errs is forgiven and consulted again next week, so the rule is held to a standard no judgement is ever held to.

## Lever

The aggregate against the case, and the impossibility of arguing from the first to somebody standing in the second.
The carrying force is that the practitioner's position is not refuted by the tally: each override they made was defensible, some were correct, and being told that overrides in general are net harmful tells them nothing about the file in front of them -- an aggregate finding is genuinely poor guidance for a particular decision, and they are right to notice that; the resistance is that this is precisely why the finding cannot be acted on from inside, since there is no vantage point within the case from which the tally is visible, and the only persona who could apply it is one who has agreed in advance not to consult their own judgement about when to.
The Reckoning rides the composite's keystone knife-edge: the exception is real and cannot be identified in advance, so the value of taking it is destroyed by the frequency with which it is taken.

## Echo

A practitioner who was right about several cases and wrong about the practice.
It shows the two engines complete -- an instrument blind to the residue, and a perception unable to audit itself -- and carries beneath it the composite's keystone: the persona best placed to find the one case the rule cannot see is the persona most likely to find fifty.
This is why the Reckoning is the hardest of the three to stage and the only one worth closing on: the practitioner must be genuinely good, the overrides must be genuinely reasoned, at least one of them must be genuinely right, and the audience must be able to see that the tally is still against them.
A play that stages the practitioner as arrogant has replaced the mechanism with a flaw, and a play that stages the instrument as inhuman has replaced it with a villain.
The Reckoning is dated from the audit, and it arrives years after every decision it is about.
