# Overriding: Reference

## Line of Work

Overriding as **composite**: what a practitioner does when a standardised instrument and their own
judgement disagree. It is not a third mechanism but a wiring of two. The **measure** engine supplies
the instrument -- a common scale, able to travel to people who did not make it, answerable in a way a
person's word is not, and blind by construction to whatever was not recorded. The **expertise** engine
supplies the judgement -- a competence that has moved out of deliberation into perception, so the case
arrives already read, with the reasons assembled afterwards.

The joining is Meehl's, and it is in neither atom. In 1954 he reviewed the studies comparing clinical
prediction with simple actuarial formulas and found the formulas equal or better; the finding has held
up across seven decades and scores of domains, and Grove and colleagues' meta-analysis of well over a
hundred studies put mechanical prediction ahead or level in the overwhelming majority. The margin is
not small, it holds where the practitioners are experienced and sincere, and Dawes showed that even
**improper linear models** -- unit weights on the practitioner's own stated criteria -- beat the
practitioner applying those criteria by hand.

That last result is the one that tells you what is actually happening. The instrument's advantage is
**not insight, it is consistency**: it weights the same factors the same way every time, does not get
tired, does not remember the last case, and is not moved by how somebody presents. Most of what beats
the practitioner is the elimination of noise rather than the addition of knowledge -- which is why the
composite reads the rule as unglamorous rather than clever, and why a play should not stage it as an
oracle.

And it is why the composite needs the expertise atom rather than merely a sceptical note about
professionals. The **call** -- the moment the case is seen whole and does not match the output -- is
the expert stage doing exactly what that engine says it does: situation and response arriving
together, no interval in which anything was weighed, and reasons produced afterwards as
reconstructions. The practitioner is frequently acting on information that is real and that the
instrument was never given. Meehl insisted on this himself with the **broken leg** case: if you know
the man has broken his leg, you do not consult the actuarial table on whether he will go to the cinema
tonight.

Hence the keystone, which is the composite's twist. **The exception is real and cannot be identified
in advance.** The signal that fires on the broken leg is the same signal that fires on ordinary cases;
it fires more confidently the more expert the persona is; and it cannot be told apart from inside. So
practitioners given the instrument and the freedom to depart from it do worse than the instrument
alone -- not because their exceptions are all wrong, but because the good ones are outnumbered. The
value of the exception is destroyed by the frequency with which it is taken, and the persona best
placed to find the one case the rule cannot see is the persona most likely to find fifty.

There is a second asymmetry the composite keeps because it explains the politics of the argument.
An instrument that errs once is abandoned; a colleague who errs is forgiven and consulted again next
week. Dietvorst and colleagues showed this directly -- people who had seen an algorithm make a mistake
chose the human forecaster even after watching the algorithm outperform them -- so the rule is held to
a standard no judgement is ever held to. The same work found the aversion largely dissolves if people
are allowed to adjust the algorithm's output even slightly, which is the most practically useful thing
in this literature and the reason the argument is usually settled by giving the practitioner a small
formal discretion rather than none.

It is the first composite to wire either atom, and the pairing is deliberate rather than convenient:
the measure engine's **residue** and the expertise engine's **tacit** movement are the same absence
seen from two sides -- what the record has no column for, and what the practitioner cannot state -- and
this composite is the argument between the two people standing on either side of it. Neither atom is
owned here: an atom carries a phenomenon, and a composite carries a question asked of it.

The domain does not model either side alone -- commensuration, the warrant for quantifying, and the map
that rebuilds the ground are measure's; the stages, deliberate practice, and the blind spot are
expertise's. Nor is it how a persona resolves between competing options, which is decision's; nor how
a persona is treated by a procedure and what compliance that purchases, which is heeding's. It owns
the contest: an authority settled case by case, and what the settlements come to.

**On the evidence, stated plainly.** The joining is unusually well supported and the composite is still
an inference across two atoms. **The clinical-versus-actuarial finding is among the most replicated
results in applied psychology** -- seven decades, many domains, consistent direction, and a mechanism
that is understood. What is **narrower than it is usually reported** is where it applies: the studies
concentrate on repeated predictive judgements with defined outcomes and reasonable data, which is
exactly the condition under which a model can be fitted at all, and generalising to judgements that are
one-off, contested, or made under information the record does not contain is an extension rather than a
finding. **The override result is real and less studied than the headline**: that discretionary
adjustment degrades mechanical prediction on net is well attested, but the size varies by domain and
the studies rarely separate practitioners who override rarely from those who override constantly, which
is probably the distinction that matters. **Algorithm aversion is the weakest leg here.** Dietvorst's
demonstrations are clean, but Logg and colleagues found the opposite pattern in many tasks --
**algorithm appreciation**, where people weight algorithmic advice above human advice -- and the
current honest summary is that the direction depends on the task and on whether the person considers
themselves an expert in it, with expertise predicting aversion. The composite uses the asymmetry to
explain a dynamic and should not be read as claiming people always distrust machines. What should not
be taken from the composite is that practitioners should be removed from decisions, that judgement is
worthless, or that instruments are neutral -- the instrument encodes whatever its builders recorded,
and the residue is where the harm concentrates. The claim is narrow: the rule wins on the ordinary
case, the exception is real, and nobody inside the case can tell which one they are in.

## Origin

Clinical versus actuarial judgement, wired as a composite over the measure and expertise engines. The
first row is the **joining** -- the published work that sets a practitioner's judgement against a
standardised instrument and counts -- and the rest anchor the individual bridges and their bounds.

## Restrictions

What the composite refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The two sides (the atoms)**: The composite wires the two but owns neither. Commensuration, the
  warrant for quantifying, Goodhart's collapse, and the map that rebuilds the ground are the measure
  engine's. The five stages, deliberate practice, the plateau, the low-validity environment, and the
  blind spot are expertise's. Overriding reads only what happens where the two disagree.
- **Resolving between competing options (decision)**: How a persona chooses when the outcome is
  uncertain and only one path can be taken -- intuitive, deliberate, heuristic, satisficing, or
  paralysed -- is the decision engine's. This composite's persona is not choosing between options,
  they are deciding whether to accept somebody else's answer.
- **Tilts in handling a figure (bias, availability)**: Anchoring on the output, base-rate neglect, and
  frequency estimation by ease of recall are those engines'. The call is not a bias; it is a
  perception that is frequently correct and unreliably timed.
- **Being treated by a procedure (heeding, recognition, law)**: What a fair hearing buys, the
  claim-encounter-standing sequence, and the discretion of the official at the counter are the heeding
  composite's, recognition's, and the law engine's gap axis. Overriding reads the practitioner's side
  of the counter, not the applicant's.
- **What measurement does to a workplace (employment)**: Metric control as one way an employer closes
  the effort bargain is the employment engine's. A practitioner overriding an instrument may also be
  gaming a target, and those are different acts with different evidence.
- **Whether the instrument is fair (the world)**: The composite models the contest and not the model.
  Whether an instrument encodes a discriminatory pattern, whether its training data were sound, and
  whether it should exist at all are real and serious questions this composite does not answer, and a
  play should not use it to settle them -- the residue is where that harm lives, and it belongs to the
  measure engine.
- **The contest as authored symbol (the play's pitch)**: A person against a machine made to **stand
  for** a theme -- the checklist that mirrors a motif of diminishment -- is refused: that is authored
  key, the play's pitch. Overriding sets what the departure cost; the meaning read into it is the
  play's.

## Encoding

Source to constraint. A composite root over two engine atoms it declares as dependencies, with three
bridges.

- **[overriding](process_overriding.md)** (the root): what a practitioner does when the rule and their
  judgement disagree, read on the tally and never on the merits of one case. Anchored by Meehl (1954).
- **[the rule](process_overriding_rule.md)** (bridge): the instrument, winning on average by
  consistency rather than insight, and structurally unable to notice when it is wrong. Anchored by
  Grove et al. (2000) and Dawes (1979).
- **[the call](process_overriding_call.md)** (bridge): the case seen whole and not matching, on
  information that is real and frequently unstateable, defended by reasons that are reconstructions.
  Anchored by Meehl's broken-leg case and the expertise engine's expert stage.
- **[the reckoning](process_overriding_reckoning.md)** (bridge): the departures added up, and the
  asymmetry by which an erring instrument is abandoned where an erring colleague is not. Anchored by
  Meehl (1986), Dietvorst et al. (2015), and Kahneman, Sibony & Sunstein (2021); the composite's twist
  and keystone, that the exception is real and cannot be identified in advance.
