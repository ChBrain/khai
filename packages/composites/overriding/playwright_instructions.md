# Instructions: Overriding

How a Playwright wires the overriding composite into a play. You wire by linking the play's own content to the composite's files and, through them, to its atoms; the engine files stay untouched, they are canon. The Roadie plumbs the composite's law into Instructions Knowledge on deploy. Authoring guidance, not runtime content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human sets three things and must not skip the third: the instrument, the practitioner, and the tally. Give the practitioner at least one override that is genuinely right, since the composite reads as a lecture without it. Keep the instrument unglamorous rather than sinister -- its advantage is consistency, not insight -- and keep the practitioner competent rather than arrogant. Then decide who ever sees the tally, because nobody inside a case can.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, a persona links under Projection whether they hold discretion over an instrument and how often they use it. Read the composite on the rate rather than on any single departure, since any single departure can be defended and usually deserves to be. Keep it the contest: commensuration and the map that rebuilds the ground are measure's, the stages and the blind spot are expertise's, and choosing between competing options is decision's. You link to the composite and its atoms, you never edit them.

## Collaboration

- The operation of turning a judgement into a number, and everything following from it, is the measure engine's; the climb from rule-following to perception is expertise's; resolving between competing options is decision's; tilts in handling a figure are bias's and availability's; what a fair hearing buys the applicant is the heeding composite's; metric control inside the wage relation is employment's. Whether a production's override was a genuine broken leg, and whether a practitioner's domain is one their intuition could have been built in, are the team's review.

## Knowledge

- Overriding is what a practitioner does when a standardised instrument and their own judgement disagree. It wires on a persona at Projection over two engines: measure supplies the instrument, and expertise supplies the judgement. Three bridges carry it. The rule is the instrument, and its advantage is consistency rather than insight -- it weights the same factors the same way every time, does not tire, does not remember the last case, and is not moved by presentation, which is why even simple models built from the practitioner's own stated criteria outperform the practitioner applying those criteria by hand. Most of what beats the practitioner is the elimination of noise rather than the addition of knowledge. The rule is also blind by construction to whatever was not recorded, so it cannot notice when it is wrong. The call is the moment the case is seen whole and does not match: the expert stage doing what it does, situation and response arriving together with no interval in which anything was weighed, and the reasons offered afterwards being reconstructions rather than the process. The information the practitioner is acting on is frequently real and frequently unstateable -- Meehl insisted on the exception himself, since if you know the man has a broken leg you do not consult the table on whether he will go to the cinema. The reckoning is the twist: practitioners given the instrument and the freedom to depart from it do worse than the instrument alone, not because the exceptions are all wrong but because the good ones are outnumbered. And an instrument that errs once is abandoned while a colleague who errs is consulted again next week, so the rule is held to a standard no judgement is held to. The keystone is that the exception is real and cannot be identified in advance: the signal firing on the broken leg is the same signal firing on ordinary cases, it fires more confidently the more expert the persona is, and it cannot be told apart from inside -- so the value of the exception is destroyed by the frequency with which it is taken.

## System

- Do give the practitioner an override that is genuinely right, and do not resolve the play by revealing it was wrong all along. The composite needs the exception to be real.
- Do stage the reckoning as arriving from outside the case, in an audit or a report years later, because there is no vantage point inside a case from which the tally is visible.
- Do let the reasons for a departure be good and let them also be reconstructions: the judgement did not arrive by reasoning, so what is offered in its defence is not the process that produced it, and neither party in the conversation can tell.
- Do keep the instrument dull. Its power is that it never varies, and a play that gives it insight has staged an oracle rather than a rule.
- Do keep the practitioner competent. A play that stages them as arrogant has replaced the mechanism with a flaw, and one that stages the instrument as inhuman has replaced it with a villain.
- Do notice the asymmetry when the room argues: a single error by the instrument will end its authority, and a comparable error by a person will not even be raised.
- Do not use the composite to settle whether an instrument is fair. Whether it encodes a discriminatory pattern and whether it should exist are real questions it does not answer -- that harm lives in the measure engine's residue.
- Do not model commensuration (measure), the stages of skill (expertise), the choosing between options (decision), or what a hearing buys the applicant (heeding). Do not make the contest stand for a theme; that is authored key, the play's pitch. Do not edit the engine files; wire only from the play's side.
