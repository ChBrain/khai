# Drift: Reference

## Line of Work

Drift as **process**: the slow slide of a running process's baseline, without a
decision and without an event, until the process is somewhere no one chose. The
domain does not model the sudden turn, which is the reversal engine's; nor the
inertia or lock-in of a state, which is the momentum engine's; nor the
threshold-crossing into a new regime, which is the phase-shift engine's. It
models the gradual, undecided migration of the baseline itself, and the moved
yardstick that hides the distance from inside.

The spine is the organizational-accident literature on drift: the finding, across
disaster after disaster, that a running system slides off its own standard by
steps that are individually reasonable and collectively catastrophic. The engine
takes the three mechanisms that literature separates -- the norm that moves with
the practice, the practice that comes loose from the rule, and the standing
pressure that tilts the floor -- and gives each its own form, so a process can
declare not only that it drifts but by which mechanism.

## Origin

The organizational safety and accident-analysis literature on drift, applied
directly as engine constraints across process.

| ------------------ | --------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Diane Vaughan**  | _The Challenger Launch Decision_ (1996)       | Normalization of deviance: the tolerated exception becomes the new norm, the yardstick moving with the practice so no single step reads as a fall. The normalization form.             |
| **Scott A. Snook** | _Friendly Fire_ (2000)                        | Practical drift: local practice uncouples from global procedure, each unit sensible alone and the system fragile in combination. The uncoupling form.                                  |

| **Sidney Dekker**  | _Drift into Failure_ (2011)                   | Drift as a systemic, undecided process rather than a sequence of bad decisions -- the frame the whole engine takes, and a builder on all three mechanisms.                             |

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The sudden turn (reversal)**: A reversal is a specific moment of inversion; drift
  is the general slide that has no moment. The reversal engine's own root disclaims
  "a general deterioration or drift" -- that disclaimer is this engine. A drift may
  end in a reversal when it finally breaks, but the slide is not the break, and the
  break is not modelled here.
- **Inertia and lock-in (momentum)**: Momentum is a state resisting change, staying
  put or self-reinforcing; drift is a baseline in motion. Momentum is why a thing
  does not change; drift is a change no one chose. A drifted process may then lock in
  (momentum), but the drifting is not the staying.
- **The threshold crossing (phase-shift)**: A phase-shift is a regime change at a
  threshold, an event with a before and an after; drift is the sub-threshold slide
  that has no event. Drift often carries a process to a threshold and phase-shift
  trips it, but the slide is not the tip.
- **A persona's own aimlessness (meaning)**: A persona feeling directionless or
  unmoored is the meaning engine's "Adrift" position, a persona's felt state. Drift
  is a property of a running dynamic, not a mood in someone watching one, and the
  two share only a word.
- **The mechanism is not engine content**: The engine declares the three mechanisms
  exist; which one a given process drifts by lives on the process, linked under its
  Direction chapter. The engine refuses to fix a process's drift for it.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per file.

- **[drift](process_drift.md)** (the root): the slow slide of a baseline with no
  decision and no event, invisible from inside because the yardstick moves with it.
  Anchored by Dekker (drift as a systemic process) and Rasmussen (the undecided
  migration).
- **[normalization](process_normalization.md)** (the moving yardstick): each
  tolerated exception resets the norm, so the standard follows the practice down and
  no single step looks like a fall. Anchored by Vaughan (the Challenger O-ring, safe
  because the last flight survived).
- **[uncoupling](process_uncoupling.md)** (practice off the rule): local
  adaptations that work in their own corner accumulate into a system that is
  sensible everywhere and coherent nowhere. Anchored by Snook (practical drift,
  locally sound and globally fragile).
- **[gradient](process_gradient.md)** (the tilted floor): a standing pressure draws
  the baseline down an efficiency gradient toward the boundary, each step trading an
  unpriced margin for a counted gain. Anchored by Rasmussen (migration to the
  boundary) and Dekker (drift into failure).
