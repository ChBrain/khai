# Instructions: Drift

How a Playwright wires the drift engine into a play. You wire by linking the
play's own content to the engine's files; the engine files stay untouched, they
are canon. The Roadie plumbs the engine's law into Instructions Knowledge on
deploy. Authoring guidance, not runtime content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human sets which running processes in the world are allowed to slide, and off what standard.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, a process that drifts carries under Direction the mechanism of its slide: the yardstick moving with the practice (normalization), practice coming loose from the rule (uncoupling), or a standing pressure tilting the floor (gradient). Play the slide as undecided and invisible from inside, never as a decision or an event. You link to the engine, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- The felt sense that something is off is the emotion engine's; the moment the drift finally breaks is reversal's; the state that locks in after is momentum's; the regime it tips into is phase-shift's. Whether the slide reads true for the process is the team's review.

## Knowledge

- Drift is the slow slide of a running process's baseline, by one of three mechanisms: the yardstick moving with the practice (normalization), practice coming loose from the rule (uncoupling), or a standing pressure tilting the floor (gradient). No step decides it, and each looks reasonable against the last. The process ends somewhere no one chose, and it reads as normal from inside because the yardstick moved with it; the gap shows only when something finally measures against the origin.

## System

- Do give a drifting process one mechanism under Direction; do play the slide as undecided, reasonable step by step, and invisible from inside.
- Do not stage it as a decision or a turning point; that is reversal, not drift. Do not collapse the three mechanisms to one. Do not edit the engine's files; wire only from the play's side.
