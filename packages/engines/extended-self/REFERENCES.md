# Extended Self: Reference

## Line of Work

Extended self as **piece**: a possession experienced as a part of the self -- an ordinary object into which a persona's identity has extended, so that having it enlarges the self and losing it diminishes it. The domain does not model the persona's present self-story, who it takes itself to be, which is the identity engine's; nor the bond to a specific place, which is the place-attachment engine's; nor the mere over-valuation of what one happens to own, which is the bias engine's endowment member; nor the felt grief of a loss, which is the grief engine's. It models the object-as-self: the thing that is owned like property but felt like a limb, and the taking-in and tearing-out by which it becomes and unbecomes self. It is the ordinary private case beneath the heirloom (the possession made inalienable to a line), the totem (made sacred to a collective), and the document (made an inscribed record) -- the base phenomenon those three specialize.

The spine is the psychology of possessions and self. James's account of the material self is the origin: a person's self is the sum total of all that they can call theirs -- not only body and mind but clothes, house, and goods -- so that a loss of possessions is felt as a shrinkage of the person. Belk's "Possessions and the Extended Self" is the modern statement: we regard our possessions as parts of ourselves, we appropriate objects into the self by controlling, creating, and knowing them, and the involuntary loss of possessions is experienced as a lessening of self. Csikszentmihalyi and Rochberg-Halton's study of domestic objects shows the mechanism at home -- things become self through the psychic energy invested in them, and cherished objects store memory, relationship, and identity. McCracken adds the divestment ritual: the drawing of self-meaning back out of an object before it is let go, so the parting need not tear the self with it. The altitudes are those: the object-as-self, the possessor who holds it, and the appropriation and dispossession that carry self in and out.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **Identity**: The persona's present self-story -- who it takes itself to be, its standing sense of self -- is the identity engine's. Extended self does not model the self-concept as a whole; it models the specific piece of self that has been stored in an object, the material self rather than the narrative one.
- **Place-attachment**: The enduring bond to a specific meaningful place is the place-attachment engine's. Extended self carries the movable object held as self; a cherished place is that engine's, even though both extend the self outward into the world.
- **The specializations**: The possession made inalienable to a lineage is the heirloom engine's; the object made sacred to a collective is the totem engine's; the inscribed record made to bear witness is the document engine's. Extended self is the ordinary private case those three specialize -- the everyday possession-as-self beneath the lineage, the sacred, and the inscribed.
- **The endowment effect**: The mere over-valuation of a thing because one owns it -- the ownership premium as a judgment bias -- is the bias engine's endowment member. Extended self is the incorporation of the object into identity, not the pricing distortion; the two are related (what is self is valued past its market worth) but distinct: one is a store of self, the other a quirk of valuation.
- **The felt cost**: The acute grief of a loss, and any shame at having let a thing go, are the grief and shame engines'. Extended self names the object-as-self and the self-diminishment its loss causes; what that diminishment feels like as an emotion is delegated.

## Encoding

Source to constraint. A piece anchor (the object-as-self) with one position (who holds it as self) and two processes (its taking-in and its tearing-out).

- **[extended-self](piece_extended_self.md)** (the anchor): The object itself -- load-bearing because it stores a part of the persona's identity outside the body. Anchored by Belk (possessions as parts of self) and James (the material self).
- **[possessor](position_possessor.md)** (position): The stance of holding an object as a part of oneself rather than as bare property -- enlarged by having it, diminished by losing it, and varying in how much self is invested. Anchored by Belk (the extended self) and McCracken (the owner's investment and divestment).
- **[appropriation](process_appropriation.md)** (process): The taking of an object into the self -- through controlling, creating, knowing, and investing psychic energy in it. Anchored by Belk (the routes to extension) and Csikszentmihalyi & Rochberg-Halton (self through invested energy).
- **[dispossession](process_dispossession.md)** (process): The tearing of an object out of the self -- loss, theft, or divestment -- and the shrinkage of the self it carried. Anchored by James (loss as a lessening of the person), Belk (involuntary loss as loss of self), and McCracken (divestment).
