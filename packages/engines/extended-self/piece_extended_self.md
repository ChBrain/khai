# Piece: Extended Self

## Taxonomy

Self / a possession experienced as a part of the self (no khai file of its own).

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: extended-self

## Place

In the persona's intimate keeping -- close to the body and the daily round, held as mine and not quite fungible even where the market would call it so. Unlike the heirloom, it is not formally withheld from sale; an ordinary object can be sold or replaced. But to its owner it is set a little apart from interchangeable goods: this coat, this car, this ring, this room's contents, marked by use and history as belonging to a particular self and not readily swapped for an identical one. Its place is the zone where property shades into person -- owned like a thing, felt like a limb.

## Load Bearing

What it holds up is a piece of the persona's own identity -- a store of self kept outside the body (Belk; James's material self). It can carry memory (the object that holds a time or a person), capability (the tool through which the persona acts and is competent), status and taste (the goods that say who one is), continuity (the possessions that make a self stable across a life). Remove it and the self it held does not vanish, but it loses a support it was leaning on: the persona is, in a real and measurable sense, made smaller. To have is one way to be, and this is the having that has become a being.

## Apparent

To a stranger it reads as ordinary property, priced by its market form; to its owner it reads as self, and the two readings do not match. What shows the self-meaning is not the object's appraisal but the owner's relation to it -- the care past its worth, the refusal to part with it, the story told when it is handled, the disproportion between its price and the distress its loss would cause. The extension is invisible in the thing and visible in the having: a cheap object fiercely kept betrays more self than a costly one held lightly.

## Yearbook

The arc runs from appropriation to dispossession. An object enters the self -- taken in through mastery, making, knowing, gift, or the sheer sediment of time and use -- and for a while it is worn as part of the persona, enlarging the self that holds it. It peaks when the object is most fully self: most used, most known, most laden with what the persona has invested in it. It fails when the object is lost, stolen, broken, or given up, and the failure is a shrinkage of the self, not merely a gap in the inventory -- grieved past the cost of replacement, unless the persona has first drawn its self back out of the object in a deliberate divestment that lets the thing go without tearing the self with it.
