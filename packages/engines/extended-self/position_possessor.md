# Position: Possessor

## Taxonomy

[Extended Self](piece_extended_self.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: extended-self

## Has

The object as a part of itself, not as bare property. The possessor holds the thing the way one holds a limb -- owned, yes, but felt as self, so that its condition is the possessor's own condition and its loss a loss of self (Belk). How far a given persona extends into its things varies: some invest much of who they are in what they own and are enlarged or wounded accordingly, others hold their possessions lightly and keep the self mostly in the body. What the possessor has is not the title to the object but the self stored in it.

## Orders

Guard it as self, and act through it. The position is to keep the object, tend it, and use it as an instrument of who the persona is -- to present the self through it, act competently by it, and hold it against the ordinary pressures that would move a mere good along. What the possessor does with the object it does partly to itself: to neglect it is to neglect a part of the self, to display it is to display the self, to defend it is to defend the self.

## Loses

The freedom to treat the object as replaceable. The possessor cannot swap the thing for an identical one without losing what only this one carries, cannot sell it under need without cutting into the self, cannot see it damaged without feeling the damage as its own. The deeper the self is invested, the sharper this cost: the most cherished possession is the one whose loss would take the most self with it, and holding it makes the possessor hostage to its fate.

## Drives

On that loss:
holds tighter -- keeps the object close, guards and tends it, refuses the price that would part them, and lets the having go on being a way of being -- or, forced or choosing to let go, draws the self back out of the object first where it can, divesting the meaning before the thing, so the parting costs less self; or, torn from it without that grace, carries the diminishment of a self made smaller by the loss. Either way the position turns on how much of the self the object holds, and what it costs to keep or to surrender it.
