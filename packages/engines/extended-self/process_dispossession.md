# Process: Dispossession

## Taxonomy

[Extended Self](piece_extended_self.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: extended-self

## Initiated by

The tearing of an object out of the self. The process opens when a thing that had become self is lost, stolen, broken, seized, or given up -- involuntarily, when loss or theft or disaster takes it, or by choice, when the persona parts with it. The threshold is crossed the moment the object leaves the self's keeping: what goes is not only a possession but the piece of self the possession held (Belk).

## Direction

From self to not-self -- outward, the object pulled back across the boundary, and a part of the self going with it. Where appropriation carried self into the object, dispossession drags it out, and because the self had come to lean on the object, its leaving is felt as a subtraction from the person: the persona is made smaller, less capable, less continuous, less itself. The direction is loss run backward through the same channel that made the object self.

## Lever

The disproportion between the object's price and the self it carried. Dispossession spends what appropriation stored: the labor, memory, and identity poured into the thing are what its loss now takes, which is why the grief runs past the cost of replacement -- an identical object would restore the property but not the self, since the self was in this one and its history. The lever is that the loss is measured in self, not in money, and no market substitute pays it back.

## Echo

The [possessor](position_possessor.md) is diminished -- carrying the wound of a self made smaller, mourning the object as more than a good. Where the loss was involuntary, the diminishment is sharp and the object's absence becomes part of the persona's story. Where the parting was chosen, it costs less if the persona first drew its self back out -- a deliberate divestment, the meaning withdrawn before the thing, so the object can go without taking the self. The acute grief of a loss is the grief engine's; this process owns the self-diminishment specifically -- the shrinkage of a self that had extended into what is now gone.
