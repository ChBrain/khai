# Process: Reuse

## Taxonomy

[Reclamation](process_reclamation.md), the stance this intervention takes.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: reclamation

## Initiated by

The intervention decides the place is saved not by keeping it as a monument but by giving it a living new use.
Reuse starts where a fabric has outlived its purpose but not its worth: the factory that no longer makes, the church that no longer prays, the station that no longer runs -- kept because a new use is found to fill it.
It reaches in to re-inhabit, so the place survives by working again.

## Direction

Onto the Shown, keeping the old fabric visibly in a new function.
The mill becomes lofts, the power station a gallery, the warehouse a market -- the industrial bones left legible under the new use, the age worn openly rather than restored away or embalmed.
Reuse moves the place from disused to re-inhabited, the past kept in service rather than kept behind glass.

## Lever

The saving of the fabric against what the new use costs the place and its people.
The carrying force is that a used place endures where a monument decays or a ruin is cleared; the resistance is what the new use displaces -- for the same adaptive reuse that saves a fabric can be the vector that gentrifies it, the loft that keeps the mill and prices out the neighborhood.
Keep the reuse in service of the place and its people and it reclaims; let it serve the yield and it tips into gentrification, the fabric saved and the community lost.

## Echo

The old place living again in a new use, its past kept working.
It shows the exposed brick, the retained crane, the industrial window in the new interior, and carries beneath it the double edge -- the fabric genuinely saved, and the question of whom the saving served.
Reuse is dated from the new occupancy, and it is the stance that rides closest to gentrification: reclamation when it keeps the place for its life, displacement when it keeps the shell and sells the rest.
