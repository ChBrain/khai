# Instructions: Reclamation

How a Playwright wires the reclamation engine into a play. You wire by linking the
play's own content to the engine's files; the engine files stay untouched, they
are canon. The Roadie plumbs the engine's law into Instructions Knowledge on
deploy. Authoring guidance, not runtime content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human sets which fallen places in the world are being taken back, by whom, and what stance the intervention takes toward the age it inherits.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, a reclaimed place links under Shown the stance its intervention takes: keeping the fabric and its age as found (conservation), rebuilding it whole to an ideal that may never have existed (reconstruction), keeping it by giving it a living new use (reuse), or faking a past it never had (facsimile). Play it as the place showing what the intervention made of its age -- the honest arrest, the confident rebuild, the working reuse, or the too-perfect fake -- and keep it the deliberate intervention on the fabric, never a persona's feeling about the past. Hand the material decay it arrests to decay, the withdrawal before it to dereliction, and the displacing capital to gentrification. You link to the engine, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- The material decay the intervention arrests is decay's; the withdrawal that let the place fall is dereliction's; the capital re-entry that displaces for yield is gentrification's. Reuse is the shared seam with gentrification -- adaptive reuse can save a fabric or gentrify it, and only whom the new use serves tells them apart. Conservation keeps faith with decay's patina; facsimile betrays it. Whether the stance rings true for the place and its age is the team's review.

## Knowledge

- Reclamation is the deliberate intervention -- restoration and conservation -- that arrests a place's decay and takes the fallen place back, turning on what it makes of the age it inherits. Four stances, the poles of the oldest debate in the field. Conservation is Ruskin's: keep the fabric and its age as found, arrest without erasing, do the least. Reconstruction is Viollet-le-Duc's: rebuild to a completeness that may never have existed, the design realized over the decay, one past chosen over the rest. Reuse keeps the fabric by giving it a living new function, the place saved because it is used -- the stance nearest gentrification. Facsimile fakes a past the place never had, heritage in place of history, the reproduction mistaken for the real (Lowenthal) -- rescue as falsification. Read a place by the stance its reclamation takes.

## System

- Do give a reclaimed place its stance under Shown; do play it as the place showing what the intervention made of its age; do keep the reading the deliberate intervention on the fabric, not a feeling about the past.
- Do let each stance carry its cost honestly: conservation keeps the age and forgoes the whole; reconstruction gains the whole and edits the age; reuse saves the fabric and risks the community; facsimile satisfies and falsifies. Read facsimile as rescue that lies, closing the family's ambivalence.
- Do not model the material decay (that is decay), the withdrawal before it (that is dereliction), or the displacing capital (that is gentrification). Do not make the reclaimed place mirror a persona's relation to their own past (that is the play's pitch). Do not edit the engine's files; wire only from the play's side.
