# Reclamation: Reference

## Line of Work

Reclamation as **process**: the deliberate intervention -- the field called
restoration and conservation -- that arrests a place's decay and takes the fallen
place back, shown in what it makes of the age it inherits. (The engine is named
reclamation rather than restoration because restoration, as a stem, is the grief
engine's, and because restoration in the strict Viollet-le-Duc sense is only one
of the stances this engine holds -- here the reconstruction pole.) The domain does
not model the material decay it arrests, which is the decay engine's; nor the
social withdrawal that preceded it, which is the dereliction engine's; nor the
capital re-entry that displaces for yield rather than keeps for age, which is the
gentrification engine's. It models the intervention on the age: what the arrest
decides the years are worth.

The spine is the oldest quarrel in the field: what a place's gathered age is worth,
and whether an intervention should keep it, complete over it, or replace it. Ruskin
refused restoration outright -- the marks of the years are not ours to wipe, and to
restore is to destroy; the honest act is to conserve, to shore and shelter and do
the least. Viollet-le-Duc held the opposite -- to restore is to reinstate the place
in a completeness that may never have existed, realizing the design over the
accidents of decay. Riegl set the values in conflict: age-value, historical-value,
and newness-value, each pulling the intervention a different way. And Lowenthal
named the modern danger -- heritage in place of history, the past manufactured and
staged for the present, the facsimile that reads as old and is not. So the engine
gives the intervention four stances: conservation, reconstruction, reuse, and the
facsimile that closes the family's ambivalence -- rescue as falsification.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The material decay it arrests (decay)**: Reclamation is the intervention; the
  rust, rot, and crumble it reaches in to stop are the decay engine's. Decay is
  what the fabric does; reclamation is what a hand does about it. The two meet at
  patina, which conservation keeps and reconstruction and facsimile wipe.
- **The withdrawal that preceded it (dereliction)**: The neglect, abandonment, and
  disinvestment that let the place fall are the dereliction engine's; reclamation is
  the reversal of that withdrawal, the care resumed. Dereliction is the keeping that
  stopped; reclamation is the keeping taken up again -- and where dereliction's ruin
  is kept as excess rather than cleared, that keeping is reclamation's conservation
  stance.
- **The capital re-entry that displaces (gentrification)**: Both reclamation and
  gentrification re-enter a fallen place and repair it; they part on what they
  serve. Reclamation keeps the place for its age; gentrification withholds it for
  its yield. The reuse stance is where they ride closest -- adaptive reuse can save a
  fabric or gentrify it, and only whom the new use serves tells them apart.
- **A persona's feeling about the past (the persona's engines)**: The nostalgia,
  the reverence, the unease a restored or faked place stirs in a person are the
  persona's own engines' (nostalgia, and its kin). Reclamation delivers the
  intervention on the fabric, not the feeling it provokes. And a restored place made
  to **stand for** a character's relation to their own past is refused: that is
  authored key, the play's pitch, not the place's force.
- **The stance itself is not engine content**: The engine declares the four stances
  exist; which one a given place's reclamation takes lives on the place, linked under
  its Shown chapter. The engine refuses to fix a place's reclamation for it.

  Viollet-le-Duc; wipes the patina for the newness.

  the place saved because it is used. The stance nearest gentrification.
- **[facsimile](process_facsimile.md)**: the intervention that fakes a past it never
  had, heritage in place of history, rescue as falsification. Anchored by Lowenthal;
  closes the family's ambivalence.
