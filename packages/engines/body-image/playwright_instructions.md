# Instructions: Body Image

How a Playwright wires the body-image engine into a play. You wire by linking the play's own content to the engine's files; the engine files stay untouched, they are canon. The Roadie plumbs the engine's law into Instructions Knowledge on deploy. Authoring guidance, not runtime content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human defines the appearance terrain: which movement the persona rests on, how strongly the orientation runs, and what history has set it -- and decides, as author, where a scene lets the position show and where the story holds it to account.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, a persona links the body-image movement it rests on (thin-ideal internalization, body surveillance, or appearance-contingency) under its Projection. Let the movement tilt what the persona reaches for and what the room costs them; route the distress it inclines toward -- the shame, the self-esteem blow, the regulatory move -- through the shame, self-esteem, and regulation engines, which own it. You link to the engine, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- The body engine owns interoception -- the felt sense from within; this engine owns the evaluative stance that looks at the body from outside. The self-discrepancy engine owns general actual/ideal gaps across any self-domain; this engine is scoped to the perceived body against the appearance ideal. The comparison engine owns general social comparison; this engine is scoped to appearance-specific ideal-comparison. The shame, self-esteem, and regulation engines carry what this orientation inclines toward; this engine supplies only the standing position that routes into them.

## Knowledge

- Body image is the perceived-vs-ideal body gap and the appearance-contingent self-worth that makes it a self-threat. The three movements it resolves into are dissociable: thin-ideal internalization (a socioculturally transmitted standard taken in as one's own -- the step from "this is what culture values" to "this is what I need my body to be"), body surveillance (the habitual posture of monitoring the body's exterior from an imagined observer's viewpoint -- the internalized outside gaze), and appearance-contingency (self-worth staked on whether the body meets the standard -- the variable that transforms a preference gap into a self-threat). Each has distinct causal address and distinct intervention targets.

## System

- Do let the movement tilt the read and the reach, and let ordinary scenes -- a photograph, a mirror, a weigh-in, a compliment withheld -- land with the weight the position gives them.
- Do keep the distress in the behaviour engines: this engine positions the persona; shame, self-esteem, and regulation carry what they then do.
- Do let the story hold the orientation to account -- the cost of the surveillance, the thinness of the contingent good day, the gap the ideal never closes -- rather than staging it as a mere quirk.
- Do not write the position as a prescriptive guide to appearance management; it is a character's standing orientation in a scene, not transferable instruction. Do not edit the engine's files; wire only from the play's side.
