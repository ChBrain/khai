# Position: Body Image

## Taxonomy

Self-concept / body-related self-evaluation (no khai file of its own yet).

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: body-image

## Has

A standing orientation toward the body as an object to be judged against an ideal.
The persona arrives carrying a perceived body -- the image held of their own size, shape, and appearance -- and a standard that image is held against: a gap between the two that may be narrow or wide, and a degree of self-worth staked on closing it.
This is not sensation from within the body but the evaluative stance that looks at the body from outside and measures what it sees.

## Orders

Close the gap between the perceived body and the ideal.
The orientation turns the persona toward the mirror, toward others' evaluating eyes, toward comparisons that measure -- and reads every social scene for what it returns to the body's standing against the standard.
The weight of that reading depends on how much the self is riding on the result.

## Loses

The option of inhabiting the body without evaluating it.
The persona cannot move through a scene in which the body is visible -- to others or to themselves -- without the orientation activating; the question of how the body measures is always already running.
When self-worth rides on the answer, the loss is sharper: an ordinary moment of being seen becomes a test the self may fail.

## Drives

Toward closing the perceived-vs-ideal gap -- and, when self-worth is staked on the gap, toward protecting the self from the verdict.
Which shape the drive takes depends on how the gap is held: through the standard that has been taken in as one's own ([thin-ideal internalization](position_thin_ideal_internalization.md)), through the habitual posture of watching the body from outside ([body surveillance](position_body_surveillance.md)), or through the self-worth that is staked on whether the body meets the standard ([appearance-contingency](position_appearance_contingency.md)).
The disposition inclines the persona toward appearance-related distress; the distress itself -- the shame, the restriction, the avoidance -- is carried by the shame, self-esteem, and regulation engines, not asserted here.
