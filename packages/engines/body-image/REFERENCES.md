# Body Image: Reference

## Line of Work

Body image as **position**: the gap between the body a persona perceives and the ideal they hold it against, amplified when self-worth is staked on closing that gap. The domain models a _standing orientation toward the body as an object of evaluation_ -- the three shapes that orientation takes (the internalized ideal, the habitual surveillance, the contingent self-worth) -- not the acts the orientation inclines toward: the shame, the restriction, the avoidance are owned by the shame, self-esteem, and regulation engines, and this engine routes to them rather than performing the distress itself.

The spine is the tripartite influence model (Thompson et al.) and its empirical derivatives: the dual-pathway model (Stice) showing that thin-ideal internalization and body dissatisfaction predict disordered eating via distinct routes; objectification theory (Fredrickson & Roberts) showing that body surveillance -- the internalized observer's gaze -- is the proximate mechanism between cultural objectification and its psychological consequences; and Cash's contingencies-of-self-worth framework showing that staking self-worth on appearance is what makes the perceived-vs-ideal gap a self-threat rather than a preference gap. Each of the three movements has been manipulated or measured independently, blocking or leaving the others unaffected; they are dissociable by the science as well as by the theory.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **Interoception / bodily sensation**: The engine models the evaluative stance that looks at the body from outside, not the felt sense of the body from within. The sensory and interoceptive experience of inhabiting a body is owned by the `body` engine, which explicitly delegates appearance evaluation away; this engine owns what `body` delegates.
- **General social comparison**: The engine models appearance-specific ideal-comparison, not the general evaluation of the self against others. The general process of measuring one's outcomes, abilities, or opinions against a reference other is owned by the `comparison` engine; this engine is scoped to the body-specific ideal gap.
- **General actual/ideal self-discrepancy**: The engine models the appearance-domain gap between the perceived body and the ideal body. The general structure of actual-vs-ideal and actual-vs-ought self-guide gaps across any self-domain is owned by the `self-discrepancy` engine (Higgins); this engine is scoped to the perceived body as the object and the appearance ideal as the standard.
- **Shame**: The engine models the position that makes body shame structurally likely, not the shame response itself. The evaluative emotion that collapses self-worth on exposure to a perceived failure is owned by the `shame` engine; this engine supplies the orientation that routes into it.
- **Self-esteem**: The engine models appearance-contingent self-worth as a standing stake, not the self-evaluative state that fluctuates with outcomes. The overall positive or negative evaluation of the self and its moment-to-moment movement is owned by the `self-esteem` engine; this engine governs the domain-specific contingency that makes appearance outcomes drive that movement.
- **Regulation**: The engine models the position that inclines toward appearance-focused behavioral regulation (restriction, avoidance, checking), not the regulatory moves themselves. The effortful management of emotion and behavior in response to a stressor is owned by the `regulation` engine; this engine supplies the orientation that makes the body an appearance stressor.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per file.

- **[body image](position_body_image.md)** (the root): The perceived-vs-ideal body gap and the appearance-contingent self-worth that makes it a self-threat -- the shared evaluative orientation beneath three movements. Anchored by Thompson et al. (the tripartite model) and Cash (contingencies of self-worth).
- **[thin-ideal internalization](position_thin_ideal_internalization.md)** (movement): A socioculturally transmitted size-and-shape standard taken in as personally binding; the internalized ideal that makes the gap motivationally active. Anchored by Stice (dual-pathway model and dissonance paradigm) and Thompson et al.
- **[body surveillance](position_body_surveillance.md)** (movement): The habitual posture of monitoring the body's exterior from an imagined observer's viewpoint; the attentional mechanism between cultural objectification and its psychological consequences. Anchored by Fredrickson & Roberts (objectification theory).
- **[appearance-contingency](position_appearance_contingency.md)** (movement): Self-worth staked on whether the body meets the appearance standard; the variable that transforms the perceived-vs-ideal gap from a preference gap into a self-threat. Anchored by Cash (contingencies-of-self-worth framework).
