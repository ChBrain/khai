# Photograph: Reference

## Line of Work

Photograph as **piece**: an image that certifies a moment occurred and cannot say what it was.

The engine's subject is an object with an unusual power and an unusual silence. Barthes supplies the
power: a photograph's whole content, before anything in the frame is considered, is _that-has-been_ --
it is a certificate of presence, and no other object issues one. Sontag supplies the cost: to
photograph is to appropriate the thing photographed, and certifying an experience is also a way of
refusing it. Between those two claims sits everything this engine reads.

The boundary with what khai already carries is clean. The possession into which an identity has
extended, so that losing it diminishes the person, is **extended-self**'s -- a photograph frequently
becomes one and the mechanism is not this engine's. The object deliberately withheld from exchange to
carry a lineage's authority is **heirloom**'s. The record an institution compiles and keeps about a
person is **document**'s. How memory is stored, altered by later questioning, and reconstructed at
recall is **memory**'s; this engine reads only what happens when the remembering is handed to a
device. And the longing for a past that the image provokes is **nostalgia**'s.

**The pictured** persona holds a likeness that is in somebody else's keeping. They must manage a face
continuously, because the shutter takes a hundredth of a second out of an hour and any hundredth may
be the one that is kept, and they cannot recall the image, write its caption, or stop a version they
would not have chosen from becoming the standard one. Objecting to a photograph is a larger act than
taking one, so the burden of the exchange sits with whoever wants it not to happen.

**The taking** is where the engine is seen for one thing. The camera does not merely add to the
occasion; it takes over the job of registering it, and the mind lets it. What has been photographed is
treated as saved, and the effort that would have gone into holding it is not spent. The important
qualification is in the mechanism: the impairment comes from offloading rather than from photography,
which is why photographing with attention does not produce it and photographing to capture does.

**The showing** puts a fragment with the edges removed in front of somebody who was not there, and a
photograph carries no account of itself. The caption is supplied by whoever holds the picture, which
is why the same image serves a celebration and an accusation without being altered, and why the viewer
receives a claim as though it were a property of the image. Bourdieu's observation about the family
album belongs here: the pictures are taken at the occasions where the group most needs to affirm that
it is a group, so the album is an argument rather than a record.

**The outliving** is the arc's end and its point. A photograph becomes more precious every year for
the same reason it becomes less legible, since the distance that makes it worth keeping is the
distance that removes the people who could identify it. What survives is the certification and not the
account: the picture proves somebody existed and cannot say who.

Hence the **twist**, at the root. **A photograph is taken to keep a moment and is the reason the
moment is not kept.** The persona raises the camera instead of attending, hands the remembering to the
device, and afterwards has an image where the memory would have been -- and the image is what they now
recall, in its frame, from its angle, with everything outside the edge gone. The photograph is not
false. It is the only version left, and it was made by a machine pointed at part of the room.

The engine is built so that nobody has to be vain or shallow for any of it to run, and a play should
keep it that way. The taker wanted the picture, which is a good reason and the only one most people
have. The picture is worth having. The moment goes anyway.

**On the evidence, stated plainly.** This engine pairs two celebrated essays with one small
experimental literature, and the two do not carry the same weight. **Barthes and Sontag are readings,
not findings.** _Camera Lucida_ is a grief-written meditation and _On Photography_ is polemical; both
are enormously influential, neither is testable, and the engine takes their structural claims -- the
certificate of presence, and the appropriation -- while declining the rest, including Sontag's stronger
line that the camera is predatory by nature. **The photo-taking impairment effect is real and is
qualified in the opposite direction, and the engine states both.** Henkel's museum studies found that
photographing objects reduced later recall of them; Barasch and colleagues then found that volitional
photo-taking, where the person chooses the shot and engages with the scene, _improves_ visual memory
for it. Taken together the mechanism is attentional offloading rather than the camera, and the popular
version of this finding -- that taking pictures damages memory -- is not what the literature says. The
engine's taking member is written to the mechanism and not to the headline. **The effect sizes are
modest and the samples are small**, in the way of most laboratory memory work of that period. **The
family-album evidence is dated.** Bourdieu's survey work is 1960s France, when film had a per-frame
cost and photographs were taken at a small number of ritual occasions; the structural claim about
group affirmation travels, and the specific findings do not. **The digital shift is the engine's
largest unknown.** Volume, automatic capture, the fact that most photographs are now never looked at
again, and the collapse of the interval between taking and showing are changes of a size the
literature has not caught up with, and this engine's arc -- taken, viewed for a year, filed, inherited
-- is written for an era whose successor is not yet described. What should not be taken from this
engine is that photographing harms memory, that photographs are inauthentic, or that any of this is
new. The claim is narrow: the image certifies and cannot explain, the caption belongs to the holder,
the delegating costs the moment, and what outlives everybody is a face without a name.

is Barthes' and Sontag's; the mechanism behind the taking member is Henkel's and Barasch's.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The possession that is part of the self (extended-self)**: An object into which a persona's
  identity has extended, and the appropriation and dispossession that run on it, are the extended-self
  engine's. A photograph frequently becomes one, and the mechanism is not read again here.
- **The object withheld from exchange for a lineage (heirloom)**: The thing kept out of the market to
  carry a line's authority across generations is the heirloom engine's. A family photograph can be one;
  the engine reads the image, not the inalienability.
- **How memory works (memory, the-unconscious)**: Encoding, alteration by later questioning, and
  reconstruction at recall are the memory engine's. This engine reads one narrow thing -- what happens
  when the remembering is delegated to a device -- and hands the rest across.
- **The record an institution keeps (document, legibility, measure)**: The file, the state's readable
  categories, and the figure are those engines'. A passport photograph sits in both, and this engine
  reads only what the image does and cannot do.
- **Longing for the past (nostalgia, bittersweet)**: The ache a picture provokes is the nostalgia
  engine's and the bittersweet composite's. The photograph engine reads the object that provokes it.
- **The series and its gap (collection)**: A set of objects bounded by a criterion only its keeper can
  state is the collection engine's. An album is often one, and the two are wired separately.
- **The managed public self (presentation, face, self-monitoring)**: Impression management and
  facework are those engines'. The pictured position reads the specific difficulty of managing a face
  against a shutter, and delegates the rest.
- **The image as art or as craft (the world)**: Composition, technique, the history of the medium, and
  the market in prints are matters of practice and trade. They set what a photograph can look like and
  are not the operation.
- **The picture as authored symbol (the play's pitch)**: An image made to **stand for** a theme -- the
  photograph left face-down that mirrors a motif of refusal -- is refused: that is authored key, the
  play's pitch. The photograph sets what the taking cost and certified; the meaning read into it is the
  play's.

## Encoding

Source to constraint. A piece with a subject and three fates, hanging flat beneath the object.

- **[photograph](piece_photograph.md)** (the anchor): an image that certifies a moment occurred and
  cannot say what it was, read on what is outside the frame, carrying the twist -- a photograph is
  taken to keep a moment and is the reason the moment is not kept. Anchored by Barthes (1980) and
  Sontag (1977).
- **[the pictured](position_pictured.md)**: who is in it, holding a likeness in somebody else's
  keeping, foreclosed from recalling the image, captioning it, or ageing privately. Anchored by Sontag
  (1977) and Berger (1972).
- **[the taking](process_the_taking.md)**: the remembering handed to a device, where the engine is seen
  for one thing. Anchored by Henkel (2014) and Barasch et al. (2017).
- **[the showing](process_the_showing.md)**: the fragment given a meaning by whoever holds it.
  Anchored by Berger (1972) and Bourdieu (1965).
- **[the outliving](process_the_outliving.md)**: the certification surviving the account, keeping the
  face and losing the name. Anchored by Hirsch (1997) and Barthes (1980).
