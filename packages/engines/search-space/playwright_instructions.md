# Instructions: Search Space

How a Playwright wires the search-space engine into a play. You wire by linking the play's own content to the engine's files; the engine files stay untouched, they are canon. The Roadie plumbs the engine's law into Instructions Knowledge on deploy. Authoring guidance, not runtime content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human defines the bounds of the search space: what the goal is, what resources are available (time steps, memory), and which structural complexity class (P, NP, NP-complete, NP-hard, PPAD) governs the scene.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, a persona or scene links the complexity class or problem piece it encounters under its Projection or Place. The agent must inhabit the computational limits, showing the tension between checking a candidate and producing it, or searching a space that has no quick path.

## Collaboration

- Computational intractability interacts with other engines. The decision styles a persona uses to search are governed by the decision engine. The emotional toll of searching in vain belongs to the mood or stress engines. The persona's knowledge of the map belongs to the belief or memory engines.

## Knowledge

- The search space is a landscape of structural refusal: P is cheap to solve. NP is cheap to verify but expensive to solve. NP-complete forms a gallery where unlocking one door unlocks all. NP-hard is at least as difficult as NP-complete but lacks easy verification. PPAD guarantees a solution exists, but offers no efficient path to reach it.

## System

- Do let the intractability be structural; the environment itself refuses to release the answer.
- Do not let a persona solve a hard problem (NP-complete, NP-hard, PPAD) instantly by magic; it must cost resources or require a reduction.
- Do not bypass the limits of the search space; they are physical laws, not secrets.
- Do not edit the engine's files; wire only from the play's side.
