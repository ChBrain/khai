# Dissociation: Reference

## Line of Work

Dissociation as **process**: a failure of integration, not an act of avoidance. The domain owns what happens when an experience can neither be absorbed nor escaped and the mind stops assembling it into one continuous person -- and it owns the three depths that failure reaches, which are depths and not stages: a persona may run the shallowest for a lifetime and never approach the others. What runs deeper is not a worse episode but a longer history.

The spine is two accounts that fit together and a scale that maps their range. Putnam's discrete behavioural states supply the mechanism for the middle depth: experience is laid down within states and access runs along the state rather than along the timeline, so material is unreachable rather than degraded -- which is why time goes missing while memory does not go vague. Van der Hart and colleagues describe what the same division becomes when the pressure is developmental and unrelenting: a part organised to carry on with daily life and one or more fixed in the original defensive response, not one person with gaps but parts that do not share a first person. Carlson and Putnam's revision of the Dissociative Experiences Scale is what establishes that these sit on one dimension rather than being separate conditions, and that its shallow end is ordinary.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The unconscious**: The engine models a failure of integration, in which nothing is pushed anywhere and the pieces simply do not arrive together. Material kept from awareness by repression -- one unified mind holding something down -- is owned by the `the-unconscious` engine; the mechanisms are opposites and this engine owns only the failure.
- **PTSD**: The engine models the disconnection itself. The emotional numbing, estrangement and intrusive re-experiencing that follow a trauma are owned by the `ptsd` engine, which this engine routes into rather than restating.
- **Defense**: The engine models one mechanism at three depths, not a taxonomy. The category of defensive operations and their ordering by maturity is owned by the `defense` engine; dissociation appears there as a member of a class and here as a mechanism in its own right.
- **Absorption**: The engine models the failure of the pieces to arrive together. Attention narrowed onto something engrossing -- the lost hour in a book, which sits at the ordinary end of the same scale -- is owned by the `absorption` composite; this engine begins where the narrowing stops being chosen.
- **Memory**: The engine models state-bound unavailability. Ordinary encoding, storage, retrieval and decay are owned by the `memory` engine; nothing here is forgotten in that sense.
- **Betrayal trauma**: Freyd's account of why a child cannot both know and stay attached is the obvious fourth anchor for this root, and it is already carried by the `betrayal` engine. Citing it here would restate a spine another engine owns, so the root states the closing of both exits in its own terms and delegates the betrayal case.
- **Diagnosis**: The engine models a persona's disconnection for dramatic production. It is authored character architecture, never a clinical assessment, screening instrument, or diagnosis of a living person, and it carries no criteria, thresholds, or duration rules.
