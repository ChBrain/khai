# Moral Disengagement: Reference

## Line of Work

Moral disengagement as **process**: the cognitive moves by which a persona selectively switches off its own moral self-sanctions to do harm without self-censure -- and still regard itself as decent. The domain does not model the moral lens by which a persona judges another's conduct, which is moral-judgment's; nor the self-sanction that fires when a harm is owned rather than exempted, which is guilt's; nor the harmful act itself, which is aggression's; nor the misleading of another for gain, which is deception's; nor the standing cold devaluation of another, which is contempt's. It models the switching-off itself: the deactivation of moral self-regulation for a specific act, and the four loci -- act, agency, harm, victim -- at which it is done.

The spine is Bandura's social-cognitive account of moral agency. People regulate their conduct by moral self-sanctions: the anticipated self-condemnation that deters acts they hold to be wrong. This self-regulation can be selectively disengaged, act by act, so that a person does harm while keeping a favorable self-regard -- the standards intact for others and for other deeds, suspended just for this one. Bandura locates eight mechanisms at four points in the self-regulatory process: reconstruing the conduct (moral justification, euphemistic labeling, advantageous comparison); obscuring or displacing agency (displacement and diffusion of responsibility); distorting the consequences (disregarding, minimizing, or misconstruing the harm); and devaluing the recipient (dehumanization and attribution of blame). The four forms are those loci: justification, displacement, minimizing, blaming.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **Moral judgment**: The engine models the switching-off of one's own conscience for one's own act, not the moral lens by which another's conduct is judged. Which moral foundation a persona reads a transgression through -- care, fairness, loyalty, and the rest -- is owned by the moral-judgment engine; moral disengagement takes the standards that engine supplies and selectively exempts the persona's own deed from them, and owns the exempting, not the judging.
- **Guilt**: The engine models the deactivation of the self-sanction, not the sanction when it fires. The transgressor's anguish at an owned wrong is owned by the guilt engine; moral disengagement is precisely what forestalls that guilt -- the successful disengagement is the one that keeps the guilt from arriving -- so it owns the switching-off, and guilt owns the self-censure it switches off.
- **Aggression and Deception**: The engine models what licenses the harm, not the harm itself nor the misleading of another. The drive to injure and the act of injuring are owned by the aggression engine; the strategic misleading of another for gain is owned by the deception engine; moral disengagement is the self-directed exoneration that clears the way -- it misleads the self rather than the other, and permits the aggression rather than performing it.
- **Contempt and Intergroup**: The engine models the devaluing of a victim in service of exempting a harm, not the standing sentiment nor the group relation. The cold down-look that holds another beneath as a lasting attitude is owned by the contempt engine; the between-group enmity and dehumanization are held by the intergroup composite; blaming borrows the devaluation but bends it to a specific purpose -- placing this victim beyond the reach of the persona's morals so this harm can be done.
