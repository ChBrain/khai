# Process: Moral Disengagement

## Taxonomy

Morality / the social psychology of self-exemption from one's own conscience.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: moral-disengagement

## Initiated by

A persona is about to do, or has done, something its own morals would ordinarily condemn -- and rather than refrain or feel the guilt, it switches off the self-sanction that would have stopped it.
This is moral disengagement: the set of cognitive moves by which a persona exempts a specific act from its own conscience, so it can do harm and still regard itself as decent.
The morals are not abandoned wholesale; they are selectively suspended, just here, just for this.

## Direction

Toward a harmful act performed without the self-censure that should attend it.
Moral disengagement reroutes the persona's moral self-regulation around a particular deed: the standards stay in place for other acts and other people, but for this one the anticipated self-reproach is deactivated, so the act proceeds and the self-regard survives it.
The direction is a carve-out, not a conversion -- the persona keeps its sense of itself as good precisely by exempting the bad thing from the reckoning that would threaten it.

## Lever

The selective deactivation of moral self-sanctions, spent to permit the act.
What moral disengagement spends is the persona's own moral self-regulation: ordinarily, the foreseen self-condemnation deters a harmful act, and disengagement disables that deterrent at one of four points -- the act, the agency, the harm, or the victim -- so the sanction never fires and the conduct is freed.
The lever is the switch, not the standard: the persona need not stop believing the act would be wrong for someone else; it need only, for itself and this once, arrange not to feel it.

## Echo

The harm is done and the self is spared, conscience routed around the deed.
Moral disengagement is how ordinary, otherwise-decent people do cruel things and live with themselves -- the euphemism over the atrocity, the order that excuses the hand, the harm unseen, the victim who "had it coming" -- the mechanism behind harm-doing that no monstrousness is required to explain.
It runs as [justification](process_justification.md), [displacement](process_displacement.md), [minimizing](process_minimizing.md), or [blaming](process_blaming.md) -- the act relabeled, the agency obscured, the harm distorted, the victim devalued. The moral lens by which a persona judges another's conduct is the moral-judgment engine's; the self-sanction that fires when the harm is owned rather than exempted is the guilt engine's; the harmful act itself is the aggression engine's; the misleading of another is the deception engine's, where this misleads the self. This engine owns the selective switching-off of one's own conscience itself.
