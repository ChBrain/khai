# Instructions: Politics

How a Playwright wires the politics engine into a play. You wire by linking the play's own
content to the engine's files; the files stay untouched, they are canon. Authoring guidance,
not runtime content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human sets which bodies in the play decide things, what rule each decides by, and who wants an outcome they cannot simply take.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, read each persona who wants something a body must decide, and link under Projection what is running: the face the contest sits at -- the open contest where the question is tabled and the numbers decide, the agenda where the question is kept from the table, or the settled premise where nobody has a way to want it raised; the levers their footing permits -- agenda-setting, dimension-splitting, scope-shifting, coalition-building, logrolling, stalling, the rule contest; and the footing itself -- the veto point, the broker seat, the bloc, the mandate, or the outsider. Give the body a rule and let the rule be contestable, since a body whose procedure is fixed and neutral has almost no politics in it. You link to the engine, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- Changing somebody's mind is the persuasion engine's and the swaying composite's, and this engine is the complement: a persona who won by changing minds ran swaying, and one who changed no minds and won ran this. What a persona can move others with -- reward, coercion, legitimacy, reference, expertise, information -- is the power engine's six bases, and politics takes them as given, so the bases are the resource and the levers are the play. Staying, leaving, and pressing from inside are the loyalty engine's, which carries Hirschman's exit and voice whole; this engine begins after a persona has decided to press rather than leave. A bargain between two parties is negotiation's and dealing's, and politics needs a body of more than two with a rule for deciding among them. Rank in the deference order is hierarchy's, status's, and standing's, and a persona may be low in rank and strong in footing or the reverse. Being made compliant is obedience's, discipline's, and subjection's -- politics reads a persona acting on a body, subjection reads a body acting on a persona. Who pays to keep a shared thing is commons's, altruistic-punishment's, and upkeep's. Whether it is safe to speak at all is airing's. Who belongs is membership's and intergroup's, and a bloc is not an identity: it is assembled for a question and dissolves when the question is settled.

## Knowledge

- Politics is a persona read while they try to get an outcome out of a body that decides collectively, where the rule for deciding is itself in play. The founding fact is Arrow's: no rule for turning individual rankings into a collective one satisfies a short list of obviously reasonable conditions at once, so there is no neutral way to add preferences up and the rule is always a party to the outcome. McKelvey sharpened it -- where a question has more than one dimension, an agenda-setter free to order the votes can walk a body to almost any outcome by a sequence of pairwise choices each of which the body genuinely prefers. The engine runs at three altitudes. The faces are where a contest sits: the open contest, visible and decided by numbers on a tabled question, leaving a record; the agenda, the power of the non-decision, which leaves no trace by construction and cannot be proved even by those it defeats; and the settled premise, where a question is not raised because nobody has a way to want it raised, and where everyone in the body would honestly say nothing is at issue. The levers are Riker's heresthetics, a craft distinct from rhetoric: agenda-setting exploits order, which a body will accept without comment because somebody has to decide it; dimension-splitting introduces a cross-cutting question that divides the winning coalition without changing a single mind; scope-shifting changes who is watching, outward for the loser and inward for the winner, since every fight has an audience and the audience decides most fights; coalition-building aims at the minimum winning coalition rather than the largest, because spoils divide among members and every surplus partner was paid for nothing; logrolling trades across questions where intensities differ and the counting rule is blind to intensity; stalling spends the clock using the body's own safeguards, so a decision not taken by a date is a decision taken; and the rule contest fights over procedure, which must be argued in general terms and applied in particular ones, and whose winner keeps winning long after they stop paying attention. The footings decide which levers bite: the veto point can stop everything and start nothing; the broker seat is worth exactly the width of the gap it sits in; the bloc can deliver others and is commanded by them in return; the mandate speaks for people not present and cannot afford to be personally convinced; and the outsider is the absence of all four, carrying scope-shifting alone. The twist is what a complete victory leaves behind: a win at the open contest leaves a record of a fight, a win at the agenda leaves an absence, and a win at the settled premise leaves nothing at all -- so the more completely a persona has won, the less there is to see, and a body's quiet reads as consent to everybody in it, including to whoever arranged it.

## System

- Do give every deciding body a rule and let the rule be contestable, since the rule contest is a lever and a body with a fixed neutral procedure has almost nothing here to run.
- Do keep the levers situational: a persona who wins by changing minds has run swaying, and the characteristic move of this engine leaves every preference in the room exactly as it was.
- Do put more than one dimension in play where a persona is to win from behind, because a single-dimension body mostly gets what the median wants and dimension-splitting has nothing to split.
- Do read a quiet body carefully: quiet is the signature of the settled premise and also of ordinary agreement, and the engine does not tell them apart -- so a play that treats every silence as management is making a claim the engine does not.
- Do let the agenda-setter be unaware they hold the strongest lever, since order looks administrative and somebody has to decide it.
- Do give the outsider the full force of scope-shifting, and let them be told they went outside the proper channels, which is accurate and is the point.
- Do not stage the third face as a diagnosis a character can hand down: naming somebody's wants as not their own requires a standard of real interests, and whoever supplies it is doing something rather like the thing being objected to.
- Do not model persuasion (that is swaying), the bases of power (that is the power engine), exit and voice (that is loyalty, which owns Hirschman whole), a two-party bargain (that is negotiation and dealing), rank (that is hierarchy, status, and standing), being made compliant (that is subjection), who pays to keep a shared thing (that is upkeep), whether it is safe to speak (that is airing), or who belongs (that is membership and intergroup). Do not make a committee fight stand for a theme (that is the play's pitch). Do not edit the engine's files; wire only from the play's side.
