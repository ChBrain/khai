# Process: Politics

## Taxonomy

How a persona gets an outcome out of a body that decides collectively, where the rule for deciding is itself in play.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: politics

## Initiated by

A persona wants something, and it is not theirs to decide. There is a body -- a committee, a household, a board, a village, a parliament -- and a rule by which that body settles things. The rule was made by somebody, for reasons, and it can be moved.

## Direction

Toward an outcome, by a route that is not persuasion. The characteristic political move leaves everybody's preferences exactly as they were and changes the situation so that those same preferences produce a different result: what is voted on, in what order, against what alternative, in front of whom, under which rule, and with the clock at what hour. A persona who changed somebody's mind has done something else; a persona who changed nobody's mind and won has done this.

The engine reads three altitudes. The **faces** are where a contest actually sits: [the open contest](position_first_face.md), decided by numbers on a question already tabled; [the agenda](position_second_face.md), decided by what reaches the table; and [the settled premise](position_third_face.md), where a question is not raised because nobody has a way to want it raised. The **levers** are what a persona does: [agenda-setting](process_agenda_setting.md), [dimension-splitting](process_dimension_splitting.md), [scope-shifting](process_scope_shifting.md), [coalition-building](process_coalition_building.md), [logrolling](process_logrolling.md), [stalling](process_stalling.md), and [the rule contest](process_rule_contest.md). The **footings** are what makes a lever bite: [the veto point](position_veto_point.md), [the broker seat](position_broker_seat.md), [the bloc](position_bloc_hold.md), [the mandate](position_mandate_claim.md), and [the outsider](position_outsider_stand.md).

## Lever

There is no neutral way to add preferences up. Arrow's result is that no rule for turning individual rankings into a collective one satisfies a short list of obviously reasonable conditions at once, and McKelvey's is sharper still: where a question has more than one dimension, an agenda-setter free to order the votes can walk a body to almost any outcome by a sequence of pairwise choices each of which the body genuinely prefers. So the rule is never a neutral instrument for finding what the body wants. It is a party to the result, and every participant who understands this has an interest in the rule as well as in the question.

That is what makes politics a domain rather than a manner. A body with a fixed, neutral, uncontestable procedure and a single dimension of disagreement has very little politics in it and will mostly get what the median wants. Every real body has a procedure somebody wrote, several dimensions in play, and a clock -- and all three are available to be worked.

## Echo

An outcome, reached by a route that will be described afterwards as what the body wanted. The engine's twist is what this leaves behind: the more completely a persona has won, the less there is to see. A victory at the open contest leaves a record of a fight. A victory at the agenda leaves an absence -- something that never came up. A victory at the settled premise leaves nothing at all, because the question was never a question, and the body's quiet reads as consent to everybody in it, including to the persona who arranged it. The best-governed-looking body is not reliably the one with the least politics in it; it may be the one where the important things were settled before anybody arrived.
