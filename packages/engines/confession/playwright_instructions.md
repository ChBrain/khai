# Instructions: Confession

How a Playwright wires the confession engine into a play. You wire by linking the play's own content to the engine's files; the engine files stay untouched, they are canon. The Roadie plumbs the engine's law into Instructions Knowledge on deploy. Authoring guidance, not runtime content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human decides who required the telling before writing any scene of it, and keeps that decision off the page, because the engine is read on the requirement and the requirement is what nobody in the scene can see. Give both sides -- the listener is a position in the engine, not a bystander to it. And keep the listener kind, since the arrangement is at its most interesting when nobody is behaving badly and it works anyway.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, a persona links under Projection where they stand in the telling: whether they are the one accounting or the one receiving, what the account was required by, which movement is running, and what has been produced. Read the engine on who required it, never on whether it felt good, because it nearly always feels good. Keep it the telling under authority: the voluntary opening that deepens a bond is self-disclosure's, the withheld fact is secret's, the account after a wrong is repair's. You link to the engine, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- The voluntary layer-by-layer opening is the self-disclosure engine's; the withheld fact and its keeping are secret's; the account rendered after a wrong is repair's and the felt debt is guilt's; the releasing of it by the wronged party is forgiveness's; surveillance, the norm, and the examination are discipline's; the standing a procedure confers is heeding's and recognition's; what the record becomes once it is a number is measure's. Police procedure and legal admissibility are matters of law and practice and are not modelled here. Whether a production's telling was required or offered, and whether a detail was recalled or supplied, are the team's review.

## Knowledge

- Confession is an account of a self required rather than offered: telling under a relation of authority, where the one who listens has standing the one who speaks does not. It is not self-disclosure's voluntary opening, not secret's withheld fact, and not repair's account after a wrong. It wires on a persona at Projection and runs in four movements. The demand is the movement nobody remembers: the obligation is a standing expectation that never has to be invoked, because a persona who knows they will be asked prepares and experiences the preparing as conscientiousness; the question works by being answerable rather than enforceable, since declining is available and is not free; and the silence read closes the exit, because a pause in this relation is data whether or not the persona put any in it. The composing is the engine's central claim: nobody has an account ready, so the telling assembles one -- filled in to the detail the listener presses for and fitted to the categories the listener has available -- and the version then replaces the material it was made from, which is genuinely clarifying and is also somebody else's work on the persona's life. The listener is never merely a hearer: the asymmetry is the arrangement's design rather than a failure of intimacy, since a confessor who confessed back would have destroyed the thing the speaker came for, and it is also its whole cost; the absolving is what the persona was waiting for from the first sentence, and release requires an authority; the record is the confession's only permanent form, in the listener's categories, and the persona does not hold a copy. The relief is the movement the engine turns on: the unburdening is real and arrives whether or not anything has changed, the transformation is the operation's actual product -- not information transferred but a persona altered -- and the return follows because nothing about it concludes. The engine's twist is at the root: confession makes a requirement feel like a release, so the persona is obliged to speak and experiences the speaking as liberation, and the more relieved they feel the less visible the requirement becomes.

## System

- Do decide who required the telling before anything else, and keep it out of the scene. Nobody in the room can see the requirement, which is the whole difficulty.
- Do make the listener kind. The priest, the therapist, the assessor, and the friend are usually exactly what they appear to be, and the engine runs at full strength with all of them behaving well.
- Do not stage the listener as sinister. That replaces the mechanism with an interrogation, and it collapses the movement the engine exists for.
- Do make the relief real. It is the mechanism rather than a consolation, and a play that stages it as hollow has replaced the engine with a lesson.
- Do let the persona describe the telling as their own idea. That is what an internalised requirement sounds like from inside, and it is the most accurate line the persona has.
- Do show the account being made rather than recalled: the gap bridged, the motive supplied, the order imposed. The persona is not lying and does not notice building anything.
- Do put the detail where it belongs. A follow-up question that presses for specifics manufactures them, and the more specific the account the more it will be believed -- which is why a false confession outperforms a true one.
- Do close on the return rather than the confession. The moment worth staging is the persona afterwards, grateful, unburdened, and booking the next one.
- Do not model self-disclosure, the secret, repair or guilt or forgiveness, discipline, heeding or recognition, or what the record becomes as a number (measure). Do not make the telling stand for a theme; that is authored key, the play's pitch. Do not edit the engine's files; wire only from the play's side.
