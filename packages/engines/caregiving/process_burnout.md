# Process: Burnout

## Taxonomy

[Caregiving](process_caregiving.md), the depleting form.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: caregiving

## Initiated by

The persona gives care past the point their own supply can bear, and is emptied by it -- the attention worn thin, the patience gone, the feeling for the other dulled by sheer demand.
This is burnout: care that depletes the giver, the tending that empties the one who tends.
It is what sustained answering of need costs when the cost is never replenished.

## Direction

Toward exhaustion, and a care that curdles as it runs dry.
Burnout moves the caregiver from full to spent: the demand outruns the resource, and what began as attuned answering becomes effortful, then mechanical, then resented -- the giver still giving but no longer able to feel the one they give to.
The direction is inward depletion showing outward as a care gone cold: withdrawal, numbness, a compassion worn to its end.

## Lever

The gap between demand and replenishment, spent down to nothing.
Burnout runs on an imbalance: care draws continuously on attention, patience, and feeling, and when the draw is not met by rest, relief, or return, the account empties -- exhaustion first, then distance from the other, then a sense that the giving no longer avails.
The lever is the unreplenished cost: the more relentless the need and the less the giver is themselves cared for, the faster the supply runs out, and overshooting care runs it out faster still.

## Echo

The giver is empty, and the care fails from the inside.
It is the failure mode of the caregiver rather than the cared-for -- exhaustion, depersonalization, the felt loss of efficacy -- and it corrodes the very responsiveness caregiving depends on, so that the one who most needs to be cared for is now the caregiver.
It is [sensitivity](process_sensitivity.md) starved of replenishment and the cliff [overprotection](process_overprotection.md) runs toward, its boundless giving having no floor to stop the fall. The resting depletion of a persona's regulatory reserves is the executive-function and regulation engines'; the felt-with distress that fatigues is the empathy engine's; this owns the caregiver emptied by the giving of care.
