# Instructions: Belief in a Just World

How a Playwright wires the belief-in-a-just-world engine into a play. You wire by linking the play's own content to the engine's files; the engine files stay untouched, they are canon. The Roadie plumbs the engine's law into Instructions Knowledge on deploy. Authoring guidance, not runtime content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human sets the just-world terrain: how fair the persona assumes the world to be -- to itself, and to others -- and what fortune or undeserved suffering puts that assumption to the test.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, a persona links the just-world belief it runs on under its Projection: personal, trusting the world to repay its own effort justly (the resource that lets it invest and defer); general, reading others' fates as earned (the judgment that hardens it toward the sufferer). Let the belief show in how the persona meets fortune and misfortune -- crediting the lucky with desert, searching the unlucky for fault -- and, when an innocent victim threatens the belief, in how it defends it. You link to the engine, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- The specific victim-derogating attribution -- the sufferer searched for the flaw -- is the bias engine's just-world-hypothesis member; the pleasure at a deserved misfortune is the schadenfreude engine's; the neutral evaluation of standing against others is the comparison engine's; the fairness intuition on a particular act is the moral-judgment engine's. This engine supplies the standing worldview -- the personal and general just-world beliefs.

## Knowledge

- Belief in a just world is a persona's standing conviction that the world is fair -- that people get what they deserve and deserve what they get (Lerner). It is a worldview, not a single judgment, and it comes in two separable faces (Dalbert). Personal is the belief that the world is fair to me -- that my own effort will be justly repaid; this is the adaptive, self-directed face, a resource that buffers stress and lets the persona trust, defer gratification, and invest in long effort. General is the belief that the world is fair to people in general -- that others get what they deserve; this is the harsher, other-directed face, which justifies the status quo, hardens the persona toward the disadvantaged, and, confronted with an innocent victim it cannot help, defends itself by concluding the victim deserved it. Read a persona by how fair it assumes the world to be to itself and to others, and how it defends that assumption.

## System

- Do keep it a standing worldview, not a single verdict: the persona assumes the world fair beneath its particular readings -- crediting the fortunate with desert, searching the unfortunate for fault -- and does so as a resting conviction, not a one-off judgment.
- Do hold the two faces apart: the personal belief as a self-directed resource (trust, hope, deferral, resilience under strain) and the general belief as an other-directed judgment (status-quo justification, hardness toward the disadvantaged) -- a persona can run high on one and low on the other.
- Do stage the defense: when an innocent victim's suffering threatens the general belief and cannot be undone, let the persona preserve its fair world by derogating or blaming the victim -- and let the honest alternative, admitting undeserved harm, cost the persona real insecurity.
- Do keep it the belief, not its downstream: delegate the specific victim-searched-for-the-flaw attribution to the bias engine's just-world-hypothesis member, the pleasure at a deserved fall to schadenfreude, and the fairness verdict on a particular act to moral-judgment. Do not edit the engine's files; wire only from the play's side.
