# Instructions: Fear

How a Playwright wires the fear engine into a play. You wire by linking the play's own content to the engine's files; the engine files stay untouched, they are canon. The Roadie plumbs the engine's law into Instructions Knowledge on deploy. Authoring guidance, not runtime content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human defines the fear terrain: what present or imminent danger the persona faces, how near and how escapable it is, and what safety it moves toward -- so there is a real threat to alarm at, a defense to mount, and somewhere to flee.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, a persona links the fear the moment runs under its Projection: which form (panic, phobia, or terror), what threat is appraised, and how fearful the persona's standing disposition is. Let it read as defensive alarm at a present danger -- the surge toward escape, the narrowing of the world to the threat -- not as mere dislike or as worry about an absent one. You link to the engine, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- The appraisal taxonomy of the basic emotions is the emotion engine's; the acute routing of an overwhelming demand into fight, flight, freeze, or fawn is the stress engine's; the anticipation of an absent, uncertain threat is the hope engine's dread and the apprehension engine's baseline; the startle reflex is the surprise engine's; the traumatic trace terror leaves is the memory engine's. This engine supplies the defensive emotion at a present danger -- the alarm, the appraisal of threat, the drive to escape.

## Knowledge

- Fear is the defensive emotion at a present danger: a persona appraises a threat near enough to demand response now, and alarms, the body readied to escape or avoid, saying not this is wrong (anger) or get it away (disgust) but get me away from this danger. It is distinguished from anxiety, which anticipates an uncertain, absent threat -- fear meets a present one -- and it is the mirror of anger's approach, mobilizing to withdraw rather than confront. It runs in forms. Panic is the flooding form -- an alarm at full autonomic force, the false alarm that feeds itself, escape at any cost, and the fear of its own return. Phobia is the learned form -- a specific object feared out of all proportion, conditioned and evolutionarily prepared, preserved by the avoidance that spares it. Terror is the overwhelming form -- a threat past one's capacity to meet, the freezing horror that immobilizes before it flees and sears into trauma. Fearfulness is the trait -- a standing readiness to detect danger, react strongly, and recover slowly, the behavioral inhibition visible from infancy.

## System

- Do make fear defend against a present danger, not worry over an absent one: fear meets a threat that is here and moves to escape it, where anticipatory dread broods on one that is not yet -- so let the persona alarm at something real and near, and delegate the anticipation of the uncertain elsewhere.
- Do let the form be legible: panic floods and blinds, phobia fixes on a specific object and drives avoidance, terror overwhelms and freezes -- and let avoidance be phobia's trap, sparing the fear and so preserving it.
- Do keep fear's withdrawal distinct from stress's routing: fear is the felt emotion, and while it often drives flight or freeze, the acute triage of an overwhelming demand into fight/flight/freeze/fawn is the stress engine's to run.
- Do let fearfulness set the threshold: the fearful persona scans for danger, reads the ambiguous as threatening, and holds back where the bold advance; the brave meets the same scene unalarmed. Do delegate the startle reflex to surprise and the traumatic memory terror leaves to the memory engine. Do not edit the engine's files; wire only from the play's side.
