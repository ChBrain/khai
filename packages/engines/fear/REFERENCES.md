# Fear: Reference

## Line of Work

Fear as a **multi-type** engine: the defensive emotion through which a persona alarms at a present or imminent danger -- a threat appraised, the body readied to escape or avoid -- taken across its forms and the trait that tunes it. The domain does not model the appraisal taxonomy of the basic emotions, which is the emotion engine's; nor the acute routing of an overwhelming demand into fight, flight, freeze, or fawn, which is the stress engine's; nor the anticipation of an absent, uncertain threat, which is the hope engine's dread and the apprehension engine's baseline; nor the startle reflex, which is the surprise engine's. It models the emotion itself: the appraised present threat, the alarm, and the defensive withdrawal it drives.

The spine is the psychology of fear, from its defensive circuit to its trait. Fear is the response to a present or imminent danger -- distinguished, in the modern account (Barlow; Davis; LeDoux), from anxiety, which is the sustained apprehension of an uncertain future threat: fear is phasic and cued, anxiety diffuse and anticipatory. It runs on a fast subcortical defensive circuit that fires ahead of conscious appraisal, mobilizing escape and narrowing attention to the danger. Its intensity scales with the imminence of the threat (Blanchard's defensive-distance account), from wariness through freezing to flight to panic. It is readily learned -- conditioned to specific cues (Watson), and learned faster and more durably to evolutionarily prepared dangers than to modern ones (Seligman; Öhman) -- and, once learned, preserved by the avoidance that forecloses its correction. Individual differences in fearfulness (behavioral inhibition, Kagan; the Behavioral Inhibition System, Gray; harm avoidance, Cloninger) are among the most stable of early traits and the strongest temperamental predictors of anxiety disorder. The altitudes are those: the emotion, its three forms, and its trait.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **Emotion**: The engine models fear in its own depth -- the defensive emotion, its forms, its trait -- not its place in the taxonomy of the basic emotions. The appraisal structure by which an event becomes joy, sadness, anger, fear, or disgust is owned by the emotion engine; this engine unfolds the one node the emotion taxonomy names fear. The two compose: emotion keeps fear as one basic affect, this engine unfolds it.
- **Stress**: The engine models the emotion of a present threat, not the acute routing of an overwhelming demand. The triage of a demand that exceeds resources into fight, flight, freeze, or fawn is owned by the stress engine; fear frequently drives flight and freeze, but the routing of overwhelming demand is stress's, and the felt defensive emotion is this engine's. This engine keeps the feeling; stress keeps the acute response-routing.
- **Apprehension and Hope**: The engine models fear of a present, locatable danger, not the anticipation of an absent, uncertain one. The baseline nervous-system filter and threat-sensitivity a persona arrives with is owned by the apprehension engine (a position), and the anticipation of a bad outcome -- the dread that is hope's negative pole -- is owned by the hope engine; fear meets a danger that is here, and delegates the sustained anticipation of one that is not.
- **Surprise**: The engine models fear, not the startle reflex. The fast, reflexive startle to a sudden stimulus is owned by the surprise engine; startle can trigger or accompany fear, but the reflex itself is surprise's, and the appraised defensive emotion is this engine's.
- **Memory**: The engine models the terror that traumatizes, not the trace it leaves. The laying-down of a traumatic memory and its involuntary return -- the flashback, the intrusion -- are owned by the memory engine (and the stress engine's acute-stress aftermath); terror is the emotion at its overwhelming extreme, the memory and its return are theirs.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per file.

- **[fear](process_fear.md)** (the root, process): The defensive emotion at a present danger -- threat appraised, the body readied to escape or avoid, attention narrowed to the danger and the exits. Anchored by LeDoux (the defensive survival circuit) and Barlow/Davis (the fear/anxiety distinction).
- **[panic](process_panic.md)** (process, form): The flooding form -- an alarm at full autonomic force, the false alarm that feeds itself and teaches the fear of its own return. Anchored by Barlow and Klein (panic as a fear response and false alarm).
- **[phobia](process_phobia.md)** (process, form): The learned form -- a specific object feared out of all proportion, conditioned and evolutionarily prepared, preserved by the avoidance that forecloses its correction. Anchored by Watson & Rayner (conditioned fear) and Seligman / Öhman (preparedness).
- **[terror](process_terror.md)** (process, form): The overwhelming form -- a threat past one's capacity to meet, the freezing horror that immobilizes before it flees and sears into trauma. Anchored by the Blanchards (defensive-distance, freezing at high imminence) and the trauma literature.
- **[fearfulness](position_fearfulness.md)** (position, trait): Trait fear -- the standing readiness to detect danger, react strongly, and recover slowly, the behavioral inhibition visible from infancy and predictive of later anxiety. Anchored by Kagan (behavioral inhibition), Gray (the Behavioral Inhibition System), and Cloninger (harm avoidance).
