# Process: Mingling

## Taxonomy

[Street-life](process_street-life.md), the facet of the casual exchange between strangers.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: street-life

## Initiated by

The people out begin to meet.
Mingling starts wherever a public place brings strangers into casual exchange -- the nod, the small talk at the counter, the word with the neighbor, the question to the vendor, the two people a shared sight turns into talkers -- the light, unforced meeting a busy place makes possible.
It is the figure to presence's ground: not the sheer being-there of people but what happens between them, the web of small public exchanges that a lively street spins without anyone planning it.

## Direction

Across, from stranger to stranger, in exchanges that ask nothing deeper.
Mingling knits the place's users together -- the repeated small dealings that let a neighborhood's people know each other's faces without knowing their lives, the plaza where a busker or a fountain or an odd sight gives two strangers a reason to speak, the corner where paths cross and greetings pass.
It moves the place from a crowd to a public: not people merely near each other but people lightly in touch, the sidewalk building the casual, low-stakes trust that lets a stranger be asked the time, watched for, or trusted with a held door.

## Lever

The place's power to mix people against the retreat into private worlds.
The carrying force is the good public place's gift for the unforced meeting -- the shared external thing that starts the talk, the counter and the bench and the crossing that put people in each other's way; the resistance is everything that keeps strangers apart, the design that moves people through without pause, the private car and the private screen, the fear that reads a stranger as a threat rather than a neighbor.
Where the place invites the mixing, a web of light public trust grows; where it forbids it, people share the ground and stay strangers, and the trust never forms.

## Echo

The place woven into a public by its meetings.
Mingling leaves the place laced with its small exchanges -- the nods and words and shared glances that turn users into a loose community -- and hands the listener the web of casual trust those meetings build.
It is the field's connective tissue: the light, repeated, undemanding mixing that a lively public place hosts, out of which a neighborhood's sense of itself is quietly made.
Mingling is dated from the meeting, the moment the place turned two strangers into an exchange.
