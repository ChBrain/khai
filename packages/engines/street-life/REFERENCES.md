# Street-life: Reference

## Line of Work

Street-life as **process**: the social life a public place hosts -- the place read by the
people in it and what they do there, alive or dead by its use, as particular to a place as
its look and as much an actor as its scenery. It wires on a place at Shown, the place's
self-presentation to whoever enters, because a public place is read by its life before
anything else: the people out and using it, the casual mingling of the strangers it
gathers, the eyes on the street it keeps over itself, and the idle lingering it invites.
The domain does not model the layout and standing mood of the setting, which is space's;
nor the sound the place makes, which is soundscape's; nor the weather over it, which is
weather's; nor a persona's own errand, sociability, or solitude, which is theirs. It models
the human life the place hosts and is read by -- the life between the buildings, the
sidewalk ballet the place either holds or fails to.

The spine is the study of public urban life -- the argument that a place's worth is
measured by the life it hosts, not the form it takes. Jacobs read the city sidewalk as its
fundamental organ: the intricate sidewalk ballet of a lively street, the eyes on the street
that use itself provides, and the web of casual public contact out of which a
neighborhood's trust is made -- and the ways modern planning, by zoning that life out,
kills the street it means to serve. Whyte watched what actually happens in small urban
spaces -- where people really sit, why some plazas fill and others stay empty,
triangulation as the external stimulus that turns strangers into talkers, and people's pull
toward other people. Gehl set the life between buildings against the buildings themselves,
sorting a place's activity into necessary, optional, and social, and showing that the
optional and social life -- the part that makes a place feel alive -- appears only where the
place is good. So the engine gives street-life three facets of the living street --
presence, mingling, watch -- and keeps a fourth, lingering, the optional idle staying read
not as waste but as the surest proof of a place worth being in.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The layout and standing mood (space)**: How a place is arranged -- its size, shape,
  enclosure, and standing feel, the room's proportions and the street's width -- is space's.
  Street-life is the human life a place hosts, not the form that hosts it; the two meet in a
  place (a plaza's shape shapes who sits in it) but are not the same force. Space is how the
  place is laid out; street-life is how it is lived in.
- **The sound the place makes (soundscape)**: The place's auditory field -- the hum, the
  calls, the din -- is soundscape's. Street-life is the social use, not the sound; a market's
  life and a market's roar coincide but are different forces. Soundscape is how the place
  sounds; street-life is how its people use it.
- **The weather over it (weather)**: The rain that empties the plaza and the sun that fills
  it is weather's; street-life takes the resulting use as its own, but the weather itself it
  does not model. Weather is what falls on the place; street-life is what the people do there.
- **A persona's own sociability or errand (the persona's engines)**: Why a given persona is
  out, whether they seek the crowd or shun it, their own loneliness or ease among strangers
  -- that is the persona's, not the place's. Street-life is the place's social life, read by
  anyone who enters; it never stands in for a persona's own sociability or solitude.
- **The facet itself is not engine content**: The engine declares the four facets exist;
  which one a given place shows lives on that place, linked under its Shown chapter. The
  engine refuses to fix a place's social life for it.

  Anchored by Jacobs (the sidewalk as the city's public organ) and the peopled street against

- **[presence](process_presence.md)**: the people out and using the place -- the density and
  rhythm of use, the peopled street against the empty one, people drawing more people.
  Anchored by Whyte (people attract people) and Gehl (necessary activity).
- **[mingling](process_mingling.md)**: the casual exchange between strangers -- the nod, the
  small talk, the stranger met, the web of light public trust a busy sidewalk builds, the
  triangulation that turns strangers into talkers. Anchored by Jacobs (casual public contact)
  and Whyte (triangulation).
- **[watch](process_watch.md)**: the eyes on the street -- the informal, unbought
  surveillance that use itself provides, the safety of the peopled place minded by its own
  users. Anchored by Jacobs (eyes on the street).
- **[lingering](process_lingering.md)**: the idle, optional staying read as the proof of a
  place worth being in -- the sitting, the loitering, the hanging around that comes only where
  the place is good. The engine's twist; anchored by Gehl (optional and social activity) and
  Whyte (the stayers).
