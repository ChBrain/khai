# Process: Watch

## Taxonomy

[Street-life](process_street-life.md), the facet of the eyes on the street.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: street-life

## Initiated by

The peopled place keeps an eye on itself.
Watch starts wherever the ordinary users of a place, doing nothing but being present, keep an informal eye on the ground they share -- the shopkeeper at the door, the neighbor at the window, the regulars who would notice a wrong note, the passing crowd that a street of use never lacks.
It is the safety a place makes for itself out of nothing but its own life: not the paid guard or the camera but the eyes on the street, the natural, unbought watch that use itself provides.

## Direction

Outward onto the public ground, from every user who happens to be looking.
Watch covers the place with attention -- the many small unofficial gazes of people who live and work and pass along it, the windows that overlook the walk, the doorways that face it, the sidewalk busy enough that someone is always about; the place minded because it is used.
It moves the place from exposed to held: a street with eyes on it is a street where trouble is seen, where a child can be let out and a stranger is noticed, where the mere likelihood of being watched keeps the peace no patrol could.

## Lever

The safety of use against the danger of the empty place.
The carrying force is that a peopled, watched street polices itself -- the users' own eyes, drawn by nothing but presence, make the place safe without anyone assigned to it; the resistance is the emptying that blinds it, the blank wall and the dead frontage and the deserted hour that leave no one looking, so the place turns unsafe, and the unsafety empties it further.
Eyes on the street and the place is held; take the eyes away -- wall it off, empty it, turn the buildings' backs to it -- and it is neither safe nor felt to be, and the fear finishes what the design began.

## Echo

The place held by its own attention.
Watch leaves the place minded -- covered by the countless small gazes of its users, safe by its own life rather than by guard or gate -- and hands that safety to everyone who uses the ground.
It is the field's quiet keeper: the protection a lively public place gives itself for free, the first thing lost when a place goes empty and the hardest to restore, for safety and use each depend on the other.
Watch is dated from the looking, the standing attention a peopled place keeps over itself.
