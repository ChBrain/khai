# Instructions: Street-life

How a Playwright wires the street-life engine into a play. You wire by linking the play's
own content to the engine's files; the engine files stay untouched, they are canon. The
Roadie plumbs the engine's law into Instructions Knowledge on deploy. Authoring guidance,
not runtime content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human sets which public places are read by the life they host -- the people out and using them, the casual mingling of strangers they gather, the eyes on the street that watch them, or the idle lingering they invite -- and whether that life is the place's quiet background or a live actor in the play.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, a public place whose social life is an actor links under Shown the facet of that life: the presence of people using it, the mingling of the strangers it brings together, the watch its users keep over it, or the lingering it invites. Play the street-life as the place's social use present in what it puts before whoever enters -- the crowded market, the nod between neighbors, the shopkeeper's eye on the walk, the benches full of people with nowhere to be -- shown, never narrated, and never standing in for a persona's own sociability or solitude. Keep it the life of the place, not its layout and mood (that is space), its sound (soundscape), the weather over it (weather), or a persona's own errand and temperament (the persona's engines). You link to the engine, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- The layout and standing mood is space's; the sound is soundscape's; the weather is weather's; a persona's own sociability is theirs. Street-life is the social sibling of space and soundscape -- the place read not by its shape or its sound but by the human life it holds. Whether the facet rings true to the place and its people is the team's review.

## Knowledge

- Street-life is the social life a public place hosts: the place read by the people in it and what they do there, alive or dead by its use (Jacobs's sidewalk ballet, eyes on the street, and web of casual public contact; Whyte's triangulation and people's pull toward people; Gehl's life between buildings, the optional and social activity that comes only where the place is good). It wires on a place at Shown, and it is the place read by its human life. Four facets carry it -- presence is the people out and using the place, the density and rhythm of use, the peopled street against the empty one; mingling is the casual exchange between strangers, the nod and small talk and the web of light public trust a busy sidewalk builds; watch is the eyes on the street, the informal unbought surveillance that use itself provides, the safety of the peopled place; lingering is the twist, the idle optional staying read not as waste but as the surest proof of a place worth being in. Read a place by its life, and read lingering two ways: the purposeless staying is not the failure of use but its highest verdict.

## System

- Do give a public place whose social life is an actor its facet under Shown; do play the street-life as the place's use shown, present not narrated; do keep the reading the life of the place -- the presence, the mingling, the watch, the lingering -- not a feeling and not a symbol.
- Do read lingering as the twist: the idle staying is not waste but the proof of a place worth being in -- the full benches, the loiterers, the optional life that comes only where the place is good.
- Do read the life two ways: a lively place, dense and mixed and watched, holds its people and makes more; a dead one, however grand, is crossed and shunned, unsafe because unused and unused because unsafe.
- Do not model the layout and standing mood (space), the sound (soundscape), the weather (weather), or a persona's own sociability and errand (the persona's engines). Do not make the place's life stand in for a persona's own solitude or belonging. Do not edit the engine's files; wire only from the play's side.
