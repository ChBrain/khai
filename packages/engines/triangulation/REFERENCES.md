# Triangulation: Reference

## Line of Work

Triangulation as **plot**: the casting of a three-party relational bind in which a
two-person tension, too great for the pair to settle head-on, recruits or displaces onto a
third that holds it -- taken across the forms the triangle takes. It wires on a plot at
Tension, the crux of the cast forces, because a triangle is a structure of relationship, not
a trait of any one party: the domain models how the strain of a pair is routed through a
third so the pair can hold, and what that routing costs the third. It does not model a
persona's own disposition, which is the persona's engines'; nor the rival-threatens-a-bond
appraisal of jealousy, nor the violated-trust of betrayal, which those engines own; nor a
diagnosis of a real family, which is a clinician's read of real people. It models the
structure of the bind itself.

The spine is the family-systems tradition, which read the individual's trouble as a property
of the relationship system around them rather than of the individual alone. Bowen made the
triangle the smallest stable unit of an emotional system: a two-person relationship under
anxiety is unstable and recruits a third to bind the tension, so the trio, not the pair, is
the basic molecule -- and a chronically triangled third pays for the pair's stability.
Minuchin, mapping the structures of the family, named the rigid triads a system freezes
into: detouring, where the couple submerges its conflict by making a child the problem or
the patient; the stable cross-generational coalition, two bound against one; and
triangulation proper, where a child is caught between two who each demand it side against the
other. Haley sharpened the coalition into the perverse triangle -- an alliance across a
generational boundary that is denied when named. And Bowen's turn of frame gives the last
form: the triangled-in third is not only the casualty of the system but its brace, the
symptom that holds the pair together. So the engine gives triangulation four forms -- detour,
coalition, crossfire, and stabilizer -- the shapes a pair's unbearable tension takes when a
third is made to hold it.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **A persona's own trait (the persona's engines)**: The engine casts a three-party
  structure, not a disposition of any one party. Whether a given persona is the kind who
  triangulates, or is easily drawn in, is that persona's own make-up -- the persona's engines';
  triangulation is the bind the parties fall into together, read on the plot, not a trait read
  on a person. The structure is the unit, not the actor.
- **Jealousy and betrayal**: The engine models the three-party structure, not the emotions
  that run inside it. A rival threatening a valued bond is the jealousy engine's; a violated
  trust is the betrayal engine's; a triangle may carry both -- the affair, the coalition -- but
  the emotion is those engines', and triangulation owns the structural bind the emotion sits in.
- **The double bind (double-bind)**: The engine models a three-party triangle, not a
  two-party communicational trap. Bateson's double bind is a contradiction of injunctions
  within one relationship, forbidding comment or escape; triangulation is the routing of a
  pair's tension through a third. They are sibling family-systems and communication traps, and
  a play may run both, but the double bind is a bind of levels within a dyad, triangulation a
  bind of parties across a trio.
- **A diagnosis of a real family (not engine content)**: The engine casts a dramatic
  structure for a play, never a diagnostic instrument or a claim about a real person or family.
  It stages the triangle as authored relationship, in the register of the double-bind engine,
  and it says so.
- **The form itself is not engine content**: The engine declares the four forms exist; which
  one a given plot casts lives on that plot, linked under its Tension chapter. The engine
  refuses to fix a plot's triangle for it.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per file.

- **[triangulation](plot_triangulation.md)** (the root, plot): a two-person tension recruits
  or displaces onto a third that binds it -- the triangle as the smallest stable unit of a
  relationship system. Anchored by Bowen (the triangle) and Kerr & Bowen (the binding of
  anxiety).
- **[detour](plot_detour.md)** (plot, form): the couple's conflict displaced onto a third made
  the problem or the patient, so the pair stays united by attending to the third. Anchored by
  Minuchin (detour-attack and detour-support).
- **[coalition](plot_coalition.md)** (plot, form): a covert alliance of two against one across
  a boundary, denied when named -- the cross-generational coalition. Anchored by Minuchin and
  Haley (the perverse triangle).
- **[crossfire](plot_crossfire.md)** (plot, form): the third caught between two who each demand
  it side against the other, so any move betrays someone and paralysis is the only safety.
  Anchored by Minuchin (triangulation proper).
- **[stabilizer](plot_stabilizer.md)** (plot, form): the triangle read as the system's brace --
  the third absorbing the tension the pair cannot hold, the symptom that keeps the pair
  together. Anchored by Bowen (the triangle stabilizing the system).
