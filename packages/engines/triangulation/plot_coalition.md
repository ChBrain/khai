# Plot: Coalition

## Taxonomy

[Triangulation](plot_triangulation.md), the form where two bind against the one across a boundary.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: triangulation

## Cue

Three parties with a line of authority or generation running between them -- a parent, the other parent, and a child; a boss, a deputy, and a rival; any trio where two sit on one side of a boundary and one on the other. Between two of them a covert closeness forms, warmer than either will admit and aimed, quietly, at the third.

## Action

Two of the three form an alliance against the one, and the alliance crosses a line it will not name. Haley called it the perverse triangle: a coalition between two people across a boundary that is denied when the third asks about it -- the parent and child who side together against the other parent while insisting nothing of the kind is happening. The covertness is the point: an open alliance is a mere disagreement, but a coalition that operates while disavowing its own existence undermines the boundary it crosses, so the excluded one fights an alliance no one will admit is there.

## Stage

The two act in concert -- the shared look, the private understanding, the small betrayals of the third -- while the third is left to sense an alignment against them that dissolves into denial the moment it is named. The crossed boundary, parent and child bound over and against the other parent, is worn as ordinary closeness, so the exclusion is real and unprovable at once.

## Tension

The excluded one cannot win, because the coalition is denied as fast as it is felt. Name it and the two call it imagination; act on it and the third looks like the aggressor against an innocent closeness; ignore it and the alliance tightens. And the coalition corrodes what it crosses: the child pulled into a partnership against a parent is handed a power that is not theirs to hold and a loyalty that will cost them, and the excluded parent's standing erodes from a bond they can neither see clearly nor call out. The triangle holds because the alliance draws its strength precisely from staying unspoken.
