# Plot: Detour

## Taxonomy

[Triangulation](plot_triangulation.md), the form where the pair's conflict is displaced onto a third.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: triangulation

## Cue

A pair whose own conflict is too dangerous to face -- a couple who cannot fight without threatening the marriage, partners who need each other too much to let the real quarrel run. A third stands available to take the weight: most often a child, but any party the pair can make their shared concern. The tie between the two holds, but only because the strain has somewhere else to go.

## Action

The couple submerges its conflict by turning it on the third. Minuchin named the two routes: detour-attack, where the pair unites against the third as the problem -- the difficult child, the bad influence, the one whose fault it all is; and detour-support, where the pair unites around the third as fragile -- the sick child, the one who needs protecting, whose care is the thing they can agree on. Either way the two stand together by facing the third instead of each other, and the marital tension drains into the management of a third-party problem.

## Stage

The pair presents as united -- a common front against a troublemaker or a common vigil over a patient -- and the third wears the symptom the couple cannot own: the child acts out, sickens, fails, absorbing into their own trouble the conflict the parents will not have. The unity of the two is real and is built entirely on the third's carrying.

## Tension

The third's trouble is load-bearing, so it cannot be allowed to resolve. Let the scapegoat reform or the patient recover and the couple's own conflict, no longer detoured, comes back into the room between them -- so the system quietly needs the third to stay the problem, and improvement in the third is met, without anyone intending it, by a fresh crisis that restores the detour. The pain sits on the one least able to refuse it, and the pair's peace is bought at the price of the third never being allowed to get well.
