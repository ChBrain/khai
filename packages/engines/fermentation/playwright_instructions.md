# Instructions: Fermentation

How a Playwright wires the fermentation engine into a play. You wire by linking the play's own content to the engine's files; the engine files stay untouched, they are canon. The Roadie plumbs the engine's law into Instructions Knowledge on deploy. Authoring guidance, not runtime content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human keeps the failures geometric rather than moral: nothing is contaminated, no organism is a villain, and the piece of cabbage above the brine is the whole of what went wrong. Show the intervention as small and early -- a weighing, a lid, a shelf -- because after that nobody in the play has any authority over the vessel. And decide who draws the line, since the process supplies no endpoint and somebody with a spoon has to.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, a piece links under Apparent which movement its vessel is in, and a place links under Shown the conditions it supplies: the temperature, the cellar, the draught, the sun on the shelf. Read the engine on what was favoured, never on what was prevented. Keep it the directed case: matter losing its imposed order is decay's, degradation through use is wear's, transformation by heat is combustion's. You link to the engine, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- Rust, rot, crumble, and patina are decay's; the surface abraded and the consumable depleted are wear's; burning, pyrolysis, and the household fire are combustion's, fire's, and hearth's; what a shared table does and what wanting food is are meal's, hospitality's, and appetite's. Recipes, quantities, equipment, commercial cultures, canning standards, and food law are the trade and set the format, and whether a specific vessel is safe to eat is a question for controls and testing rather than for this engine. Whether a production's jar has turned or arrived, and whether a household's line is the same line as its neighbour's, are the team's review.

## Knowledge

- Fermentation is decay pointed somewhere: a substrate with sugars in it, held under chosen conditions, where nothing is excluded and something is favoured. It is not decay's matter losing its imposed order, not wear's degradation through use, and not combustion's transformation by heat. It wires on a piece at Apparent and on a place at Shown, and runs in four movements. The substrate is a resource with several claimants and no default winner: the sugars are present, the organisms are already on the material, and the cut puts them in contact, so doing nothing selects the spoilage rather than avoiding it; the products anybody wants -- alcohol, lactic acid, acetic acid, gas -- are intermediates an organism abandoned rather than finished. The setting does not defend the jar, it rigs the contest inside it: salt is a price rather than a poison, lowering water activity so that living costs more for everybody and most for the wrong ones; excluding air withdraws an advantage from the fastest spoilers and grants nothing to the ferment; and the hurdles are lethal only in combination, each set well below the level that would work alone, which is why the food stays edible. The succession runs itself and cannot hold still, because each population is displaced by the acid it produced, and the acid that protects the vessel is manufactured by the vessel from the food over the very days when the least is defending it. The engine's twist is at the root: a ferment is not defended from rot, it is rotted first by something you chose -- everything that would have ruined it is present, viable, and waiting, and the only reason the jar is food is that something else got there first.

## System

- Do put the intervention at the beginning and then remove the practitioner. A weighing, a weight, a shelf, and after that the vessel is on its own for weeks.
- Do keep everything present. The spoilage organisms never leave the jar; they are outrun, and a scene that stages them as arriving has the mechanism backwards.
- Do make the failure geometric. A leaf above the brine, a lid not seated, a warm week -- small, physical, and nobody's fault.
- Do let the vessel build its own protection. The dangerous days are the first three, before the acid is up, and nothing the practitioner added is doing much yet.
- Do let the smell change and mark the handover. Nobody scheduled it, nobody can speed it up except by temperature, and it happens while the household is out.
- Do give the endpoint to a person. Somebody tastes it and says it is ready, or that it has gone, and the jar contains nothing that would settle the question.
- Do close on the next jar rather than this one. A spoonful goes forward, the lineage continues, and the culture is a record of the kitchen it has been living in.
- Do not model undirected decay (decay, erosion, palimpsest), degradation through use (wear), transformation by heat (combustion, fire, hearth), or the table that eats it (meal, hospitality, appetite). Do not treat the engine as a food-safety guide; hazards are the trade's. Do not make the crock stand for a theme; that is authored key, the play's pitch. Do not edit the engine's files; wire only from the play's side.
