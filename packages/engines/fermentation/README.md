# Fermentation

Fermentation as process: directed spoilage, read on what was favoured rather than on what was prevented -- not matter losing the order imposed on it, which is decay's, and not degradation through use, which is wear's -- across four movements: the substrate (a sugar spent badly on purpose so the leavings are the food, a wild load that is the whole cast present from the beginning, and a wound that starts the process and the spoilage together), the setting (salt that is a price rather than a poison, shut air that withdraws an advantage and grants nothing, and hurdles that work together or not at all), the succession (a first bloom that is the wrong organism doing the necessary work, an acid the vessel makes to protect itself, and a handover arranged by the outgoing tenant), and the turn (a line drawn by a trained palate at a boundary the process does not contain, an arrest that chooses between a ferment that lasts and a ferment that is working, and a next jar that continues a population which is no longer the population) -- with the twist that a ferment is not defended from rot; it is rotted first by something you chose.
