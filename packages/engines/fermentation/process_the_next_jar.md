# Process: The Next Jar

## Taxonomy

[The Turn](process_turn_fermentation.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: fermentation

## Initiated by

A spoonful of this batch goes into the next one.
Or a piece of the dough, or a cup of the brine, or the mat off the top.

## Direction

Toward a lineage that is continuous and unowned.
Backslopping is the oldest technique in the practice: seeding a new vessel from an old one skips the uncertain opening days by installing the winning population at full strength on day zero. A sourdough starter, a vinegar mother, a yoghurt culture, and a kimchi brine are all the same move.
What is passed on is not a recipe and not a species but a community, which drifts with every transfer, adapts to the household's flour and water and kitchen temperature, and is never quite the same as the one it came from.

## Lever

The reliability the transfer buys against what it quietly changes.
The carrying force is that backslopping is the single most effective intervention available and needs no understanding: it removes the dangerous early window, it makes the result consistent batch to batch, and cultures maintained this way have been kept going in households for generations; the resistance is that the community is not preserved by being carried, it is selected by being carried -- each transfer favours whatever suited the last vessel, so a culture is a record of the kitchen it has been living in rather than the one it came from.
The Next Jar continues a population that is no longer the population.

## Echo

A lineage in a cupboard, drifting.
It shows the loop closing, and why two people with the same starter do not have the same starter after a year.
The Next Jar is dated to the last transfer and is older than anybody can establish.
