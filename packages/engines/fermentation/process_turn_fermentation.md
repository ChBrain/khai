# Process: The Turn

## Taxonomy

[Fermentation](process_fermentation.md), where it stops being wanted, and where the engine is seen for one thing.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: fermentation

## Initiated by

Somebody tastes it and decides it is ready, or decides it has gone.
The Turn is that decision and what follows: [a line drawn](process_the_line_drawn.md) by a person, [an arrest](process_the_arrest.md) that halts nothing permanently, and [a next jar](process_the_next_jar.md) seeded from this one.

## Direction

Toward an endpoint that is set from outside the vessel.
The process has no endpoint of its own. Left alone, a ferment continues past every stage anybody wanted: the sauerkraut goes soft, the wine goes to vinegar, the vinegar goes to a mat and then to something nobody has a name for, and at no moment does the chemistry change character.
What changes is who is reading it. The same jar at the same hour is a delicacy in one kitchen and a bin in the next, and both readings are correct about the contents.

## Lever

The reality of the difference between good and spoiled against the absence of any line in the material.
The carrying force is that the distinction is not arbitrary and matters enormously: people can tell, consistently, whether something is right, cuisines agree closely within themselves, and some of the boundary is not taste at all but toxicology, which is fixed and not up for discussion; the resistance is that the process itself contains no marker for the boundary -- nothing switches, nothing completes, and the sequence that made the food is the identical sequence that will take it away.
The Turn rides the engine's keystone knife-edge: a ferment is not defended from rot; it is rotted first by something you chose.

## Echo

A jar declared finished by somebody with a spoon.
It shows the three earlier movements complete -- a contested substrate, a rigged setting, a succession that ran itself -- and adds the only judgement in the process, which is made from outside it.
This is why the Turn is the hardest of the four to stage and the only one worth closing on: nothing has failed, nothing has been contaminated, no organism has done anything but live, and the audience must be able to watch the same process be a triumph and a loss depending on when somebody looked.
A play that stages the ferment as an infection has replaced the mechanism with an accident, and one that stages the practitioner as controlling it has replaced it with a recipe.
The Turn is dated to a taste and is different in every household.
