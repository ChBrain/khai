# Fermentation: Reference

## Line of Work

Fermentation as **process**: decay pointed somewhere, and everything that follows from the pointing
being the only intervention.

The engine's subject is a preservation method that preserves nothing by keeping anything out. Katz's
framing is the spine, and it states the thing plainly enough to build on: fermentation is controlled
decomposition, the practitioner is not sterilising anything, and the organisms that would spoil the food
are present throughout. What is applied is a set of conditions that changes which population thrives.
The technical literature says the same in different vocabulary -- selective pressure, competitive
exclusion, hurdle technology -- and this engine reads the consequence, which is that a jar of food is
food because of a race rather than because of a barrier.

The boundary with what khai already carries is clean. Matter losing the order a maker imposed on it --
rust, rot, crumble, patina -- is **decay**'s; this engine reads the same chemistry aimed at an outcome
somebody wants. Degradation through use is **wear**'s. The transformation of a substance by heat is
**combustion**'s and **fire**'s. The gathering that eats the result is **meal**'s, and the household
hearth it is kept near is **hearth**'s.

**The substrate** is a resource with several claimants and no default winner. **The sugar** is spent
badly on purpose: fermentation is an inefficient way to extract energy, and every product anybody wants
-- alcohol, lactic acid, acetic acid, carbon dioxide -- is an intermediate an organism could not be
bothered to finish digesting. **The wild load** is the whole cast, present from the beginning; a starter
culture changes the proportions rather than the cast. **The wound** starts the process and the spoilage
together, since an intact cabbage keeps for months only because the sugars are inside the cells and the
bacteria are outside them.

**The setting** does not defend the jar; it rigs the contest inside it. **The salt** is a price rather
than a poison: it lowers water activity, which taxes every organism in the vessel and taxes the wanted
ones least. **The shut air** withdraws an advantage and grants nothing, which is why the commonest
failure in the practice is a leaf floating above the brine. **The hurdles** work together or not at
all, in the sense the preservation literature means: several mild factors that no organism can adapt to
simultaneously, each set well below the level that would work alone, which is exactly why the food
stays edible.

**The succession** runs itself and cannot hold still. **The first bloom** is the wrong organism doing
the necessary work. **The acid made** is the vessel producing the condition that will protect it, from
the food, over the days when the least is defending it. **The handover** is a change of tenant arranged
by the outgoing one.

Hence the **twist**, at the root. **A ferment is not defended from rot; it is rotted first by something
you chose.** Preservation here is occupation rather than exclusion. Salt, acid, and the absence of air
do not kill what would spoil the food; they make the vessel expensive for it and cheap for something
else, and the something else takes the ground and holds it. Everything that would have ruined the batch
is still present, still viable, and still waiting.

The engine is built so that nothing has to have failed for any of it to run, and a play should keep it
that way. Nothing was contaminated. No organism is a villain. The piece of cabbage above the brine is
the whole of what went wrong.

**On the evidence, stated plainly.** This is the rare khai engine whose spine is measured rather than
argued, and the important caveats are about scope and about safety rather than about the mechanism.
**The microbial succession is well characterised.** That vegetable and dairy ferments proceed through a
sequence of populations, each displaced by the acid it produces, is established by sequencing of real
ferments and is not in dispute; the succession movement is the firmest part of the engine. **Hurdle
technology is an engineering framework with food-specific parameters.** The principle that several mild
factors combine to do what none does alone is standard and widely applied, and the actual numbers --
how much salt, what pH, which temperature -- differ by product, and nothing in this engine should be
read as a specification. **This engine is not a food-safety guide and must not be used as one.** The
turn movement says the boundary between wanted and spoiled is set by a cuisine rather than by the
chemistry, and that is true of taste and false of toxicology: botulism in an under-acidified or
improperly canned vessel, mycotoxins on a mould that a tradition did not select, and pathogens
surviving a fermentation that stalled are not matters of judgement, they are fixed hazards with
established controls, and the engine's reading of the endpoint as cultural applies strictly to the
palatable range and not to the safe one. **Katz is a practitioner synthesis, not a scientific source.**
It is an excellent and honest one, it is explicit about where it is reporting practice rather than
findings, and its framing is used here for the framing. **The engine flattens very different
processes.** Lactic vegetable ferments, alcoholic ferments, acetic conversion, mould-ripened foods, and
alkaline ferments differ in their organisms, their chemistry, and their hazards, and the common shape
this engine reads is thinner than any one of them. What should not be taken from this engine is that
spoilage is only an opinion, that any jar left long enough becomes food, or that traditional practice
needs no controls. The claim is narrow: nothing is excluded, the conditions rank the competitors, the
sequence is self-driving, and the endpoint comes from outside the vessel.

## Origin

The microbiology of fermented foods and the practice built on it long before it, wired as one process on
a piece and a place. The twist follows from Katz's framing; the mechanism of the setting movement is the
preservation literature's.

| Source                | Key Work                                                                                | Scope                                                                                                                                                                         |
| :-------------------- | :-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Sandor Ellix Katz** | _The Art of Fermentation_ (2012); _Wild Fermentation_ (2003)                            | **The twist.** Fermentation as controlled decomposition: nothing is sterilised, the spoilage organisms are present throughout, and the intervention is selection. The anchor. |
| **Robert W. Hutkins** | _Microbiology and Technology of Fermented Foods_ (2006)                                 | The succession of populations in vegetable, dairy, and cereal ferments, and what starter cultures actually change. The substrate and succession movements.                    |
| **Lothar Leistner**   | _"Basic aspects of food preservation by hurdle technology"_ (Int. J. Food Micro., 2000) | Several mild factors combining to exceed what any one achieves, because an organism cannot spend its adaptation twice. The setting movement and the hurdles form.             |
| **Louis Pasteur**     | _Etudes sur le Vin_ (1866); _Etudes sur la Biere_ (1876)                                | Fermentation as the work of living organisms rather than spontaneous chemical change, and spoilage as the work of different ones. The engine's founding distinction.          |
| **Harold McGee**      | _On Food and Cooking_ (revised 2004)                                                    | The chemistry of the products -- acids, alcohols, and gases as abandoned intermediates -- and what each does to the material. The sugar and the acid-made forms.              |

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **Matter losing its imposed order (decay, erosion, palimpsest)**: Rust, rot, crumble, and patina, and
  the slow undoing of what a maker arranged, are those engines'. This engine reads the same chemistry
  aimed somewhere, and hands the undirected case back.
- **Degradation through use (wear, ergonomics)**: The surface abraded, the part flexed to failure, and
  the consumable depleted are wear's. A ferment is not being used; it is running.
- **Transformation by heat (combustion, fire, hearth)**: Burning, pyrolysis, cooking, and the household
  fire are those engines'. Heat appears here only as a rate control and as one way of arresting the
  process.
- **The gathering that eats it (meal, hospitality, appetite)**: What a shared table does, what a host
  owes, and what wanting food is are those packages'. The engine stops at the jar.
- **The trade and its rules (the world)**: Recipes, quantities, equipment, commercial starter cultures,
  HACCP plans, canning standards, and food law are matters of practice and statute. They set the
  format; this engine reads what the conditions do to the competition.
- **Safety determination (the world, and the honesty note above)**: Whether a specific vessel is safe to
  eat is a question for controls and testing, not for this engine. The turn movement's reading of the
  endpoint as cultural applies to the palatable range only.
- **A jar as authored symbol (the play's pitch)**: A ferment staged in order to **stand for** a theme --
  the crock that mirrors a motif of patience -- is refused: that is authored key, the play's pitch.
  Fermentation sets what was favoured and what followed; the meaning is the play's.
