# Process: The Shut Air

## Taxonomy

[The Setting](process_setting_fermentation.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: fermentation

## Initiated by

The material goes under the brine and the lid goes on.
Or an airlock, or a weight, or a cloth and a daily push.

## Direction

Toward the removal of an advantage rather than the addition of a defence.
Oxygen is what the fastest spoilage organisms are best at using. Moulds, many rot bacteria, and the film yeasts all do far better with it than without, and the organisms that make the ferment do not need it at all.
Excluding air therefore does nothing to the ferment and a great deal to its competition, which is why the single most common failure in the practice is a piece of material floating above the brine.

## Lever

The simplicity of keeping air out against how completely it must be kept out.
The carrying force is that the measure is free and decisive: no equipment is required beyond something heavy, the organisms being favoured are indifferent to the change, and a submerged vessel behaves predictably for months; the resistance is that the exclusion is not partial in its effects -- one leaf above the surface is an aerobic site inside an anaerobic vessel, and it will grow the thing the whole arrangement was designed to disadvantage.
The Shut Air withdraws an advantage and grants nothing.

## Echo

A surface that has to stay under.
It shows why the failures of this process are almost always geometric rather than chemical.
The Shut Air is dated to the weight going on and is checked daily.
