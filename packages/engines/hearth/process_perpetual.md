# Process: Perpetual

## Taxonomy

[Hearth](process_hearth.md), the mode where the flame must never die, kept burning across centuries by an unbroken chain of keepers, and the ritual crisis when it goes out.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: hearth

## Initiated by

Some fires are charged with burning forever -- not for a household but for a city, a god, a sanctuary, a nation, or a memory -- and the keeping becomes not a duty of one keeper but an institution of many, a relay that cannot be broken.
Perpetual flames start wherever a fire is sworn to burn without end -- the eternal fire of Vesta tended by six Vestals whose 30-year watch ensures no one keeper carries the burden alone, the Atash Bahram of the Zoroastrian temples fed five times daily by the keepers of the sacred fire, the sanctuary lamp burning above an altar since the dedication of the building, the memorial flame lit for the dead that must outlast the living.
It is the fire that belongs not to one hearth but to time itself, and the keeper's labour is the price of that immortality.

## Direction

Through the succession of keepers who give their lives to the unbroken watching, and toward the fire as a thing that outlives any single person or generation.
Perpetual works the fire as a sacred deposit -- the fire of Vesta teaching every keeper in their watch that they are merely links in a chain that stretches back centuries, each keeper's years a moment in the fire's unbroken life; the Zoroastrian fires that have burned since Cyrus or earlier, tended through empire and exile; the sanctuary lamps that burn across generations of worship in the same place; the memorial flames that speak an obligation to the dead that runs deeper than the years.
The keeper of the perpetual flame lives differently than the keeper of the household fire: they are a servant to time, not to the place.

## Lever

The immortal fire against the human lifetime and the keeper's mortality that would otherwise end the watching.
The carrying force is the institution itself -- the rule that no one keeper watches alone, that the duty passes from one to the next before either can fail, that if one falls sick another takes the post, that the keeping is reinforced by ritual and law so no carelessness can stop it -- and the resistance is the same chaos that threatens any human work: the flood, the siege, the revolution, the forgetting, the keeper who falls ill on the night of the changeover, the one woman or man who must hold it alone for an hour and loses focus.
Let the chain hold and the fire burns for centuries; let it break once and the work of a thousand keepers dies in a night.

## Echo

The place where the fire has never died, and the keepers who have never ceased their watching, and the crisis that comes when the unthinkable happens -- the flame goes out, the chain is broken, the perpetual is broken.
A perpetual fire is read in the colour of its coals, the age of its ash, sometimes in the very building it inhabits that seems to grow old around the unchanging flame; and a perpetual fire that goes out is read as apocalypse, the loss of protection, the betrayal of an oath sworn across centuries, the sign of an age ending and a new world beginning.
Perpetual flames are dated from their founding -- Vesta's fire from the founding of Rome, the Yazd fire from the days of the early Sassanid kings, the sanctuary lamp from the building's dedication -- and when they go out, the date of the extinction becomes the date that divides before from after.
