# Process: Going Cold

## Taxonomy

[Hearth](process_hearth.md), the mode where the hearth is extinguished -- by abandonment, by sanction, by grief, by eviction, by the household's dissolution into defeat or diaspora.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: hearth

## Initiated by

A household can end, and when it does, the fire is not banked but let to die, and the cold hearth announces to the world that a family has ceased, or failed, or been driven out.
Going cold starts wherever a fire is deliberately let to die -- the keeper departs and no one tends, the household is cast out and the hearth is quenched as a sanction, the keeper dies and the flame is let go as an act of grief or release, the family surrenders and the fire is abandoned to cold.
It is the fire's death as a social statement, the hearth unlit the way other households are lit, the ash growing cold a sign as visible as any words could be.

## Direction

Against the household itself, and toward the dissolution of the place organized around the flame.
Going cold works the fire as a ending -- not a banking that promises return, but a cessation, a breaking of the duty, the ash allowed to grow chill, the fuel untouched, the keeper's watch ended and not given to another.
The cold hearth is read as powerfully as the lit one: a place that has no more claim on warmth, a family that has ended or been ended, a loss not hidden but put on public display.

## Lever

The breaking of the keeper's duty against the fire's own will to burn as long as fuel remains and kindling stirs it.
The carrying force is the household's decision or catastrophe -- deliberate withdrawal, or expulsion by force, or death of the keeper with none to follow, or the surrender of the family to what has befallen it -- and the resistance is the fire's own persistence, the coal that would burn for hours if not smothered, the keeper's knowing that the flame could be revived with a touch of fuel and breath, but the choice to refuse that touch.
Let the keeper abandon the post and the fire teaches loss; let the quenching be deliberate and the cold hearth becomes a curse.

## Echo

The place unmade by the fire's absence, and the household's dissolution written in the cold that spreads from the hearth through every room.
The cold hearth is read as a death -- the empty place where warmth was, the silence where the snap of burning used to mark time, the household known no more and the visitors to the house understanding in a single breath that whatever was here has ceased.
Going cold is dated from the last tending, the last time the keeper's hand laid fuel, and from the dawn when the ash is touched and found to be cold as stone -- and the household counts its years now not from the founding but from that cold.
