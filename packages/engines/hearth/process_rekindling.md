# Process: Rekindling

## Taxonomy

[Hearth](process_hearth.md), the mode where the fire is relit -- from one's own banked coal, from a carried ember, from a neighbour's hearth, or from the new fire of a festival -- and the relight as both renewal and as debt.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: hearth

## Initiated by

The fire has gone cold, either by accident or by the household's own choice, and it must be brought back -- but not always from scratch, and never without cost.
Rekindling starts wherever a fire is sought again -- from the coal buried in the ash and coaxed back to life, from an ember carried from the neighbour's hearth wrapped in bark, from the fire kindled afresh at a sanctuary or a festival and brought home as a gift, from the fire stolen or earned or traded or borrowed.
It is the fire returned, but every relight carries a weight: the knowledge that this flame once died, or the debt owed to whoever's hearth the ember came from.

## Direction

Through the source of the new flame, and toward the household's reinstatement in the community of the kept fire.
Rekindling works the fire as a statement of dependency or of renewal -- if the coal still lived beneath the ash, the relight costs only the keeper's care and patience; if an ember was borrowed from a neighbour, the relight is an obligation, a tie, a dependence that holds as long as the houses stand near each other; if the fire comes from the sanctuary or the festival, the relight is a blessing, a restoration to grace or to time.
The keeper reads the source of the flame and knows what it means for the household's standing.

## Lever

The source that provides the fire -- be it the household's own coal or another's kindness or the sacred festival's giving -- against the danger of the cold hearth and the shame it carries.
The carrying force is the network of fires that a place holds -- the neighbour's hearth always having flame to share (if the relationship holds), the sanctuary's fire always kept for the giving, the festival's new fire meant to restore and renew -- and the resistance is the cold hearth itself, the time it takes to relight, the patience the keeper must have with the reluctant flame, the obligation incurred by taking from another.
Let a keeper's coal survive and the relight costs nothing but work; borrow fire and the debt returns to the hearth with every flame that rises from it.

## Echo

The household returned to the community of the burning, and the flame that rises either from the household's own reserve or from the kindness and obligation of others.
The rekindled fire is read in its quickness to rise or its stubbornness, in the keeper's knowledge of where it came from -- proud if the coal was their own, grateful if borrowed, renewed if the source was sacred -- and the fire's colour and character never quite the same as before, as if it carries the memory of its absence or its borrowed origin.
Rekindling is dated from the cold, the day the fire went out, and from the day it rose again, and the keeper knows both dates by the heat's return.
