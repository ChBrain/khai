# Hearth: Reference

## Line of Work

Hearth as **process**: the kept flame as an institution of a place -- a fire that is tended daily by a keeper, banked to survive the night, rekindled when lost, extinguished as loss or sanction, and perpetuated across centuries in sanctuary and temple -- shown in the place's warmth and continuity before anyone names it. It wires on a place at Shown, the place's life around the fire, because a kept fire puts the household's state in front of whoever enters: the steady flame that speaks of order and care, the banked coals that show the keeper's knowledge, the cold hearth that announces dissolution. The domain does not model the combustion itself -- the chemistry, the dynamics, the mechanism of burning -- which is the combustion engine's; nor the keeper's emotional state, the love or burden they feel, which is the persona's own engines'; nor the ritual structure of fire-keeping across cultures, which is the ritual engine's. It models the hearth itself as an institution that organizes a place around a flame, and reads it two ways: as the daily work that sustains a household, and as the perpetual duty that binds keeper to fire across time.

The spine is Fustel de Coulanges' argument that the hearth is not merely a fire but a social fact -- a sacred deposit that grounds the family, and the keeping of it a fundamental obligation that defines membership and status in the household. Goudsblom showed that fire-keeping preceded fire-making by over a million years in human history: the long age when humans kept found fire before they could make it themselves. Boyce documented the Zoroastrian fire temples and their daily tending, the Atash Bahram as the highest form of kept fire. Bachelard read the reverie of the tended fire, the intimacy between keeper and flame. Beard and classical scholarship revealed the Vestals of Rome and their unbroken watch, the sacred fire as the city's very life. So the engine gives hearth five modes of the kept flame -- stoking that feeds and watches it daily, banking that puts it to sleep in ash through the night, rekindling that revives it from coal or borrowed ember, going cold that marks the household's end, and perpetual that binds the flame to centuries and to duty beyond any single life -- and reads each mode as both the keeper's labour and the place's state mirrored in the fire's steadiness or darkness.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The combustion itself (combustion)**: The chemistry, the dynamics, the mechanism of how fuel burns and what makes the flame rise or fall -- the physics and chemistry of fire -- is the combustion engine's. Hearth models the keeping, not the burning.
- **The ritual structure (ritual)**: The sacred rules of fire-keeping across traditions, the rites of lighting and banking and the offerings that accompany them, the words spoken over the fire -- is ritual's. Hearth reads the keeping as an institution, not the religious form it takes.
- **The keeper's emotional state (persona)**: How the keeping makes the keeper feel -- the love, the burden, the ritual meaning they invest in the work, the fire as a mirror of their own burning or grief -- is the persona's own engines'. Hearth delivers the duty and its visible effect, not the feeling.
- **The burn regime and the escaped fire (fire)**: A place's combustibility, and the fire that runs beyond the keeping -- the hearth spark that takes the house, the burn regime the land lives within -- is the fire engine's. Hearth ends where the flame leaves the keeper's hands.
- **A persona's feeling, and the fire as authored symbol**: How the fire makes someone feel -- the warmth, the comfort, the terror of the cold hearth as the loss of home -- is the persona's own engines'. And the fire made to **stand for** a persona's own tending or duty or loss, the household's warmth as the mirror of a keeper's devotion, is refused: that is authored key, the play's pitch, not the institution on the place.
- **The mode itself is not engine content**: The engine declares the five modes exist; which one a given place shows lives on that place, linked under its Shown chapter. The engine refuses to fix the state of the hearth for it.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per file.

- **[hearth](process_hearth.md)** (the root): the kept flame as an institution -- a place organized around a fire that is tended, the keeping a duty and an identity, the flame's state the household's state. Anchored by Fustel de Coulanges (the hearth as a sacred deposit that grounds the family) and Goudsblom (the institution of fire-keeping as humanity's first regime).
- **[stoking](process_stoking.md)**: the daily keeping -- feeding, watching, the keeper's role (the vestal, the youngest child, the stoker), the flame as a clock of care; the fire is never "on", it is kept. Anchored by Goudsblom (the institution of fire-keeping) and Bachelard (the reverie of the tended flame and its intimacy).
- **[banking](process_banking.md)**: the fire put to sleep -- coals buried in their own ash to survive the night with fuel and air withheld but heat held, the skill of banking, the morning's revival from the seed coal; the fire hidden but alive. Anchored by Goudsblom (fire-keeping across the day-night cycle) and Bachelard (the reverie of the hidden but living fire).
- **[rekindling](process_rekindling.md)**: the relight -- from one's own banked coal, from a carried ember, from a neighbour's hearth (with all the dependency and obligation borrowing fire means), from the new fire of a festival; the relight as renewal and as debt. Anchored by Fustel de Coulanges (the hearth as a bond and obligation between households) and Goudsblom (the role of fire in social dependency).
- **[going-cold](process_going-cold.md)**: the hearth extinguished -- the cold hearth as the social event (departure, eviction, death, defeat, the extinguished line), deliberate quenching as sanction or grief, the cold hearth as public statement. Anchored by Fustel de Coulanges (the hearth as the sign of household standing) and classical scholarship on the Vestals (the extinction as catastrophe).
- **[perpetual](process_perpetual.md)**: the perpetual flame -- the eternal flame as institution (Vesta's fire and its keepers, the Zoroastrian Atash Bahram fed five times daily, the sanctuary lamp, the memorial flame, the pilot light as its industrial descendant), and the crisis when a perpetual flame goes out. Anchored by Boyce (the Atash Bahram and its five daily feedings), Beard and classical scholarship (the Vestals' unbroken watch and the city's continuity), and Fustel de Coulanges (the fire as the bond across generations).
