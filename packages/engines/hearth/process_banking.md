# Process: Banking

## Taxonomy

[Hearth](process_hearth.md), the mode where the fire is put to sleep in its own ash, coals held alive through the night by fuel withheld and air cut off.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: hearth

## Initiated by

The fire must survive the night, but the keeper must sleep, and the flame cannot run unwatched through the dark without burning through all the fuel and leaving only cold by dawn.
Banking starts wherever the fire is laid to sleep deliberately -- the coals raked together, the ash drawn up around them to bury the heat but not smother it, the fuel held away, the air kept sparse so the fire slows to almost nothing but does not die.
It is the fire hidden but alive, the seed coal still glowing at its heart, waiting in the ash like a sleeper whose breath has slowed to barely visible.

## Direction

Through the keeping of the coal alive below the surface, and toward the fire's revival at first light without new ignition.
Banking works the fire as a reservoir -- the heat held, the coals sleeping in their blanket of ash, the fuel reserved for the morning when the keeper tends again, the whole art aimed at one thing: that the same fire that burned yesterday and will burn tomorrow does not have to be made new.
The keeper reads the depth of ash needed to hold the heat without freezing it, and lays the coals as if tucking a child into sleep -- held safe but not crushed, warm but not burning.

## Lever

The ash that holds the coal against the cold of the night and the temptation to add fuel that would waste it while no one watches.
The carrying force is the keeper's knowledge -- how deep the ash must be, how tightly the coals must be raked, what wood banks best (the slow-burning kind, or the wood that holds ember longest) -- and the resistance is the fire's own slope toward cold, the heat that bleeds away through the ash, the moisture and air that undo the banking if the art is wrong.
Let the keeper bank well and the coal survives the night; let the banking be careless and the keeper tends a dead fire in the morning.

## Echo

The place whose fire runs through the night unseen, preserved rather than lit anew, and the morning that begins not with fire-making but with the revival of what never died.
The banked fire is read in the morning's quick rise -- the coal still red, the ash still holding heat, the fire brought back to flame by the lightest touch, the keeper's confidence that what was yesterday will be today without labor of ignition.
Banking is dated from the nightfall, and from the morning the coal has gone cold, when the household realizes the keeper either slept too soundly or the ash was laid wrong, and the fire has to be made, not revived.
