# Process: Stoking

## Taxonomy

[Hearth](process_hearth.md), the mode where the fire is kept alive through the day by feeding, watching, and the keeper's hand.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: hearth

## Initiated by

The fire burns because someone tends it, and the keeper must not sleep or turn away while the day is bright and fuel is needed.
Stoking starts wherever a fire is not left to burn on its own but watched -- the kindling laid, the fuel added as the flame sinks, the ash stirred to keep the coal alive underneath, the keeper's eye on the rise and fall, the hand always ready.
It is the fire kept by labor and attention, the daily proof that the household has made tending its work.

## Direction

Through the keeper's constant hand, and toward the fire that holds its character day after day.
Stoking works the fire as a clock of care -- feeding it when it dips, stirring it when it grows sluggish, backing it from the wind if it runs too high, holding the heat and light steady enough that the household can rely on it.
The keeper reads the fire like a language, each shade and height a word, and answers with fuel and air, so the flame stays within the bounds the home needs.

## Lever

The keeper's watching against the fire's hunger and its drift toward either starvation or excess.
The carrying force is the keeper's presence and the knowledge held in the keeper's hands -- what fuel the fire takes best, how quickly it burns, what height of flame holds the right heat without roaring -- and the resistance is the fire's indifference to the household's needs, its readiness to burn wild or die according to its nature alone.
Let the keeper watch and the fire becomes a partner; let the keeper sleep and the fire becomes a stranger.

## Echo

The place whose rhythm is set by the fire the keeper tends, and the keeper's life shaped by the fire's needs.
The stoked fire is read in its steadiness -- the house warm, the light reliable, the keeper known and honored for the constancy of their work, the fire's state a mirror of their faithfulness.
Stoking is dated from the first lighting, and from the day the keeper's hand grows tired, or the keeper dies or departs, and the fire begins to fail because the watching has ended.
