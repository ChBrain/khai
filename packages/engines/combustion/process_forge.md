# Process: Forge Fire

## Taxonomy

[Combustion](process_combustion.md), the made fire blown to a small hot bed to work metal.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: combustion

## Initiated by

A small bed of fuel is force-fed air until a concentrated spot glows hot enough to bring steel to working heat.
Forge fire burns coal, coke, charcoal, or gas with a forced draft from bellows or blower concentrating air on a small bed, reaching a localised 1100-1600 C; steel is worked at an orange-yellow 900-1200 C and forge-welded at a near-white heat of about 1150-1300 C, at the very edge of sparking, with a reducing, fuel-rich fire preventing scale and decarburisation. The smith cokes green coal at the edges and pushes coke to the hot centre.
It is fire concentrated to a point: a bed made intensely hot in one spot and kept just short of consuming the metal in it.

## Direction

Into the hot core the smith builds, its heat and its chemistry set by the air on the tuyere.
Forge fire's temperature and its reducing-or-oxidising balance are set by the air rate on the tuyere, with borax flux shielding the joint at welding heat; the reducing fire protects the steel, the oxidising fire attacks it. The escalation is too much air, which tips the fire to burn the work.
The direction is a tended concentration: heat driven into a small core at the balance of air that heats the steel without consuming it.

## Lever

The reducing fire that protects the steel against the oxidising fire that eats it.
The carrying force is a forced-draft bed hot enough to work iron; the resistance fails in overheating -- an oxidising fire burns the steel, and the sparkler-like iron sparks that means the metal is being consumed leave it burnt and unsalvageable, scrapped not saved, while too much air scales the surface and too little leaves a smoky, cool, sulfur-embrittling fire. The same fire that shapes the metal will consume it if the air is wrong.
Keep it reducing and it brings the steel to heat; let it turn oxidising and it burns the very metal it was lit to work.

## Echo

The deep orange-to-white core, the short blue flames, the bright sparks at welding heat.
Forge fire is read by its signature -- a deep orange-to-white core, a clean coke fire with little smoke and short blue carbon-monoxide flames on top or a yellow sulfurous green-coal fire, a coal-and-sulfur smell, bright iron sparks at welding heat, and the huff of the bellows. It hands its heat to the steel, and, over-blown, hands the steel to ruin.
Forge fire is dated from the lighting, the moment the bed was blown up to work metal.
