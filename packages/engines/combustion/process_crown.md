# Process: Crown Fire

## Taxonomy

[Combustion](process_combustion.md), the wildland phenomenon of the fire in the canopy.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: combustion

## Initiated by

A surface fire climbs the ladder fuels into the treetops, and the forest burns from its crown.
Crown fire starts where flame and convective heat reach the canopy foliage and fine branchwood and ignite it -- first single trees torching (passive, the candling), then the whole canopy running with the surface fire beneath it (active), and rarely the crowns racing ahead of the surface fire entirely (independent). It burns at roughly 800-1200 C, with momentary peaks above 1300 C, at enormous fireline intensity.
It is the wildland fire at full power: not the surface front but the sheet of flame overtopping the forest, driven by wind and slope through a continuous, dense canopy.

## Direction

Overhead and forward, the canopy carrying the fire tree to tree.
Crown fire advances as a coupled front -- surface and crown together in the active case -- throwing a towering convection column, drawing its own in-draft, and casting firebrands far ahead to seed new fires. It needs canopy bulk density above a threshold and strong wind or steep slope; below that it drops back to surface fire, above it it makes its own weather.
The direction is escalation: from a torch, to a run, to the plume-dominated column that spots kilometres downwind and dictates its own behaviour.

## Lever

The continuous canopy and driving wind against every line that would hold it.
The carrying force is the dense, dry, wind-driven canopy feeding the fire faster than the surface alone could; the resistance, and the trap, is that past a certain intensity the fire outruns suppression -- the radiant heat and spread rate forbid direct attack, and aerial drops largely evaporate or disperse above such heat, so water thrown at a crown fire is spent on air.
Give it a continuous canopy and wind and it runs beyond any hand; break the canopy (fuel breaks, thinning, lighter fuels ahead) or lose the wind and it falls back to a fire that can be fought.

## Echo

The forest crowned out, black to the ground, and the column that seeded fires beyond it.
Crown fire leaves the canopy gone and the stand killed, and it is read by its signature -- a wall of flame above the trees, a deep freight-train roar, dark brown-grey smoke towering into pyrocumulus, ember rain downwind. It hands the run's extreme behaviour to the spotting that outflanks every line and the plume that couples to the atmosphere.
Crown fire is dated from the climb, the moment the surface fire reached the canopy and the forest began to burn from the top.
