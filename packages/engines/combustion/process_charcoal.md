# Process: Charcoal Burn

## Taxonomy

[Combustion](process_combustion.md), the made fire starved of air so wood chars instead of burning to ash.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: combustion

## Initiated by

Wood is stacked and covered and lit at its core, then held in starved air so a small part burns to drive the rest to char, not ash.
Charcoal burn -- carbonisation, the earth-mound clamp or retort kiln -- burns a small portion of the wood in starved air to drive the pyrolysis of the rest: drying below 200 C, carbonisation beginning near 280 C and turning exothermic at 300-400 C, finished at 400-500 C, yielding fixed-carbon charcoal, wood gas, pyroligneous acid, and tar. In a clamp, cordwood is stacked around a central chimney, covered with turf and earth, and lit at the core.
It is fire held on a leash: a burn deliberately kept too poor in air to consume its own fuel.

## Direction

Inward and downward through the stack at the collier's chosen pace, set by the draft holes.
Charcoal burn is throttled by opening and closing draft holes -- the whole craft is controlling air so the wood chars rather than burns to ash; a retort instead seals the wood and heats it from outside. The escalation is any vent left too open, which lets the char catch and burn away.
The direction is a governed smoulder: carbonisation walked through the pile at exactly the air the collier allows.

## Lever

The starved air that makes charcoal against the open vent that burns it to nothing.
The carrying force is a pile kept just alight in poor air; the resistance fails because success means not letting it burn -- one over-open vent admits air and the charge combusts to worthless ash, a blown pile, so colliers slept beside the meiler to catch breakthroughs -- and the finished charcoal is pyrophoric, warm fresh char reigniting in air unless sealed and cooled first. The fire must be kept alive and starved at once.
Hold the air low and it makes charcoal; let it breathe and the same fire eats the charcoal it just made.

## Echo

The near-flameless mound, the vent smoke turning from white steam to blue haze.
Charcoal burn is read by its signature -- almost no flame, only vent smoke: white and blue steam early, thick white at peak, thinning to a faint blue haze that signals the char is made, with a tar and sour pyroligneous-acid odour, and quiet. It hands its product to the sealed char and its danger to the reignition of that char in air.
Charcoal burn is dated from the lighting, the moment the core was fired to char the pile without consuming it.
