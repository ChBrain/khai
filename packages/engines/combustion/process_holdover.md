# Process: Holdover Fire

## Taxonomy

[Combustion](process_combustion.md), the wildland phenomenon of the fire that overwinters and re-emerges.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: combustion

## Initiated by

A fire declared out keeps smouldering, hidden and insulated, and comes back the next season.
Holdover fire -- the overwintering or "zombie" fire -- starts as a ground, peat, duff, or root smoulder that survives from one fire season to the next, glowing at roughly 450-650 C in organic soil or root layers shielded from snow and cold by the overlying peat and duff.
It is not a distinct combustion but a temporal one: the subsurface smoulder read across time, the fire that outlasts the season that lit it.

## Direction

Down through the frost, dormant in the cold, up again when the surface dries.
Holdover fire persists below the frost and snow layer through winter, then re-emerges to flaming when spring dries the surface fuels and air reaches it. It is most prevalent in deep-organic boreal and peatland systems and after severe prior-season burns -- a recognised but quantitatively uncertain source of early-season ignitions, an active research area rather than a firm figure.
The direction is return: a fire crossing the winter unseen and standing back up where everyone believed it dead.

## Lever

The insulation of the deep organic layer against the belief that the fire is out.
The carrying force is a smoulder buried deep enough to survive winter on retained heat; the resistance fails because the fire is easy to declare out in autumn while it continues under the snow, monitoring and re-detection are difficult, and re-detection often comes only after re-ignition -- the same deep-saturation problem as peat and ground fire, compounded by frozen, snow-covered access.
Call it out in the cold and it proves you wrong in the thaw; only rewetting and monitoring the deep layer through the winter truly ends it.

## Echo

The new season's first fire that was last season's, never actually extinguished.
Holdover fire is read by its signature -- through winter, faint smoke or melt-holes in the snow and warm ground, the melt rings diagnostic; on re-emergence it takes on ordinary surface-fire behaviour and is mistaken for a fresh start. It hands the lesson that a wildland fire is not out until the deep organic layer is cold.
Holdover fire is dated from the surviving, the winter the buried smoulder crossed to burn again.
