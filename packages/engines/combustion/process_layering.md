# Process: Thermal Layering

## Taxonomy

[Combustion](process_combustion.md), the compartment phenomenon of the two-layer hot-and-cool structure.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: combustion

## Initiated by

Buoyancy sorts a burning room into a hot upper layer and a cooler lower one, with a plane between.
Thermal layering starts as combustion gases stratify -- hot products (300-800 C and above, carbon monoxide and hydrogen cyanide concentrated) riding above cooler air below. At a vertical opening the neutral plane is the height where inside and outside pressures equalise: hot gas flows out above it, fresh air in below.
It is the structure a compartment fire organises itself into, and the gauge a firefighter reads it by.

## Direction

Up and down into two layers, the plane between reporting the fire's state.
Layering works by density difference; the neutral plane's height reports the ventilation state, and a lowering plane signals worsening conditions and imminent rollover or flashover. Composition stratifies too -- the toxic and flammable gases pool aloft.
The direction is a legible order: the clearer air below and dense smoke above, the interface a live readout of how the fire is going.

## Lever

The stratification that keeps a survivable lower zone against the stream that destroys it.
The carrying force is buoyancy holding the layers apart, preserving a lower zone occupants and crews can survive in; the resistance fails when a straight stream is put into the layer -- steam and turbulence mix the hot gases down, collapsing the stratification and scalding everyone below. Pulsed fog and nozzle discipline preserve the layer; wrong application collapses it.
Read and cool the layer gently and the survivable zone holds; hit it with a straight stream and you bring the fire's heat to the floor.

## Echo

The room read at a glance by the height and behaviour of its smoke interface.
Thermal layering is read by its signature -- a visible sharp boundary between clearer air below and dense smoke above, the plane's height and stability telling the story; a turbulent, dropping, or vanishing layer means rollover or flashover is near. It hands the crew the single most useful reading of a compartment fire.
Thermal layering is dated from the stratification, the moment the room sorted into the two layers a fire is fought by.
