# Process: Kiln Firing

## Taxonomy

[Combustion](process_combustion.md), the made fire held to a curve to transform mineral ware.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: combustion

## Initiated by

Ware is brought up through a controlled temperature curve so heat drives an irreversible mineral change without cracking or slumping it.
Kiln firing burns wood, gas, oil, or runs electric, under natural or forced draft, to transform ware: earthenware at 1000-1150 C, stoneware at 1200-1300 C, porcelain near 1400 C, lime decomposing calcium carbonate near 825 C, cement clinkering near 1450 C in a 1900-2000 C flame -- all endothermic mineral transformations. It runs a firing curve: ramp, soak, and controlled cooling.
It is fire as a slow transformation: a burn whose whole art is the path of temperature, not its peak.

## Direction

Up the ramp, held at the soak, then down through the cooling, atmosphere set at the damper.
Kiln firing follows a firing curve where the cooling matters as much as the peak, the damper and burner setting the draft and the atmosphere -- oxidation with excess air, or reduction, fuel-rich and deliberately starving oxygen to pull it from clay and glaze and change the colour chemistry; lime and cement kilns run continuously counter-current. The escalation is any part of the curve taken too fast.
The direction is a governed climb and descent: the transformation walked up to heat and back down without shock.

## Lever

The controlled curve against the thermal shock that cracks the ware on the way up or down.
The carrying force is heat driving the mineral change; the resistance fails because heating and cooling too fast both destroy -- thermal shock cracks the ware, and the quartz inversions near 573 C and 226 C are danger points on the way down -- while over-firing slumps the ware to a puddle, under-firing leaves it porous, and an unstable cement flame forms rings that block the kiln. The peak is not the risk; the path to and from it is.
Walk the curve and the ware transforms whole; rush the ramp or the cooling and the same heat shatters it.

## Echo

The interior climbing from dull red through orange to white, the cones bending, the burner roar.
Kiln firing is read by its signature -- an interior dull-red near 600 C climbing through orange to white-hot above 1300 C, judged by colour and by the slumping of pyrometric cones, wood firing giving smoke and fly-ash, a reduction fire showing yellow flame at the spy holes and a rich smoky smell, the burner's roar. It hands its outcome to the transformed ware, or to the crack and the slump.
Kiln firing is dated from the ignition, the moment the ware began its climb up the curve.
