# Process: Boilover

## Taxonomy

[Combustion](process_combustion.md), the industrial phenomenon of a burning tank erupting when its heat wave reaches water.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: combustion

## Initiated by

A long tank fire drives a hot zone down through the oil until it reaches the water bottom and flashes it to steam.
Boilover starts in an open-top storage tank of a wide-boiling-range fuel (crude, some heavy fuels) over a water bottom; a surface fire heats the oil and a downward-moving heat wave forms, its surface 150-300 C and above, until it reaches the tank-bottom water, which flash-boils at roughly 1700 times expansion and violently ejects burning oil over the rim. Slopover is the milder frothing of cooling water on the hot surface; frothover is the same steam expansion without combustion.
It is a delayed eruption: a stable-looking tank fire that, hours in, throws itself over the countryside.

## Direction

Up and out over the rim as a fireball and a rain of burning oil, hundreds of metres.
Boilover self-times hours after ignition -- the heat wave descends at its own rate until it meets the water -- and then hurls burning fuel far beyond the tank, so tank-farm fires are fought from long distance behind bunds. The escalation is the eruption, not the steady burn that precedes it.
The direction is a patient descent ending in a sudden throw: the fire loading its magazine for hours, then firing it all at once.

## Lever

The descending heat wave against the water that is both the trigger and a hazard on the surface.
The carrying force is a hot zone sinking toward the tank bottom; the resistance fails because water is doubly dangerous -- the bottom water is the trigger, and water on the hot surface causes slopover -- so the only safe response is non-disturbing foam blanketing plus patience and a wide cleared radius. There is no cooling the heat wave out of the oil in time.
Wait behind the bund and the eruption falls short of you; stand close or add water and the tank throws its fire over your head.

## Echo

The swelling humming surface, then a rumble and a fountain of burning oil far beyond the tank.
Boilover is read by its signature -- an increasing intensity, a swelling and humming bubbling surface, then a sudden rumble and a massive fountain of burning oil, heavy black smoke throughout. It hands its danger not to the tank but to everything the ejected fuel lands on.
Boilover is dated from the eruption, the moment the heat wave reached the water and the tank threw itself open.
