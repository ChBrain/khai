# Process: Jet Fire

## Taxonomy

[Combustion](process_combustion.md), the industrial phenomenon of a pressurised release burning as a torch.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: combustion

## Initiated by

A pressurised leak ignites and burns as a roaring, directional flame like a blowtorch.
Jet fire starts at a loss of containment under pressure -- a flange, seal, or relief releasing gas, flashing liquid, or two-phase -- igniting as a momentum-driven turbulent diffusion flame; it burns at roughly 1200-1450 C with localised heat flux up to 250-350 kW/m2, the highest of common industrial fires.
It is fire under pressure: not a pool but a jet, its shape and reach set by the release that feeds it.

## Direction

Along the release, persistent and stabbing, toward whatever it impinges on.
Jet fire is highly directional and stubbornly persistent, its length scaling with the release rate to tens of metres; its escalation danger is flame impingement on adjacent vessels and pipework, driving them toward BLEVE or further rupture.
The direction is a sustained lance: a flame that does not spread so much as point, heating hard whatever stands in its path.

## Lever

The pressure that feeds it against the intuition that the flame should be put out.
The carrying force is a pressurised source that will not stop feeding the flame; the resistance fails in the signature paradox -- do not extinguish a gas jet fire; isolate the source and shut the valve, because a burning jet is safer than an extinguished one that keeps releasing and re-accumulates into an unconfined cloud (flash fire or vapour cloud explosion). Firewater cools the exposures, not the flame.
Put the jet out without stopping the flow and you trade a flame for an explosion; shut the valve and the flame starves itself.

## Echo

The roaring directional torch, cooling the steel around it or killing it.
Jet fire is read by its signature -- an intense roaring, whistling directional flame with a lifted base, a blue root and luminous tip, or (for hydrogen) an almost colourless daylight flame, a notorious invisibility hazard, and a continuous jet-engine roar. It hands its heat to the vessel it impinges and, through that, to the BLEVE it can trigger.
Jet fire is dated from the release, the moment the pressurised leak found an ignition and pointed its flame.
