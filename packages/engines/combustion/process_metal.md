# Process: Metal Fire

## Taxonomy

[Combustion](process_combustion.md), the industrial phenomenon of a metal burning as its own fuel.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: combustion

## Initiated by

A combustible metal ignites and oxidises exothermically, stripping oxygen from the very agents sent to smother it.
Metal fire -- Class D -- burns the metal itself as fuel: magnesium flaming near 3100 C, capped by the vaporisation of its own oxide, titanium and zirconium of the same order, sodium with a surface near 800 C but violently water-reactive; many react with water, CO2, and even nitrogen, taking oxygen back out of would-be extinguishants. Chips, turnings, and powders ignite readily; fine powders can transition to metal-dust explosions.
It is fire that eats its rescue: the more common agents you throw, the more fuel and oxygen you feed it.

## Direction

Hotter and self-sustaining, sputtering molten metal, toward dust-explosion if the fines loft.
Metal fire, once lit, sustains itself at extreme temperature; alkali metals ignite on moisture, titanium and zirconium fines are pyrophoric, and fine powder clouds can deflagrate. The escalation is from a burning pile to a molten pool to, with the fines airborne, a metal-dust explosion.
The direction is intensification by attack: an oxidation so hot it reduces the agents meant to stop it.

## Lever

The metal's hunger for oxygen against the water and CO2 that carry it.
The carrying force is a metal oxidising at temperatures that decompose everything near it; the resistance fails because water is catastrophic -- decomposed to hydrogen, flashed to steam that ejects molten metal -- and CO2 is reduced, the metal burning in it, and ABC powder and foam fail. Only Class D powders (graphite, sodium chloride, copper for lithium) that smother and conduct heat, applied gently, or dry sand and burn-out under isolation, work.
Hit it with water and you hand it hydrogen and an explosion; blanket it gently with the right dry powder and it suffocates in its own heat.

## Echo

The blinding white or coloured flare, the dense oxide fume, the spitting sparks.
Metal fire is read by its signature -- magnesium a blinding white with a retina hazard, sodium an intense yellow, lithium crimson, barium green, copper blue-green, strontium red, with a dense white metal-oxide fume and spitting, sparking, sputtering. It hands its light and its heat with a violence that the wrong agent only multiplies.
Metal fire is dated from the ignition, the moment the metal itself began to burn as fuel.
