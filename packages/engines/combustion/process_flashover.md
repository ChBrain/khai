# Process: Flashover

## Taxonomy

[Combustion](process_combustion.md), the compartment phenomenon of the whole room igniting at once.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: combustion

## Initiated by

A growing room fire heats every surface toward its ignition point until, in a few seconds, they all light together.
Flashover starts when a compartment's upper gas layer reaches roughly 500-600 C and the radiant heat flux at floor level reaches roughly 15-20 kW/m2 -- the threshold at which every exposed pyrolysing surface in the room ignites nearly simultaneously and gas temperatures spike toward 900-1100 C.
It is not an event but a rapid transition: the room crossing, in seconds, from a fire in it to a fire that is all of it.

## Direction

Outward across every surface, all at once, an order-of-magnitude jump in heat release.
Flashover works by accumulated radiant flux driving every surface above its pyrolysis temperature together; it needs enough ventilation to reach the threshold -- an underventilated room may never flash over, trending toward backdraft instead. It is preceded by rollover across the ceiling and a rapidly dropping neutral plane.
The direction is total involvement: the survivable room becomes, between one second and the next, an unsurvivable one.

## Lever

The radiant feedback of the hot layer against the ventilation that either feeds or starves it.
The carrying force is the growing upper layer radiating onto the fuel until all of it pyrolyses at once; the resistance, and the trap, is that ventilating or opening a door to improve conditions can supply the missing air and trigger the very flashover it was meant to prevent -- so the counter is pre-flashover gas cooling (short pulsed water fog into the layer), not a straight stream at the seat.
Read the layer and cool it and the transition is held; open the room for relief and you complete it.

## Echo

The room fully involved, non-survivable to any unprotected occupant instantly.
Flashover is read by its signature -- flames rolling across the ceiling then the entire room igniting, light flaring orange-white, the neutral plane dropping to the floor, a loud sustained roar. It is lethal even to protected firefighters, and it hands the compartment to the fully developed, ventilation-controlled burn.
Flashover is dated from the transition, the seconds in which the fire stopped being in the room and became the room.
