# Process: Pyrophoric Ignition

## Taxonomy

[Combustion](process_combustion.md), the industrial phenomenon of a substance igniting on mere contact with air.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: combustion

## Initiated by

A pyrophoric substance meets air or moisture and ignites at once, with no ignition source at all.
Pyrophoric ignition needs no spark -- exposure is the trigger: white phosphorus, alkyl-aluminiums and organometallics like triethylaluminium, silane, finely divided metals such as Raney nickel and refinery iron-sulfide scale, some hydrides, all igniting on contact with atmospheric oxygen (or water for the water-reactive class) at or below roughly 54 C.
It is fire that waits on nothing but exposure: a material for which opening the container is the ignition.

## Direction

Instant flame or glow on contact, self-sustaining, wherever the air reaches it.
Pyrophoric ignition is instantaneous and self-sustaining once exposed; refinery iron-sulfide scale self-heats and ignites when vessels are opened to air during maintenance, a turnaround hazard, and silane can detonate. The escalation is simply more surface meeting more air.
The direction is ignition-on-contact: no delay to interrupt, the reaction beginning the instant the substance is uncovered.

## Lever

The air that is the trigger against the water that can make it worse.
The carrying force is a substance that oxidises the moment it is exposed; the resistance fails because there is no ignition source to remove, water worsens the water-reactive class and only temporarily submerges white phosphorus, which reignites on drying -- so air exclusion is the game, inert blanketing and storage under oil or water as appropriate, plus Class D powder or dry sand, with the agent choice substance-specific and getting it wrong (water on alkyl-aluminium) reacting violently. You cannot remove the spark that was never there; you can only keep the air away.
Let the air in and it is already burning; seal it under an inert blanket and it has nothing to react with.

## Echo

The sudden flame on exposure, the white fume, the garlic or the pop.
Pyrophoric ignition is read by its signature -- a sudden flame or glow on exposure, white phosphorus giving dense white smoke and a garlic odour, organometallics flashing with white-grey fume, silane detonating, often a puff or pop on air contact. It hands its danger to the moment of opening, when the substance meets the atmosphere.
Pyrophoric ignition is dated from the exposure, the moment air first reached the substance and it lit.
