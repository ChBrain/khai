# Process: Swidden

## Taxonomy

[Combustion](process_combustion.md), the made fire that clears forest to farm its ash.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: combustion

## Initiated by

A patch of forest is felled, dried through a season, and burned so its nutrients fall as ash onto the ground to be planted.
Swidden -- slash-and-burn, shifting cultivation, milpa, chitemene -- burns felled, sun-dried biomass in the open at 400-900 C; the agronomic point is the ash, a pulse of potassium, calcium, magnesium, and phosphorus and a pH rise that limes acid tropical soil, though the nitrogen is largely volatilised and lost. The patch is cut, dried through the dry season, and burned at its driest for the most complete consumption and the richest ash.
It is fire used as a plough and a fertiliser at once: a burn whose product is the fertile ash it leaves for the crop.

## Direction

Across the cut patch, hottest and most complete at the driest point, edges held by firebreaks.
Swidden is burned at the driest point of the season for the fullest consumption, with edges cut as firebreaks; a hotter, drier burn releases more nutrients but risks escape. The escalation is the fire crossing its cut edge into standing forest.
The direction is a deliberate reduction to ash: the more complete the burn, the richer the seedbed and the greater the risk.

## Lever

The ash that fertilises against the fallow that must be long enough to renew it.
The carrying force is a hot clean burn releasing the biomass as ash; the resistance fails when it is worked too hard -- sustainable only at low density with long fallows, and shortened fallows flip it so the fire that fertilises now impoverishes, mining nutrients and inviting grass invasion, while escaped swidden fires drive tropical deforestation haze. The same fire renews the land or strips it, depending on how long the forest is given back.
Give the fallow its years and the ash feeds the crop; shorten it and the same burn mines the ground toward ruin.

## Echo

The large orange front, the heavy landscape haze, the woodsmoke and green-vegetation note.
Swidden is read by its signature -- a large orange front, heavy grey-white to brown landscape smoke that hazes a region, woodsmoke with a green-vegetation note early in the burn. It hands its danger to the escape past the firebreak and to the regional haze it feeds.
Swidden is dated from the burn, the moment the dried patch was lit to become a field.
