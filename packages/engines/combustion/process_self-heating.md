# Process: Self-Heating

## Taxonomy

[Combustion](process_combustion.md), the industrial phenomenon of a bulk pile oxidising to its own ignition.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: combustion

## Initiated by

A large pile of organic matter oxidises slowly at ambient temperature, holds the heat it makes, and climbs to autoignition with no flame to start it.
Self-heating -- spontaneous combustion -- runs in bulk organics that oxidise exothermically at ambient temperature: oil-soaked rags of linseed and drying oils, hay and silage (also microbial), coal, lignite, biomass pellets; air is the oxidiser and the trigger is heat retention, not a spark.
It is a fire with no ignition event: a heap that lights itself because it cannot shed the heat of its own slow oxidation.

## Direction

Inward and upward in temperature over hours to weeks, into deep-seated smoulder that flames when opened.
Self-heating generates oxidation and, in hay, microbial heat faster than a large insulated pile can shed it; the internal temperature climbs over hours to weeks to autoignition -- low surface-to-volume favours it -- ending in a smoulder that flames when opened to air. The escalation is silent until the pile is disturbed.
The direction is an internal creep to ignition: the centre of the pile heating itself in the dark.

## Lever

The insulating bulk that holds the heat against the digging that would admit air.
The carrying force is a pile generating heat faster than it loses it; the resistance fails because breaking the pile apart admits air and turns smoulder into open fire, yet water often cannot penetrate a compacted mass and the deep-seated smoulder reignites -- so the response is careful cooling, wetting-agent penetration, controlled small-quantity removal, and monitoring, never aggressive digging. The mass is both the cause and the shield.
Dig it out to reach the heat and you give it the air to flame; cool and probe it slowly and it never gets its breath.

## Echo

The warm surface, the steam and the burnt-sugar odour before any flame.
Self-heating is read by its signature -- a warm-to-hot surface, steam or vapour and a musty, tarry, caramel burnt-sugar odour before flame, internal charring on probing, often no visible fire until the deep-seated smoulder surfaces. It hands its heat inward for hours before it ever shows a flame.
Self-heating is dated from the smoulder, the point the pile's core reached ignition on its own.
