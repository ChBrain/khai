# Process: Tunnel Fire

## Taxonomy

[Combustion](process_combustion.md), the compartment phenomenon of fire in a long confined tube.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: combustion

## Initiated by

A vehicle burns inside a tube that can neither shed its heat nor its smoke.
Tunnel fire starts with a burning vehicle -- a heavy goods load can release tens to hundreds of megawatts -- in a confined bore where the rock and lining reflect the heat back and the smoke has only the tube to fill; the Mont Blanc tunnel fire of 1999 killed thirty-nine people, most in their vehicles, kilometres from the burning truck.
It is the compartment fire stretched into a line: one fire, one duct, and everyone in the duct sharing its air.

## Direction

Along the tube with the ventilation, and back against it when the airflow is too weak.
Tunnel fire drives its smoke down the bore with whatever longitudinal airflow exists; below a critical velocity the hot layer backlayers upstream over the heads of those escaping, and a stratified, survivable smoke layer soon mixes down to fill the whole section -- so distance from the fire is no safety, only distance along the smoke's path.
The direction is one-dimensional and total: everything the tunnel holds is either upstream or downstream of the fire, and the ventilation decides which is which.

## Lever

The ventilation against the fire it feeds and the people it chooses between.
The carrying force is confinement -- heat feedback raising the burning rate, one axis for all the smoke; the resistance fails in the ventilation dilemma: the airflow that clears the escape route upstream fans the fire and delivers the smoke to everyone downstream, and reversing or stopping it re-deals the smoke to the other side -- a control decision that allocates the danger rather than removing it.
Vent hard and you choose who gets the smoke; vent too little and it backlayers over the escape; the fire is fought first at the fan controls, not the nozzle.

## Echo

A line of burned-out vehicles in a soot-black bore.
Tunnel fire is read by its signature -- smoke pouring from one portal while the other exhales heat-shimmer, the roar ducted down the tube, and afterwards the queue of gutted vehicles in place, spalled lining overhead -- the record of where the smoke was sent.
Tunnel fire is dated from the moment the smoke owned the bore, when the tube stopped being a road and became the fire's duct.
