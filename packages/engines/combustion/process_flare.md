# Process: Flare

## Taxonomy

[Combustion](process_combustion.md), the made fire kept always burning to destroy waste gas safely.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: combustion

## Initiated by

Waste and relief gas is burned off at the top of a stack in the open air, kept continuously alight so it never releases raw.
Flare -- the flare stack, gas flaring -- burns waste or relief hydrocarbon gas in open air as a diffusion flame at 1000-1200 C and above, with steam or air assist for smokeless burning, converting methane and toxic hydrogen sulfide to carbon dioxide, sulfur dioxide, and water at an ideal efficiency above 98 percent that drops in high wind or low flow. A continuous pilot ensures ignition, and a knock-out drum and water seal guard against liquids and flashback.
It is fire kept as a safety valve: a burn held always alight so the gas is destroyed rather than vented.

## Direction

Up and out at the tip, turbulent with steam to burn clean, visible for miles.
Flare adds steam or air injection at the tip for turbulence and air entrainment to suppress soot, the knock-out drum removing liquids and the water or molecular seal preventing flashback down the stack. The escalation is loss of the pilot, or over-steaming that quenches the flame.
The direction is a continuous open burn: a flame held at the tip so nothing leaves the stack unburned.

## Lever

The always-lit flame against the pilot loss that vents raw toxic gas.
The carrying force is a continuously burning tip; the resistance fails because a flare must never go out with gas still flowing -- an unlit flare vents raw methane and hydrogen sulfide, an invisible explosive and toxic cloud, so pilot loss is the emergency, not the flame -- while over-steaming to kill smoke can quench the flame into the same unburned release, and flashback down the stack can detonate the header. The flame is the safe state; its absence is the disaster.
Keep it lit and the waste gas is destroyed; let the pilot fail and the stack vents the very cloud the flame was there to burn.

## Echo

The orange-yellow luminous flame, the roar, the rotten-egg trace of hydrogen sulfide.
Flare is read by its signature -- an orange-yellow luminous flame turning smokeless and quieter when over-steamed or smoking black when under-steamed, a roaring rumble, hydrogen sulfide smelling of rotten eggs at trace then deadening the sense at lethal concentration, visible for miles at night. It hands its danger to the moment the flame is lost with gas still flowing.
Flare is dated from the lighting, the moment the pilot set the stack burning to keep the gas from venting raw.
