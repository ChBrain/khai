# Process: Pyrocumulonimbus

## Taxonomy

[Combustion](process_combustion.md), the phenomenon of the fire that makes its own thunderstorm.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: combustion

## Initiated by

A fire's convection column punches high enough that its moisture condenses and a thunderstorm grows on top of it.
Pyrocumulonimbus -- pyroCb -- starts when a vigorous plume reaches the condensation level and water vapour, from the fire and the ambient air, condenses on soot and ash nuclei; the latent heat released reinvigorates the updraft, and a deep-convective storm builds above the fire.
It is the fire become weather: a storm the fire has manufactured over itself.

## Direction

Up to the tropopause, then down as lightning, downdraft, and new fire.
Pyrocumulonimbus grows by positive feedback -- latent heat driving the updraft higher, cloud tops reaching roughly 10-16 km -- and it makes lightning (new ignitions), strong downdrafts and outflow, and can inject smoke into the lower stratosphere; it can spawn fire tornadoes beneath it.
The direction is a self-made storm system: the fire's plume returning to the ground as fresh ignitions and violent, shifting winds.

## Lever

The storm the fire has made against any hand that would fight it.
The carrying force is a plume deep enough to trigger deep convection; the resistance is that it is beyond suppression entirely -- it is weather the fire has made. Its downdrafts reverse the fire's spread direction unpredictably and endanger crews and aircraft, its lightning starts fires far downwind, and its column collapse washes embers and hot gas back to the surface.
There is nothing to extinguish; you can only get clear of the weather it has become.

## Echo

The cauliflower cloud over the smoke column, its lightning starting fires miles away.
Pyrocumulonimbus is read by its signature -- a cauliflower cloud capping a smoke column, grey-brown at the base grading to a white anvil aloft, distant thunder and lightning, abrupt wind shifts and downdraft gusts, ash fallout. It hands the fire an atmosphere of its own making, and the landscape downwind new ignitions.
Pyrocumulonimbus is dated from the condensation, the moment the fire's plume grew a storm on its own head.
