# Process: Candle

## Taxonomy

[Combustion](process_combustion.md), the made fire that feeds itself just enough wax to burn.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: combustion

## Initiated by

A flame melts a little wax, draws it up the wick, and vaporises it to burn as gas, feeding itself exactly enough to keep going.
Candle -- the wick, the diffusion flame -- burns paraffin, beeswax, or soy where the solid is not what burns: heat melts the wax, capillarity draws it up the wick, and it vaporises and cracks to burn as gas in a diffusion flame, zoned from a cool base through a vaporising dark inner and a luminous yellow soot glow near 1000 C to a faint blue outer near 1400 C. The incandescent soot gives the light.
It is fire self-regulated to a small steady flame: a burn that melts just enough of its own fuel to keep itself alight.

## Direction

Up in a teardrop drawn by convection, self-trimming, self-feeding.
Candle is self-regulating -- the flame melts just enough wax to feed itself -- the braided wick curling to self-trim, convection drawing air up to give the teardrop shape (round in microgravity). The escalation is a draft, a tunnelled pool, or an untrimmed wick that unbalances the steady feed.
The direction is a self-sustaining small flame: fuel and air balanced so it neither floods nor starves.

## Lever

The self-feeding flame against the vaporised-wax trail that carries fire back to a blown-out wick.
The carrying force is a flame feeding itself just enough wax; the resistance shows in a relighting paradox -- blow it out and the white smoke is vaporised wax, and a flame touched to that trail can travel down and reignite the wick without contact -- while tunnelling, guttering in a draft, and a mushrooming untrimmed wick are the domestic failure modes, and a knocked-over candle is a common house-fire ignition. The extinguished flame leaves a path back to itself.
Trim it and shield it and it burns steady; blow it out near a flame and the wax-smoke carries the fire back down to the wick.

## Echo

The yellow luminous flame on a blue base, the curl of soot, the burnt-wick smell on extinguishing.
Candle is read by its signature -- a yellow luminous flame with a blue base, a thin curl of soot if it gutters, a wax odour, a puff of pungent vaporised-wax smoke and the burnt-wick smell on extinguishing, near-silent. It hands its danger to the draft, the untrimmed wick, and the knock-over.
Candle is dated from the lighting, the moment the wick was set to draw and burn its own wax.
