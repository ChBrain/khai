# Process: Polymer Fire

## Taxonomy

[Combustion](process_combustion.md), the industrial phenomenon of a plastic burning with lethal smoke.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: combustion

## Initiated by

A synthetic polymer burns as an ordinary solid but off-gases combustion products that kill long before the flame does.
Polymer fire burns PVC, polyurethane foam, polystyrene, polyethylene, ABS, nylon, and epoxies as air-breathing solids, mostly Class A with some dripping as B; the defining hazard is the highly toxic pyrolysis and combustion gas -- carbon monoxide the dominant killer, hydrogen cyanide from nitrogen-containing polyurethane, nylon, and ABS, hydrogen chloride from PVC, plus phosgene, nitrogen oxides, acrolein, styrene, and dense soot -- at high heat release rate.
It is a fire whose flame is the least of it: an ordinary burn wrapped around a cloud of poison.

## Direction

Up as flame and, for thermoplastics, down as flaming drips, with the smoke going everywhere.
Polymer fire spreads by high heat release that drives room flashover, especially with polyurethane foam, while many thermoplastics melt and drip flaming, carrying fire downward as a Class-B-like running hazard, and thermosets char; smouldering polymer off-gasses toxic, oxygen-depleted, ignitable smoke that fuels backdraft. The escalation is fast flashover and a spreading toxic atmosphere.
The direction is a double reach: flame up and drips down, and a killing smoke through every opening.

## Lever

The easily quenched flame against the smoke that is the real fire.
The carrying force is an air-breathing solid; the resistance is not the problem -- extinguishment is usually easy with water or foam -- and the lethal problem is the smoke, not the flame: hydrogen cyanide and carbon monoxide act synergistically and incapacitate before burns do, hydrogen chloride is corrosive, and dripping thermoplastics spread fire past a knocked-down front. Doctrine is SCBA always, aggressive ventilation, all smoke treated as toxic gas, and care with water on molten pools that splash burning polymer.
Knock the flame down easily and the danger stays in the air; the fire you must fight is the one you breathe.

## Echo

The dense greasy black smoke, the acrid sting, the flaming drips.
Polymer fire is read by its signature -- dense greasy black smoke, PVC giving a white-grey acrid smoke with a sharp hydrogen-chloride sting, polyurethane and nylon an acrid bitter-almond note of hydrogen cyanide, sweetish styrene and melting-plastic odours, dripping flaming beads. It hands its danger to the smoke it makes, not the flame it shows.
Polymer fire is dated from the ignition, the moment the polymer began to burn and to gas.
