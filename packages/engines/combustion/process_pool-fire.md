# Process: Pool Fire

## Taxonomy

[Combustion](process_combustion.md), the industrial phenomenon of a liquid burning as a pool.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: combustion

## Initiated by

A flammable liquid spreads across a surface and burns from its face as a steady sheet of flame.
Pool fire starts wherever a combustible liquid -- petrol, oil, solvent, LNG -- burns as a horizontal pool, a diffusion flame fed by vapour evaporating from the surface, air-breathing and fuel-rich near the liquid; hydrocarbon pools flame at roughly 900-1200 C.
It is the Class B fire at rest: not a spreading front but a burning surface, its rate set by the heat the flame radiates back to the liquid.

## Direction

Up off the surface, steady, as fast as the flame can boil the fuel beneath it.
Pool fire's burning rate is fixed by radiative feedback from flame to liquid (a regression of a few millimetres a minute for large hydrocarbon pools); it burns steady once developed and grows only if the pool spreads, so a bund that contains the liquid contains the fire. Large pools become optically thick and turbulent, puffing.
The direction is a standing burn: the pool consuming itself from the top at a rate the flame's own heat dictates.

## Lever

The vapour blanket that starves it against the water that spreads it.
The carrying force is a liquid evaporating fuel as fast as the flame radiates heat down; the resistance fails because water jets spread the floating burning liquid and can cause slopover on hot oils -- so the correct agent is film-forming or alcohol-resistant foam blanketing the surface to cut the vapour, applied gently, with reflash likely if the blanket breaks.
Blanket the surface with foam and the vapour is cut and the fire dies; hit it with water and the fire floats off across the spill.

## Echo

The spill alight, a rolling sheet of flame and heavy smoke over the bund.
Pool fire is read by its signature -- a luminous orange-yellow flame, heavy black rolling smoke for aromatics and diesel (cleaner and blue-tinged for alcohols and LNG), a roaring crackle, the parent fuel's odour plus acrid partial-combustion products. It hands its heat to the exposures the radiation reaches and, over a hot tank, to a possible boilover.
Pool fire is dated from the spread, the moment the liquid found a surface and began to burn from its face.
