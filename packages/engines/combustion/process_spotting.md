# Process: Spotting

## Taxonomy

[Combustion](process_combustion.md), the wildland phenomenon of the fire that leaps ahead of itself.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: combustion

## Initiated by

The fire throws burning fragments downwind, and new fires start ahead of the front.
Spotting starts wherever a fire's convection column lofts firebrands -- bark plates, cones, needles, twigs, moss, and, at the wildland-urban interface, structural debris -- that smoulder or glow in transit and ignite receptive fine fuels where they land.
It is the fire reproducing at a distance: not the contiguous front but the discontinuous leap, the same fire breaking out beyond itself again and again.

## Direction

Downwind and ahead, from tens of metres to, in extreme cases, kilometres.
Spotting is governed by plume strength, wind, the aerodynamics and combustion life of the brand, and the fine-fuel moisture where it lands; ordinary spotting reaches tens to hundreds of metres, and rare long-range spotting in plume-dominated crown fires can exceed roughly 10 km (an event-specific figure, not a typical one). Spot fires ignite ahead of the front and merge back with intensified behaviour.
The direction is proliferation: one front becoming many, each landing a new ignition the crews must also fight.

## Lever

The lofted firebrand against every firebreak it simply flies over.
The carrying force is a plume strong enough to loft brands and a wind to carry them; the resistance fails because spotting defeats containment by design -- it jumps any break, road, or river, forces crews to fight fires behind their own line, and can trap resources between the main fire and the spot fires. No line width fully guarantees against long-range spotting, and at the interface, embers entering vents and gutters ignite structures hours after the front has passed.
Hold the front and the embers land behind you; the fire arrives where there is no flame front to see.

## Echo

The map of the fire dotted with ignitions the line never touched.
Spotting is read by its signature -- showers of glowing embers arcing downwind, a "snowstorm of fire," multiple new smoke columns appearing ahead of the visible front, the hiss and patter of landing brands. It is the dominant reason large fires escape control, and it hands the interface its ignitions long after the front is gone.
Spotting is dated from the loft, the moment the fire's own column carried its fire beyond any line drawn to hold it.
