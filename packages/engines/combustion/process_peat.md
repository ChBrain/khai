# Process: Peat Fire

## Taxonomy

[Combustion](process_combustion.md), the wildland phenomenon of the flameless organic-soil smoulder.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: combustion

## Initiated by

Fire sinks into the peat and glows on below the surface, without a flame to show for it.
Peat fire starts wherever the organic soil of a bog, moor, or drained wetland ignites -- from a surface fire, lightning, or a human source -- and burns as heterogeneous, oxygen-diffusion-limited smouldering at roughly 450-700 C; it self-sustains even at surprisingly high moisture because peat's energy density and low thermal loss retain the heat.
It is combustion below the flame: the char oxidising slowly underground while the surface looks spared, one of the largest and most persistent fires on Earth.

## Direction

Down and out, on a clock of months, through the deposit.
Peat fire creeps millimetres to centimetres an hour, multi-directional, downward and lateral, following the peat and the water table; it can burn for months to years, overwinter under snow, and re-surface. Drainage and drought are the dominant enablers -- a lowered water table is what lets it run.
The direction is persistence: not a front that passes in hours but a subterranean burn measured in seasons, emitting vast low smoke long after any flame is gone.

## Lever

The heat held in the packed peat against the water that cannot reach or wet it.
The carrying force is a fuel that holds its own heat and needs almost no oxygen; the resistance fails in the signature paradox -- inserting water or foam lances aerates the deposit and accelerates smouldering, surface flooding perches above the deep front, and dried peat is hydrophobic and sheds water, so the surest push toward extinction fans it. True extinction usually requires rewetting the whole peat body (raising the water table), not point suppression.
Try to fight it as a fire and you feed it air; drown the entire bog and only then does it stop.

## Echo

The ground consumed from beneath, subsided into ashy craters, the smoke a regional haze.
Peat fire leaves the soil carbon gone and the surface collapsed, and it is read by its signature -- little or no flame, vast low-lying white-to-yellow-grey smoke of very high particulate load, a musty sulfurous-earthy tarry odour, near-silence, warm hollow ground underfoot. It hands forward the holdover that waits under the snow to re-emerge.
Peat fire is dated from the sinking, the moment the fire left the air for the organic soil and went on without a flame.
