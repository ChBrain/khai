# Process: Aircraft Fire

## Taxonomy

[Combustion](process_combustion.md), the conveyance phenomenon of fire against the clock to land.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: combustion

## Initiated by

A fire starts in an aircraft, and the only extinguisher that matters is the ground.
Aircraft fire ignites in flight -- the wiring arc above the ceiling panels that took Swissair 111 in 1998, the cargo hold that carried its own oxygen in ValuJet 592's chemical generators in 1996 -- or on the ground, as the post-crash pool fire of a full fuel load; in flight it burns hidden behind liners and insulation where no crew can reach it, on a vehicle that cannot stop, open a window, or wait.
It is fire on a deadline: not a battle to extinguish but a race between the descent and the burn-through.

## Direction

Behind the panels, along the wiring and insulation, toward the systems that fly the plane.
Aircraft fire spreads through the concealed spaces -- burning insulation blankets, dripping molten material, tripping bus after bus -- so the aeroplane fails from the inside out, instruments and autopilot going dark in sequence while the cabin still looks whole; on the ground, the fuel fire attacks the fuselage shell against the certified evacuation.
The direction is a countdown: in the air, minutes of controllability; on the ground, the ninety seconds the certification assumes and the minutes of shell the fuselage buys.

## Lever

The descent against the checklist, and the certification against the clock.
The carrying force is a hidden fire with everything it needs -- fuel in the materials, air in the ducting, sometimes oxygen in the cargo -- on a vehicle whose crew cannot see it, reach it, or leave it; the resistance fails in the troubleshooting trap: the orderly diagnosis and fuel-dumping that normal procedure asks for is exactly the time the fire needs, and the lesson written in the Swissair report is land now, land anywhere, land overweight -- the runway is the extinguisher.
Fly the checklist first and the fire outlasts the systems; dive for the nearest runway at the first smoke and the aircraft is on the ground before the fire owns it; once stopped, everything hangs on the ninety-second door-to-slide sprint the certification bought.

## Echo

The hull evacuated on the foam-covered runway, or the wreck that answered no more radio calls.
Aircraft fire is read by its signature -- the smell of hot insulation with nothing visible, smoke from a vent, breakers tripping in a pattern, then instruments dying one by one; on the ground, the black column off the fuel and the slides deployed -- and afterwards by the investigation that reconstructs, minute by minute, when landing immediately would still have been enough.
Aircraft fire is dated from the first smell of smoke, the moment the flight stopped being a journey and became a countdown.
