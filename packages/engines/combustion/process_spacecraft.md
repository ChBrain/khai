# Process: Spacecraft Fire

## Taxonomy

[Combustion](process_combustion.md), the conveyance phenomenon of fire where nothing rises, in the vessel that is the world.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: combustion

## Initiated by

A fire starts in the one place whose air is all the air there is.
Spacecraft fire ignites in a sealed habitat whose atmosphere is engineered -- Apollo 1's ground-test capsule of pure oxygen burned through in seconds in 1967, killing its three crew; Mir's Vika oxygen generator torched for minutes in 1997, a solid canister making its own oxidiser as it burned -- and in orbit it burns without gravity: no buoyancy, so no rising plume, flames folding into dim blue spheres, spread favouring the slow smoulder.
It is combustion in its strangest regime: fire that does not point up, smoke that does not rise, in a vessel no one can step out of.

## Direction

With the ventilation, because there is no up for it to take.
Spacecraft fire moves as the airflow moves -- in still air a microgravity flame chokes on its own combustion products, so the cabin's circulating ventilation is the fire's buoyancy, feeding it oxygen and carrying its smoke everywhere except past the detectors it was designed to meet; NASA's Saffire experiments burned large samples in an uncrewed vehicle to learn exactly how big such a fire grows and how it spreads.
The direction is the duct's: the fan is the wind, the fire follows it, and the first act of suppression is to make the wind stop.

## Lever

The engineered atmosphere against a crew with nowhere to go.
The carrying force is the habitat itself -- the oxygen fraction that sets how everything burns (Apollo 1's pure-oxygen lesson), materials pre-screened because anything flammable aboard is flammable forever, ventilation that doubles as the fire's supply; the resistance fails on the exits: there is no evacuation, no window to vent smoke casually, no brigade -- the crew fight it with the airflow killed and extinguishers designed not to poison the only air they have, and the toxic smoke stays aboard with them after the flame is out.
Kill the fans and a small fire smothers in its own exhaust; let them run and the fire has all the cabin's oxygen on delivery; either way the crew and the fire share one hull and one atmosphere to the end.

## Echo

The cabin saved but its air spent, scrubbing the smoke it cannot open a window on.
Spacecraft fire is read by its signature -- a dim spherical blue where flame should be yellow and tall, smoke pooling where the airflow leaves it rather than at the ceiling, alarms from ducts not doorways -- and afterwards by the long cleanup of a sealed atmosphere: filters, scrubbers, masks, the habitat's own lungs doing what wind does on Earth.
Spacecraft fire is dated from the ignition in the sealed air, the moment the vessel's atmosphere -- all the atmosphere there is -- became the thing burning.
