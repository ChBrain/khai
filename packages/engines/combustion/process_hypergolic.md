# Process: Hypergolic Ignition

## Taxonomy

[Combustion](process_combustion.md), the industrial phenomenon of a fuel and oxidiser igniting on contact.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: combustion

## Initiated by

A fuel and an oxidiser meet and ignite spontaneously, with no spark and a negligible delay.
Hypergolic ignition needs no ignition source -- contact is the event: hydrazine, UDMH, or MMH with nitrogen tetroxide or nitric acid in rocketry, and chlorine trifluoride or fluorine with almost anything; the reaction is self-contained and very high temperature, its millisecond delay a design metric.
It is fire made by mixing: two streams that need only touch, so a leak that lets them meet is already alight.

## Direction

Instant flame at the point of contact, wherever the streams meet.
Hypergolic ignition has no triangle to break -- mixing is the event; leaks that let the streams meet self-ignite, and the strongest oxidisers like chlorine trifluoride ignite otherwise-inert materials, glass, sand, asbestos. The escalation is wherever a fresh interface forms between fuel and oxidiser.
The direction is ignition at the interface: no source to interrupt, the flame beginning exactly where the two streams find each other.

## Lever

The contact that is the ignition against the agent that may itself be fuel.
The carrying force is a fuel and oxidiser that ignite on touch; the resistance fails because there is no ignition source to remove, and for the strongest oxidisers the extinguishing agent itself may be fuel -- chlorine trifluoride reacts with water explosively and ignites sand -- so the response is reactant separation, massive water dilution and deluge for the hydrazine class, and toxic-vapour control and evacuation, with the agent verified compatible first. Reach for the wrong agent and you have added fuel.
Keep the streams apart and there is no fire; let them meet, or fight them with the wrong agent, and the contact is the flame.

## Echo

The instant flame on mixing, the red-brown fume, the sharp ignition crack.
Hypergolic ignition is read by its signature -- instant flame on mixing, nitrogen-tetroxide systems giving red-brown nitrogen-oxide fumes, hydrazines smelling fishy and ammoniacal, a sharp ignition crack, highly toxic vapours. It hands its danger to the interface and to the toxic cloud around it.
Hypergolic ignition is dated from the contact, the moment the fuel and oxidiser first touched and lit.
