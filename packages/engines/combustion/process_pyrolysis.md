# Process: Pyrolysis

## Taxonomy

[Combustion](process_combustion.md), the phenomenon of solids gasifying into the fuel that actually burns.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: combustion

## Initiated by

Heat breaks a solid fuel down into flammable gases before, or instead of, any flame.
Pyrolysis starts wherever a solid fuel is heated to its decomposition temperature -- wood from roughly 200-300 C, many plastics lower -- releasing volatile combustible gases (carbon monoxide, hydrogen, hydrocarbons, aldehydes) and leaving char, before oxidation. The volatiles are the actual gas-phase fuel that burns; the visible flame is burning gas, not the solid.
It is the engine beneath fire: the step that makes a solid into a gas the flame can consume.

## Direction

Off the heated surface and up into the flame, feeding the fire that drives it.
Pyrolysis works by radiant and convective heat raising the surface to its decomposition point; the released volatiles mix with air and ignite above the surface, and flame radiation drives more pyrolysis -- a positive feedback that is the engine of fire growth and of the fuel-rich layer behind backdraft and flashover.
The direction is self-amplification: the fire making more of its own fuel the hotter it gets.

## Lever

The stored heat that keeps gasifying fuel against the flame-knockdown that does not stop it.
The carrying force is a heated solid off-gassing flammable vapour faster the hotter it is; the resistance fails because pyrolysis continues from stored heat after flames are knocked down, re-supplying fuel gas and causing reflash, and surfaces heated but not yet flaming across a room can ignite en masse (the flashover mechanism). Cooling the solid below its pyrolysis temperature -- not just the visible flame -- is what stops fuel production.
Knock the flame down and the hot solid keeps making fuel; cool the solid and the supply stops.

## Echo

The heated-but-unburnt surfaces gasifying, a room charged with fuel before it flames.
Pyrolysis is read by its signature -- smoke and shimmering vapour rising from heated but unburnt surfaces, bubbling and charring, pungent decomposition odours (formaldehyde, acrolein, monomers), hissing and venting of gas from heated material. It hands the fire its fuel and the compartment its charge for rapid fire progression.
Pyrolysis is dated from the decomposition, the moment the solid began to give up the gas the flame would burn.
