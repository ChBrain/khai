# Process: Oxidizer Fire

## Taxonomy

[Combustion](process_combustion.md), the industrial phenomenon of a fire that carries its own oxygen.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: combustion

## Initiated by

A fire draws its oxygen not from the air but from a chemical oxidiser or an oxygen-enriched atmosphere, so it cannot be starved.
Oxidizer fire is fed by nitrates, chlorates, perchlorates, peroxides, or ammonium nitrate, or by an oxygen-enriched atmosphere (medical oxygen, cryogenic LOX); self-oxidising materials -- propellants, nitrocellulose, some explosives -- carry fuel and oxidiser in the same molecule and need no external air, burning at very high rate and temperature.
It is a fire immune to smothering: one that brings its own supply and burns whether or not you cut off the air.

## Direction

Ferociously fast, un-starvable, and in the worst case from fire into detonation.
Oxidizer fire is not oxygen-limited, so it burns ferociously fast, cannot be starved of air, and can burn while sealed or submerged; oxygen enrichment sharply lowers ignition energy and raises flame spread, so materials that barely burn in air flare in LOX, and ammonium nitrate can transition from fire to detonation (Texas City 1947, Beirut 2020). The escalation is toward detonation where the oxidiser is a bulk solid.
The direction is unchecked acceleration: a fire with no oxygen ceiling to slow it.

## Lever

The self-supplied oxygen against the smothering that has nothing to exclude.
The carrying force is an internally carried or enriched oxygen supply; the resistance fails because smothering with CO2, foam, or inerting is useless -- you cannot exclude an oxidiser inside the fuel or displace an oxygen-enriched cloud fast enough -- so only large-volume cooling water to drop below reaction temperature helps, and even that fails on some, plus dilution and evacuation where detonation is credible. There is no oxygen to take away.
Try to smother it and there is nothing to exclude; flood it with cooling water and only the temperature, not the air, is your lever.

## Echo

The intense hard-to-quench flame, the oxidiser's colour, the roaring fast burn.
Oxidizer fire is read by its signature -- intense hard-to-quench flames often carrying the oxidiser's flame colour, a rapid roaring burn, normally slow materials flaring in oxygen-enriched settings, copious fume. It hands its danger to the speed and, where the oxidiser is bulk, to the detonation it can become.
Oxidizer fire is dated from the ignition, the moment the fuel lit against an oxygen supply it carried itself.
