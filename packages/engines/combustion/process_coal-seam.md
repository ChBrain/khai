# Process: Coal-Seam Fire

## Taxonomy

[Combustion](process_combustion.md), the wildland phenomenon of geological fuel alight underground.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: combustion

## Initiated by

A coal seam catches -- from a surface fire, a mine, lightning, or its own self-heating -- and the ground itself becomes the fire.
Coal-seam fire ignites an outcropping or mined seam and burns it underground as an oxygen-limited smoulder-and-glow, fed through cracks, old workings, and the subsidence its own burning opens; Burning Mountain in Australia has burned for roughly six thousand years, creeping about a metre a year, Jharia in India since 1916, Centralia in Pennsylvania since 1962.
It is fire on a geological footing: not a fuel the season laid down but the stored carbon of an epoch, alight, with nothing above it that can wait it out.

## Direction

Along the seam, under the surface, on a clock of decades to millennia.
Coal-seam fire advances metres a year, following the coal; its heat cracks the overburden and collapses the ground above, and every crack and pit becomes a fresh air supply, so the fire slowly builds its own ventilation as it goes.
The direction is patience beyond any human frame: a front that will still be moving when everyone now living near it is gone, venting gas and heat through a landscape it is quietly hollowing.

## Lever

The seam that is its own mine of fuel against an extinguishment that costs more than the land above it.
The carrying force is a fuel bed measured in millions of tonnes, insulated by rock, oxygen-fed through its own subsidence; the resistance fails on cost and access -- trenching and excavation open the very air paths that feed it, sealing is never complete over kilometres of cracked ground, and flooding a seam means flooding a landscape -- so the practical answer at Centralia was to move the town and let it burn.
Dig at it and you ventilate it; seal it and the ground re-cracks; price the extinguishment honestly and relocation wins.

## Echo

The ground abandoned to its own fire, venting where a town used to stand.
Coal-seam fire is read by its signature -- steaming fissures and vents, sulfurous smoke, warm ground and melt-holes in snow, subsidence pits and cracked roads, the smell of the mine with no mine in sight -- and by the emptied land above it, condemned and left.
Coal-seam fire is dated from the ignition that outlived its cause, the seam alight longer than the memory of how.
