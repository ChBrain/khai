# Process: Duff Fire

## Taxonomy

[Combustion](process_combustion.md), the wildland phenomenon of a ground fire smouldering at the limit of self-sustaining combustion.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: combustion

## Initiated by

A surface fire sinks into the forest floor and creeps on as a flameless smoulder through duff laced with mineral soil, held at the very edge of what will burn.
Duff fire is the ground fire of the forest floor -- the fermentation and humus horizons (Oe and Oa) intermixed with inert mineral soil -- burning as heterogeneous, oxygen-transport-limited char oxidation at roughly 450-650 C; unlike deep peat, its mineral fraction is an inert heat sink, so whether it propagates at all is set by the combustion limit in moisture, mineral content, and organic bulk density, and it burns right at that edge.
It is smouldering at the margin: not peat's robust self-sustaining burn but a marginal creep that lives or dies on how much inert soil is mixed through the duff.

## Direction

Down into the duff and outward along the organic-rich seams and burned-out root channels, stalling wherever the mineral rises.
Duff fire creeps by conduction at millimetres to a few centimetres an hour, following the paths richest in organic carbon -- old root channels and buried wood -- where a burned-out root leaves a hollow that feeds it air and speeds it along; it thins and stalls where inert soil dilutes the fuel below the combustion limit, and can break back into flame where it reaches the surface or an open root void.
The direction is a marginal, path-seeking crawl: the fire threading the organic seams and choking on the mineral ones, re-emerging as flame wherever it finds air.

## Lever

The inert mineral that both throttles the fire and hides it against every way of finding and fighting it.
The carrying force is a char-oxidation front that needs almost no oxygen and holds its heat; the resistance fails three ways at once -- the mineral overburden decouples the surface temperature from the front, so it is near-invisible to infrared and aerial scanning; a fire-induced hydrophobic layer and the compacted soil perch suppression water above the smoulder; and the oxygen-transport-limited front is too airless to starve -- so it is neither seen, wetted, nor smothered, and true extinction needs the ground dug out or soaked through. The same inert soil that pushes it toward its limit is what buries its signature.
Scan for it and it is dark; flood it and the water sheds; smother it and it needs no air -- only turning the soil over reaches it.

## Echo

The ground burning unseen, the surface cool, the duff gone to ash pits and hollow root traces.
Duff fire is read by its signature -- little or no flame, a faint thin smoke or none, a cool or barely warm surface over a hot front, a musty earthy char odour, and, after, white ash pits where the organic soil was consumed and hollow channels where roots burned out, the ground collapsing to trap a foot or a wheel. It hands forward the holdover that carries the smoulder through the winter, and the re-ignition wherever the front finds air.
Duff fire is dated from the sinking, the moment the fire left the surface for the forest floor and crept on at the edge of burning.
