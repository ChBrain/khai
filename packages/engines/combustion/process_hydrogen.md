# Process: Hydrogen Fire

## Taxonomy

[Combustion](process_combustion.md), the industrial phenomenon of the invisible flame.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: combustion

## Initiated by

The smallest molecule leaks through fittings nothing else escapes, and ignites on almost nothing.
Hydrogen fire starts at a leak of a gas flammable from roughly 4 to 75 percent in air -- the widest range of the common fuels -- with a minimum ignition energy near 0.02 millijoules, below a static spark off clothing, so a hydrogen release that finds any edge, valve, or discharge is best assumed already burning.
It is fire at the edge of the senses: a pale blue flame nearly invisible in daylight, little smoke, little radiant heat, a hiss where a fire should be.

## Direction

Up and away fast, as a torch at the leak, and toward detonation where it is confined.
Hydrogen fire burns as a jet flame at the orifice while the buoyant gas -- fourteen times lighter than air -- rises and disperses; but caught under a roof or in congestion the same cloud is detonable across a wide band of concentrations, turning a leak-and-flare event into a blast.
The direction is set by geometry: in the open, a hard invisible torch that vents itself; confined, an accumulating cloud whose ignition is not a fire but an explosion.

## Lever

The clean fuel against the senses and instincts that cannot find it.
The carrying force is ignitability at the threshold of measurement and a flame the eye cannot see -- crews advance behind an outstretched broom or a thermal camera because walking into the flame is the documented way it is found; the resistance fails in the extinguishing reflex: a burning jet is the safe state, and putting the flame out while the leak still flows trades a visible fire for an invisible drifting cloud hunting for its next spark.
Isolate the supply and let the flame burn itself out; extinguish it first and you have manufactured the explosion; and the steel that contains it the gas quietly embrittles from within.

## Echo

Heat-shimmer and a hiss where nothing visibly burns.
Hydrogen fire is read by its signature -- a shimmer of refracted air, a faint blue only at night, no smoke, a high jet-hiss, paint blistering and metal glowing at a distance from nothing the eye can find -- and by instruments where the senses fail: the flame detector, the thermal image, the broom scorching at arm's length.
Hydrogen fire is dated from the leak, the moment the smallest molecule found its way out and its flame began before anyone could see it.
