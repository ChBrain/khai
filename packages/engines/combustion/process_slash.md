# Process: Slash Fire

## Taxonomy

[Combustion](process_combustion.md), the wildland phenomenon of the heavy-residue burn.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: combustion

## Initiated by

Concentrated logging residue -- tops, limbs, cull logs, needles -- takes fire and burns hot and long.
Slash fire starts in the heavy, mixed, cured dead-fuel loading left on a cutover: high loading in a low fuel bed, flaming intensely at roughly 900-1100 C and then smouldering in the large-diameter pieces long after.
It is the fire of accumulated debris: high intensity and deep flame fronts despite low fuel height, with jackpots of slash producing localised extreme behaviour and spotting.

## Direction

Intense at the front, then glowing for days in the heavy fuel.
Slash fire burns as a high-intensity front over the cutover, throwing strong convection, and then transitions to a prolonged smoulder of the large-diameter pieces and stumps. Used deliberately as pile or broadcast burning, it escapes when cured slash aligns with wind and slope.
The direction is a two-clock burn: hours of intense flame followed by days of deep-seated glowing that resists being called out.

## Lever

The massive compacted loading against the mop-up that cannot reach its deep heat.
The carrying force is a fuel load far heavier than the natural stand, compacted and slow to give up its heat; the resistance fails because the large-diameter pieces smoulder for days, resist mop-up, and re-ignite, and deep-seated heat in piles and stumps must be broken apart and repeatedly wetted -- while unburned slash left as a deferral becomes a severe future hazard, and pile burns can carry down into duff and roots.
Knock the flame down and the heavy fuel glows on for days; leave the slash unburned and it arms a worse fire later.

## Echo

The cutover burned over and the large fuels still smoking a week on.
Slash fire is read by its signature -- intense localised flame and heat over cutover ground, heavy grey-brown often-prolonged smoke, strong resinous sawn-wood smell, loud crackle and popping of drying wood, long-lived glowing stumps. It hands its deep heat to a mop-up disproportionately slow relative to the visible fire.
Slash fire is dated from the ignition of the residue, the burn of the debris a harvest left concentrated on the ground.
