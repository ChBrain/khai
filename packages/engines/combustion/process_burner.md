# Process: Premixed Burner

## Taxonomy

[Combustion](process_combustion.md), the made fire premixed with air to a clean blue flame.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: combustion

## Initiated by

Gas is premixed with air before it burns, so the flame anchors on the ports as a hot, clean, near-invisible blue cone.
Premixed burner -- the Bunsen, the atmospheric burner, the gas hob -- premixes natural gas or LPG with air through a venturi; a fully aerated premixed flame burns hot and clean near 1500 C, complete to carbon dioxide and water with minimal soot, while closing the air hole starves it to a luminous sooty diffusion flame. An air collar sets the primary-air ratio and the gas valve the output, the flame anchoring on the ports at a balance of gas velocity and flame speed.
It is fire pre-mixed for cleanliness: a burn that is hottest and safest when it is almost invisible.

## Direction

Anchored on the ports, inner cone of premix, outer mantle of burnout.
Premixed burner shows an inner cone of unburnt premix and an outer mantle of secondary-air burnout, its stability a balance of gas velocity against flame speed. The escalation is either instability that unseats the flame from the ports.
The direction is a flame held at the ports: seated where gas speed and flame speed balance, unstable if either runs away.

## Lever

The clean blue flame against the twin instabilities that unseat it and vent raw gas.
The carrying force is a premixed flame anchored on the ports; the resistance fails in two opposite ways -- flashback when the velocity is too low and the flame burns back into the tube, and lift-off or blow-out when it is too high and the flame lifts and extinguishes, venting raw gas -- while a yellow tipping flame signals incomplete combustion and carbon monoxide, so the hottest, safest flame is the near-invisible one. The clean flame sits between two failures that both release gas.
Balance the gas and air and it holds a clean blue flame; tip it either way and it flashes back or lifts off and vents raw gas.

## Echo

The near-invisible blue cone, the yellow tipping that warns of soot, the low hiss.
Premixed burner is read by its signature -- a clean blue-violet near-invisible flame with a sharp inner cone, a starved flame turning yellow, luminous, and sooting, the faint mercaptan odorant that is the gas smell, a low roar or hiss. It hands its danger to the flashback or the lift-off, and to the carbon monoxide a yellow flame warns of.
Premixed burner is dated from the ignition, the moment the premix lit and seated on the ports.
