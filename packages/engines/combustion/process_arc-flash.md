# Process: Arc Flash

## Taxonomy

[Combustion](process_combustion.md), the industrial phenomenon of ionised-air plasma conducting a fault.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: combustion

## Initiated by

A fault bridges an energised conductor and the air itself ionises into a plasma that carries the current at furnace heat.
Arc flash is not a fuel fire but an electrical release: a short, ground fault, insulation breakdown, or bridging sustains an arc of ionised air conducting fault current at up to roughly 19,000-20,000 C, with a pressure blast and vaporised-metal shrapnel; the related equipment fire is resistive or insulation overheating igniting adjacent Class A and B materials -- the enclosure and cabling.
It is fire made of air and current: not something burning but the atmosphere itself turned to conducting plasma.

## Direction

Out as radiant energy, blast, and molten-metal spray, for as long as the fault current flows.
Arc flash sustains while the fault current flows; its incident energy in calories per square centimetre scales with current times clearing time, and its blast and molten-metal spray injure at distance and ignite the surroundings. The escalation is set by how long the protection takes to clear the fault.
The direction is an instantaneous energy release: not a spreading fire but a burst that lasts as long as the current is not cut.

## Lever

The energised fault against the water and foam that conduct it back.
The carrying force is fault current across ionised air; the resistance fails because water and foam conduct -- a shock and reignition risk while energised -- so the first action is to de-energise and isolate, then use CO2 or a non-conductive clean agent for the equipment fire. The arc flash itself is over in an instant, defended by PPE, de-energised work, and current-limiting protection, not by extinguishers.
Throw water at it live and it conducts to you; cut the power first and the plasma has nothing left to carry it.

## Echo

The blinding blue-white flash, the loud bang, the ozone and vaporised-metal smell.
Arc flash is read by its signature -- a blinding blue-white flash, a loud bang or crack, ozone and a burnt-insulation and vaporised-metal odour, melted and pitted conductors, often a buzzing or frying before failure. It hands its danger to the blast and the molten spray, and through the equipment fire to whatever they ignite.
Arc flash is dated from the fault, the moment the conductor bridged and the air became plasma.
