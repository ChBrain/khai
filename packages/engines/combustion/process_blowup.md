# Process: Blow-up

## Taxonomy

[Combustion](process_combustion.md), the phenomenon of the fire that erupts up a canyon or slope.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: combustion

## Initiated by

A fire in confined terrain suddenly accelerates far beyond what its fuel, wind, and slope should allow.
Blow-up -- the eruptive fire -- starts where wildland fuels in a steep gully, canyon, or chimney are preheated near ignition and a positive feedback forms between the fire-induced flow and the terrain: flame attachment to the slope, funnelled in-draft, and vortex formation combine so the fire's spread rate jumps several-fold in seconds.
It is the fire's non-linear surge: a quiet run that erupts without a clear external trigger.

## Direction

Up the confining terrain, abruptly, faster than any model predicts.
Blow-up runs on the coupling of the fire's own flow to the terrain (Mann Gulch 1949, Storm King 1994, Kornati 2007); the acceleration is sudden and outpaces the fuel-wind-slope behaviour a crew was reading a moment before.
The direction is eruption: the fire rearing up and racing uphill between one moment and the next.

## Lever

The terrain-flow feedback against the crew's judgment built on the fire's prior calm.
The carrying force is the runaway coupling of flame, indraft, and slope; the resistance fails because the acceleration outpaces a crew's ability to reach a safety zone -- the classic wildland fatality mechanism -- and it defeats intuition based on the fire's earlier slow behaviour, blowing up without an obvious cause.
Read the calm canyon fire as safe and it takes off; only recognising the terrain trap in advance keeps a crew clear.

## Echo

The gully fire that surged uphill and overran the ground a crew thought it had time on.
Blow-up is read by its signature -- a sudden roar and surge, the flame front rearing up and racing uphill, an abrupt increase in heat and wind, the fire "taking off." It hands the terrain trap its lethal speed and the crew no time it can count on.
Blow-up is dated from the surge, the moment the canyon's coupling of fire and flow slipped into runaway.
