# Process: Rollover

## Taxonomy

[Combustion](process_combustion.md), the compartment phenomenon of the gas layer igniting across the ceiling.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: combustion

## Initiated by

The unburnt gases pooled in the hot ceiling layer reach their flammable limit and light, ahead of the fire below.
Rollover -- flameover for the surface-spread sense -- starts when the accumulated pyrolysis gases and carbon monoxide in the hot upper layer reach ignition, the layer at roughly 500-600 C and the ignited tongues at 600-1000 C. It is the layer igniting, not the fuel surfaces.
It is distinct from flashover: rollover is the gas layer alight; flashover is the whole room, surfaces and all.

## Direction

Horizontally across the ceiling, riding the flammable layer toward fresh air.
Rollover sends fingers of flame travelling across the ceiling ahead of the main fire, following the flammable gas layer toward openings; it is frequently the immediate precursor and warning of flashover.
The direction is a spreading tongue overhead: the fire announcing, seconds ahead, the total ignition to come.

## Lever

The flammable layer against the crew advancing beneath it.
The carrying force is a ceiling layer rich and hot enough to burn; the resistance is time and posture -- rollover is a visible countdown to flashover that must be read and answered (gas cooling, back out) in seconds, and advancing under a rolling layer puts crews beneath the ignition, while standing up puts the head into 600 C-plus gas.
Cool the layer and back out and the warning is heeded; press on beneath it and the countdown runs out.

## Echo

The tongues of flame overhead, the last warning before the room goes.
Rollover is read by its signature -- fingers of flame ("angel fingers") rolling across the ceiling, the smoke layer rapidly brightening, an audible whoosh, increasing heat on the helmet and shoulders. It hands the compartment, usually within seconds, to flashover.
Rollover is dated from the layer's ignition, the moment the ceiling gases lit and the room's warning began to burn.
