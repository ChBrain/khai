# Piece: Constraint

## Taxonomy

[Usability](piece_usability.md), the facet this is a part of. Constraint models the form that makes the wrong use hard or impossible -- the plug that only fits one way, the key that turns one direction, the interlock that will not let the wrong step happen, the field that refuses the letters where a number goes. Norman's constraint taxonomy (physical, logical, semantic, cultural), with Verbeek's mediation: the design shaping conduct by the shape of the thing. It is distinct from agency, which owns the object as a social delegate enforcing a program on whom: a constraint governs by legible form so the user gets it right, where agency's speed bump enforces a policy on a driver. The childproof cap's difficulty is this engine; that its purpose is to keep children out is agency's.

## Place

Built into the object's form at the point of possible error -- the asymmetric socket, the one-way ratchet, the guard over the switch, the shape that will only seat correctly. Constraint lives exactly where a wrong action would otherwise be easy, standing in its way.

## Load Bearing

What constraint holds up is the narrowing of use to the right action. Remove it and the wrong uses the form was quietly forbidding become possible again: the battery inserted backward, the step taken out of order, the cable forced into the wrong port. The constraint bears the object's error-proofing -- not by warning but by making the mistake physically or logically hard -- and where it is missing, the mistake is simply available, and eventually taken.

## Apparent

The constraint is plainly there in the form as an asymmetry or a block: the notch that only lines up one way, the pin that must be pressed first, the greyed-out control, the slot shaped to one plug. A good constraint makes the right action the only easy one, so the user succeeds without knowing they were guided. A bad or absent constraint leaves every wrong action as reachable as the right one, and the form teaches nothing about which is which.

## Yearbook

The history of a constraint is the record of the errors it prevented and the ones it did not. Where a constraint held, the record is quiet -- the wrong action simply never happened, so often that no one notices the design that stopped it. Where it was missing, the record is the accumulation of the same mistake made again and again by different hands, until the pattern indicts the form: not careless users, but a design that left the wrong action open. The best constraints are invisible in use and legible only in the errors that never occurred.
