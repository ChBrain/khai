# Piece: Grip

## Taxonomy

[Usability](piece_usability.md), the facet this is a part of. Grip models affordance: the action the object's form makes possible -- the handle that affords pulling, the button that affords pressing, the flat surface that affords resting a cup. Gibson's affordance read on the object, Norman's perceived affordance for design. It is not the affordance of the setting (space's: what the room lets you do), and it is not the perceivable cue that announces the action (that is the signifier): grip is the possibility the form offers, whether or not anything points to it.

## Place

In the object's usable features -- the parts a hand or an eye meets and reads as for-doing. The graspable edge, the pressable face, the turnable ring: grip lives wherever the form meets the body's reach and offers it an action.

## Load Bearing

The object's whole use rests on what it affords. An action the form does not offer is a use the object cannot have, however the manual insists: a screen with no edge to grip cannot be picked up, a wall with no handle cannot be pulled. Remove the affordance and you remove the use; what the object is good for is the sum of what its form lets a body do.

## Apparent

A real affordance shows in the form: the cup's handle sized to fingers, the door's bar at hand height, the app's element shaped and shaded to look pressed. The form itself proposes the action. A false affordance is the object lying about what it offers -- the fake drawer-front, the flat that looks pressable and does nothing, the decorative handle that bears no weight -- and the lie is discovered only in the failed reach.

## Yearbook

The history of grip is the record of what the object was reached for and whether it gave. Each use either confirms the affordance (the handle pulled, and it opened) or indicts it (the bar shoved, and nothing moved), and over time the object earns a reputation for what it truly affords versus what it merely looks like it might. The worn places mark where the real affordances were; the untouched ones mark where the form promised an action it never had.
