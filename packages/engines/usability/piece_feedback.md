# Piece: Feedback

## Taxonomy

[Usability](piece_usability.md), the facet this is a part of. Feedback models the object's response that confirms an action registered -- the click, the light, the beep, the resistance that gives and then holds. Cybernetic feedback (Wiener) read on the object; Norman's closing of the Gulf of Evaluation, the user learning whether the act took. It is distinct from the signifier, which speaks before the action to invite it; feedback answers after, and its absence is the silence that leaves the user pressing again, unsure the first press did anything.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: usability

## Place

At the object's point of response, in the moment just after the act -- the switch that clicks under the finger, the screen that flashes, the lock that thunks home, the pedal that firms up. Feedback lives in the interval between the action and the user's knowing it worked, and it must arrive there or not at all.

## Load Bearing

What feedback holds up is the user's knowledge that the action took. Without it the loop of use never closes: the press with no click, the send with no confirmation, the control with no response leaves the user guessing, repeating, or abandoning the act half-done. Remove feedback and every action becomes provisional -- did it work? -- and the doubt is what the object was meant to answer. It bears the confidence that lets a user move on to the next act.

## Apparent

The response is what the object plainly does back: the indicator that lights, the sound that sounds, the movement that moves, the change of state the user can see or feel. Good feedback is immediate, matched to the act, and unambiguous. Bad feedback is late (the delay that makes the user press twice), absent (the dead button), or wrong (the confirmation for the thing that actually failed). What the object shows in answer to the act is the feedback.

## Yearbook

The history of feedback is the record of doubt and repetition: the button pressed three times because it never said yes the first, the form submitted twice because the first gave no sign, the door tried again because the latch was silent. Where feedback was good, the record shows clean single actions; where it was poor, it shows the hesitations and the doubled attempts. Over time an object earns trust or suspicion by how reliably it answered.
