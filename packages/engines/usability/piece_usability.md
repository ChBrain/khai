# Piece: Usability

## Taxonomy

The root of the usability engine. It models the design of an object acting on its user: how the piece's form communicates and governs its use -- what it lets you do, tells you how, confirms you did, and forbids you from doing wrong. The design of everyday things (Norman), read on the object. It is distinct from the affordance of the setting, which the space engine owns (a room scripting conduct, not a thing teaching its use); from the object's history, which biography owns (what it has carried, not what it invites); and from the object's social agency, which agency owns (the program it enforces on whom, not the operation it communicates to a user).

## Place

In the object's form itself, wherever the object is. The design does not sit somewhere on the piece the way a maker's mark does; it is the whole shape read as instruction, and it travels with the piece into every hand that meets it. A thing is usable or not in the kitchen and the cockpit alike, because the usability is in the form, not the room.

## Load Bearing

What the design holds up is the user's ability to operate the thing without being told. Remove the form's legibility -- leave the object but strip what its shape communicates -- and the object still exists, but its use collapses into trial, error, and the manual. The conceptual model the form conveys, the story it tells about how it works, is what bears the whole interaction; where that model is wrong or missing, every use is a guess, and the guesses are what the design was supposed to carry.

## Apparent

The form read as a set of instructions, before any manual: the flat plate that says push, the knob that says turn, the shape that fits one way. A well-designed piece looks like what it does, and the look is true. A badly-designed one looks like something else -- the pull-handle on the push-door, the dial that should have been a switch -- and the gap between what it looks like and what it is is exactly where the user fails. What is plainly there in the form is the first and often the only instruction the object gives.

## Yearbook

The design's history is written by everyone who used it, right and wrong: the door pulled that should have been pushed, the worn place where every thumb has landed, the step everyone skips, the setting no one ever finds. Over a life of use the object's design is validated or indicted by the pattern of conduct it drew -- the good design vanishing into effortless use, the bad one dated from the first person it defeated. A piece's usability is legible in the record of how it was actually handled, not in how its maker meant it to be.
