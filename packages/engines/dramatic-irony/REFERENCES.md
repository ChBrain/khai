# Dramatic Irony: Reference

## Line of Work

Dramatic irony as a **process** engine: the sustained gap of discrepant awareness in which the audience holds knowledge a character lacks, and reads the character's every word and act against what the character cannot see -- taken across the two forms the gap can take by where the audience's advantage lies. The domain does not model the concealment of knowledge between characters, which is the secret engine's; nor the anticipatory dread about an outcome the room is not yet sure of, which is the suspense engine's; nor the sudden reversal for a room that did not know, which is the surprise engine's; nor the character's own discovery of what the audience already knew, which is the recognition engine's. It models the audience-to-character asymmetry itself -- the room's wider horizon, held open against the character's narrower one.

The spine is Pfister's analysis of discrepant awareness and Sternberg's theory of the distribution of knowledge in narrative. Pfister sets dramatic irony within the audience's superior knowledge: the spectator is given information a figure lacks, and the resulting discrepancy between the figure's limited horizon and the audience's fuller one is what the dramatist exploits -- the audience watches the character act inside a smaller world than the one the room can see. Sternberg locates the reader's experience of narrative in the placement and withholding of information: gaps between what the text has disclosed to the audience and what its figures know generate the master effects, and where the audience knows more than the character, the effect is the sustained irony of watching from above. From these the engine takes its two forms by the temporal location of the gap: foreknowledge, the prospective gap, where the audience knows what is coming that the character does not -- the informational advantage that turns Hitchcock's revealed bomb, or Sophocles' foreknown parricide, into unbearable watching; and present-meaning, the concurrent gap, where the audience knows the true sense of the character's present words and situation, so that the character's own lines carry a second meaning only the room hears. The altitudes are those: the sustained discrepant awareness, and its prospective and present-meaning forms.

The dramaturgy and narratology of discrepant awareness, applied directly as engine constraints across process.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **Concealment between characters (secret)**: The engine models the audience-to-character gap, not the character-to-character one. Knowledge held by one figure and hidden from another -- the keeping, the risk of exposure, the disclosure -- is owned by the secret engine; dramatic irony is what arises when the audience is let in on something a character is not, whether or not another character shares the knowledge. A secret can create dramatic irony (the room told what a figure is not), but the secret is the concealment between figures and the irony is the room's vantage above the stage.
- **The anticipatory hold (suspense)**: The engine models the audience's knowing more than the character, not the audience's not knowing the outcome. The tension of an uncertain future harm the room tracks but cannot yet call -- fear and hope held open -- is owned by the suspense engine; dramatic irony's foreknowledge form is nearly its inverse, the room often certain of the outcome and pressed instead by the character's blindness to it. Dramatic irony can feed suspense and suspense can run without it; the one is a gap of knowledge, the other a hold on an unanswered outcome.
- **The reversal for the unknowing room (surprise)**: The engine models a gap the audience holds in advance, not one sprung on it. The sudden overturning of what the audience believed -- the twist that lands because the room did not see it coming -- is owned by the surprise engine; dramatic irony is its structural opposite for the audience, which is not surprised at all: only the character is. Surprise withholds from the room; dramatic irony withholds from the character.
- **The character's discovery (recognition)**: The engine models the sustained gap, not its closing in the character. The moment the character learns what the audience already knew -- anagnorisis, the turn from ignorance to knowledge -- is owned by the recognition engine; dramatic irony holds the gap open and points toward that moment, but the discovery itself, and its reversal of the character's world, belong to recognition. Dramatic irony is the stretch before; recognition is the closing.
