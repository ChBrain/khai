# Instructions: Dramatic Irony

How a Playwright wires the dramatic-irony engine into a play. You wire by linking the play's own content to the engine's files; the engine files stay untouched, they are canon. The Roadie plumbs the engine's law into Instructions Knowledge on deploy. Authoring guidance, not runtime content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human sets the gap: what the audience is given to know, which character is kept short of it, and how much rides on the character staying blind -- the outcome foreseen, the identity unrevealed, the true meaning of the moment the character misreads.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, the persona held under the irony links the form the gap takes on it under its Projection: foreknowledge, when the audience knows what is coming and watches the character move toward it blind; present-meaning, when the audience knows the true sense of the character's present words and situation, which the character misreads. The character keeps acting inside its narrower horizon; the room reads it against the wider one. You link to the engine, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- The concealment of knowledge between characters -- one figure keeping something from another -- is the secret engine's; dramatic irony is the audience-to-character gap, not the character-to-character one. The anticipatory dread about an outcome the room cannot yet call is the suspense engine's. The sudden reversal for a room that did not see it coming is the surprise engine's -- dramatic irony's opposite for the audience, which is not surprised at all. The character's own discovery of what the audience already knew is the recognition engine's. This engine supplies the sustained discrepant awareness itself.

## Knowledge

- Dramatic irony is discrepant awareness: the audience is given knowledge a character lacks, and the gap between the room's fuller horizon and the character's narrower one is held open while the character acts in ignorance (Pfister). It is the audience-to-character asymmetry, not concealment between figures. It comes in two forms by where the gap points. Foreknowledge: the room knows what is coming that the character does not -- the outcome, the threat, the identity, the fate -- and watches the character move toward it blind, each ordinary step charged with the dread or helpless hope of a room that sees the ending (Hitchcock's revealed bomb; Oedipus hunting himself). Present-meaning: the room knows the true sense of the character's present words and situation, which the character misreads -- the line that carries a second meaning only the room hears, the confidence built on a belief the room knows false. The gap presses in proportion to its size and stakes, and lasts only while the character stays blind; the instant the character's knowledge catches the room's, it closes. Read a scene by what the audience holds over the character, and in which form.

## System

- Do keep it the audience-to-character gap: the concealment between figures is the secret engine's, the anticipatory hold on an uncertain outcome is the suspense engine's, the reversal for an unknowing room is the surprise engine's, and the character's discovery is the recognition engine's.
- Do install the knowledge on the audience's side and withhold it on the character's; let the character act with full conviction inside its narrower horizon, because the character's sincerity is what the room reads against.
- Do route through the form that fits where the advantage lies -- foreknowledge for a gap that points at what is coming, present-meaning for a gap that sits in the doubled now; a single scene may hold both.
- Do let the gap press by its stakes and last only while the character stays blind; when the character learns, hand the moment of discovery to recognition. Do not edit the engine's files; wire only from the play's side.
