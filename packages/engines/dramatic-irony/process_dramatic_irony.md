# Process: Dramatic Irony

## Taxonomy

Discrepant awareness between audience and character.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: dramatic-irony

## Initiated by

The play gives the audience a piece of knowledge, and withholds it from a character who acts within the scene.
The trigger is not a concealment between figures but an asymmetry between the room and the stage: the audience is let in on something -- an outcome, an identity, a true meaning -- that the character on stage does not have, and the character keeps acting as if the scene were what it believes it to be (Pfister's discrepant awareness).
What enters the process is a gap: the audience's horizon is wider than the character's, and the play holds the two apart.

## Direction

Toward the sustained reading of the character against what it cannot see.
Dramatic irony does not resolve the moment it is set; its work is the holding, the stretch of scene in which the audience watches the character speak and move inside a smaller world than the one the room can see, and reads every line twice -- once as the character means it, once as the room knows it.
The gap points forward to the moment the character will learn what the audience already holds, and the play tightens toward that closing without hurrying it.

## Lever

The size of what the audience knows and the character does not, weighted by how much turns on it.
The irony presses in proportion to the gap and its stakes: a trivial thing the room knows and the character misses barely registers, but a fatal thing -- the room certain, the character blind, and the outcome grave -- charges the whole scene. What keeps it alive is that the character stays inside its narrower horizon; the instant the character's knowledge catches up to the room's, the gap closes and this process ends.
Change the room's advantage -- let the audience lose its knowledge, or the character gain it -- and the irony collapses.

## Echo

The gap takes one of two forms, by where the audience's advantage lies: [foreknowledge](process_foreknowledge.md), where the room knows what is coming that the character does not and watches it move toward the unseen, or [present-meaning](process_double_meaning.md), where the room knows the true sense of the character's present words and situation, which the character misreads.
The two are not stages and not exclusive; a single scene may hold both, the room hearing a doubled line from a character walking toward a fate it also foresees.
What ends the process is the closing of the gap -- but the character's own discovery of what the audience already knew is the recognition engine's, the anticipatory dread about an uncertain outcome is the suspense engine's, the concealment of knowledge between characters is the secret engine's, and the sudden reversal for a room that did not know is the surprise engine's. This engine owns the sustained discrepant awareness itself.
