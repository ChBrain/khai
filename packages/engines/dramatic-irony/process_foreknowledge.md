# Process: Foreknowledge

## Taxonomy

[Dramatic Irony](process_dramatic_irony.md), the gap that points at the future -- the room knows what is coming.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: dramatic-irony

## Initiated by

The play gives the audience the ending, or a piece of it, and lets the character walk toward it blind.
The room is shown the bomb under the table, the assassin already in the house, the letter that will arrive, the identity that will undo the searcher -- and then watches the character act as though the future were still open (Hitchcock's example: reveal the bomb, and an ordinary conversation becomes unbearable; Sophocles': the room knows Oedipus is the man he hunts before he does).
What enters is a prospective gap: the audience holds the outcome, the character holds only the present.

## Direction

Toward the collision the room can see and the character cannot.
Each thing the character does is read by the room against the end it is heading for -- the innocent plan, the confident vow, the small delay -- and the ordinary is charged with everything the character does not know it is walking into.
The gap points ahead and pulls the scene toward the moment the future the room holds becomes the character's present.

## Lever

The gravity of what is coming, multiplied by the room's certainty that it is coming, and the character's blindness to it.
The press is strongest when the outcome is grave and fixed and the character most oblivious -- a light foreknowledge barely colours a scene, a fatal one makes every gesture ache. What sustains it is the character staying blind while the room stays sure; the closer the character drifts to seeing, the tighter the hold.
Let the outcome become uncertain and the scene passes to suspense; let the character glimpse what is coming and the foreknowledge begins to close.

## Echo

The room watches a character move toward what it cannot see, and reads the present as a countdown the character is not keeping.
Because the audience holds the future, it feels for the character the dread or the helpless hope the character cannot feel for itself -- the particular ache of watching someone step, unknowing, toward the thing you already grieve.
What it points to is the closing collision -- but the anticipatory dread about an outcome the room is not yet sure of is the suspense engine's, and the character's own discovery when the future arrives is the recognition engine's. This form owns the forward-pointing gap; the [present-meaning](process_double_meaning.md) form owns the gap that sits in the doubled now.
