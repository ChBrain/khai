# Process: Emphasis

## Taxonomy

[Framing](process_framing.md), the operation this is one form of.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: framing

## Initiated by

The persona presents an issue by foregrounding one of its several true dimensions -- a controversial rally as a free-speech question, or the same rally as a public-safety question.
This is emphasis, or issue, framing: unlike valence, the two framings are not logically equivalent, and neither is false -- the issue really does have both dimensions.
The persona has chosen which of them the hearer will have in mind.

## Direction

Toward the consideration the frame makes salient.
The hearer holds many relevant beliefs about the issue; the frame decides which of them are accessible and feel applicable at the moment of judgment, and the weighted consideration drives the verdict.
Foreground free speech and tolerance rises; foreground public safety and it falls -- the same person, the same issue, the frame the difference.

## Lever

Selection and salience among competing considerations.
To frame is to select some aspects of a perceived reality and make them more salient so as to promote a particular problem definition and evaluation. The lever is not only what is put forward but what is left out: emphasis works by suppression as much as by selection.
A frame that names one dimension and is silent on the others lets the named one stand for the whole.

## Echo

The verdict follows the dimension that was foregrounded.
The effect is more contestable than valence's -- a hearer already holding a strong competing consideration can resist it, and a counter-frame can restore what was suppressed -- but on an issue the hearer has not already weighed, the first frame often sets the terms.
Which considerations belong to the issue is a matter of the domain; which one is made to weigh is [framing](process_framing.md)'s. It is distinct from [valence](process_valence.md) in that the framings here are not interchangeable descriptions but different true angles.
