# Framing: Reference

## Line of Work

Framing as **process**: how the same content -- a fact, a choice, an issue -- presented in a different frame yields a different judgment, though the information is unchanged. The domain does not model the resolving of the choice a frame tilts, the weighing of options under uncertainty, which is the decision engine's; nor the standing tilt a persona brings before any framing, the resting bias in the judge, which is the bias engine's. It models the presentation-form itself and its effect on judgment: the sign a thing is stated in, the dimension made salient, the metaphor it is construed through, the altitude -- case or pattern -- it is told at.

(The word "framing" also names a different phenomenon in the pitch-mode engine -- Oren Klaff's frame control, the sales move of setting the context before the offer. That is a same-word-different-science homonym, whitelisted as such; this engine is the cognitive framing effect of Kahneman, Entman, and Goffman, unrelated to the pitch move.)

The spine is the framing effect: because there is no frameless presentation, every telling selects some aspects of a situation and makes them salient, and the hearer judges from what is made accessible, not from the full and identical facts. Equivalence (valence) framing flips risk preference by sign alone; emphasis framing selects which of an issue's true dimensions is weighed; metaphorical framing imports a source domain's inferences; episodic-versus-thematic framing shifts where cause and cure are assigned. The four operations are the ways the same content is shaped toward a different judgment: valence, emphasis, metaphor, exemplar.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **Decision**: The engine models the shaping of a judgment by presentation, not the resolving of the choice itself. How a persona settles between competing options under uncertainty is owned by the decision engine; framing tilts the options going in -- a loss frame turns the same choice risk-seeking -- but the act of choosing is decision's.
- **Bias**: The engine models the presentation-induced shift, not the standing tilt of the judge. The resting lean a persona brings before the evidence lands is owned by the bias engine; framing is induced by how the content is put and flips the same person with the wording, where a bias is a property they carry into every framing alike.
- **Persuasion**: The engine models the framing effect, not the campaign to change a mind. The attempt to move what another believes, values, or does is owned by the persuasion engine; framing can serve a persuader, but it also operates with no persuader at all -- a choice framed by circumstance -- and works on salience and accessibility, not on argument quality.
- **Attribution**: The engine models the framing that tips where responsibility lands, not the assignment of cause itself. Whether a persona lays a cause on the person or the conditions is owned by the attribution engine; exemplar framing shifts that assignment by the altitude of the telling, but the attribution it produces is attribution's.
- **Language**: The engine models the framing effect of a metaphor, not the metaphor as a device of the language. The moving of meaning across the gap between what is said and what is understood is owned by the language engine; this engine owns the inferences a source domain imports into a judgment, not the semantics of the figure that carries them.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per file.

- **[framing](process_framing.md)** (the root): The presentation-form of content and its effect on judgment -- there being no frameless telling, the frame selects and makes salient, and the hearer judges from what is made accessible. Anchored by Tversky & Kahneman (the framing effect), Entman (selection and salience), and Goffman (frames as the organization of experience).
- **[valence](process_valence.md)** (operation): Equivalence framing -- state the same outcome as a gain or a loss, flipping risk preference by sign alone against a reference point. Anchored by Tversky & Kahneman (the framing of decisions and prospect theory).
- **[emphasis](process_emphasis.md)** (operation): Issue framing -- foreground one of an issue's several true dimensions, so the consideration made salient (and the others suppressed) drives the verdict. Anchored by Entman (selection and salience) and Chong & Druckman (framing theory).
- **[metaphor](process_metaphor.md)** (operation): Metaphorical framing -- construe the target through a source domain, importing its inferences into the judgment, mostly unnoticed as a frame. Anchored by Lakoff & Johnson (conceptual metaphor) and Thibodeau & Boroditsky (metaphor in reasoning).
- **[exemplar](process_exemplar.md)** (operation): Episodic-versus-thematic framing -- present a vivid case or the abstract pattern, shifting where cause and cure are assigned. Anchored by Iyengar (episodic and thematic framing).
