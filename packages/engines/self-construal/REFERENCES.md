# Self-Construal: Reference

## Line of Work

Self-construal as a **position** engine: a persona's resting sense of where its self is bounded -- as a separate, autonomous individual defined by its own attributes, or as a connected self defined through its relationships, roles, and groups -- taken across the two construals a persona holds. The domain does not model the identification with a specific group and the intergroup consequences of it, which is the social-identity engine's; nor the persona's present self-story, who it takes itself to be, which is the identity engine's; nor the resting sense of one's own worth, which is the self-esteem engine's; nor the strength of a culture's norms and its tolerance for deviance, which is the tightness-looseness engine's; nor the abstraction of a mental representation by psychological distance, which is the construal engine's (Construal-Level Theory -- a different sense of the word). It models the boundary of the self: the default weighting of I against we that a persona brings before any particular relationship or situation.

The spine is the cross-cultural psychology of the self. Markus and Kitayama's founding paper contrasts two divergent construals of the self -- the independent, in which the self is a bounded, unitary, stable entity defined by internal attributes and expressed by asserting them, and the interdependent, in which the self is flexible, connected, and defined by its relationships and social roles, realized by fitting in and reading others -- and shows that the construal a person holds organizes cognition, emotion, and motivation, differing on average between Western and East Asian cultural contexts. Triandis develops the cultural dimension of individualism and collectivism and its individual-level reflection (idiocentrism and allocentrism), and the horizontal and vertical variants within each. Singelis operationalizes the two construals as separable dimensions rather than opposite ends of one scale -- a person can be high on both -- so that the self-boundary is a weighting, not a strict either/or, and biculturals can hold both and shift with the frame they are primed into. The altitudes are those: the self-boundary, and the independent and interdependent construals a persona weights between.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **Construal (Construal-Level Theory)**: The engine does not model the abstraction of a mental representation by psychological distance. The construal engine owns Construal-Level Theory -- how a thing is represented more abstractly the more distant it is in time, space, social distance, or hypotheticality; self-construal shares only the word. One is the level of a representation, the other the boundary of the self; they are unrelated constructs that happen to be named alike, and are kept strictly apart.
- **Social identity**: The engine models the boundary of the self, not the identification with a particular group. Social identity -- the part of the self derived from membership in a specific group, and the in-group favoritism and intergroup comparison it drives (Tajfel & Turner) -- is owned by the social-identity engine; self-construal is the prior, general weighting of self-definition toward the individual or the collective, which disposes a persona toward group identification without being any particular one. The interdependent construal makes group identifications weightier; which group, and its intergroup dynamics, are social identity's.
- **Identity**: The engine models where the self is bounded, not the content of the self-story. Identity -- the persona's present narrative of who it is, its roles and commitments -- is owned by the identity engine; self-construal is the structural weighting beneath any such story, the default of I against we, not the story itself.
- **Self-esteem**: The engine models the construal, not the worth. Self-esteem -- the resting sense of one's own value -- is owned by the self-esteem engine; self-construal sets where a persona sources that worth (in standing out and being consistent, or in belonging and fitting in) without being the worth itself. The construal points to the source; the esteem is the level.
- **Tightness-looseness**: The engine models the individual's self-boundary, not the culture's norm strength. Tightness-looseness -- how strong a culture's norms are and how much deviance it tolerates -- is owned by the tightness-looseness engine; self-construal is the person-level construal that a culture's individualism or collectivism tends to shape but is not identical to. The two form a culture cluster: the norm-system property and the self-boundary it disposes.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per file.

- **[self-construal](position_self_construal.md)** (the anchor, position): The resting sense of where the self is bounded -- the default weighting of the individual against the collective. Anchored by Markus & Kitayama (the two construals) and Singelis (the weighting).
- **[independent](position_independent.md)** (position, construal): The self as a bounded, autonomous unit defined from within, realized by expressing and asserting and holding consistent, worth sourced in standing out. Anchored by Markus & Kitayama (the independent self) and Triandis (idiocentrism).
- **[interdependent](position_interdependent.md)** (position, construal): The self as fundamentally connected, defined through its ties, realized by fitting in and adjusting, worth sourced in belonging. Anchored by Markus & Kitayama (the interdependent self) and Triandis (allocentrism).
