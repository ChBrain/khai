# Coping: Reference

## Line of Work

Coping as a **process** engine: the effortful, appraisal-driven repertoire a persona deploys against a stressor -- sizing up what is at stake and what can be done, acting on the problem, or turning away from it. The domain does not model the involuntary autonomic routing a threat triggers, which is the stress engine's; nor the management of an emotional response along its timeline -- emotion-focused coping -- which is the regulation engine's; nor the drawing of meaning from values and beliefs under enduring stress, which is the meaning engine's; nor the long perseverance toward a distant goal, which is the grit engine's. It models the effortful handling of the stressor itself: the cognitive appraisal that launches and steers the response, and the two stressor-directed families of the response, acting on the demand and escaping it.

The spine is the transactional theory of stress and coping. Lazarus and Folkman define coping as the constantly changing cognitive and behavioral efforts to manage demands appraised as taxing or exceeding one's resources, and make cognitive appraisal its engine: primary appraisal of what is at stake (harm, threat, or challenge) and secondary appraisal of the coping options, continually updated as the transaction unfolds. They distinguish the two broad functions of coping -- problem-focused, aimed at the stressor, and emotion-focused, aimed at the distress -- and show that problem-focused coping predominates where the stressor is appraised as controllable and emotion-focused where it is not. Carver, Scheier and Weintraub's COPE inventory elaborates the repertoire, isolating an avoidant family -- denial, behavioral and mental disengagement, distraction -- alongside the active, planful, and support-seeking forms. Folkman's later work adds meaning-focused coping, the drawing on beliefs, values, and goals that sustains wellbeing under chronic, uncontrollable stress. This engine owns the appraisal and the two stressor-directed families -- problem-focused and avoidant -- and hands emotion-focused coping to the regulation engine and meaning-focused coping to the meaning engine. The altitudes are those: the coping response, its appraisal, its problem-focus, and its escape.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **Stress**: The engine models the effortful response, not the involuntary one. Stress -- the autonomic mobilization a threat triggers, the body's arousal and routing that require no effort or choice -- is owned by the stress engine; coping is the deliberate, appraisal-driven repertoire the persona mounts in response, the effortful handling as against the reflex. The stressor and the body's alarm are stress's; what the persona then does about it is coping's.
- **Regulation (emotion-focused coping)**: The engine models the handling of the stressor, not the management of the feeling. Emotion-focused coping -- soothing, reframing, expressing, distracting from, or suppressing the emotional response to a stressor -- is the regulation engine's domain (Gross's situation selection, reappraisal, and suppression along the emotion timeline); coping owns the stressor-directed families and hands the emotion-directed one across. In particular, the reappraisal that changes the emotional meaning of a situation to manage a feeling is regulation's, distinct from coping's cognitive appraisal of the stressor and one's options, which is a reading of the demand rather than a reframing of the emotion.
- **Meaning**: The engine models the appraisal-driven repertoire, not the meaning drawn from it. Meaning-focused coping -- drawing on beliefs, values, and goals to find significance and sustain wellbeing under enduring, uncontrollable stress (Folkman) -- is handed to the meaning engine; coping owns the effortful handling of the stressor, not the meaning-making that a chronic stressor can occasion. The two compose: coping works the demand, meaning reframes the life it falls in.
- **Grit**: The engine models the response to a stressor, not the pursuit of a goal. Grit -- sustained passion and perseverance toward a distant goal across years -- is owned by the grit engine; coping is the handling of a present demand appraised as taxing, a shorter and more reactive arc than the long voluntary pursuit grit names. A persona may cope with the stressors a gritty pursuit throws up, but the pursuit itself is grit's.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per file.

- **[coping](process_coping.md)** (the root, process): The effortful, appraisal-driven management of a stressor, transactional and dynamic. Anchored by Lazarus & Folkman (1984).
- **[appraisal](process_appraisal.md)** (process, form): The sizing-up that launches and steers coping -- primary appraisal of the stakes (harm/threat/challenge) and secondary of the options, updated as the encounter turns. Anchored by Lazarus & Folkman (cognitive appraisal) and Folkman & Lazarus (coping as process).
- **[problem](process_problem.md)** (process, form): Acting on the stressor -- planning, active steps, instrumental support, altering or removing the source, deployed where the stressor is appraised as changeable. Anchored by Lazarus & Folkman and Carver (active/planful coping).
- **[escape](process_escape.md)** (process, form): Avoidant coping -- distraction, denial, wishful thinking, and behavioral or mental disengagement, relieving now and, sustained, leaving the demand to grow. Anchored by Carver (the avoidant family).
