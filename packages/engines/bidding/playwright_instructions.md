# Instructions: Bidding

How a Playwright wires the bidding engine into a play. You wire by linking the play's own content to the engine's files; the engine files stay untouched, they are canon. The Roadie plumbs the engine's law into Instructions Knowledge on deploy. Authoring guidance, not runtime content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human decides what the thing is actually worth and keeps the figure off the page, because the engine runs on nobody in the contest being able to see it. Check first that the value is common rather than private: if each party is pricing the thing for themselves, the mechanism is doing precisely the right thing and there is no curse to read. And keep the winner competent -- the engine does not need a reckless bidder and stops being interesting if it gets one.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, a persona links under Projection where they stand in the contest: how many others are in, what their own number is built from, what the field did to their appetite, and what the last thing they won has cost them since. Read the engine on the spread of the estimates, never on the size of the winning one. Keep it the selection: the exchange of concessions is negotiation's, commitment growing on what has been spent is escalation's, what a thing is worth is worth-logic's. You link to the engine, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- The exchange of concessions toward terms is the negotiation engine's; commitment growing on the sunk is escalation's; what a thing is worth and by whose standard is worth-logic's, money's, and capital's; rarity as a cue is scarcity's and desire's; overconfidence in a figure is bias's and metacognition's; being beaten by a rival is the outdone composite's and comparison's; how a format shapes behaviour is choice-architecture's. Auction law, procurement rules, and reserve prices are the trade and set the format rather than the operation. Whether a production's winner overpaid, and whether the item had a common value or a private one, are the team's review -- and the second decides whether the engine applies at all.

## Knowledge

- Bidding is one indivisible thing wanted by several parties who each have to price it themselves. It is not negotiation's exchange of concessions toward terms, not escalation's commitment growing on what has been sunk, and not worth-logic's question of by whose standard a thing is valuable. It wires on a persona at Projection and runs in four movements. The contest makes the rivals part of the object: the prize cannot be divided, so second place is identical to never having bid; the field is read as confirmation and treated as a reason to bid higher, when a larger field draws the winner from further out in the tail and is therefore a reason to bid lower; and other people's appetite arrives labelled as a judgement about the item. The estimating is done properly by everybody: the private number is a range with the range removed, because a bid must be a figure; the scatter across all the numbers is the engine's whole content and is the one quantity no bidder can observe, since each sees only their own; and the unshared fragments would fix it if pooled, which the contest forbids, so the field is worse informed than its members could be and every party is right not to fix it. The winning takes the maximum, which under a common value is a measurement of the largest error and under private values is exactly the right rule -- and the triumph arrives at the point of least information, since the one fact learned is that the number was above everybody else's. The reckoning is chronic: the overpay shows up as everything being slightly harder and is never itemised, the defence converts the number into the winner's judgement so the case closest to hand becomes unavailable as evidence, and the next contest shows the learning to have been about the market rather than about the mechanism. The engine's twist is at the root: winning is the evidence that you were wrong.

## System

- Do fix the true value before anything else and never put it in the play. Nobody in the contest can see it, and the audience should only infer it late.
- Do check that the value is common. If each persona is pricing the thing for themselves, the highest bidder is simply the keenest and the engine has nothing to say.
- Do keep the winner competent. The estimate was well made, the process was fair, and the price was defensible on the day -- staging a reckless bidder replaces the mechanism with a character.
- Do let the field be read backwards. Somebody learns how many rivals there are and bids higher, and everybody in the room agrees that is the sensible response.
- Do stage the triumph as a celebration. The persona has just learned the one fact that should worry them and is delighted by it.
- Do make the overpay chronic rather than dramatic. Nothing collapses; everything is slightly harder, for years, with a dozen good explanations available.
- Do let the winner defend the price with real arguments. The number is their judgement now, the arguments are sound, and the defence is what stops the case ever being studied.
- Do close on the next contest rather than the loss. Same methods, same confidence, a new market, and a correction that turns out not to travel.
- Do not model the concession exchange (negotiation), the sunk commitment (escalation), the standard of worth (worth-logic, money), rarity (scarcity), overconfidence (bias), or being beaten (outdone, comparison). Do not make the contest stand for a theme; that is authored key, the play's pitch. Do not edit the engine's files; wire only from the play's side.
