# Bidding

Bidding as process: one indivisible thing wanted by several parties who each have to price it themselves -- not the exchange of concessions toward terms, which is negotiation's, and not commitment growing on what has been sunk, which is escalation's -- across four movements: the contest (a prize that makes second place identical to not having tried, a field read as a reason to bid more and which is a reason to bid less, and a wanting that is other people's appetite felt as one's own), the estimating (a private number that is a range with the range removed, a scatter that is the answer and is held by the seller, and unshared information that everybody keeps and nobody benefits from), the winning (a top estimate that cannot tell keenness from error and does not need to, a pushing decided by whoever tires last, and a triumph that is the first news of the curse received as its opposite), and the reckoning (an overpay that is real, moderate, continuous and never itemised, a defence that protects the winner and destroys the data, and a next contest where the lesson turns out to have been about the market) -- with the twist that winning is the evidence that you were wrong.
