# Process: Bidding

## Taxonomy

Contested acquisition (no khai file of its own yet).

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: bidding

## Initiated by

Several personas want one thing, and only one can have it.
Bidding starts wherever a single indivisible object is competed for by people who each have to price it themselves: the tender, the offer on the house, the counter-offer for the candidate, the lease, the contract, the acquisition, the last place on the list.
It is not the exchange of concessions between two parties working toward terms, which is negotiation's, and not the commitment that grows because of what has already been spent, which is escalation's. It is the selection mechanism, and what it selects for.

## Direction

Toward the thing going to whoever was furthest from right about it.
The engine reads four movements: [the contest](process_contest_bidding.md), one object and a field of people who want it; [the estimating](process_estimating_bidding.md), the private number each of them arrives at; [the winning](process_winning_bidding.md), a mechanism that reads only the top of the distribution; and [the reckoning](process_reckoning_bidding.md), the twist -- what the winner finds out, late, and what it does not teach them.
The four are one operation, and the third is not a reward.

## Lever

The efficiency of letting the keenest party take it against what keenness turns out to measure.
The carrying force is that competitive allocation is a good arrangement and mostly works: it puts things where they are most valued, it is transparent, it beats every alternative anybody has proposed for allocating one item among strangers, and the winner is usually somebody who really did want it most; the resistance is that when the thing has a value the parties are estimating rather than a value they each set for themselves, keenness and error are indistinguishable from the inside -- and the mechanism cannot tell them apart either.
Bidding is read on the spread of the estimates, never on the size of the winning one.

## Echo

Somebody holding the thing and a number they will now have to defend.
Bidding does not close; it is a selection being made on the tail of a distribution, gathered into one reading -- a contest, an estimating, a winning, a reckoning.
The engine's twist is here. **Winning is the evidence that you were wrong.** Where the object has one true value and each party estimates it, the estimates scatter, and the mechanism hands the thing to the highest -- which is by construction the party that most overestimated. The winner cannot see this from inside, because their own number is the only one they ever knew the reasoning behind, and the moment of finding out is a celebration.
It hands off what it is not: the exchange of concessions toward terms is negotiation's; commitment growing on what has been sunk is escalation's; what a thing is worth and by whose standard is worth-logic's and money's; rarity as a cue that raises wanting is scarcity's; the overconfidence in one's own estimate is bias's; and a contest staged to **stand for** a theme is authored key, the play's pitch.
The persona is dated from the last thing they won.
