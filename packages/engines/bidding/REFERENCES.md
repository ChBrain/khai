# Bidding: Reference

## Line of Work

Bidding as **process**: one indivisible thing wanted by several parties who each have to price it
themselves.

The engine's subject is a selection rule with a property nobody designed into it. Where the object has
a single underlying value and each party estimates it independently, the estimates scatter -- and the
mechanism awards the thing to the highest, which is by construction the party furthest above the
truth. Capen, Clapp and Campbell named it from offshore oil leases, where the pattern was that
companies which won blocks earned systematically less on them than the industry's cost of capital;
Bazerman and Samuelson reproduced it in a room with a jar of coins; and it has since turned up
wherever anybody has looked for it.

The boundary with what khai already carries is clean. The exchange of concessions between parties
working toward terms is **negotiation**'s -- a joint search for an agreement, where this engine reads a
rule that selects among sealed positions. The commitment that grows because of what has already been
spent, and the increments that make stopping the only losing move, are **escalation**'s; the pushing
member describes what that does inside a contest and hands the mechanism back. What a thing is worth
and by whose standard is **worth-logic**'s and **money**'s. Rarity as a cue that raises wanting is
**scarcity**'s. And overconfidence in one's own estimate is **bias**'s -- this engine needs no
overconfidence at all, since the curse runs on well-made estimates.

**The contest** makes the rivals part of the object. **The one thing** is indivisible, so second place
is identical to never having bid and the whole spread of effort between them returns nothing. **The
field** is the counter-intuitive term: more rivals is read as confirmation and treated as a reason to
bid higher, when the arithmetic runs the other way -- a larger field draws the winner from further out
in the tail, so competition makes the winner's overpayment worse. **The wanting seen** transfers
appetite from the room to the party, arriving labelled as a judgement about the item.

**The estimating** is done properly by everybody. **The private number** is a range with the range
removed, because a bid must be a figure and the interval is discarded at exactly the step where it
mattered. **The scatter** is the engine's whole content -- a narrow spread produces a winner who paid
about right and a wide one produces a disaster, with the same parties behaving identically -- and it is
the one quantity no bidder can observe, since each sees only their own number. **The unshared**
fragments would fix it if pooled, and the contest forbids pooling, so the arrangement guarantees the
field is worse informed than its members could be, with every party right not to fix it.

**The winning** takes the maximum. Under a common value that is a measurement of the largest error;
under private values it is exactly the right rule and there is no curse at all. **The top estimate**
cannot tell keenness from error, and does not need to, which is what makes it auditable. **The
pushing** carries the number further in the open forms, decided by whoever tires last. **The triumph**
arrives at the point of least information: the one fact the winner has learned is that their number
was above everybody else's, which is the only evidence in the affair bearing on whether they overpaid,
and it is received as vindication.

**The reckoning** is chronic rather than acute. **The overpay** shows up as everything being slightly
harder, permanently, with no line in any account. **The defence** converts the number into the winner's
judgement, so the case they have closest access to becomes unavailable as evidence. **The next
contest** is where the learning turns out to have been local: repeat bidders in one market do shade
their numbers and the correction is real, and it does not travel to a new market.

Hence the **twist**, at the root. **Winning is the evidence that you were wrong.** The winner cannot
see it from inside, because their own number is the only one whose reasoning they knew, and the moment
of finding out is a celebration.

The engine is built so that nobody has to be reckless or foolish for any of it to run, and a play
should keep it that way. The winner was competent. The process was fair. The price was defensible on
the day.

**On the evidence, stated plainly.** **The winner's curse is one of the better-established results in
experimental economics**, replicated across designs, incentives, and populations, and it does not
depend on anybody being irrational -- it follows from order statistics. That is unusually strong ground
for a khai engine and it is worth saying so. **Its scope is narrower than the twist sounds, and the
limit is load-bearing.** The curse is a common-value phenomenon: it requires the parties to be
estimating one underlying figure. Where values are genuinely private -- somebody wants the painting
because they like it -- the highest bidder is simply the person who wants it most, the mechanism is
doing precisely the right thing, and there is no curse. Real contests are usually mixed, and how much
of each is present is rarely knowable in advance, so a reader should treat the engine as applying in
proportion rather than universally. **The correction is real and local.** Kagel and Levin's programme
found that experienced bidders in a familiar market learn to shade, which is the main escape anybody
has, and that the learning is stored as a rule about that market rather than as an understanding of the
mechanism, so it does not transfer. The next-contest member is written to that finding rather than to
the stronger claim that nobody ever learns. **The field evidence is weaker than the laboratory
evidence.** Acquirer underperformance after competitive takeovers is well documented and has several
available explanations -- hubris, agency problems, market timing, and the curse among them -- and the
engine does not claim the curse is the main one. **The extension beyond auctions is plausible and
largely untested.** Hiring, tendering, and competitive recruitment have the right structure on paper;
nobody has measured the curse in most of them, and the members that reach into those settings are
argument rather than evidence. What should not be taken from this engine is that competitive processes
are bad, that winners are fools, or that a party should bid below their estimate as a general policy.
The claim is narrow: the spread decides the outcome, nobody in the contest can see it, the rule reads
only the maximum, and the loss arrives disguised.

## Origin

The economics and psychology of competitive bidding, wired as one process on a persona. The twist is
Capen, Clapp and Campbell's, demonstrated by Bazerman and Samuelson and mapped by Kagel and Levin.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The exchange of concessions toward terms (negotiation)**: Positions, interests, anchors,
  concessions, and the joint search for an agreement are the negotiation engine's. A bidding contest
  has no exchange in it: the parties do not talk to each other, and the rule does the deciding.
- **Commitment growing on what has been spent (escalation)**: The increments that make stopping the
  only definite loss, and the sunk cost that pulls the next one, are the escalation engine's. The
  pushing member reads what that does inside a contest and hands the mechanism back.
- **What a thing is worth (worth-logic, money, capital)**: By whose standard something counts as
  valuable, how a price is a number, and the forms of capital are those engines'. This engine takes the
  value as given -- as a figure the parties are estimating -- and reads the estimating.
- **Rarity that raises wanting (scarcity, desire)**: Limited supply as a cue, and appetite for an
  object, are those engines'. The wanting-seen member reads one narrow case, appetite arriving from the
  presence of rivals, and delegates the rest.
- **Overconfidence in one's own estimate (bias, metacognition)**: The tilts in handling a figure, and
  the persona's read on their own knowing, are those engines'. The curse needs no overconfidence: it
  runs on well-made estimates that happen to scatter, which is why it is in this engine and not there.
- **Being outdone by a rival (outdone, comparison, envy)**: What it is to be beaten, and the reading of
  oneself against others, are those packages'. This engine reads the mechanism, not the feeling about
  losing.
- **The instrument's design (choice-architecture, captology)**: How a format shapes what people do,
  and the engineering of behaviour through interfaces, are those engines'. Auction format matters
  enormously and belongs to the trade rather than here.
- **The market and its rules (the world)**: Auction law, procurement regimes, reserve prices, and
  antitrust are practices with their own standards. They set the format; this engine reads what any
  format that awards to the maximum does.
- **The contest as authored symbol (the play's pitch)**: A bidding war staged to **stand for** a theme
  -- the ruinous victory that mirrors a motif of pride -- is refused: that is authored key, the play's
  pitch. Bidding sets what the winning cost and selected for; the meaning read into it is the play's.

## Encoding

Source to constraint. A process tree: one root, four movements, twelve forms.

- **[bidding](process_bidding.md)** (the root): one indivisible thing priced separately by several
  parties, read on the spread of the estimates, carrying the twist -- winning is the evidence that you
  were wrong. Anchored by Capen, Clapp & Campbell (1971).
- **[the contest](process_contest_bidding.md)**: one object and a field of people who want it. Anchored
  by Kagel & Levin (2002). [the one thing](process_the_one_thing.md), [the field](process_the_field.md),
  [the wanting seen](process_the_wanting_seen.md).
- **[the estimating](process_estimating_bidding.md)**: the private number each party arrives at.
  Anchored by Bazerman & Samuelson (1983). [the private number](process_the_private_number.md),
  [the scatter](process_the_scatter.md), [the unshared](process_the_unshared.md).
- **[the winning](process_winning_bidding.md)**: a mechanism that reads only the top of the
  distribution. Anchored by Klemperer (2004) and Capen et al. (1971).
  [the top estimate](process_the_top_estimate.md), [the pushing](process_the_pushing.md),
  [the triumph](process_the_triumph.md).
- **[the reckoning](process_reckoning_bidding.md)**: what the winner finds out, and where the engine is
  seen for one thing. Anchored by Roll (1986) and Kagel & Levin (2002).
  [the overpay](process_the_overpay.md), [the defence](process_the_defence.md),
  [the next contest](process_the_next_contest.md).
