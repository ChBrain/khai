# Self-Discrepancy: Reference

## Line of Work

Self-discrepancy as a **multi-type** engine: the gap a persona feels between its actual self and a self-guide it is measured against, and the specific affect that gap produces -- taken across the two discrepancies the theory distinguishes and the standing profile that tunes them. The domain does not model the strategic manner in which a persona pursues a goal -- eager toward gains under promotion, vigilant against losses under prevention -- which is the regulatory-focus engine's (Higgins's later, separate theory); nor the dejection emotions themselves, sadness and disappointment, which are the sadness engine's; nor the agitation emotions themselves, anxiety and guilt, which are the anxiety and guilt engines'; nor the global sense of self-worth, which is the self-esteem engine's; nor the present self-story, which is identity's; nor the future selves a persona could become, which are the possible-selves engine's. It models the comparison itself: the actual self held against a guide, and the patterned emotion the shortfall releases.

The spine is Higgins's self-discrepancy theory. Its central claim is that different kinds of self-discrepancy produce different kinds of emotional discomfort. A persona holds self-guides -- the ideal self (its hopes, wishes, and aspirations for who it wants to be) and the ought self (its duties, obligations, and responsibilities, who it believes it must be) -- and compares its actual self against them. A discrepancy between the actual self and the ideal self signifies the absence of positive outcomes and produces dejection-related emotions (sadness, disappointment, discouragement, dissatisfaction). A discrepancy between the actual self and the ought self signifies the presence of negative outcomes -- a threat of sanction -- and produces agitation-related emotions (anxiety, guilt, unease, self-reproach). The magnitude and the chronic accessibility of a given discrepancy govern how strongly and how readily it is felt, so a persona can carry a standing vulnerability to one guide's shortfall. The altitudes are those: the comparison, its ideal and ought discrepancies with their distinct affective signatures, and the standing discrepant profile.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **Regulatory focus**: The engine models the gap between the self and its guide, not the manner in which goals are pursued. Regulatory focus -- the resting orientation toward how a goal is pursued, eager toward gains under promotion or vigilant against losses under prevention -- is owned by the regulatory-focus engine; self-discrepancy is the felt distance between the actual self and the ideal or ought guide, and the dejection or agitation it produces. The two share Higgins's ideal/ought self-guide vocabulary but are distinct theories: self-discrepancy is about the gap and its emotion, regulatory focus about the strategic style of pursuit. They compose -- a promotion-focused persona is often the one whose ideals govern, a prevention-focused one the one whose oughts do -- but the gap is not the pursuit style.
- **Sadness**: The engine models the ideal discrepancy, not the dejection it produces. Sadness -- the low, deflated affect of loss and disappointment as a felt emotion -- is owned by the sadness engine; self-discrepancy's ideal form owns the specific gap (actual self short of the ideal, a hoped-for good absent) that produces dejection, handing the emotion itself across. The discrepancy is the cause, the sadness the feeling.
- **Anxiety and guilt**: The engine models the ought discrepancy, not the agitation it produces. Anxiety and guilt -- the apprehensive, threatened, self-reproachful affects as felt emotions -- are owned by the anxiety and guilt engines; self-discrepancy's ought form owns the specific gap (actual self short of the ought, a duty breached and a threat incurred) that produces agitation, handing the emotions themselves across. The discrepancy is the cause, the anxiety and guilt the feelings.
- **Self-esteem**: The engine models the discrepancy against a specific guide, not the global evaluation of the self. Self-esteem -- the overall sense of one's own worth -- is owned by the self-esteem engine; self-discrepancy is the specific, guide-relative gap and its patterned affect, which feeds self-esteem but is not the global appraisal. A persona's chronic discrepancies shape its self-worth; the worth itself is the self-esteem engine's.
- **Identity and possible-selves**: The engine models the present gap against a standard, not the self-content on either side of it. Identity -- the persona's present self-story, who it takes itself to be -- is the identity engine's; the future selves a persona could become, hopes for, or dreads are the possible-selves engine's. Self-discrepancy takes the actual self and the guides as given and models the comparison between them and its affect; the hoped-for possible self can supply the content an ideal guide is built from, but the possible self is the future picture, the self-discrepancy the present measurement of the actual self against a guide.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per file.

- **[self-discrepancy](process_self_discrepancy.md)** (the root, process): The comparison of the actual self against a self-guide, and the affect of the gap. Anchored by Higgins (1987).
- **[ideal](process_ideal.md)** (process, form): The actual:ideal discrepancy -- the persona short of the self it hoped to be, a positive outcome absent, producing dejection (sadness, disappointment). Anchored by Higgins (ideal discrepancy predicting dejection).
- **[ought](process_ought.md)** (process, form): The actual:ought discrepancy -- the persona short of the self it is bound to be, a negative outcome present, producing agitation (anxiety, guilt). Anchored by Higgins (ought discrepancy predicting agitation).
- **[discrepant](position_discrepant.md)** (position, trait): The standing profile -- how large and accessible a persona's discrepancies chronically are, and whether the ideal or ought guide dominates. Anchored by Higgins (chronic accessibility).
