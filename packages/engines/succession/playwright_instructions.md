# Instructions: Succession

How a Playwright wires the succession engine into a play. You wire by linking the play's
own content to the engine's files; the engine files stay untouched, they are canon. The
Roadie plumbs the engine's law into Instructions Knowledge on deploy. Authoring
guidance, not runtime content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human sets which places are being reclaimed by life -- the raw ground first taken, the sequence marching, the mature community holding, or the wild retaking what was human -- and whether that reclaiming is the place's slow background or a live movement in the play.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, a place being reclaimed links under Shown the stage of its succession: the pioneers on the raw ground, the progression of communities, the mature end-community, or the rewilding of what was human. Play the succession as the life returning shown -- the weed in the crack, the scrub on the burn, the old-growth, the forest closing over the street -- present in what the place puts before whoever enters, never narrated, and never as a mirror of a persona's own recovery or a civilization's fall. Keep it the life returning, not the disturbance that cleared the ground (that is geomorphology, fire, hydrology, erosion), the rot that precedes it (decay), the owner's withdrawal (dereliction), or the climate that sets the destination (climate). You link to the engine, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- The disturbance that bared the ground is geomorphology's, fire's, hydrology's, and erosion's; the material rot is decay's; the owner's withdrawal is dereliction's; the climate that sets which mature community is possible is climate's. Succession is the land's-forces family's answer to all the rest -- the reclaiming that follows every ruin -- and the family's keystone, the wild returning. Whether the stage rings true to the place and its ground is the team's review.

## Knowledge

- Succession is the biological reclaiming of a place: life returning in an ordered sequence once the ground is bared or the human hand withdraws (Cowles's sequence first read on the dunes; Clements's directional climb to a climax; Gleason's caveat that the end-state is a tendency, not a destiny; Weisman's world without us). It wires on a place at Shown, and it is the living reclaiming the ground -- the answer to every disturbance the other forces deal. Four stages carry it -- pioneer is the first tough life on the raw ground, the lichen and the fireweed that ready it; progression is the ordered march of communities, each changing the ground and giving way to the next, toward maturity; maturity is the mature, self-perpetuating community the sequence tends to, the old-growth and the climax, a tendency not a destiny; rewilding is the twist, the wild taking back what was human -- the field gone to forest, the abandoned city retaken, the ruin in the wood. Read a place by the stage of its reclaiming, and read rewilding two ways: the wild's return that is the human's loss.

## System

- Do give a place being reclaimed its stage under Shown; do play the succession as the life returning shown, present not narrated; do keep the reading the reclaiming -- the pioneers, the progression, the maturity, the rewilding -- not a feeling and not a symbol.
- Do read rewilding as the keystone twist: the wild retaking the human place is not only the human's loss but the land's return, the ruin of the one and the reclaiming of the other in one green advance.
- Do let the sequence turn: no maturity is permanent, and a fresh disturbance -- a burn, a flood, a fall -- resets the reclaiming to bare ground and the sequence begins again.
- Do not model the disturbance that cleared the ground (geomorphology, fire, hydrology, erosion), the rot (decay), the withdrawal (dereliction), or the climate that sets the destination (climate). Do not make the reclaiming mirror a persona's recovery or a civilization's fall (that is the play's pitch). Do not edit the engine's files; wire only from the play's side.
