# Process: Patina

## Taxonomy

[Decay](process_decay.md), the mode this force works in.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: decay

## Initiated by

The same slow entropy, laid down evenly and gently, becomes a surface that time comes to prize.
Patina starts wherever decay is slow and unbroken enough to skin rather than eat -- the bronze greening to a even coat, the copper roof gone soft blue, the oak silvered, the brass mellowed, the stone step hollowed by a century of feet.
It is decay read the other way: not the fabric failing but the years made visible on it.

## Direction

Across the surface, evenly, deepening.
Where rust flakes and crumble sheds, patina holds -- an oxide or a wear that seals rather than opens, thickening into a skin that protects the matter beneath even as it marks it.
It moves toward the look of age: the sheen of handling, the softened edge, the mellow that no new-made thing can be given, only earned.

## Lever

The value of the age against the impulse to clean it back to new.
The carrying force is the slow even settling of time onto the surface; the resistance is the polish, the strip, the restorer's hand that reads the mark as dirt and takes it off.
Here the place's fabric and its worth pull opposite ways from every other mode: to arrest this decay by cleaning it is to destroy the very value it made, and the choice of whether the mark is loss or worth is the seam decay hands to restoration.

## Echo

The object enriched by time, and the choice it forces.
The place shows the earned surface -- the threshold worn true, the green bronze, the wall that could only have come by age -- and carries the question beneath: to keep it is to let decay stand as value; to renew it is to erase the years for a newness that reads as false.
Patina is dated from the time it took to earn, and it is the mark a restoration must decide to preserve or wipe -- the age-value the modern eye came to prize over the clean.
