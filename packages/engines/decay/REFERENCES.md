# Decay: Reference

## Line of Work

Decay as **process**: the material entropy a place undergoes -- matter losing the
order a maker imposed on it, shown in the place's own fabric before anyone names
it. The domain does not model the setting itself, which is the space engine's;
nor the live atmospheric load that feeds it, which is the weather engine's; nor
the surface worn from outside by an agent, which is the erosion engine's; nor the
owner's withdrawal that lets it run, which is the dereliction engine's; nor the
intervention that arrests it, which is the restoration engine's. It models the
slow breakdown in the matter, and reads it two ways -- as loss, and as the value
of age.

The spine is the ambivalence at the heart of ruin: that decay is not only the
negation of a made thing but, held in a certain light, a new form with a worth of
its own. Simmel read the ruin as the equilibrium where human will, which built
upward, and nature's pull, which draws down, meet in a shape that is neither and
has its own aesthetic. Riegl named the modern turn: the cult of age-value, the eye
that prizes the marks of time over the clean surface of the new. So the engine
gives entropy four modes -- rust in the metal, rot in the organic, crumble in the
mineral -- and keeps a fourth, patina, that is the same process laid down slowly
enough to be prized rather than mourned, the seam where decay hands the choice to
restoration.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The setting itself (space)**: The engine models the decaying of a place, never
  the place as such. Its layout, scale, affordances, and standing induced mood are
  the space engine's; decay borrows the place's Shown face and works its fabric,
  and re-owns none of the furniture. Space builds the fabric; decay is the entropy
  in it.
- **The live atmospheric load (weather)**: The front that hits the place today --
  the wind, the rain, the heat -- is the weather engine's; decay is the slow
  aftermath that the front's residue feeds. Weather wets and bleaches in the hour;
  decay is what years of that wet then make the matter do. Weather hands its
  residue to decay, and decay carries it forward in the fabric.
- **The surface worn from outside (erosion)**: Erosion is material removed from
  the outside by an agent -- wind, water, frost abrading the surface away; decay is
  matter losing its own internal order, oxidising, rotting, and disintegrating from
  within. Erosion carves the cliff; decay rusts the rail. The boundary is the
  agent: erosion is the world wearing the surface off, decay is the matter breaking
  itself down.
- **The owner's withdrawal (dereliction)**: Decay is the material process;
  dereliction is the social non-act -- an authority ceasing to maintain -- that
  leaves the place to it. A maintained place still decays, slowly; a derelict one
  decays fast because nothing arrests it. Decay is impersonal and runs in any
  matter; dereliction is the human withdrawal that unleashes it, and the two meet
  where the upkeep lapses.
- **The intervention that arrests it (restoration)**: The deliberate act against
  decay -- the repair, the conservation, the renewal -- is the restoration engine's;
  decay is the fall restoration acts upon. Patina is where they meet: whether to
  preserve the age-value or wipe it back to new is restoration's choice on decay's
  own product.
- **A persona's feeling, and the ruin as authored symbol**: How the decay makes a
  persona feel -- the melancholy of the ruin, the unease of rot -- is the persona's
  own engines'; decay delivers the material fact, not the feeling. And decay made
  to **stand for** a persona's fall, the crumbling house that mirrors the dying
  line, is refused: that is authored key, the play's pitch, not the place's force.
- **The mode itself is not engine content**: The engine declares the four modes
  exist; which one a given place shows lives on the place, linked under its Shown
  chapter. The engine refuses to fix a place's decay for it.
