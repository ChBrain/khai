# Grounding: Reference

## Line of Work

Grounding as **process**: how two personas build mutual understanding turn by turn, each contribution accepted only on the evidence that it landed, against the common ground they already share -- the joint work of reaching the mutual belief that the addressee has understood well enough to go on. The domain does not model the one-pass gap between what is said and what is understood, the loss in a single act of transmission, which is the language engine's; nor the mending of a damaged relationship, a rupture taken to resolution, which is the repair engine's; nor the individual utterance-act being grounded, which is the speech-act engine's. It models the collaborative construction of understanding itself: the evidence given and read, the criterion reached or missed, the repair that saves it, the failure that dooms it.

The spine is the grounding theory of communication: language use is a joint action, and understanding is not delivered by the speaker but achieved by the two together. A contribution has a presentation and an acceptance phase, and it is grounded only when the addressee returns evidence -- a continuer, an acknowledgment, a demonstration, a fitting next turn -- sufficient for current purposes, by the least collaborative effort that will serve. Where evidence is missing or negative, conversational repair mends the trouble before the talk goes on; where evidence is skipped or faked, the parties misground -- proceed on ground they only believe they share, and the error compounds unseen. The four moves are the ways understanding is reached, shown, mended, or missed: acknowledgment, demonstration, clarification, misgrounding.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **Language**: The engine models the collaborative closing of the understanding-gap, not the gap itself. The one-pass loss between what is said and what is understood -- a persona moving meaning across the gap, in a single act -- is owned by the language engine; this engine governs the joint, multi-turn work by which two personas establish understanding despite that gap, which the one-pass account does not stage.
- **Repair**: The engine models the mending of a misunderstanding in the moment, not the mending of a relationship. The rupture in a tie taken to resolution -- an account offered and a bond restored -- is owned by the repair engine; this engine's clarification repairs an utterance-level trouble in understanding before the talk resumes, a different repair at a different altitude.
- **Speech act**: The engine models the acceptance that grounds an utterance, not the utterance-act itself. The illocutionary force that makes a saying an act -- the assertive, the directive, the promise -- is owned by the speech-act engine; this engine governs the uptake half, the evidence by which such an act is shown to have landed and enters the common ground.
- **Empathy**: The engine models the construction of shared understanding of a message, not the reading of another's inner state. The capacity to take in what another feels is owned by the empathy engine; grounding is a joint, evidenced construction of mutual understanding of what was meant, not a one-way apprehension of feeling.
- **Memory**: The engine models the live accretion of common ground in interaction, not the store of shared knowledge as held. The traces a persona keeps and rebuilds are owned by the memory engine; this engine governs the moment-to-moment adding-to and drawing-on of the shared base as two personas talk, not its standing contents.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per file.

- **[grounding](process_grounding.md)** (the root): The joint construction of mutual understanding to the grounding criterion, each contribution grounded only on sufficient evidence and then entering the common ground. Anchored by Clark & Brennan (grounding and the evidence ladder) and Clark (language as joint action).
- **[acknowledgment](process_acknowledgment.md)** (move): Light positive evidence -- the continuer, the token, the fitting next turn that signals receipt and lets the talk go on; cheap, usually enough, but evidence of receipt more than of comprehension. Anchored by Clark & Brennan and Clark & Schaefer (the low rungs of the evidence ladder).
- **[demonstration](process_demonstration.md)** (move): Strong evidence -- the readback, paraphrase, or enactment that shows understanding and lets the speaker confirm it before the talk builds on it; costly but secure. Anchored by Clark & Brennan and Clark & Schaefer (display and demonstration).
- **[clarification](process_clarification.md)** (move): Repair -- trouble in understanding flagged (self- or other-initiated) and mended in a side-sequence before the talk resumes. Anchored by Schegloff, Jefferson & Sacks (the organization of repair) and Schegloff (misunderstanding in interaction).
- **[misgrounding](process_misgrounding.md)** (move): The failure -- evidence skipped or faked, the criterion assumed met when it is not, so the parties proceed on unshared ground and the error compounds unseen. Anchored by Clark (least collaborative effort misapplied), Schegloff (uncaught misunderstanding), and Stalnaker (presupposing common ground not shared).
