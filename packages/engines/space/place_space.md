# Place: Space

## Taxonomy

Space is a place engine (TO SHOW). It models the physical setting as a party to what happens in it, not a backdrop to it: the built and natural environment that acts on the persona who enters -- affording some conduct and refusing other, and carrying a mood -- and that the persona acts back on, claiming and marking the space and charging it with meaning, so that bare space becomes lived place. It models the setting itself as a mover, and holds that apart from the other people who happen to be inside it, whose presence is a different thing.

## Shown

What the setting presents before anyone in it acts: its scale and its bounds, the way in and the way out, the light and the layout, the arrangement of things that greets a persona at the threshold. The space announces what kind of place it is -- open or closed, grand or mean, guarded or free -- before a single word is spoken in it, and the persona reads that announcement before they read anyone present.

## Holds

The standing behavioral program the setting carries, and the marks of those who dwell in it. A place holds a pattern of conduct that persists across whoever occupies it -- the hush a sanctuary keeps, the volume a market allows -- a program prior to its current occupants and pressed on each new one; and it holds the territory-structure of its dwellers, the boundaries drawn, the ground claimed, the personal marks left, the arrangement that says who belongs where. What the space holds outlasts the particular people in it.

## Offers

What the setting affords and lays on the persona: the actions its layout and features make possible and invite -- a path to walk, a seat to take, a corner to hide in, a stage to be seen on -- and the mood it induces, the ease or the unease, the draw or the flight. It offers the persona ground to make their own, a region they can bound and mark and return to; and it offers the meaning a space takes on once dwelt in, the plain room become a home, the field become a battlefield.

## Withheld

What the setting refuses: the conduct it will not afford or forbids outright, the region a persona cannot enter or claim, the exit it may deny, the shelter it may lack. A place withholds as surely as it offers -- the low ceiling that forbids standing tall, the open plain that withholds all cover, the locked ground that admits no claim -- and it withholds its meaning from those who only pass through, yielding it only to those who dwell. What a space denies shapes the persona as much as what it grants.
