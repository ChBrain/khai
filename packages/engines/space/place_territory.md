# Place: Territory

## Taxonomy

Territory is an expression of the space engine. It models the persona acting on the setting: the region a persona bounds, claims, marks, personalizes, and defends -- space made one's own by drawing borders across it and regulating who crosses them. It is the dweller's hand on the place, the counter-direction to what the setting affords, and it is a claim over a region, not the spacing a persona keeps from another person.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: space

## Shown

The marks a claim leaves on the space: the coat on the chair, the fence at the property line, the name on the door, the arrangement of a desk that says this is mine and that is yours. Territory shows itself in signs meant to be read -- the persona announces the border before anyone tests it, so the claim is seen and, mostly, honored without a word.

## Holds

The boundary a persona draws and the control they keep over it: territory is the regulation of access -- who may enter, who is kept out, when the border opens and when it closes -- the dialing of privacy up and down by the walls one raises and lowers. It holds the personalization that makes a space an extension of the self, the marks and objects that carry identity into the ground, so that the region comes to hold who the dweller is and the intrusion on it is felt as an intrusion on them.

## Offers

What claiming a space gives the persona: a ground of their own, a base secured, a privacy achieved by controlling the border rather than by hiding; the settledness of a place that answers to them, the ease of a room arranged to their hand. Territory offers control over the meeting of self and world -- a place to withdraw to and a place to admit from -- and the identity a personalized ground reflects back, the home that says who lives there.

## Withheld

What the claim refuses and what refuses the claim: to the outsider, territory withholds entry, use, and welcome -- the border that keeps them out, the ground they may not take. And the persona is refused the claim where the space forbids it -- the region already held by another, the place that admits no marking, the transient ground one passes through but cannot own. A territory defended withholds from the intruder; a space that cannot be claimed withholds from the would-be dweller.
