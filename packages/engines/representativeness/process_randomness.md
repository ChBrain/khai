# Process: Randomness

## Taxonomy

[Representativeness](process_representativeness.md), the chance form.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: representativeness

## Initiated by

The persona expects a chance process to produce results that look like the prototype of randomness -- evenly mixed, streak-free, self-correcting -- and reads any run that fails to as nonrandom.
This is the misconception of chance: randomness judged by resemblance to what random ought to look like, so real chance, which is streakier than expected, is read as loaded.
A short run is trusted to mirror the whole, and a streak taken to owe a correction.

## Direction

Toward a chance that looks representative, and away from how chance behaves.
The misconception reads local resemblance as the signature of randomness: a coin's six heads in a row is judged unlikely or rigged because it does not look random, a small sample is expected to show the population's proportions, and after a run one way the other is felt to be due.
The direction expects the prototype of randomness at every scale -- the law of small numbers, that small samples should represent the parent as faithfully as large ones -- when only the long run smooths out, and the short run is lumpy and streaky by nature.

## Lever

The prototype of randomness, spent as a law the process must obey.
The misconception runs on representativeness applied to chance: the persona holds a picture of what a random sequence should look like -- balanced, alternating, without long runs -- and judges real sequences by their fit to it, reading the ubiquitous streaks and clusters of genuine chance as departures needing explanation.
The lever is the expectation of self-correction and local balance: because a fair process is believed to look fair at every stretch, a streak is read as an imbalance that must be repaid, or as evidence the process is not fair at all.

## Echo

Real chance is read as pattern, and small samples are over-trusted.
It is the representativeness error behind the gambler's fallacy and its twin the hot hand, the clustering illusion, and the faith that a handful of cases settles a question -- each a case of chance failing to look like its own prototype and being misread for it.
It is [priors](process_priors.md) and [conjunction](process_conjunction.md)'s sibling: statistics overridden by likeness, here the behavior of random processes specifically. The seeing of a false pattern in noise as a standing tilt is the bias engine's; this owns the expectation that chance resemble the prototype of chance.
