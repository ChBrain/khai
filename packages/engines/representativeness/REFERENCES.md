# Representativeness: Reference

## Line of Work

Representativeness as **process**: the judgment through which a persona reads the probability that a thing belongs to a category, or came from a process, off how much it resembles the prototype -- and lets that likeness override the statistical structure that should govern. The domain does not model the sister heuristic that judges by ease of recall, which is availability's; nor the one that draws an estimate toward a reference figure, which is anchoring's; nor the mode by which a choice among options is reached, which is decision's; nor the fixed social stereotypes this look hardens into and the standing catalogued tilt it leaves, which the bias and stigma engines hold. It models the reading of resemblance as probability itself: similarity to the type substituted for the odds of the type.

The spine is the psychology of judgment under uncertainty. Asked for the probability that an object belongs to a class, or that a sample came from a population, or that a process will produce an outcome, a person often answers by how much the case resembles the prototype -- a heuristic that ignores base rates, sample size, and the reliability of the evidence, because resemblance is insensitive to all three. It is one of the original three heuristics of judgment, the sister of availability and anchoring, and its errors are systematic: base-rate neglect, the conjunction fallacy, and the misconceptions of chance that follow from the belief that chance should look representative at every scale. The three forms are those: priors, conjunction, randomness.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **Availability**: The engine models probability read from resemblance to a prototype, not from ease of recall. Judging frequency or likelihood by how readily instances come to mind is owned by the availability engine; the two are sister heuristics of judgment under uncertainty with distinct levers -- similarity to a type here, fluency of retrieval there.
- **Anchoring**: The engine models the read from resemblance, not the estimate pulled toward a reference value. The drawing of a numeric judgment toward a first figure is owned by the anchoring engine; representativeness judges by fit to a prototype, anchoring by distance from a starting number, and the two are distinct routes.
- **Decision**: The engine models a category or probability judgment, not the mode by which a choice among options is reached. How a persona decides -- intuitively, deliberately, by heuristic or satisficing -- is owned by the decision engine; representativeness supplies a judgment such choosing may use, and owns the estimation, not the choice.
- **Bias and Stigma**: The engine models the live reading of resemblance as probability, not the fixed social stereotypes it hardens into or the standing catalogued tilt it leaves. The prejudiced category applied to persons and the codex entry are owned by the bias and stigma engines; representativeness is the judgment process of which those are the residue, and owns the resemblance-for-probability substitution, not the settled stereotype.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per file.

- **[representativeness](process_representativeness.md)** (the root): The probability read off resemblance -- likeness to the prototype substituted for the odds of the type, overriding base rate, sample size, and reliability. Anchored by Kahneman & Tversky (subjective probability as a judgment of representativeness).
- **[priors](process_priors.md)** (form): The base-rate route -- a case judged by fit to the stereotype with the prior probability set aside, so a rare type is read from a fitting description however rare it is. Anchored by Kahneman & Tversky (on the psychology of prediction) and Tversky & Kahneman (insensitivity to prior probability).
- **[conjunction](process_conjunction.md)** (form): The added-detail route -- a specific, prototype-fitting story judged likelier than the broader class that contains it, resemblance rising as probability falls. Anchored by Tversky & Kahneman (the conjunction fallacy, the Linda problem).
- **[randomness](process_randomness.md)** (form): The chance route -- a random process expected to produce prototypically-random results, so streaks are read as loaded and small samples trusted to mirror the whole. Anchored by Tversky & Kahneman (belief in the law of small numbers).
