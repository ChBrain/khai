# Process: Priors

## Taxonomy

[Representativeness](process_representativeness.md), the base-rate form.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: representativeness

## Initiated by

The persona judges which category a case belongs to by how well it fits the stereotype, and sets aside how common the categories are to begin with.
This is the neglect of priors: base-rate information ignored in favor of resemblance, the description trusted over the prevalence.
A case that looks like a rare type is read as that type, however rare it is.

## Direction

Toward the category the case resembles, regardless of the category's prior odds.
Neglect of priors reads the fit between the case and the stereotype and lets it decide, so a personality sketch that matches the picture of a small profession is assigned to it even when a larger, duller profession is far likelier on the numbers alone.
The direction ignores the frequency: the more vivid and diagnostic-seeming the description, the more completely the base rate is crowded out, and a worthless description can push it out as well as a good one.

## Lever

The resemblance, spent as if the prior did not exist.
Neglect of priors runs on the insensitivity of similarity to frequency: how much a case looks like a type says nothing about how common that type is, so a judgment made on likeness carries no information about the base rate and behaves as though it were uniform.
The lever is the missing prior: the persona has an easy signal, resemblance, and a hard one, prevalence, and reports the easy one -- so the estimate tracks the stereotype and not the odds it should have started from.

## Echo

The estimate matches the stereotype, and the base rate is lost.
It is the core representativeness error -- the reason a described individual is judged more likely a librarian than a farmer though farmers vastly outnumber librarians -- and it holds even when the persona knows the base rate, because the resemblance arrives with a force the number does not.
It is [conjunction](process_conjunction.md) and [randomness](process_randomness.md)'s sibling in the same fault: statistics overridden by likeness, here the prior probability specifically. The fixed social stereotype the neglected prior hardens into is the bias and stigma engines'; this owns the ignoring of the base rate under resemblance.
