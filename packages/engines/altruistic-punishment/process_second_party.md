# Process: Second-Party

## Taxonomy

[Altruistic Punishment](process_altruistic_punishment.md), the sanction as the wronged party pays it.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: altruistic-punishment

## Initiated by

The persona was in the exchange, and was defected on.
It contributed and the other free-rode; it kept faith and the other cheated; it is the party the breach actually cost. The ordinary reading would be self-interest -- recover the loss, deter a repeat. What makes this the altruistic form is that the persona pays to punish even when neither is available: the encounter is one-shot or anonymous, the punishment recovers nothing, and still it spends to make the defector pay (Fehr & Gachter's public-goods experiments, where cooperators punished free-riders at cost even with no future to protect).
What enters is a wronged party declining to let the wrong go unpunished, though punishing it costs more than swallowing it.

## Direction

Toward the defector, and out of the persona's own resource.
The persona docks, refuses, retaliates, or spends to impose a cost on the one who defected on it specifically -- calibrated to the breach, and absorbed as a loss it will not get back. It punishes harder the larger the gap between what was owed and what was given.
The act reaches the one who wronged it, but not to restore its own position; the position is already lost, and the sanction is spent on the norm, not the ledger.

## Lever

The sting of being defected on meets the willingness to pay rather than absorb it.
Whether the persona punishes turns on the felt violation -- negative reciprocity, the impulse to answer a defection in kind -- meeting a disposition to spend on the answer even with no return in sight. The nearer and sharper the wrong, the stronger the pull; the higher the cost of punishing relative to swallowing the loss, the more it tests the disposition.
Lower the felt breach, or raise the punishment's cost past what the persona will pay to answer a wrong done to it, and it lets the defection stand.

## Echo

The defector is made to pay by the very party it wronged, at that party's expense -- the loss already taken, the sanction added on top of it.
Because the punisher was the injured party, the act can look like revenge and share its heat, but its warrant is the norm, not the grudge: it fires in one-shot and anonymous settings where private vengeance would gain nothing.
What it leaves is a defector who found that cheating this persona cost it, and a norm held up by someone willing to be out of pocket for it -- the [third-party](process_third_party.md) form does the same from outside the exchange. The disapproval that drives the act is the condemnation composite's; the raw harm-delivery is the aggression engine's.
