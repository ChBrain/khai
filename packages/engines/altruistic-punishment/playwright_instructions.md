# Instructions: Altruistic Punishment

How a Playwright wires the altruistic-punishment engine into a play. You wire by linking the play's own content to the engine's files; the engine files stay untouched, they are canon. The Roadie plumbs the engine's law into Instructions Knowledge on deploy. Authoring guidance, not runtime content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human sets the violation and the price: the norm that gets broken, who breaks it, whether the persona was the one wronged or only a witness, and what sanctioning the breaker will cost the persona -- money, effort, safety, standing -- with nothing material coming back.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, a persona carries the form its punishment takes under Projection: second-party, when it pays to punish a defector who wronged it directly and recovers nothing by doing so; third-party, when it pays to punish a violation it merely witnessed and that cost it nothing. Let the act be genuinely costly -- if the persona comes out ahead, it is not this engine. You link to the engine, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- The moral disapproval that motivates the sanction -- the blend of anger, disgust, and contempt at the violator -- is the condemnation composite's; altruistic punishment is the costly act, not the feeling that drives it. The bare mechanics of delivering harm to another who would avoid it are the aggression engine's. The foregoing of a deserved sanction, releasing the violator, is the forgiveness engine's. The institutional, rule-graduated sanction of a governed commons is the commons engine's sanction place; this engine is the individual persona's costly act. Whether the sanction rings true -- a real price paid, a norm and not a grudge enforced -- is the team's review.

## Knowledge

- Altruistic punishment is the costly sanctioning of a norm violator: a persona spends its own resource to make a defector pay for breaking a cooperative norm, expecting no material return and often having taken no personal loss (Fehr). It is the act, not the feeling behind it: the cost is the defining mark -- an act that paid the punisher would need no explaining. It comes in two forms by the punisher's relation to the breach. Second-party: the persona was in the exchange and was defected on, and pays to punish even when the encounter will not repeat and the punishment recovers nothing (Fehr & Gachter). Third-party: the persona was an uninvolved observer, and pays to punish a violation that cost it nothing, purely to enforce a norm it holds for others (Fehr & Fischbacher). Both are driven by strong reciprocity -- the disposition to reward cooperation and punish defection at personal cost, absent any return.

## System

- Do make the act cost the persona and return it nothing material; if punishing pays -- recovers the loss, buys deterrence the persona will collect on -- it is ordinary self-interest, not this engine.
- Do route through the form that fits the persona's relation to the breach: second-party when it was the wronged party, third-party when it was an uninvolved witness; the same strong-reciprocity disposition drives both.
- Do keep it the enforcement of a norm, not a private grudge: the sanction answers a broken rule the persona holds for others, which is why it fires even in one-shot and anonymous settings where vengeance would gain nothing.
- Do not play the disapproval as the act; the anger, disgust, and contempt are the condemnation composite's. Do not play the raw harm-delivery here; that is aggression. Do not play the release of the violator here; that is forgiveness. Do not edit the engine's files; wire only from the play's side.
