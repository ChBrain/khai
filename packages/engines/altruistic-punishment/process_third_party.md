# Process: Third-Party

## Taxonomy

[Altruistic Punishment](process_altruistic_punishment.md), the sanction as an uninvolved observer pays it.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: altruistic-punishment

## Initiated by

The persona watched a norm broken between others, and it was not the one harmed.
A defector cheated a third party, or broke a rule that touched the persona not at all. It has no loss to recover and no stake in the exchange -- the pure case of enforcement for its own sake. That people pay to punish here at all is the sharpest evidence the motive is the norm and not the self (Fehr & Fischbacher's third-party punishment game, where uninvolved observers spent to punish unfair allocations they merely witnessed).
What enters is a bystander who could walk on untouched, choosing instead to spend on a wrong done to someone else.

## Direction

Toward a violator the persona has no quarrel with, on behalf of a norm it holds for everyone.
The persona imposes a cost on the transgressor -- sanctions, exposes, withholds, spends to make the breach expensive -- with no injury of its own to answer and no return to collect. It punishes in proportion to the violation's severity, not to any harm it took, because it took none.
The act reaches a stranger's wrong and settles nothing personal; it enforces the rule as a rule, for the community that shares it.

## Lever

A witnessed violation meets a persona that holds the norm for others, not only itself.
Whether it punishes turns on reading the act as a breach of a shared norm -- one that binds regardless of who was hurt -- meeting a disposition to enforce that norm at personal cost even from outside. The generality of the commitment is the lever: the norm is felt to apply to all, so its breach demands an answer even when the answerer was untouched.
Narrow the norm to only-what-touches-me, or raise the cost past what the persona will spend for a stranger's compliance, and the bystander stays a bystander.

## Echo

A violation that cost the persona nothing is answered by the persona anyway, out of its own resource and for the norm's sake alone.
Because there is no personal injury behind it, third-party punishment is altruistic in the plainest sense -- pure enforcement, the clearest signal that the persona will pay to keep a shared rule shared.
What it leaves is a norm defended past the circle of those it directly protects, and a violator sanctioned by someone with no reason but the rule -- the [second-party](process_second_party.md) form does the same from inside the exchange. The disapproval that drives the act is the condemnation composite's; the raw harm-delivery is the aggression engine's.
