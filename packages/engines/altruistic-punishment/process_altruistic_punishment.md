# Process: Altruistic Punishment

## Taxonomy

Costly enforcement of a cooperative norm.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: altruistic-punishment

## Initiated by

A persona sees a cooperative norm broken, and moves to make the breaker pay -- out of its own pocket.
The trigger is a defection: a free-rider who took the benefit and withheld the contribution, a cheat who broke faith in an exchange, a violator who crossed a rule the persona holds. What sets the process apart from a mere grievance is the willingness to spend on it -- money, effort, safety, standing -- with nothing material coming back (Fehr).
What enters the process is not the wish that the violator suffer but the readiness to pay to make it so.

## Direction

Toward a sanction the violator cannot ignore, at a price the persona absorbs.
The persona does not merely disapprove; it acts -- docks the defector, refuses the deal, exposes the cheat, spends to impose a cost -- and it does so knowing the act will not recover its own loss and may draw retaliation.
The cost is the point, not an accident: an act that paid the punisher would need no explaining; this one enforces the norm precisely because the persona will bear a loss to enforce it.

## Lever

A violated norm meets a persona willing to be the one who answers it.
Whether the process fires turns on two things meeting: that the breach is read as a norm violation and not a private slight, and that the persona is disposed to pay the enforcement cost rather than leave it to someone else or let it slide. Strong reciprocity is that disposition -- to reward cooperation and punish defection even absent any personal return.
Change either and the force is recalled: reframe the breach as no violation, or let the cost run past what the persona will spend on a stranger's compliance, and the sanction does not land.

## Echo

The sanction takes one of two forms, by the persona's relation to the breach: [second-party](process_second_party.md), where the persona was in the exchange and was defected on and pays anyway to punish, or [third-party](process_third_party.md), where the persona was an uninvolved observer and pays to punish a violation that cost it nothing.
The two are not stages; a persona meets some breaches as a wronged party and others as a bystander, and the same disposition drives both.
What ends the process is the sanction delivered and the cost paid -- and what it leaves is a norm made a little more expensive to break, for the violator and for everyone watching. The moral disapproval that motivates the act -- the anger, disgust, and contempt at the violator -- is the condemnation composite's; the bare mechanics of delivering harm are the aggression engine's; this engine owns the costly, norm-enforcing sanction itself.
