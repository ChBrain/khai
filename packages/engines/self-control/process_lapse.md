# Process: Lapse

## Taxonomy

[Self-Control](process_self_control.md), the failure -- the impulse wins.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: self-control

## Initiated by

The override gives out, and the persona does the wanted-but-unwise thing.
This is the lapse, self-control's failure: the impulse wins over the standard -- the diet broken, the retort loosed, the purchase made, the resolution abandoned -- despite the persona knowing better and, often, meaning otherwise.
It is the ordinary defeat of the will, not a rare collapse but a common event: temptations are resisted far less often than they are felt.

## Direction

With the impulse, over the standard -- the line crossed the persona meant to hold.
The lapse is the control's collapse in a particular moment: the want carries the deed, the ought overridden by the very impulse it was meant to master, and the persona acts against its own endorsed goal.
Its direction is the yielding -- the impulse's way, taken.

## Lever

What the lapse spends -- the standard, and sometimes the persona's belief in its own control.
A lapse comes when the pull outstrips the hold: a temptation too strong, a standard too abstract, a will already taxed by prior exertion -- the strength model's account of a limited resource run down, though how much the exertion truly depletes is contested and the effect unreliable, so the lapse is better read as the pull exceeding the hold than as a fuel gauge hitting empty.
A production holds this lever by making the failure human: the impulse genuinely stronger than the effort available, the lapse earned by a real temptation, not staged as mere weakness.

## Echo

The impulse is acted, the standard broken -- and what follows can compound it or repair it.
A single lapse can spiral, the broken resolution read as proof the whole effort is lost, so the persona abandons the standard entirely; or it can be a stumble the persona resumes control after, the line re-held.
Where the yielding is not a lapse but a settled compulsion -- the craving of dependence, control overwhelmed as a standing condition rather than a moment -- the phenomenon is the addiction engine's; the lapse is self-control's ordinary, recoverable failure, the [restraint](process_restraint.md) that did not hold.
