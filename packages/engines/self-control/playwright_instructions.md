# Instructions: Self-Control

How a Playwright wires the self-control engine into a play. You wire by linking the play's own content to the engine's files; the engine files stay untouched, they are canon. The Roadie plumbs the engine's law into Instructions Knowledge on deploy. Authoring guidance, not runtime content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human sets the self-control terrain: what impulses and temptations pull at the persona, and what standards it endorses that they run against -- so a moment holds a genuine conflict between a wanted impulse and a held goal.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, a persona links the self-control a moment runs under its Projection: what impulse pulls against what standard, whether it is restrained in the moment, delayed across an interval, or lost to a lapse, and how disciplined its standing disposition is. Let it read as the effortful holding of the ought over the want, and its cost. You link to the engine, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- The cold cognitive inhibition of a prepotent response for accuracy is the executive-function engine's; the valuation and discount that make a temptation pull are the reward engine's; the long-horizon passion and perseverance toward a distant goal is the grit engine's; the if-then plans that pre-empt the conflict are the implementation-intention engine's; the compulsive craving of dependence is the addiction engine's. This engine supplies the in-the-moment override of impulse -- the restraint, the delay, and the lapse, and the disciplined disposition beneath them.

## Knowledge

- Self-control is the effortful, in-the-moment override of a prepotent impulse in favor of a standard the persona endorses -- a hot, impulsive want checked by a cool, reflective ought (Metcalfe & Mischel; Baumeister; Tangney). It runs in forms. Restraint is the override itself -- inhibiting the wanted-but-unwise response at the edge of action, the effortful no. Delay is delay of gratification -- forgoing a smaller-sooner reward for a larger-later one, and the cooling strategies (distraction, abstraction, distancing) that hold the interval (Mischel). Lapse is the failure -- the impulse winning, the standard broken, an ordinary and recoverable defeat, not a rare collapse. Disciplined is the trait -- a standing capacity to override impulse reliably, working largely through habits and structured situations that spare the persona the fight. Read a persona by what pulls at it, what it holds against the pull, and whether it restrains, delays, or lapses.

## System

- Do keep self-control the hot motivational override, not the cold cognitive one: it is a struggle against an impulse the persona actually wants, in favor of a goal it endorses -- let the desire be real and the holding effortful, so control reads as a fight, not a reflex.
- Do stage the forms: the impulse held back at the edge of action, the wait borne with cooling tactics, the lapse when the pull outstrips the hold -- and let restraint cost effort and delay cost an interval.
- Do make lapses human: the impulse genuinely stronger than the effort available, the failure earned by a real temptation and often ordinary; and let a lapse either spiral (the whole standard abandoned) or be resumed after. Do not lean on a literal willpower fuel-gauge -- the depletion state is unreliable; read the lapse as the pull exceeding the hold.
- Do let the disciplined persona win largely by arranging a life with fewer fights -- habits and structures that carry the standard -- not only by heroic resistance; and let over-control cost spontaneity. Do delegate the cold inhibition to the executive-function engine and the compulsive craving of dependence to the addiction engine. Do not edit the engine's files; wire only from the play's side.
