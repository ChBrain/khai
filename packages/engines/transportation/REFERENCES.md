# Transportation: Reference

## Line of Work

Transportation as a **multi-type** engine: a persona's absorption into a narrative world, taken across the three components it converges from and the trait that tunes them. The domain does not model the total absorption in a matched challenge one is acting in, which is the flow engine's; nor the general, content-agnostic absorption of attention, which is the absorption composite's; nor the audience's moral bond to particular characters, which is the allegiance engine's; nor the general mechanism of influence, which is the persuasion engine's. It models the narrative-specific state: being carried out of the here-and-now and into the world of a story, and the belief-softening that carrying works.

The spine is the media- and narrative-psychology of transportation. Gerrig introduced narrative transportation as a traveler's journey: the reader is carried into the story world and returns changed, having been, for a time, somewhere else. Green and Brock operationalized it as the convergence of three mental systems upon a narrative -- attention (all mental resources on the story), imagery (the story world vividly pictured), and feelings (genuine emotion for the tale's events and people) -- and showed its consequence: transportation is a mechanism of narrative persuasion, reducing the reader's ability to counterargue and bending story-consistent beliefs, an effect distinct from and often stronger than argument. Busselle and Bilandzic's narrative-engagement work refines the components (narrative understanding, attentional focus, narrative presence, emotional engagement). Dal Cin, Zanna and Fong established transportability as a stable individual difference in the readiness to be transported. The construct is bounded against flow: one can be transported -- moved, absorbed, persuaded -- with none of the challenge-skill matching or sense of agency that flow requires, because transportation is the absorption of a recipient, not a performer. The altitudes are those: the state, its three components, and its trait.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **Flow**: The engine models absorption into a narrative one receives, not into a challenge one performs. Flow -- action and awareness merged in a task whose challenge matches skill, with a sense of control and agency -- is owned by the flow engine; transportation is the absorption of a recipient carried by a story, with no challenge-skill matching and no agency, and the two are distinct states, one can be transported by a film while sitting perfectly still. The two compose only by contrast: flow is doing absorbed, transportation is receiving absorbed.
- **Absorption composite**: The engine models the narrative-specific carrying, not content-agnostic absorption. The absorption composite reads over attention, flow, and time-perception to model absorbed engagement in general; transportation is that absorption turned specifically onto a story world, with the imagery and belief-softening signatures the general case does not carry. Transportation is one occasion of absorption; the composite is the wider capacity.
- **Allegiance**: The engine models entry into the story world, not the moral bond to its characters. The audience's evaluation of particular characters into liking and disliking, and the enjoyment that rides on their fortunes, is owned by the allegiance engine; transportation is being carried into the tale as a whole, which allegiance to its people deepens but is distinct from -- one can be transported into a world whose characters one has not yet sorted.
- **Persuasion**: The engine models narrative transportation as a route to influence, not the general mechanism of influence. Persuasion -- the central and peripheral routes, the cues and appeals of changing minds -- is owned by the persuasion engine; transportation is the specific narrative route, changing belief by carrying the audience into a story rather than by argument, and it hands the general account of influence across while owning the narrative one.
- **Empathy**: The engine models emotional engagement with a narrative, not the capacity to feel into another person. Empathy -- the channel between persons for taking in another's inner state -- is owned by the empathy engine; transportation's resonance is feeling for a story's people and events as a reader, which empathy may feed but which is directed at the narrative, not at a present other.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per file.

- **[transportation](process_transportation.md)** (the root, process): Being carried into a narrative world by converging attention, imagery, and feeling, returning changed with belief softened. Anchored by Gerrig (the journey) and Green & Brock (the convergent process and its persuasion).
- **[entry](process_entry.md)** (process, component): The attentional crossing -- the real world receding as the story world becomes the present. Anchored by Green & Brock (attention) and Busselle & Bilandzic (attentional focus, narrative presence).
- **[imagery](process_imagery.md)** (process, component): The sensory picturing -- the story world seen and felt vividly in the mind's eye. Anchored by Green & Brock (imagery); the transportation-imagery model.
- **[resonance](process_resonance.md)** (process, component): The emotional engagement -- feeling the fortunes of the tale and its people. Anchored by Green & Brock (feelings) and Busselle & Bilandzic (emotional engagement).
- **[transportability](position_transportability.md)** (position, trait): A standing readiness to be carried into stories, from the reader lost in any book to the one who stays outside every one. Anchored by Dal Cin, Zanna & Fong (transportability).
