# Register: Reference

## Line of Work

Register as **position**: a specialized variety of language a persona commands -- a code layered on the base tongue, tied to a domain and the community that works in it -- and what that code does to the line between those who can follow and those who cannot. The domain does not model the base language itself -- the channels of speaking, hearing, reading, writing, and thinking, and how close the tongue sits to the body -- which is the language engine's; nor the self a persona draws from the community, which is the social-identity engine's. It models the code as a membership layer: the specialized speech that binds insiders and gates outsiders at once.

The spine is the sociolinguistic finding that language comes in varieties tied to use and to community -- registers, jargons, codes -- and that a specialized register does two things in one act: it lets those who share it be fast and exact (communion), and it holds those who do not on the far side of understanding (closure). A defining constraint is that the register rides its base tongue: it is a variety within a language, not a universal code, so two experts of the same field but different mother tongues share the concepts and not the register. The four modes are what the code does at the boundary: shorthand (bind), badge (mark), closure (shut out), translation (cross over).

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **Language**: The engine models the specialized variety layered on the base tongue, not the base tongue itself. The channels of using language -- speaking, hearing, reading, writing, thinking -- and how far a whole language sits from the body are owned by the language engine; this engine governs a code carried on top of that language, orthogonal to the persona's proficiency in it (one may be mother-tongue fluent yet not command a register, or shaky in general yet fluent in one).
- **Social identity**: The engine models the code that marks a community, not the self a persona draws from belonging to it. The group-belonging "we" -- the categorisation of the self into the in-group -- is owned by the social-identity engine; this engine governs the specialized speech through which that membership is shown and gated, not the self-concept behind it.
- **Status**: The engine models the register as a code, not the rank it can signal. The place in a deference order that command of a prestige register may badge is owned by the status engine; this engine governs the code itself, which status may then read as a mark.
- **Socialization**: The engine models the register once commanded, not the process of being brought into the community that teaches it. How the objective social world is internalised into a member is owned by the socialization engine; this engine governs the code acquired, not the acquiring.
- **Role**: The engine models the specialized speech a role speaks, not the role's mandate. The socially sanctioned expectations that govern what a persona may do and be are owned by the role engine; this engine governs the register that a role, among other things, talks in.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per file.

- **[register](position_register.md)** (the root): A specialized variety of language layered on the base tongue, tied to a domain and community, doing communion and closure at once. Anchored by Halliday (register as variety by use) and Swales (the discourse community).
- **[shorthand](position_shorthand.md)** (mode): The compressed, presupposing code that lets insiders who share the ground be fast and exact. Anchored by Bernstein (the restricted code) and Halliday (register economy).
- **[badge](position_badge.md)** (mode): The register as a mark of membership -- commanding it shows belonging, and reads others' belonging from their command. Anchored by Swales (discourse community membership) and Eckert & McConnell-Ginet (community of practice).
- **[closure](position_closure.md)** (mode): The register as a wall -- the same code that binds insiders shuts the lay listener out, by design or by side effect. Anchored by Halliday (the antilanguage, marking off a group).
- **[translation](position_translation.md)** (mode): The bridge -- dropping the code to carry the meaning across to the outsider in the plain tongue they share. Anchored by Gumperz (code-switching) and Bernstein (the elaborated code, made explicit).
