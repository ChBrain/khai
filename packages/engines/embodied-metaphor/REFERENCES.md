# Embodied Metaphor: Reference

## Line of Work

Embodied metaphor as a **position** engine: how a persona's abstract concepts are scaffolded on recurring, near-universal embodied source domains, so that bodily and sensorimotor experience gives the abstract its shape and leaks into abstract judgment -- taken across five of the primary metaphors a persona lives by. The domain does not model the general mechanism of mapping any source onto any target, which is the analogy engine's; nor the rhetorical use of a metaphor to persuade, which is the framing engine's; nor the target concepts themselves -- affection, power, morality, valence -- which are their own engines'. It models the specific bodily-to-abstract scaffolding: the primary metaphors in which a physical sensation and an abstract appraisal are quietly the same, and the leakage between them in both directions.

The spine is Lakoff and Johnson's conceptual metaphor theory and the experimental social-psychology of embodiment. Lakoff and Johnson argued that abstract thought is pervasively structured by metaphor, and that the most basic metaphors are grounded in bodily experience: we understand affection through warmth, importance through weight, good and power through verticality, morality through cleanliness, the positive through light, because these correlations are learned in the body from infancy. Later theorists named these primary metaphors and traced their origin to the repeated co-occurrence of a bodily sensation and a subjective judgment. The experimental turn made the scaffolding testable: Williams and Bargh showed that incidental physical warmth raises interpersonal warmth and generosity; Meier and Robinson, that good is judged up and bad down; Jostmann, Lakens, and Schubert, that physical weight raises felt importance; Zhong and Liljenquist, that moral transgression prompts a wish to cleanse and that washing eases guilt; Meier, Robinson, and Clore, that brightness carries positive and darkness negative valence. The engine takes the scaffolding principle as its anchor and five well-evidenced primary metaphors as its expressions. The altitudes are those: the embodied scaffolding, and its temperature, verticality, weight, cleanliness, and brightness forms.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The mapping mechanism (analogy)**: The engine models specific, recurring embodied mappings, not the general capacity to map a source onto a target. The alignment of relational structure between any base and target and the transfer across it is owned by the analogy engine; embodied metaphor is a special, entrenched case -- a fixed set of near-universal mappings whose source is always the sensorimotor body -- rather than the open-ended mapping mechanism. Analogy is the machinery that could map anything; this engine owns the handful of bodily mappings a persona lives by without choosing them.
- **Rhetorical metaphor (framing)**: The engine models the cognitive scaffolding, not the persuasive device. A metaphor deployed to frame a claim and move an audience is owned by the framing engine; embodied metaphor is the pre-rhetorical structuring by which the persona already thinks the abstract through the body, whether or not it ever speaks a metaphor to persuade. Framing wields a metaphor on purpose; this engine is the metaphor the persona cannot help thinking in.
- **The target concepts themselves**: The engine models the bodily scaffolding of an abstract concept, not the concept. Affection is the love and attachment engines'; status and power the hierarchy engine's; moral judgment the moral-judgment engine's and disgust the disgust engine's; emotional valence the emotion engine's. Embodied metaphor owns only the fact that these abstractions are felt through warmth, height, weight, dirt, and light -- the scaffolding, not the building.
- **Construal-level distance**: The engine models bodily source domains scaffolding abstract targets, not the setting of representational level by psychological distance. How near or far a thing is construed, and the high or low level of abstraction that follows, is owned by the construal engine; embodied metaphor's mappings are from the body's sensations, a different axis from construal's distance-to-abstraction. Both connect the concrete and the abstract, but by different routes.
