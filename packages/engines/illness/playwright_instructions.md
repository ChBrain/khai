# Instructions: Illness

How a Playwright wires the illness engine into a play. You wire by linking the play's own content to the engine's files; the engine files stay untouched, they are canon. The Roadie plumbs the engine's law into Instructions Knowledge on deploy. Authoring guidance, not runtime content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human decides two things before anything else, because everything follows from them: whether the persona has a diagnosis, and whether the condition will end. The first decides what is available to them at all; the second decides whether the bargain a society offers the sick can hold. Remember the engine models what a condition costs a persona in standing and story, not what it does to their body, and that it is built on a frame the social model of disability rejects -- read the references before writing a play about disability.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, a persona links under Projection where they stand: whether they hold a ticket, which terms of the role they are meeting or failing, and which story shape is available to them. Run the four movements in any order and several at once -- a persona can be rewriting their biography months before a diagnosis exists, and can be refused a ticket and never reach the role at all. Keep it the social fact: the body's demands are the body engine's, the discrediting mark is stigma's, the handling is coping's. You link to the engine, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- What the body is doing is the body engine's; the discrediting attribute and its management are stigma's; appraisal and problem-focused action are coping's; the carer's side is caregiving's; the stance toward one's own ending is mortality's and loss is grief's; socially sanctioned expectations in general are role's, of which the sick role is one documented instance; how a persona is treated by a procedure and what it buys is the heeding composite's. Whether a production's condition is genuinely contested, and whether a persona's account is chaos or a restitution story coming apart, are the team's review.

## Knowledge

- Illness is what a condition makes of a persona socially and biographically rather than physically. It begins where a bodily fact becomes a social one -- where the persona has to tell somebody, ask for something, be believed, be excused, or be treated, and what happens next depends on other people. It wires on a persona at Projection and runs in four movements. The disruption (Bury): the taken-for-granted floor gives way and what was automatic becomes a decision, so the persona pays attention for facts everybody else gets free; they cannot say why this happened and will build an explanation out of nothing, and the most available material is self-blame; resources are mobilised at a cost that lands on a household; and the biography is rewritten backward as well as forward, which is a bereavement with no funeral. The ticket: a diagnosis is a currency rather than an explanation, since time off, treatment, benefit, and being believed are issued against a name and not against symptoms -- the naming reorganises the past into evidence, the contested condition leaves its bearer doing the work of proof instead of the work of recovery, the unseen condition is believed on paper and doubted in the room, and the wrong name admits the persona to a category that then discounts every fact against it. None of this says whether a condition is real; it says what a society can currently issue a name for. The role (Parsons): exemption from ordinary obligations and absolution from blame, against an obligation to regard being ill as undesirable and cooperation with those competent to treat it -- two gifts and two prices, with absolution rationed morally rather than medically, so conditions read as self-inflicted receive a fraction of it. The telling (Frank): restitution, the resolvable shape the room wants, which cannot bend; chaos, the account with no sequence and no agency, which is what some illness is actually like from inside and which empties a room; and quest, the illness told as a journey that yielded something, available only from enough distance. The engine's twist is at the root: the exemption is a loan against a recovery, so a condition that will not end has no role available -- the persona performs recovery-seeking past the point of belief, or stops and loses the absolution with the exemption, and is then read not as ill but as difficult.

## System

- Do decide the diagnosis and the prognosis first, and let the rest follow: a persona without a ticket is in a different play from one with a ticket, and a persona whose condition will not end is in a different play again.
- Do stage the broken account, since it is where an engine about bodies turns out to be about justice: the persona will convict themselves of causing it, and so will the room, and neither needs a villain.
- Do read the absolution as morally rationed rather than medically: a persona whose condition is read as self-inflicted carries the condition and the verdict together, and the rationing is where a society shows what it actually thinks illness is.
- Do let the obligation be a duty about an attitude, dischargeable only by display -- adjustment reading as complacency, equanimity as giving up, and a persona's expertise in their own long condition reading as non-compliance.
- Do give a persona chaos and let it be unbearable. Do not resolve it into a lesson, which is quest, and do not have them pull themselves together, which is restitution. A play that stages chaos honestly must let somebody sit in it.
- Do let a persona reach quest, and never make it the reward for having suffered correctly: a room that cannot bear chaos and has lost restitution will start demanding meaning, and that demand is itself a thing being done to the persona.
- Do not model the body's demands (body), the discrediting mark (stigma), the handling of a stressor (coping), the carer (caregiving), the stance toward death (mortality), or role expectations in general (role). Do not use the engine to say whether a condition is real. Do not make the illness stand for a theme; that is authored key, the play's pitch. Do not edit the engine's files; wire only from the play's side.
