# Illness: Reference

## Line of Work

Illness as **process**: what a condition makes of a persona socially and biographically rather than
physically. The engine begins where a bodily fact becomes a social one -- where the persona has to
tell somebody, ask for something, be believed, be excused, or be treated, and what happens next
depends on other people rather than on the condition.

That boundary is the reason the engine exists. khai already carries the body's demands, the
discrediting mark, the effortful handling of a stressor, and the stance toward death. What none of
them carries is the arrangement a society makes with the sick, and what it costs to be admitted to it.

**The disruption** is Bury's. Illness, particularly chronic illness, is a disruption of biography
rather than an interruption of a schedule: the structures of everyday life come apart, the
explanatory systems a persona held stop applying to them, and both have to be rebuilt while they are
unwell. The engine breaks it into four, and two of them are where the drama sits. The **broken frame**
converts what was automatic into a decision, so the persona pays attention for facts everybody else
gets free, and the exhaustion that produces is routinely mistaken for a symptom. The **broken account**
is worse: most illness is simply an event with no reason in it, and a persona will construct an
explanation out of nothing rather than hold none -- so the most available material is self-blame, and
the persona arrives at a story that costs them. Around them, others build the same kind of account,
and theirs mostly lands on the persona too.

**The ticket** is the engine's own framing of a well-documented mechanism: the diagnosis is not
primarily an explanation, it is a **currency**. Time off, treatment, benefit, accommodation, and being
believed by an employer or a spouse are issued against a name and not against symptoms. Its four
branches are the four things that can happen at the gate -- the **naming**, which reorganises the past
into evidence and issues a future somebody else wrote; the **contested** condition, where the tests
come back normal and the persona's effort moves from getting better to being believed, a project in
which every reasonable move (becoming expert, gathering documentation, finding others, seeking the
practitioner who will say it) reads from outside as wanting to be ill; the **unseen** condition, which
is believed on paper and doubted in the room and taxes the persona for their good days; and the
**wrong name**, where the category is accepted and misdescribes, and the evidence that would overturn
it is precisely what the category teaches everybody to discount. The engine says plainly, in the
member text and here, that **none of this is a claim about whether a condition is real** -- it is a
claim about what a society can currently issue a name for, and those two sets have never matched.

**The role** is Parsons'. Illness treated as a recognised social position rather than a private
misfortune, with two entitlements -- **exemption** from ordinary obligations and **absolution** from
blame -- and two duties attached: an **obligation** to regard being ill as undesirable and to want to
leave it, and **cooperation** with those competent to treat it. The engine takes the arrangement
seriously as a good thing before it takes it apart, because it is one: being excused work, released
from duty, and held blameless for one's own body is considerable, is extended routinely, and genuinely
protects people. Two of the four then do the damage. Absolution is **rationed morally rather than
medically** -- conditions read as self-inflicted receive a fraction of it, so a persona in those
categories carries the condition and the verdict together. And the obligation is a duty about an
**attitude**, dischargeable only by display: adjustment reads as complacency, equanimity reads as
giving up, and expertise in one's own long condition reads as non-compliance.

**The telling** is Frank's. Three shapes, unequally available. **Restitution** -- yesterday healthy,
today sick, tomorrow healthy again -- is the culturally preferred shape by a wide margin, is what
medicine tells and visitors want, and is true for most illness; its property is that it cannot bend,
so a condition that fails to resolve does not modify it, it falsifies it. **Chaos** is the
anti-narrative: no plot, no agency, no because. Frank's insistence matters and the engine keeps it --
chaos is not a failure of the teller or a stage to be moved through, it is what some illness is
actually like from inside, and it is unbearable to hear, so it is interrupted and answered with advice,
and the persona stops offering it. **Quest** tells the illness as a journey that yielded something,
requires enough distance to be telling from somewhere, and is the only shape available for an
unresolving condition -- which is exactly why a room that cannot bear chaos will start demanding it.

The **twist** is at the root and the whole engine turns on it. The exemption is **a loan against a
recovery**. Because relief is granted on condition that the persona wants to leave it, a condition
that will not end has no role available: the persona must perform recovery-seeking indefinitely, past
the point where anybody believes it, or stop -- and the absolution goes with the exemption, since one
bargain supplied both. A persona who has lost the absolution is no longer read as ill. They are read
as difficult. So the personas who need the arrangement most permanently are the ones least able to
hold it.

**On the evidence, stated plainly.** This is a sociological engine and its material is theory and
qualitative research rather than measurement, which is a real limit and not a hedge. **Parsons' sick
role is a functionalist ideal type from 1951**, and the criticisms of it are as canonical as the model:
it was built for acute illness and does not fit chronic illness, disability, or most mental illness;
it assumes a compliant patient and a benevolent, competent profession; it assumes care is reachable
and affordable, which excludes a great deal of the world; and it treats illness as deviance to be
restored to normality. The engine uses it as a description of the **bargain a society still visibly
operates**, not as an account of what illness is -- and the twist is precisely the model's best-known
failure, held as the engine's centre rather than smoothed over. The **social model of disability
rejects this frame outright**, and the objection deserves stating rather than absorbing: on that view
the problem is a world built for some bodies and not a condition to be cured, and a persona is
disabled by arrangements rather than by a body. This engine does not settle that argument and is not
equipped to; a play about disability should read the objection first, and should not take an engine
built on the sick role as neutral ground. **Bury's biographical disruption** is durable and widely
applied but is interpretive work from a small interview study, and later research qualifies it --
for personas already living hard lives, or ill from early in life, there may be no undisrupted
biography for illness to disrupt. **Frank's three narratives** come from his own illness and his
reading of illness memoirs, are a typology rather than a measured taxonomy, and chaos is defined
partly by being untellable, which makes it close to unevidenceable by design. The **contested illness**
material is ethnographic and advocacy-adjacent, and it is the place where the engine is most easily
misused in either direction. What should not be taken from the engine: that a condition lacking a
diagnosis is not real, that a diagnosis settles what somebody's life is like, or that any of these
shapes is a stage a persona ought to be progressing through.

separately sourced; the twist is the sick role's own best-known failure.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **What the body is doing (body)**: Hunger, fatigue, pain, arousal, and sickness as physical states
  making their demands felt are the body engine's. This engine begins after that, at the point where
  the condition has to be told to somebody, and a persona can be very ill here with the body engine
  saying nothing new.
- **The discrediting mark (stigma)**: A deeply discrediting attribute, the discounting it triggers,
  and the work of managing a mark that can still be hidden are the stigma engine's. Many conditions
  carry a mark and this engine does not model it -- the absolution member reads only the moral
  rationing of blame, which is a term of the bargain rather than a spoiled identity.
- **Handling a stressor (coping)**: Appraisal, problem-focused action, and avoidance are the coping
  engine's. Illness says what the persona is in; coping says what they do with it.
- **The carer (caregiving)**: The behavioural system moving a persona to protect and provide for a
  vulnerable other is the caregiving engine's. This engine reads the ill persona, and notes only that
  the mobilising lands on a household.
- **The stance toward death (mortality, grief)**: A confrontation with one's own ending is
  mortality's, and loss is grief's. A condition may be terminal and the engine still reads only what
  it does to the persona's standing and story.
- **Socially sanctioned expectations in general (role)**: What a persona may do and be under a given
  set of expectations is the role engine's. The sick role is one such position, held here because it
  has a specific documented structure -- two entitlements and two duties -- and because its failure
  mode is this engine's twist.
- **Being believed by an institution (heeding, law, recognition)**: How a persona is treated by a
  procedure and what that buys is the heeding composite's; the claim-encounter-standing sequence is
  recognition's. The ticket reads the gate itself and not the encounter at it.
- **The condition as authored symbol (the play's pitch)**: An illness made to **stand for** a theme
  -- the wasting disease that mirrors a motif of decline -- is refused: that is authored key, the
  play's pitch. Illness sets what the condition costs in standing and story; the meaning read into it
  is the play's.

- **[illness](process_illness.md)** (the root): the point at which a bodily fact becomes a social one,
  carrying the twist -- the exemption is a loan against a recovery, so the personas who need it most
  permanently are the ones least able to hold it. Anchored by Parsons (1951).
- **[the disruption](process_disruption.md)**: the taken-for-granted breaking, and a biography
  rewritten in both directions. Anchored by Bury (1982) and Charmaz (1983).
  [the broken frame](process_broken_frame.md), [the broken account](process_broken_account.md),
  [the mobilising](process_mobilising.md), [the rethinking](process_rethinking.md).
- **[the ticket](process_ticket.md)**: the name without which nothing is available. Anchored by
  Freidson (1970), Brown (1992), and Dumit (2006). [the naming](process_naming.md),
  [the contested](process_contested_illness.md), [the unseen](process_unseen.md),
  [the wrong name](process_wrong_name.md).
- **[the role](process_sick_role.md)**: the bargain and its terms -- two gifts, two prices. Anchored
  by Parsons (1951) and Conrad & Schneider (1980). [the exemption](process_exemption.md),
  [the absolution](process_absolution.md), [the obligation](process_obligation.md),
  [the cooperation](process_cooperation.md).
- **[the telling](process_telling.md)**: the shapes of story the condition permits. Anchored by Frank
