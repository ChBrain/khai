# Illness

Illness as process: what a condition makes of a persona socially and biographically rather than physically -- not the body's demands and not the discrediting mark, but what happens once a bodily fact has to be told, believed, excused, and treated -- across four movements: the disruption, where the taken-for-granted breaks and a biography is rewritten in both directions (Bury); the ticket, where a diagnosis gates every entitlement and a condition without a name leaves its bearer indistinguishable from a malingerer; the role, the bargain of exemption and absolution against an obligation to want to get well and to cooperate with treatment (Parsons); and the telling, the three shapes of story a condition permits -- restitution, chaos, and quest (Frank) -- with the twist that the exemption is a loan against a recovery, so the personas who need it most permanently are the ones least able to hold it.
