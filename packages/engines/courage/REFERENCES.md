# Courage: Reference

## Line of Work

Courage as **process**: how a persona moves toward a thing they fear for the sake of a good worth the facing -- not the absence of fear but action taken with it present and against its pull. The domain does not model the felt fear itself, the emotion the courage is taken against, which is the emotion engine's; nor the whole state of being under a threat that has not yet fallen -- the anticipation and the stress response -- which is the jeopardy composite's; nor the long stamina toward a distant goal, the sustained pursuit distinct from the acute facing of a fear, which is the grit engine's. It models the facing itself: the movement through fear toward a good, rightly sized between cowardice and recklessness.

The spine is the account of courage as a virtue and an act. Courage is not fearlessness -- the fearless have nothing to overcome -- but the performance of an action with the fear present and the physiological pull to flee felt and overridden; it is the mean between cowardice (fear felt too much, the good abandoned) and recklessness (fear felt too little, the danger courted for itself), and the mean is a reading of how much a danger deserves fearing and whether a good is worth that much. The kinds of courage divide by what is at risk: the body, one's standing, or the self. The three movements are those courages: physical, conviction, vital.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **Emotion**: The engine models the acting against a fear, not the fear itself. The felt fear the courage is taken against -- the appraisal of a threat and the aversive state it brings -- is owned by the emotion engine; courage begins where the fear is already present and governs the movement through it, not the feeling.
- **Jeopardy**: The engine models the act toward a feared thing, not the state of being under threat. The whole condition of a persona under a threat not yet fallen -- the baseline disposition, the anticipatory dread, the stress response -- is owned by the jeopardy composite; courage is the choice and the movement toward the threat within that state, not the state itself.
- **Grit**: The engine models the acute facing of a fear, not the long stamina toward a distant goal. The sustained perseverance and passion that carry a persona through years of obstacles are owned by the grit engine; courage faces a fear in the deed, where grit endures a distance over time -- kin, but not the same.
- **Status and Membership**: The engine models the facing of a social cost, not the standing or belonging risked. The rank a persona holds in a deference order is the status engine's, and the bind of belonging to a group is the membership composite's; conviction courage moves toward the loss of these for a principle, but the standing and the belonging themselves are theirs.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per file.

- **[courage](process_courage.md)** (the root): Movement toward a feared thing for the sake of a good worth the facing -- fear present and overridden, the mean between cowardice and recklessness. Anchored by Aristotle (the mean) and Rachman (acting despite fear).
- **[physical](process_physical.md)** (movement): The courage that faces bodily danger -- injury, pain, death moved toward for a good worth the body; the oldest and most legible courage, a reflex overridden. Anchored by Aristotle (martial courage) and Rachman.
- **[conviction](process_conviction.md)** (movement): Moral courage -- facing social cost, reputation, belonging, livelihood, for a principle; the slower, lonelier courage paid without the crowd's witness. Anchored by Putman (moral courage).
- **[vital](process_vital.md)** (movement): Psychological courage -- facing an inner or existential threat, illness, despair, a truth about oneself, rather than fleeing into denial; endured more than enacted, quiet and unwitnessed. Anchored by Putman (psychological courage) and Pury & Lopez.
