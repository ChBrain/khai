# @chbrain/khai-engine-courage

## 0.1.1

### Patch Changes

- 13b8812: Add the courage engine: how a persona moves toward a thing they fear for the sake of a good worth the facing -- not the absence of fear but action taken with it present and against its pull. The root (`process_courage.md`) declares courage as the mean between cowardice (fear felt too much, the good abandoned) and recklessness (fear felt too little, the danger courted for itself): fearlessness is not courage but its absence of occasion, and the courageous act is the paying of fear as a price for a good held to be worth more. Three movements divide the courages by what is at risk: `physical` (the body -- injury, pain, death -- moved toward for a good worth it, the oldest and most legible courage, a reflex overridden), `conviction` (moral courage -- facing social cost to reputation, belonging, or livelihood for a principle, the slower and lonelier kind paid without the crowd's witness), and `vital` (psychological courage -- facing an inner or existential threat, illness, despair, a truth about oneself, endured more than enacted).

  Found via a gap audit: the `virtue` engine is built on the seven-sin/virtue axes and omits the cardinal virtues, so courage was genuinely unowned. The clean complement to the `jeopardy` composite -- jeopardy is the state of being under threat, courage the act toward it. Bounded against `emotion` (the felt fear vs the acting against it), `jeopardy` (the state under threat vs the movement toward it), `grit` (the long stamina toward a distant goal vs the acute facing of a fear), and `status`/`membership` (the standing and belonging risked in moral courage vs the facing of their loss). The `moral` movement is named `conviction` because the stem `moral` is already an `awe` member. Warranted (LORE) on Aristotle (courage as the mean, martial courage), Rachman (fear and courage separated -- acting despite arousal), Putman (the three types and psychological courage), and Pury & Lopez (the modern synthesis and taxonomy). Set at patch as the free level; a new engine may warrant a minor at the maintainer's `bump:minor` label.
