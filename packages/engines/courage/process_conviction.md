# Process: Conviction

## Taxonomy

[Courage](process_courage.md), the courage that faces social cost for a principle -- moral courage.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: courage

## Initiated by

The threat is not to the body but to standing -- reputation, belonging, livelihood -- and the persona acts on a principle anyway: the whistleblower, the dissenter, the one who says the unpopular true thing in a room that does not want to hear it.
This is moral courage: the risk is to the persona's place among others, and the good is a truth or a right held to outweigh that place.
The fear here is of exclusion, not of injury.

## Direction

Toward the social cost, for the principle worth it.
The persona speaks or acts knowing it will cost them -- the room's regard, the group's welcome, the job, the friendship -- and does it because the principle is judged to matter more than the standing it will spend.
The direction is toward a loss deliberately incurred: not a danger survived but a price paid.

## Lever

The fear of exclusion and lost standing, overridden for a principle.
This fear is slower and less visible than the body's, and often harder to face precisely because there is no adrenaline and no single moment -- the cost is paid over weeks, alone, without the crowd's witness that steels physical courage. The lever is the holding of a principle above the need to belong, which the persona feels pulling the whole time.
It is the courage most easily talked out of, because the danger is deniable until the moment it is not.

## Echo

The principle is stood for, at a price to the persona's standing.
It is often the loneliest courage -- performed against one's own people, without applause, sometimes without ever being understood as courage at all.
The belonging risked is the membership composite's and the standing lost is the status engine's; this owns the facing of that loss for a principle -- the moving toward the cost, where those engines own the belonging and the rank themselves.
