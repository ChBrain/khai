# Instructions: Courage

How a Playwright wires the courage engine into a play. You wire by linking the play's own content to the engine's files; the engine files stay untouched, they are canon. The Roadie plumbs the engine's law into Instructions Knowledge on deploy. Authoring guidance, not runtime content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human defines the courage terrain: what the persona fears, what good facing it would serve, and what it will cost -- so the fear is real, the good is worth it, and the act has a price that makes it courage rather than ease.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, a persona links the courage the moment runs (physical, conviction, or vital) under its Projection. Let the fear be present and moved through -- the flinch overridden, the unpopular word said, the inner truth faced -- and let the act read as costly, neither rash nor easy. You link to the engine, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- The felt fear the courage is taken against is the emotion engine's; the whole state of being under a threat not yet fallen is the jeopardy composite's; the long stamina toward a distant goal is grit's. The standing risked in moral courage is the status engine's and the belonging risked is the membership composite's. This engine supplies the facing itself -- the movement through fear toward a good.

## Knowledge

- Courage is moving toward a feared thing for a good worth the facing -- not fearlessness but action with the fear present, the mean between cowardice (fear too much) and recklessness (fear too little). Physical courage faces bodily danger -- injury, pain, death -- the oldest and most legible. Conviction is moral courage -- facing social cost, reputation, belonging, livelihood, for a principle, the slower and lonelier kind. Vital is psychological courage -- facing an inner or existential threat, illness, despair, a truth about oneself, endured more than enacted.

## System

- Do keep the fear present: courage without felt fear is not courage but fearlessness, which has nothing to overcome -- so let the persona feel the pull to flee and move against it.
- Do make the act cost: remove the price and it is merely easy; let the courage be legible in what it risks -- the body, the standing, or the self.
- Do size the fear rightly: too little tips into the recklessness that courts danger for its own sake, too much into the cowardice that flees a risk worth taking -- courage is the mean, and the mean is a judgment.
- Do delegate the felt fear to the emotion engine and the state of being under threat to the jeopardy composite. Do not edit the engine's files; wire only from the play's side.
