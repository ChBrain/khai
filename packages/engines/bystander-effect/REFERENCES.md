# Bystander Effect: Reference

## Line of Work

The bystander effect as **process**: how the presence of other witnesses inhibits a persona from helping in an emergency, so that the more people who could act, the less likely and slower any one does. The domain does not model the effort-drop when a persona's contribution is pooled in a task, which is social-facilitation's loafing; nor the caregiving system that tends a victim once help is under way, which is caregiving's; nor the anxiety of being judged, which is apprehension's; nor the diffusion that exempts a harm one commits rather than a help one omits, which is moral-disengagement's. It models the inhibition of helping itself: the presence of co-witnesses working, through the steps by which a bystander decides, as a brake on the individual's own intervention.

The spine is Latané and Darley's social psychology of bystander intervention. Whether a witness helps in an emergency depends on passing a series of steps -- noticing the event, interpreting it as an emergency, assuming responsibility, knowing how to help, and deciding to act -- and the presence of other bystanders inhibits helping at several of them, so that individuals in groups intervene less than individuals alone. Three mechanisms carry the inhibition: diffusion of responsibility, by which the duty to act is divided among the witnesses; pluralistic ignorance, by which each reads the others' calm as evidence there is no emergency; and audience inhibition, by which the fear of misjudging the intervention before onlookers holds the persona back. The three forms are those: diffusion, pluralism, audience.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **Social facilitation**: The engine models the inhibition of emergency helping by co-witnesses, not the effort-drop in a pooled task. The slackening of a persona's exertion when their contribution is submerged in a group's output is owned by the social-facilitation engine's loafing; the two share the diffusion family, but bystander diffusion is of the responsibility to help in a crisis, and loafing is of effort in a shared task -- distinct settings and stakes.
- **Caregiving**: The engine models whether a persona initiates help amid others, not the behavioral system that tends a vulnerable other. The sensitive provision of care once helping is under way is owned by the caregiving engine; the bystander effect is upstream of it, governing whether the witness moves to help at all when others are present, not how the tending then goes.
- **Apprehension and Face**: The engine models the inhibition of helping, not the anxiety or the image that the audience form rides on. The anxious anticipation of being judged is owned by the apprehension engine; the image a persona guards from a watched error is owned by the face engine; audience inhibition recruits both but owns the specific result -- help withheld before onlookers for fear of a public misstep.
- **Conformity and Moral disengagement**: The engine models the misreading of an emergency from others' calm and the division of the duty to help, not the general matching of others nor the exemption of a committed harm. Taking others' responses as an information source is the conformity engine's informational influence, turned here on an emergency; the diffusion that excuses a harm one does, rather than a help one omits, is the moral-disengagement engine's displacement. The bystander effect owns the omission before witnesses, not the conformity or the commission.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per file.

- **[bystander effect](process_bystander_effect.md)** (the root): The inhibition of helping by the presence of other witnesses -- the more who could act, the less any one does, across the steps of the intervention decision. Anchored by Darley & Latané (diffusion of responsibility) and Latané & Darley (the unresponsive bystander).
- **[diffusion](process_diffusion.md)** (form): The responsibility route -- the duty to act divided among witnesses until each feels too little of it to move. Anchored by Darley & Latané (diffusion of responsibility) and Latané & Nida (group size and helping).
- **[pluralism](process_pluralism.md)** (form): The interpretation route -- each reading the others' calm as evidence of no emergency, the situation defined away by mutual composure. Anchored by Latané & Darley (group inhibition, the smoke-filled room).
- **[audience](process_audience.md)** (form): The evaluation route -- the fear of a misjudged intervention before onlookers holding the persona from acting. Anchored by Latané & Darley (audience inhibition) and the evaluation-apprehension account.
