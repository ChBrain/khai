# Process: Frost

## Taxonomy

[Erosion](process_erosion.md), the mode where ice breaks the ground.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: erosion

## Initiated by

Water seeps into the ground's cracks and freezes, and the ice prises the rock apart.
Frost starts wherever water gets into a joint and the temperature crosses freezing again and again -- the cliff in the mountain cold, the paved road in winter, the rock face that thaws by day and freezes by night -- each freeze wedging the crack a little wider.
It is the patient breaker: no single freeze does much, but the cycle, repeated season on season, shatters the soundest rock.

## Direction

Apart, along every crack, and down as the loosened pieces fall.
Frost widens what is already open -- the joint prised, the flake split off, the boulder cracked along its bedding -- and gravity takes the freed pieces down to gather at the foot.
It moves the place toward the shattered and the strewn: the frost-riven crag, the scree slope of broken rock below it, the heaved and broken ground, the shattered pavement -- and where the ice was a glacier, the whole valley ground to a U by the rock it carried.

## Lever

The expansion of freezing water against the rock's strength to hold together.
The carrying force is the roughly one-tenth swelling of water as it turns to ice, an irresistible wedge driven into every crack it fills; the resistance is the rock's own soundness and the absence of water or of the freeze-thaw cycle to drive it.
Give it water and a temperature that crosses freezing often, and the hardest rock is levered apart; keep it dry, or always frozen, or always warm, and the rock stands.

## Echo

The ground shattered and the broken pieces strewn below.
Frost leaves the place riven -- the cracked crag, the scree, the heaved road, the glaciated valley -- and hands the broken rock to gravity and to water, which carry the pieces on and down.
It is the agent of the cold and the high: the mountains are shaped as much by the ice prising them apart as by the water that carries the pieces off.
Frost is dated from the shattering, the rock given to the ice crack by crack.
