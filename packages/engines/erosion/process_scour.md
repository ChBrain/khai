# Process: Scour

## Taxonomy

[Erosion](process_erosion.md), the mode where water cuts the ground.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: erosion

## Initiated by

Running water meets loose or soluble ground and begins to lift and carry it.
Scour starts wherever water moves over a surface with force enough to take it -- the rain sheeting a bared slope, the stream biting its bed, the wave working the foot of the cliff, the flood tearing at the bank.
It is the strongest of the agents: water is heavy, it gathers into channels, and it grinds with the very load it carries.

## Direction

Down the line the water runs, cutting as it goes.
Scour incises -- the rill deepening to gully to ravine, the river cutting down toward its base level, the coast undercut until the overhang falls, the slope rilled and stripped of its soil.
It moves the place toward the channel and the cut: the ground opened along the water's path, the surface carved into the shapes running water leaves, the high held only where the water cannot reach.

## Lever

The water's cutting power against the ground's resistance to being lifted.
The carrying force is the water's mass, speed, and the abrasive load it already bears; the resistance is the ground's binding -- the vegetation that slows the sheet, the rock that will not dissolve, the cohesion of the soil.
Bare the slope or steepen the channel and the water bites deep and fast; hold the cover and the same water runs off clear, taking little.

## Echo

The ground cut and opened along the water's way.
Scour leaves the place carved -- the gully, the incised river, the undercut coast, the washed-out slope -- and hands the lifted material downstream to settle wherever the water slows.
It is the agent that shapes the most of the inhabited land: the valley the river cut, the coast the sea works, the field the rain takes a harvest of soil from each year.
Scour is dated from the cutting, the ground given to the running water grain by grain.
