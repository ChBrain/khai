# Process: Deflation

## Taxonomy

[Erosion](process_erosion.md), the mode where wind strips the ground.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: erosion

## Initiated by

Wind crosses a dry, loose, unbound surface and lifts the fine material off it.
Deflation starts wherever the ground is dry and bare enough for the air to take it -- the ploughed field in drought, the dune field, the dry lakebed, the land stripped of the cover that held its soil down.
It is the wind's theft: the fine grains carried off as dust, the coarser bounced along, the surface left behind lowered and coarsened.

## Direction

Downwind, and down to what the wind cannot lift.
Deflation strips the fines and leaves the stones -- the desert pavement of lag gravel too heavy to move, the blowout hollowed from the dune, the field's topsoil gone as dust on the horizon.
It moves the place toward the winnowed surface: the light carried away, the heavy concentrated, the ground both lowered and armoured by what is left, and the lifted grains flung against whatever stands to sandblast it.

## Lever

The wind's lifting power against the ground's weight, damp, and binding.
The carrying force is the wind's speed and turbulence, and the grains it already carries to blast with; the resistance is anything that pins the surface -- moisture, root, crust, the cover of plants, or a stone too heavy to lift.
Dry the ground and strip its cover and the wind takes it; wet it or bind it with roots and the same wind passes over, taking nothing.

## Echo

The surface winnowed, the fines gone and the coarse left.
Deflation leaves the place stripped and armoured -- the pavement, the blowout, the scoured pan, the sandblasted rock -- and hands the lifted dust far downwind to fall as loess, sometimes a continent away.
It is the agent of the dry land and the bared field: the dust bowl is deflation at human scale, the soil of ages lifted off in a season of drought and plough.
Deflation is dated from the stripping, the ground given to the wind grain by grain.
