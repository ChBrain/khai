# Piece: Script

## Taxonomy

[Agency](piece_agency.md), the facet this is a part of. Script models the program of action inscribed in the object's form -- the scenario the designer builds in: the roles it casts its users into, the sequence it imposes, the competences it assumes they have. Akrich's script of the technical object, read back out by the user in the using (her de-scription). This is not a play's script and carries no dialogue; it is the plan of conduct written into a thing's design, legible only when the thing is operated. It is distinct from prescription (the specific must and cannot): the script is the whole scenario the object proposes, prescription is the deontic force inside it.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: agency

## Place

In the object's whole configuration, wherever it is set to work -- not on one part but in the arrangement that assigns roles and orders steps. The kitchen laid out to make one cook and one server; the kiosk built to make one queue and one clerk: the script lives in the layout, and travels with it.

## Load Bearing

What the script holds up is the division of roles and the order of acts the object takes for granted. Remove it -- give the same task an object with no inscribed scenario -- and the roles blur, the sequence scatters, and the users must negotiate from scratch what the design had settled silently. The script bears the unspoken agreement about who does what and in what order; where it is absent, that agreement must be made by hand every time.

## Apparent

The scenario is mostly not shown; it is inferred from the form by anyone who reads it well -- the analyst who sees, in a machine's design, the disciplined worker it presumes, the language it expects, the world it was built for. To most users the script is invisible and simply lived; to the one who de-scribes it, the object confesses the society its maker imagined. What is apparent is a shape; what is inscribed is a scenario.

## Yearbook

The history of a script is the record of users who fit the role it cast and users who did not -- the ones the design assumed and served, and the ones it never imagined and so excluded or forced to adapt. Over time the gap between the scripted user and the actual one becomes the object's indictment or its vindication: a design dated by whom it was secretly written for.
