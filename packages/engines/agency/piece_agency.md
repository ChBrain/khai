# Piece: Agency

## Taxonomy

The root of the agency engine. It models the object as a social delegate: a designed thing that carries a program of action and enforces it on whoever meets it -- the speed bump that slows the driver, the cap that keeps the child out, the bench built so no one can sleep on it. The politics of artifacts (Latour, Winner), read on the object. It is distinct from the object's usability, which the usability engine owns (how the form communicates its use, not what conduct it enforces); from a persona's own agency, which self-efficacy and the like own (a person's capacity to act, read on a position, not the object's force); and from a setting's behavioral program, which space owns (an ambient field acting on the co-present, not a portable delegate carrying a specific maker's program).

## Place

Wherever the object is put to work. The program travels with the delegate into every hand, road, and doorway it is placed in: a speed bump enforces on this street and on any street, a lock on this door and on any door. The politics are in the object, not the site, and they go where it goes -- which is what makes the delegate portable in a way a place's program is not.

## Load Bearing

What the object holds up is a piece of the social order: a policy of conduct enforced with no person present. Remove the delegate and the policy it bore collapses -- take out the bump and the cars speed, pull the cap and the barrier drops, strip the bench and the plaza is sleepable again. The program is invisible until the object is gone and the conduct it held reverts. This is the object's whole force: it bears a rule in the world of action, in the maker's absence, and the rule stands only as long as the thing does.

## Apparent

Little of the program shows. A good delegate reads as mere design -- the bench looks like a bench, its dividing armrests like comfort, not like a law against lying down. The politics are embodied, not displayed, and that is why they work: the conduct is enforced before anyone recognizes a rule was set. What is apparent is form; what is borne is policy; and in the well-made delegate the two are meant not to match, so the enforcement passes for nature.

## Yearbook

The history of a delegate is the record of the conduct it enforced and on whom -- the drivers slowed, the sleepers turned away, the ones kept out and the one who got past. Over time the object's politics become legible in the pattern of who it served and who it excluded, and the delegate is judged, late and by others, for the program its maker inscribed and never signed. What an object did to people is written in who was stopped by it.
