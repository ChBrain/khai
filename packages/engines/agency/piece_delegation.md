# Piece: Delegation

## Taxonomy

[Agency](piece_agency.md), the facet this is a part of. Delegation models the source of the object's program: a human intention or morality handed to the object to enforce in the maker's absence -- the automatic door-closer standing in for the porter, the seatbelt buzzer nagging in the engineer's stead, the sleeping policeman doing the work of a constable who cannot be everywhere. Latour's delegation, the "missing masses" of social order handed down to things (Where Are the Missing Masses?), with Akrich. It is distinct from prescription (what conduct is enforced): delegation is who is absent and enforcing through the thing -- the object as a stand-in for a person who is not there.

## Place

In the object, standing where a person would otherwise have to be -- the doorway the porter no longer watches, the road the constable no longer patrols, the counter the clerk no longer minds. Delegation puts the object exactly where the absent human's authority used to stand.

## Load Bearing

What delegation holds up is the maker's will, made to persist without the maker. Remove the delegate and the will it carried has no one to enforce it: the door needs a porter again, the road a patrol, the rule a person. The object bears an absence -- it is a human decision frozen into a thing so it can keep acting when the human has gone home -- and where the object fails, the whole edifice of unattended order it was holding up comes down to be done by hand.

## Apparent

The absent hand rarely shows; the object seems to act on its own, and its origin in someone's decision is easy to forget. A good delegation reads as the nature of the thing, not as a policy someone chose -- the door "just closes," the belt "just beeps" -- and the maker who inscribed the intention is invisible in the artifact that carries it out. What is apparent is an object acting; what is borne is a person's will, still working after they have left.

## Yearbook

The history of a delegation is the record of the work a thing did in a person's place -- the doors closed, the belts fastened, the rules kept, all in the maker's long absence. Over time the delegate accretes the authority of the one it replaced, and the decision behind it hardens into fact: no one recalls the engineer who chose it, only the object that has done their bidding for years.
