# Achievement Motive: Reference

## Line of Work

Achievement motive as a **position** engine: a persona's resting dispositional motive in achievement situations -- the standing pull, prior to any particular goal, toward the satisfaction of success or away from the cost of failure -- taken across the two motives that pull can run on. The domain does not model the achievement goal a persona then adopts, mastery or performance, approached or avoided, which is the goal engine's; nor the promotion-or-prevention orientation of how a pursuit is regulated, which is the regulatory-focus engine's; nor the source of the drive itself, intrinsic to external, which is the motivation engine's. It models the upstream dispositional motive -- the hope or the dread a persona brings to a test of its competence, before it frames a goal at all.

The spine is Atkinson and McClelland's theory of achievement motivation and Elliot's hierarchical placement of it. McClelland identified the need for achievement as a measurable motive disposition, and Atkinson formalized the dynamics: achievement behaviour is governed by two separable dispositional motives -- the motive to approach success (the hope of success) and the motive to avoid failure (the fear of failure) -- whose difference yields the resultant tendency, so that a persona in whom hope predominates approaches achievement situations and prefers moderate, meaningful challenges, while one in whom fear predominates avoids them or retreats to the safely easy or the impossibly hard. Elliot and Church set these motives in a hierarchical model: they are upstream of the achievement goals a person adopts, energizing the goal and colouring which goal is chosen -- the need for achievement fostering approach goals, the fear of failure fostering avoidance goals. Conroy specified the fear of failure as multidimensional, an appraisal of failure as threatening several things at once -- shame and embarrassment, a devalued self-estimate, an uncertain future, a loss of social influence, and the upsetting of important others. The engine takes the dispositional motive as its anchor and the two motives as its expressions. The altitudes are those: the achievement motive, and its hope-of-success and fear-of-failure forms.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The achievement goal (goal)**: The engine models the dispositional motive, not the goal it energizes. Which achievement goal a persona adopts -- mastery or performance, in an approach or an avoidance form -- is owned by the goal engine; the achievement motive is upstream of it, the standing pull that colours which goal is chosen and lends it its energy (Elliot & Church's hierarchy). The motive is the disposition a persona brings to any test; the goal is the particular orientation it frames for a given pursuit. Same word-family, two altitudes: the resting motive here, the adopted goal there.
- **The regulatory orientation (regulatory-focus)**: The engine models the motive toward success or away from failure, not the promotion-or-prevention manner of pursuing a goal. How a persona regulates a pursuit -- eagerly reaching for gains under promotion, or vigilantly guarding against losses under prevention -- is owned by the regulatory-focus engine; achievement motive is the dispositional appetite or dread that precedes and feeds it. The two rhyme -- approach with promotion, avoidance with prevention -- but the achievement motive is specific to competence situations and to the hope-or-fear disposition, while regulatory focus is a general strategic orientation.
- **The source of the drive (motivation)**: The engine models the direction of the achievement motive, not where the drive springs from. Whether a persona acts from intrinsic interest, identified value, introjected pressure, external reward, or not at all is owned by the motivation engine's self-determination continuum; achievement motive is orthogonal to it, a pull toward success or away from failure that can be energized from any of those sources. Motivation asks why the persona is driven; achievement motive asks whether it hopes or dreads.
- **The felt emotions (anxiety, shame, pride)**: The engine models the standing motive, not the emotions that register its stakes. The anxiety that suffuses the fear of failure, the shame threatened by failing, and the pride promised by success are owned by the anxiety, shame, and pride engines; achievement motive sets what those emotions are about in a test of competence, but the feelings themselves are theirs. The motive is the disposition; the emotions are its charge.
