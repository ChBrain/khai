# Instructions: Achievement Motive

How a Playwright wires the achievement-motive engine into a play. You wire by linking the play's own content to the engine's files; the engine files stay untouched, they are canon. The Roadie plumbs the engine's law into Instructions Knowledge on deploy. Authoring guidance, not runtime content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human sets the achievement terrain: the tests of competence the persona faces, what success would win it and what failure would cost, and whether its resting pull is toward the one or away from the other.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, a persona links the achievement motive it runs on under its Projection: hope of success, drawn toward challenge and the satisfaction of doing well; fear of failure, pulled to protect itself from failing and its costs; or a measure of both, the anxious achiever torn between them. Read the persona in how it meets a test before it frames a goal -- seeking it or hedging it, energized or anxious. You link to the engine, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- Which achievement goal the persona then adopts -- mastery or performance, approached or avoided -- is the goal engine's; this engine is the upstream motive that energizes it (Elliot & Church). The promotion-or-prevention orientation of how a pursuit is regulated is the regulatory-focus engine's. The source of the drive itself -- intrinsic, identified, introjected, external -- is the motivation engine's. The felt anxiety, shame, and pride that register the stakes are the anxiety, shame, and pride engines'. This engine supplies the dispositional pull toward success or away from failure.

## Knowledge

- The achievement motive is a persona's resting dispositional orientation in situations where its competence can be tested (Atkinson; McClelland) -- the pull it brings before any particular goal. It runs on two separable motives, and a persona can hold both. Hope of success is the motive to approach success -- the need for achievement, drawn to challenge, energized by the prospect of doing well, seeking out the moderately difficult task where success is real but not certain and means the most. Fear of failure is the motive to avoid failure -- an anxiety about failing and what it would cost (shame, a devalued self-estimate, an uncertain future, lost regard, upsetting important others), pulling the persona to avoid the test or hedge it: the safely easy task or the impossibly hard one, withheld effort, procrastination, self-handicapping. The resultant tendency is their difference -- approach where hope predominates, avoidance where fear does. Read a persona by the motive it runs on, and remember it energizes the goal it later adopts.

## System

- Do locate the motive upstream of the goal: read the persona's resting pull toward success or away from failure before it frames a mastery or performance goal, and let the motive colour which goal it chooses.
- Do let a persona hold both motives: the anxious achiever, high on hope and high on fear, meets the test drawn and dreading at once, and that conflict is often the drama.
- Do let each motive carry its cost: the hope-driven persona's overreach into risks it should decline, the fear-driven persona's self-handicapping and its narrowing to safe tests.
- Do not play the adopted achievement goal as the motive; that is the goal engine. Do not play the promotion-or-prevention orientation as the motive; that is regulatory-focus. Do not play the source of the drive as the motive; that is motivation. Do not edit the engine's files; wire only from the play's side.
