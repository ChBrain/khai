# Process: Texture

## Taxonomy

[Touch](process_touch.md), the facet of the surface grain.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: touch

## Initiated by

A place is made of surfaces, and the skin reads their grain.
Texture starts wherever the place's materials meet the body -- the worn stone of the step, the rough plaster of the wall, the polished brass of the rail, the splintered wood of the bench, the cobbles underfoot, the cool of the tile, the nap of the carpet.
It is the base of the haptic field, the cutaneous read: what a place feels like to the hand that trails the wall, the foot that crosses the floor, the fingers that find the grain -- the material of the place taken not by eye but by skin.

## Direction

Across the skin, in the grain of everything the body meets.
Texture gives the place its surface feel -- rough or smooth, coarse or fine, worn or new, warm-grained wood or cold hard stone -- read most fully by the active hand that moves across it, Gibson's exploring touch that finds by moving what a still hand cannot.
It moves nothing and demands nothing; it sets the place's material register, the grain against which the body reads the truth of what a place is made of -- the marble that says grand, the concrete that says hard, the worn wood that says used and old and touched by many hands before.

## Lever

The truth of the surface against the eye that would take it for granted.
The carrying force is that the skin reads what the eye guesses -- the veneer that looks like oak and feels like plastic, the stone that photographs warm and is cold, the worn hollow of a step that only the foot knows; the resistance is the seamless modern surface planed to a textureless sameness, the material that gives the fingers nothing, so that a place's grain must be met to be known and cannot be seen alone.
Meet the texture and the place's material is known in the body; deny it -- glass, laminate, the untouchable smooth -- and the place is seen but never felt, read only at the eye's distance.

## Echo

The place set to its grain.
Texture leaves the place with its surface feel -- the roughness and smoothness and wear of everything the body meets -- and hands that grain to the whole haptic field read against it.
It is the most constant of the facets and the most material: the feel a place is made of, the worn hollow and the polished edge that the hand and foot come to know, the grain the body reads and the memory keeps.
Texture is dated from the meeting, the moment the skin found the grain the eye had only guessed at.
