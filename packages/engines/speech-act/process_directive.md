# Process: Directive

## Taxonomy

[Speech Act](process_speech_act.md), the act this is one form of.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: speech-act

## Initiated by

A persona tries, by saying so, to get another to do something: a request, an order, a question, an invitation, a piece of advice.
The utterance is aimed at the hearer's future conduct -- it seeks to make them act, answer, or comply.

## Direction

Toward the hearer, and toward a world made to match the words.
The direction of fit runs world-to-word: the utterance is not answerable to how things are but asks the world to change to fit it -- the door to be shut, the answer to be given.
The deed is the placing: to direct is to put a call on the hearer to act.

## Lever

The pressure the utterance places, scaled by the speaker's standing.
The same content -- "the window is open" -- can land as a mild hint or a flat order depending on who says it to whom; a directive's force is set less by its words than by the right the speaker has to make the claim on the hearer, and by how the request is softened or bared.
An order from one with no standing to give it misfires into mere noise; the standing itself is the power and role engines', drawn on here.

## Echo

An obligation, light or heavy, now sits on the hearer.
They comply, refuse, or negotiate; refusal of a bare order costs differently than declining a soft request, because the two placed different weights.
Where the directive is done indirectly -- an order dressed as a question, a request left as a hint -- the hearer must read the force past the form; and where the aim is not the single act but a lasting change in what the hearer wants or believes, that campaign is the persuasion engine's, not this single move.
