# Process: Declaration

## Taxonomy

[Speech Act](process_speech_act.md), the act this is one form of.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: speech-act

## Initiated by

A persona changes the world by saying it is so: "I now pronounce you married," "you're fired," "I resign," "court is adjourned," "I name this ship."
The utterance does not describe a change and does not request one -- it _is_ the change, brought about in the saying.

## Direction

Both ways at once: the words make the world match them, and match it because they say so.
Uniquely among the acts, a declaration has both directions of fit and neither -- it does not answer to a prior fact, nor merely ask for a future one; it institutes the fact by being uttered.
The deed is the instituting: to declare is to make it so by declaring it.

## Lever

The standing that empowers the words, without which they are only words.
A declaration comes off only when the speaker holds the office, the setting is right, and the form is met -- a marriage pronounced by one with the authority, in the ceremony that licenses it. Said by anyone else, or out of place, the very same sentence institutes nothing; it misfires.
The authority the act draws on is the power and role engines'; the ceremony that frames a rite of it is the ritual engine's. This act is the utterance that, backed by them, changes the status.

## Echo

A status is changed, and the change holds because the institution holds it.
The couple are married, the employee is out, the session is closed -- and everyone bound by the institution now treats the new fact as real, whatever anyone privately feels. The world has been re-described into a new shape by a sentence.
It is the most powerful of the acts and the most conditional: where the standing is real, a few words remake a life; where it is not, they fall to the ground, and the gap between saying and standing is the whole of it.
