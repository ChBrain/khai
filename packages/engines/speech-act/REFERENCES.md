# Speech Act: Reference

## Line of Work

Speech act as **process**: how a persona does something by saying it -- the illocutionary force that makes an utterance an act rather than a description, and the meaning it carries past what it literally states. The domain does not model the channel the utterance rides -- the tongue, the medium, the reach, and how far a language sits from the body -- which is the language engine's; nor the deliberate creation of a false belief, which is the deception engine's; nor the campaign to change what another believes over time, which is the persuasion engine's. It models the deed the words do: the force by which an utterance asserts, directs, commits, expresses, or declares, and the conditions under which the deed takes or misfires.

The spine is the ordinary-language finding that to speak is often to act, not to report: saying "I promise" makes a promise, "you're fired" ends a job. An utterance carries a locution (the words), an illocution (the act done in saying them), and a perlocution (the effect on the hearer); it comes off only when its felicity conditions hold; and it can mean past the said, the hearer recovering the implicature by presuming the speaker cooperates. The five acts are the points the illocutionary force can take -- assertive, directive, commissive, expressive, declaration.

## Origin

The philosophy of language and linguistic pragmatics, applied directly as process constraints.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **Language**: The engine models the deed an utterance does, not the channel it rides. The channels of using language -- speaking, hearing, reading, writing, thinking -- and how far a language sits from the body are owned by the language engine; this engine governs the illocutionary force carried on that channel, not the moving of the meaning across it.
- **Deception**: The engine models the assertive as the act of putting a claim on the record, whether sincere or not. The deliberate creation of a false belief in another without their consent is owned by the deception engine; this engine governs the assertion as an act, and leaves the lie behind it to deception.
- **Persuasion**: The engine models the directive as a single move on the hearer's conduct, not the campaign behind it. How a persona works to change what another believes, values, or does over time is owned by the persuasion engine; this engine governs the one utterance that asks, not the influence that surrounds it.
- **Power and Role**: The engine models the utterance that exercises authority, not the authority itself. The base from which a persona can move others, and the socially sanctioned mandate a role carries, are owned by the power and role engines; a directive's weight and a declaration's very success draw on them, and misfire without them.
- **Ritual**: The engine models the declaration as an utterance, not the ceremony that may frame it. How collective action crosses a threshold and transforms its participants is owned by the ritual engine; a declaration may sit inside a rite, but this engine governs the words that change the status, not the rite around them.
- **Emotion, Repair, Forgiveness**: The engine models the expressive as the saying of a feeling-move -- the apology, the thanks -- not the state beneath it. The feeling itself, the repair it serves, and the grievance it releases are owned by the emotion, repair, and forgiveness engines; this engine governs the act of putting the move on the table.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per file.

- **[speech act](process_speech_act.md)** (the root): An utterance acts rather than describes, carries a force that fixes which act it is, means past the literal, and comes off only when its felicity conditions hold. Anchored by Austin (the performative), Searle (illocutionary force), Grice and Sperber & Wilson (implicature), and Levinson (the synthesis).
- **[assertive](process_assertive.md)** (act): Commit to the truth of a claim -- state, report, describe; word-to-world fit; the speaker becomes answerable for it. Anchored by Searle (assertives) and Austin (the constative).
- **[directive](process_directive.md)** (act): Get the hearer to act -- request, order, ask; world-to-word fit; force scaled by the speaker's standing. Anchored by Searle (directives).
- **[commissive](process_commissive.md)** (act): Bind the speaker to a future act -- promise, offer, threaten; the speaker held to the fit, the hearer given the claim. Anchored by Searle (commissives) and Austin (promising as performative).
- **[expressive](process_expressive.md)** (act): Perform a feeling about a state of affairs -- thank, apologise, congratulate; the saying is the social move, apart from the sincerity behind it. Anchored by Searle (expressives).
- **[declaration](process_declaration.md)** (act): Change the world by saying it, given the standing -- pronounce, dismiss, resign; both directions of fit; comes off only on felicity. Anchored by Austin (explicit performatives) and Searle (declarations and institutional facts).
