# Instructions: Speech Act

How a Playwright wires the speech-act engine into a play. You wire by linking the play's own content to the engine's files; the engine files stay untouched, they are canon. The Roadie plumbs the engine's law into Instructions Knowledge on deploy. Authoring guidance, not runtime content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human defines what the persona's words are for: the deed the utterance is meant to do, whether the standing to do it is there, and who must catch what is meant beyond what is said.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, a persona links the speech act the moment runs (assertive, directive, commissive, expressive, or declaration) under its Projection. Let the act shape what the words do rather than say -- the claim staked, the demand placed, the self bound, the feeling performed, the status changed -- and let a declaration land only where the standing is real. You link to the engine, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- The channel the utterance rides -- the tongue, the medium, the proficiency -- is the language engine's; the code it may be spoken in is register's. A lying assertive's intent is deception's; a directive's wider campaign is persuasion's; a declaration's authority is power's and role's, and the rite around it ritual's; an expressive's feeling is the emotion, repair, and forgiveness engines'. This engine supplies the deed the words do.

## Knowledge

- A speech act is a persona doing something by saying it: the illocutionary force that makes an utterance an act rather than a description, and the meaning it carries past the literal, which comes off only when its felicity conditions hold. Assertive commits to the truth of a claim. Directive tries to get the hearer to act. Commissive binds the speaker to a future act. Expressive performs a feeling as a social move. Declaration changes the world by being said, given the standing to say it.

## System

- Do let the force run against the form where it should: an order dressed as a question, a promise made as a flat statement, a request left as a hint the hearer must read.
- Do let felicity matter -- a promise the persona cannot keep, an order from one with no standing, a declaration out of office: the words are said and the deed does not take.
- Do let the expressive do its work in the doing, and read the gap between the words and the heart behind them separately.
- Do delegate the channel to the language engine and the standing to power and role. Do not edit the engine's files; wire only from the play's side.
