# Surprise: Reference

## Line of Work

Surprise as **process**: the response to a violated expectation -- the mind's alarm that its model of the world has just been wrong, and the interrupt-and-reorient it fires to deal with the mismatch. The domain does not model the valenced feeling the surprise resolves into -- the delight if the news is good, the dismay if bad -- which is the emotion engine's; nor the attention it seizes, which is the attention engine's; nor the belief it revises, which is the belief engine's; nor the vastness-sized surprise that overflows into accommodation, which is the awe engine's. It models the violation-response itself: the registering of a broken expectation, the interrupt, the reorientation, and the model repaired or defended.

The spine is the cognitive-evolutionary account of surprise. Surprise is triggered not by what a persona cares about but by a discrepancy between what happened and what their schema predicted -- a prediction error -- and it functions to interrupt ongoing activity, reallocate attention to the unexpected event, analyze it, and update the schema so the model tracks the world better. It is pre-valence, brief, and metacognitive: an alarm on the model, not a judgment on the event. The four forms are the reflex, the appraised interrupt, its resolution, and its refusal: startle, jolt, sensemaking, dismissal.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **Emotion**: The engine models the pre-valence violation-response, not the feeling it resolves into. Surprise itself is prior to good-or-bad; once the unexpected event is appraised, it becomes a valenced emotion -- delight, dismay, relief, fear -- which is owned by the emotion engine. Surprise is the jolt; the colour it takes afterward is emotion's.
- **Attention**: The engine models the seizing of attention by an anomaly, not the spotlight itself. How a persona allocates the limited spotlight -- selective, divided, sustained, captured, orienting -- is owned by the attention engine; surprise is what wrenches that spotlight to the unexpected, and hands the mechanics of the capture to attention.
- **Belief**: The engine models the surprise-driven turn toward revision, not the holding-true that is revised. What a persona holds true and what it does when it meets disconfirming evidence -- update, rationalize, deny -- is owned by the belief engine; surprise is the interrupt that flags a schema as faulty, and belief owns the revision it drives (or the refusal of it).
- **Awe**: The engine models ordinary expectation-violation, not the vastness that overwhelms the model. The response to a vastness that exceeds the persona's frames and forces accommodation is owned by the awe engine; surprise is the general interrupt, and awe the special case where what surprises is too large to fit.
- **Curiosity**: The engine models the reactive jolt, not the standing pull toward the unknown. The resting inclination to explore is owned by the curiosity engine; a surprise may open into curiosity, but the jolt is the momentary interrupt and curiosity the enduring appetite it can awaken.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per file.

- **[surprise](process_surprise.md)** (the root): The response to a violated expectation -- the interrupt and reorientation fired by a prediction error, pre-valence and corrective. Anchored by Meyer, Reisenzein & Schützwohl (the process model) and Darwin (the expression).
- **[startle](process_startle.md)** (form): The reflex -- a sudden intense stimulus firing a defensive jump below appraisal, faster and lower than the emotions and hard to suppress. Anchored by Ekman, Friesen & Simons (startle distinguished from surprise).
- **[jolt](process_jolt.md)** (form): The appraised interrupt -- an expectation broken, ongoing action halted, attention pinned to the anomaly; needs no loud stimulus, only a violated model. Anchored by Meyer, Reisenzein & Schützwohl (interruption and attention shift).
- **[sensemaking](process_sensemaking.md)** (form): The resolution -- turning to the anomaly, finding what was wrong, and repairing the schema, so the model tracks the world better. Anchored by Meyer, Reisenzein & Schützwohl (analysis and schema-updating).
- **[dismissal](process_dismissal.md)** (form): The failure -- the anomaly explained away and the model preserved unrevised, so the persona is set up to be surprised the same way again. Anchored by Meyer, Reisenzein & Schützwohl (the failure to update).
