# Instructions: Embarrassment

How a Playwright wires the embarrassment engine into a play. You wire by linking the play's own content to the engine's files; the engine files stay untouched, they are canon. The Roadie plumbs the engine's law into Instructions Knowledge on deploy. Authoring guidance, not runtime content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human defines the embarrassment terrain: what exposes the persona -- a blunder, a breach of convention, a spotlight, or another's predicament witnessed -- and who the audience is (real, imagined, or remembered), so there is an unwanted exposure, watchers, and a standing to recover.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, a persona links the embarrassment the moment runs under its Projection: which form (faux pas, conspicuous, cringe, or blush), what exposure is appraised, and how embarrassable the persona's standing disposition is. Let it read as the appeasing fluster of a social predicament -- the blush, the averted gaze, the scramble to recover -- and keep it lighter and more social than shame, which devalues the whole self. You link to the engine, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- The deep moral devaluation of the self is the shame engine's; the public self-image the breach costs and repairs is the face engine's; the empathic capacity that carries the secondhand cringe is the empathy engine's; the performance change under an audience is the social-facilitation engine's; and the self-directed appraisal family of guilt, shame, and pride is the self-conscious composite's. This engine supplies the flustered response to unwanted exposure -- the predicament, the appeasing display, and the social repair.

## Knowledge

- Embarrassment is the flustered response to unwanted social exposure: a persona is caught in a blunder, a breach, or a spotlight before an audience, and flushes -- saying not I am flawed (shame) but I have been improperly seen. It is lighter, sharper, and more transient than shame, tied to social convention and to an audience, and it appeases rather than hides. It runs in forms. Faux pas is the blunder -- a breach of convention exposed, the gaffe or pratfall. Conspicuous is the spotlight -- the fluster of being watched, singled out, or praised, even with no blunder committed. Cringe is the vicarious form -- empathic embarrassment, the secondhand sting felt for another. Blush is the appeasement display -- the involuntary reddening, averted gaze, and nervous smile that concede the breach and, by being uncontrollable, are believed. Embarrassability is the trait -- a standing readiness to flush, keenly attuned to how one appears.

## System

- Do make embarrassment lighter and more social than shame: it is about the eyes on you and the convention broken, not the badness of the self -- so let the persona fluster and appease, not hide and devalue.
- Do let the appeasing display do its work: the blush, the sheepish smile, and the averted gaze are involuntary and therefore believed -- let them concede the breach and win the pardon, restoring the standing the blunder cost.
- Do keep the form legible: a faux pas is a blunder exposed, conspicuousness is exposure without wrongdoing, cringe is embarrassment for another, blush is the emotion made visible -- and let embarrassment need an audience, even an imagined or remembered one.
- Do let embarrassability set the threshold: the embarrassable persona flushes at the mildest exposure and monitors how they appear; the poised meet the same spotlight unbothered. Do delegate the deep self-devaluation to the shame engine and the social image itself to the face engine. Do not edit the engine's files; wire only from the play's side.
