# Process: Upgrading

## Taxonomy

[Gentrification](process_gentrification.md), the facet this force turns on.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: gentrification

## Initiated by

Capital, re-entered, spends on the fabric to realize the gap.
Upgrading starts with the renovation -- the loft converted, the brownstone restored, the tenement gut-rehabbed, the corner shop turned to a cafe -- the physical work that raises what the place can charge.
It is the visible face of the re-entry, the improvement everyone can see, and the reason gentrification can wear the look of simple betterment.

## Direction

Onto the fabric, raising it, and through it onto the price.
The improvement is real -- the wiring sound, the roof tight, the finish good -- and it converts directly into the withholding: every grade the fabric gains is a grade the rent climbs, and the place made nicer is the place made dearer.
Upgrading moves the place upmarket, one renovation setting the comp for the next, until the street's whole standard, and its whole price, has moved.

## Lever

The improvement of the fabric against the exclusion the improvement enacts.
The carrying force is the capital realizing its gap through the visible upgrade; the resistance is any claim that improvement should serve the sitting occupants rather than price them out -- the renovation that keeps them, not the one that clears them.
Here upgrading rides closest to restoration: the same act of repair can keep a place for its people or take it from them, and only what it serves tells the two apart.

## Echo

The place made better and dearer at once.
It shows the scaffolding, the skips, the new shopfronts, and carries beneath it the conversion the improvement performs -- betterment that is also exclusion, the polish that is the instrument of the price.
Upgrading is dated from the first renovation that set the new standard, and it is the facet that makes gentrification hardest to refuse, because the thing it does is, on its face, to make the place good.
