# Instructions: Gentrification

How a Playwright wires the gentrification engine into a play. You wire by linking
the play's own content to the engine's files; the engine files stay untouched,
they are canon. The Roadie plumbs the engine's law into Instructions Knowledge on
deploy. Authoring guidance, not runtime content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human sets which places in the world capital is re-entering, whose belonging the rising price withholds, and how far the wave has run.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, a gentrifying place links under Withheld the facet its re-entry turns on: the gap that draws the capital back (rent-gap), the fabric improved to realize it (upgrading), the prior occupants pushed out by price (eviction), or the place's own character sold back as authenticity (commodification). Play it as the place converting what it offered into what it now withholds -- the rent one could meet turned to a rent one cannot, the belonging released only to those who can pay -- and read gentrification as the family's ambivalence inverted: apparent revival, borne loss. Hand the decline that came before to dereliction, the conservation of the fabric to restoration, and the displaced person's grief to the persona's own engines. You link to the engine, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- The disinvestment and abandonment that dug the rent-gap are dereliction's; the conservation of the fabric for its age is restoration's; the grief and anger of the person displaced are the persona's own engines'. Upgrading is the shared seam with restoration -- the same renovation can keep a place for its people or take it from them, and only what it serves tells them apart. Whether the facet rings true for the place and its market is the team's review.

## Knowledge

- Gentrification is the capital re-entry a place undergoes when the rent-gap that disinvestment opened grows wide enough to profit -- capital's move, not a matter of taste (Glass named it; Smith gave it the rent-gap). It converts the place's Offers into Withheld: what it freely gave is released now only to those who can pay. Four facets carry the re-entry. Rent-gap is the mechanism -- the spread between depressed current rent and potential rent that pulls capital back when wide enough. Upgrading is the physical improvement that realizes the gap, betterment that is also exclusion, and the seam nearest restoration. Eviction is the whole of displacement -- formal, priced, and exclusionary -- the Withheld made human. Commodification is the neighborhood's character sold back as authenticity, the loss marketed as the value it destroyed (Zukin). Read a place by the facet its re-entry turns on.

## System

- Do give a gentrifying place its facet under Withheld; do play it as the place converting its offer into a withholding; do read the whole as apparent revival carrying a borne loss -- the family's ambivalence inverted from decay's and dereliction's.
- Do let upgrading be genuinely better and still exclusionary: the renovation is real, and that is what makes gentrification hard to refuse -- do not caricature it as mere greed, and do keep the seam to restoration honest (only what the repair serves separates them).
- Do not model the decline that dug the gap (that is dereliction), the conservation of the fabric (that is restoration), or the displaced person's feeling (that is the persona's own engines). Do not make the gentrified place mirror a persona's alienation (that is the play's pitch). Do not edit the engine's files; wire only from the play's side.
