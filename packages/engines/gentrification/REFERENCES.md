# Gentrification: Reference

## Line of Work

Gentrification as **process**: the capital re-entry a place undergoes when the
rent-gap that disinvestment opened grows wide enough to profit -- reinvestment
that upgrades the fabric and converts what the place freely offered into
something it now withholds behind a price, displacing its prior occupants. The
domain does not model the decline that precedes it, which is the dereliction
engine's; nor the conservation of the fabric for its age, which is the
restoration engine's; nor the setting itself, which is the space engine's; nor
the feeling of the person displaced, which is the persona's own engines'. It
models the place-force of return and displacement: the Offers of a place turned
to Withheld.

The spine is that gentrification is capital's move, not a matter of taste. Glass
named it -- the middle class re-entering working-class quarters and displacing
their occupants. Smith gave it its engine: the rent-gap, the spread between a
disinvested place's depressed current rent and its potential under reinvestment,
which pulls capital back to the place it fled once the spread is wide enough to
pay -- gentrification as a back-to-the-city movement by capital, not people. Zukin
gave it its culture and its cost -- the aesthetic upgrading of the loft, and the
commodification of authenticity that sells a neighborhood's character while
dispersing the community that made it. Marcuse tied the ends together:
displacement in full -- direct, exclusionary, and by pressure -- and the paired
cycle of abandonment and gentrification on a single housing market. So the engine
gives the re-entry four facets: the rent-gap that draws it, the upgrading that
realizes it, the eviction that clears it, and the commodification that sells the
place back to itself.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The decline that precedes it (dereliction)**: Gentrification is the up side of
  the rent-gap; the disinvestment and abandonment that dug the gap are the
  dereliction engine's. Dereliction hands the widened gap across, and gentrification
  closes it -- the down and the up of one curve (Marcuse's paired cycle). The
  re-entry is gentrification's; the withdrawal that made it profitable is
  dereliction's.
- **The conservation of the fabric (restoration)**: Both gentrification and
  restoration re-enter a fallen place and repair it; they part on what they serve.
  Restoration keeps the fabric for its age, conserving the place for what it was;
  gentrification withholds the place for its yield, converting its offer to a price.
  A single renovation can do either, and the upgrading facet is where the two ride
  closest -- only what the repair serves, the sitting occupant or the incoming
  capital, tells them apart.
- **The setting itself (space)**: Gentrification moves who a place holds and
  withholds, not its layout, scale, or affordances, which are the space engine's.
  Space builds the place; gentrification changes whom it is kept for.
- **The displaced person's feeling (the persona's engines)**: The grief of losing
  a home, the anger of the priced-out, the severed bond to a place -- these are the
  persona's own engines' (grief, place-attachment, and their kin). Gentrification
  delivers the force on the place, the withholding; the feeling in the person who
  bears it is theirs. And the gentrified street made to **stand for** a character's
  alienation is refused: that is authored key, the play's pitch, not the place's
  force.
- **The facet itself is not engine content**: The engine declares the four facets
  exist; which one a given place undergoes lives on the place, linked under its
  Withheld chapter. The engine refuses to fix a place's gentrification for it.
