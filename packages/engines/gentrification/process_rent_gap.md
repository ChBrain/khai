# Process: Rent-Gap

## Taxonomy

[Gentrification](process_gentrification.md), the facet this force turns on.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: gentrification

## Initiated by

The gap opens between what a place earns now, depressed, and what it could earn reinvested.
The rent-gap starts on the way down -- disinvestment lowers the place's actual ground-rent while its potential, under a "higher" use, holds or climbs -- so the longer the decline runs, the wider the gap between the two.
It is the coiled potential in a fallen place: the money waiting for whoever moves first to close it.

## Direction

Toward the widening distance, and the moment it becomes worth closing.
The gap is not a feeling about the neighborhood but a number about the ground: current capitalized rent below potential rent, and the spread between them the profit in re-entry.
When the spread is wide enough to cover the cost of redevelopment and clear a return, capital turns back toward the place it fled -- the gap directing where reinvestment lands, block by block, at the frontier of the profitable.

## Lever

The cost of re-entering against the return the closed gap would pay.
The carrying force is the spread the decline dug; the resistance is the cost of acquisition and upgrade, and any protection that would let the sitting occupants keep the difference rather than the investor.
While the gap is narrow, capital stays away and the place is left; once it is wide, re-entry pencils out, and the place tips from abandoned to sought almost overnight.

## Echo

The place newly worth taking, and the wave that comes to take it.
The rent-gap is the seam where dereliction hands to gentrification: disinvestment digs the gap, and the gap, grown wide, calls the capital back to close it.
It shows as a sudden reversal -- the block written off now bid on -- and carries beneath it the whole logic of the return: not that the place became lovable, but that its fall made its recovery pay.
