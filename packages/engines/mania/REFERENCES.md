# Mania: Reference

## Line of Work

Mania as **process**: the elevated, expansive or irritable activation episode, modelled as a system that _rises_ rather than a mood that arrives. The domain owns the ascent and its mechanism -- pursuit escalating on its own early wins, an appraisal from which the downside has dropped out, and a forfeited need for rest that removes the interruption -- and not the states it is mistaken for. The pleasure of a good outcome, the absorption of a matched task, and rest itself are owned elsewhere and delegated to by name.

The spine is three findings that fit together as a mechanism rather than a list. Depue and Iacono's behavioral facilitation system supplies the substrate: an incentive-driven approach system whose dysregulation raises engagement and changes what a decision estimates. S. Johnson's work locates the entry condition precisely -- what precedes an ascent is goal _attainment_, not stress or loss, so the episode is triggered by success and escalates on the evidence of it. Akiskal's hyperthymic temperament describes the ground the episode rises from, which is why the first stretch reads to everyone as the persona at their best rather than as illness beginning.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **Joy**: The engine models sustained, self-feeding activation, not the appraisal node that answers a good outcome. The pleasure of a thing having gone well is owned by the `joy` engine; this engine owns the rise that does not settle when the incentive is consumed.
- **Flow**: The engine models expansive activation, not absorption. Task-locked engagement under a challenge matched to skill -- narrow, effortless, and ending with the task -- is owned by the `flow` engine; this engine owns activation that widens rather than narrows and outlives its occasion.
- **Sleep**: The engine models the episodic absence of the need for rest, not rest itself. Sleep, its architecture, its stages and the debt of losing it are owned by the `sleep` engine; this engine owns only what a forfeited need removes from the ascent.
- **Grandiosity as a trait**: The engine models an episodic loss of the downside term in a reckoning, not a standing self-inflation. Trait grandiosity and its interpersonal machinery are owned by the `dark-triad` engine; this engine's expansion is episodic and returns.
- **Diagnosis**: The engine models a persona's activation for dramatic production. It is authored character architecture, never a clinical assessment, screening instrument, or diagnosis of a living person, and it does not carry criteria, thresholds, or duration rules for one.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per file.

- **[mania](process_mania.md)** (the root): The ascent as a system whose movements supply each other, rising from an elevated baseline and ending only when something external interrupts. Anchored by Depue & Iacono (the facilitation system) and Akiskal (the hyperthymic ground).
- **[goal activation](process_goal_activation.md)** (movement): Attainment read as information about capacity; the next target set from the success rather than the resources. Anchored by S. Johnson (goal-attainment events precede ascent).
- **[expansive appraisal](process_expansive_appraisal.md)** (movement): The downside dropping out of the estimate rather than being weighed and accepted. Anchored by Depue & Iacono (the facilitation system's effect on what a decision computes).
- **[rest forfeit](process_rest_forfeit.md)** (movement): A need for rest that is absent rather than overridden, removing the nightly interruption that would end the cycle. Anchored by the episode's own criterion literature and read here as the movement that holds the other two open.
