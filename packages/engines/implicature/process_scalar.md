# Process: Scalar

## Taxonomy

[Implicature](process_implicature.md), the default-inference form.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: implicature

## Initiated by

The speaker uses a weaker term on a scale -- "some," "warm," "or," "tried" -- and the hearer takes them to mean not the stronger one they did not use: some but not all, warm but not hot, one or the other but not both.
This is scalar implicature: the generalized, default inference that, in choosing a weaker term, the speaker implies the stronger one does not hold.
It rides on quantity and needs no special occasion -- the inference comes with the word, unless something cancels it.

## Direction

Toward the negation of the stronger alternative, drawn by default from the weaker term chosen.
Scalar implicature works from quantity: had the stronger term held, a cooperative speaker as informative as required would have used it; they did not, so the hearer infers it does not hold -- "some" carrying "not all," "possible" carrying "not certain," by the mere choice of the weaker point on the scale.
The direction is generalized and defeasible: the inference attaches to the term across contexts, arising without special setup, yet cancels cleanly when the speaker adds "in fact, all" or the context makes the stronger reading live -- the default overridden without contradiction.

## Lever

The choice of a weaker scalar term over an available stronger one, spent as the default inference that the stronger fails.
Scalar implicature runs on informativeness measured against the scale: the terms sit ranked by strength, and using a weaker one, when the maxim of quantity asks for as much information as needed, signals that the speaker was not in a position to assert the stronger -- so the hearer reads the stronger as excluded.
The lever is the quantity default: because the inference is keyed to the word rather than the moment, it is generalized -- present by default wherever the term appears -- and cancelable, yielding the moment the speaker or context supplies the stronger reading.

## Echo

The weaker word is heard to deny the stronger, unless the speaker says otherwise.
It is why "some passed" implies some failed, why "you can have cake or fruit" offers a choice of one, why "it's warm" concedes it is not hot -- the everyday, automatic upgrades of meaning that ride on the words and lift the moment context pushes back.
It is [maxims](process_maxims.md) and [flouting](process_flouting.md)'s counterpart, the default read off the words where maxims are the baseline and flouting the deliberate breach. This owns the generalized, cancelable inference by which a weaker term implies the stronger does not hold.
