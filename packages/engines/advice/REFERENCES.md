# Advice: Reference

## Line of Work

Advice as **process**: a second judgement placed next to a first, and what the first one does with it.

The engine's subject is a transaction that almost everybody rates highly and almost nobody performs the
way they think they do. Harvey and Fischer's account is the spine, and it names the thing the
measurement alone would miss: taking advice does several jobs at once, and only one of them is
improving the judgement. It also shares the responsibility for the outcome, and that second function is
delivered in full by the act of consulting -- independent of whether a word of the advice is used.
Everything the engine reads follows from holding those two apart.

The boundary with what khai already carries is clean. The request for discretionary help put to somebody
free to refuse is **asking**'s; this engine reads the request for a judgement rather than for an act.
The attempt to move a mind that was not offered for moving is **persuasion**'s. Whether the adviser
actually knows anything, and what practised judgement is made of, is **expertise**'s. The disagreement
between a standardised instrument and a practitioner is the **overriding** composite's. The credit given
to an account is **credibility**'s, the sense of having been properly heard is the **heeding**
composite's and **recognition**'s, and the threat a remark poses to somebody's standing is **face**'s.

**The approach** settles what the advice will mean before anybody has evaluated it. **The invitation**
buys candour with standing: it converts an unsolicited judgement into a requested one, which removes
almost all of its threat, and people go to considerable trouble in conversation to be asked rather than
told -- at the cost of having said in front of a witness that they do not know what to do. **The
prepared answer** sets the advice to arrive after the decision, since most advice is sought late, with
the options narrowed and one quietly preferred. **The unbidden** pays for its accuracy with its
reception: uninvited advice is heard as a claim about the relationship, and being right about it usually
makes that worse.

**The weighing** moves less than it feels like and by less than it should. **The own view kept** is the
engine's firmest measured fact: given somebody else's estimate, people move about three parts in ten
toward it, plain averaging would beat what nearly everybody does, and the ratio holds even when the
adviser is better informed. **The confident voice** is followed for a signal it cannot support, since
between two advisers the surer one is taken more and confidence tracks accuracy weakly. **The agreeing
word** is taken whole because it costs nothing to take, which runs the uptake curve backwards to the
usefulness curve.

**The return** leaves the adviser better informed about the friendship and worse about the advice.
**The report back** is the only feedback in the system and is not feedback, so advisers accumulate
decades of confirmations and never learn whether they were right. **The unfollowed thanks** are true
about the person and false about the advice, and there is no version of the sentence that separates
them. **The adviser invested** loses the outsideness that made them worth asking.

Hence the **twist**, at the root. **A persona takes almost none of the advice and keeps all of the
adviser.** The content is discounted, sometimes correctly and usually more than it should be, and none
of that touches the other thing the episode produced: a second person now attached to the decision. That
attachment survives the discounting, it is what makes the consultation feel worth having, and it is
produced at full strength whether or not a single word was used.

The engine is built so that nobody has to be manipulating anybody for it to run, and a play should keep
it that way. The adviser was competent. The asker was sincere. The advice was sound.

**On the evidence, stated plainly.** This engine has an unusually firm centre and unusually soft edges,
and the shape of that matters more than any single citation. **Egocentric discounting is among the
better replicated effects in judgement research.** The rough seven-to-three ratio, its persistence
against better-informed advisers, and the fact that equal-weight averaging outperforms it are robust,
and the weighing movement is the engine's solid ground. **The paradigm it comes from is nothing like the
scenes this engine describes.** Judge-Advisor System studies use numeric estimation -- the year of an
event, the calories in a meal -- with a stranger adviser, no relationship, no stake, and a single round;
this engine applies the finding to a friend advising on a marriage or a job, and that transfer is a
deliberate stretch rather than an established one. It is the single largest liberty taken here. **The
twist's soft joint is the citation, not the sharing.** That responsibility-sharing is among the motives
for seeking advice is demonstrated; that the consultation is produced as evidence after bad outcomes and
withheld after good ones is the engine's own extension, consistent with how people account for
decisions and not measured. **The face-threat material is conversation-analytic.** How advice is placed
in talk, and the work people do to be asked, is closely described rather than experimentally
established, and the description comes from a small number of settings. **One well-known finding was
deliberately left out.** The result that advice is used more when it has been paid for would fit the
weighing movement neatly; its best-known demonstration belongs to a body of work now under serious
investigation for data integrity, so the engine does not build on it and no member depends on it -- a
reader who wants that mechanism should treat it as unverified rather than as part of this account. What
should not be taken from this engine is that advisers are useless, that seeking advice is a manoeuvre,
or that discounting is stupidity: the persona often does hold information the adviser lacks, some of
the discount is correct, and a decision two people looked at is usually better even after the shaving.
The claim is narrow: the content moves little, the attachment is total, and only one of the two is
reported.

## Origin

The judge-advisor literature and the conversational study of how advice is offered, wired as one process
on a persona. The twist is Harvey and Fischer's; the measurement of what survives is Yaniv's.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The request for help (asking, gift, debt)**: A request for discretionary help put to somebody free
  to refuse, the transfer that binds by naming no price, and the obligation it creates are those
  packages'. This engine reads the request for a judgement, where nothing is transferred but an
  opinion.
- **Moving a mind that was not offered (persuasion, reactance, influence)**: The attempt to change
  somebody's position, and what pushing produces, are those engines'. Advice is offered into a decision
  the other person owns, and its failure mode is being discounted rather than being resisted.
- **Whether the adviser knows anything (expertise, overriding, measure)**: What practised judgement is
  made of, when a rule beats a practitioner, and what a number does to a judgement are those packages'.
  This engine is indifferent to whether the advice was good and reads what happened to it.
- **The threat of the remark (face, embarrassment, status-move)**: What a face costs to maintain, what a
  slight does, and how position is asserted in a move are those engines'. The unbidden member reads only
  that uninvited advice enters as a claim and that the claim is answered before the content.
- **Being properly heard (heeding, recognition, credibility)**: What a fair hearing buys, what
  institutional acknowledgement does, and the credit given to an account are those packages'. The return
  movement reads the report, not the hearing.
- **Praise and its reception (praise, gratitude, admiration)**: What appreciation does to the person
  receiving it is those packages'. The unfollowed-thanks member reads one specific ambiguity, which is
  that the same sentence thanks a person and endorses a judgement.
- **The professional relation (the world)**: Fee structures, duty of care, regulated advice,
  professional liability, and the statutes that govern who may advise on what are matters of trade and
  law. They set the format; this engine reads what happens to a second judgement in any setting.
- **Counsel as authored symbol (the play's pitch)**: An adviser staged in order to **stand for** a theme
  -- the mentor who mirrors a motif of wisdom -- is refused: that is authored key, the play's pitch.
  Advice sets what was said and what was kept; the meaning is the play's.

## Encoding

Source to constraint. A process tree: one root, four movements, twelve forms.

- **[advice](process_advice.md)** (the root): a second judgement placed next to a first, read on what was
  kept, carrying the twist -- a persona takes almost none of the advice and keeps all of the adviser.
  Anchored by Harvey & Fischer (1997).
- **[the approach](process_approach_advice.md)**: how the advice gets into the room. Anchored by
  Goldsmith (2000). [the invitation](process_the_invitation.md),
  [the prepared answer](process_the_prepared_answer.md), [the unbidden](process_the_unbidden.md).
- **[the weighing](process_weighing_advice.md)**: what happens to its content. Anchored by Yaniv (2004)
  and Sniezek & Van Swol (2001). [the own view kept](process_the_own_view_kept.md),
  [the confident voice](process_the_confident_voice.md), [the agreeing word](process_the_agreeing_word.md).
- **[the return](process_return_advice.md)**: what goes back to the adviser. Anchored by Bonaccio &
  Dalal (2006) on what the paradigm does and does not observe.
  [the report back](process_the_report_back.md), [the unfollowed thanks](process_the_unfollowed_thanks.md),
  [the adviser invested](process_the_advisor_invested.md).
- **[the keeping](process_keeping_advice.md)**: what the advice turns out to have been for, and where
  the engine is seen for one thing. Anchored by Harvey & Fischer (1997).
  [the second name](process_the_second_name.md), [the citation](process_the_citation.md),
  [the next adviser](process_the_next_advisor.md).
