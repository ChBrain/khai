# Process: The Invitation

## Taxonomy

[The Approach](process_approach_advice.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: advice

## Initiated by

The asker opens the door.
"What would you do?" -- which is the sentence that makes the next one permissible.

## Direction

Toward advice licensed by the person it is about to be aimed at.
The invitation does a specific piece of work: it converts an unsolicited judgement into a requested one, which removes almost all of its threat. Studies of how advice is placed in conversation find people going to considerable trouble to be asked -- describing a problem, pausing, leaving a gap -- rather than receiving the same words unprompted.
Advisers know this too, which is why so much advice begins by manufacturing the invitation it needs.

## Lever

The protection the invitation gives against what the asker has conceded to get it.
The carrying force is that asking is the sensible move and costs little: it makes the other person free to speak plainly, it produces better advice because the adviser is not tiptoeing, and it is the ordinary courtesy that lets two people talk about a hard thing at all; the resistance is that the invitation is also an admission, since the asker has now said in front of a witness that they do not know what to do -- and the adviser will remember that they were asked long after both have forgotten what was said.
The Invitation buys candour with standing.

## Echo

Permission granted, and a small debt opened.
It shows why the same words are welcome from one mouth and intolerable from another.
The Invitation is dated to the question and is remembered by the adviser.
