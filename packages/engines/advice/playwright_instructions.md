# Instructions: Advice

How a Playwright wires the advice engine into a play. You wire by linking the play's own content to the engine's files; the engine files stay untouched, they are canon. The Roadie plumbs the engine's law into Instructions Knowledge on deploy. Authoring guidance, not runtime content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human keeps the advice sound and the adviser competent, because the engine needs no fool and stops working the moment it gets one, and keeps the asker sincere, because it needs no manipulator either. Decide before the scene whether the asker already knows what they are going to do, since that sets everything the adviser says into a different frame. And decide what gets reported back afterwards, because that is the only feedback anybody in this engine ever receives.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, a persona links under Projection where they stand to it: who they ask, who asks them, what they did with the last thing they were told, whose name is now on the decision, and who they would not consult again. Read the engine on what was kept, never on what was said. Keep it the second judgement: the request for discretionary help is asking's, the attempt to move a mind that was not offered is persuasion's, and whether the adviser actually knows anything is expertise's. You link to the engine, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- The request for discretionary help, the transfer that names no price, and the obligation it creates are asking's, gift's, and debt's; the attempt to move a mind that was not offered is persuasion's and reactance's; whether the adviser knows anything and when a rule beats a practitioner are expertise's and the overriding composite's; what a face costs and how position is asserted are face's, embarrassment's, and status-move's; what a fair hearing buys and the credit given to an account are the heeding composite's, recognition's, and credibility's; what appreciation does to the person receiving it is the praise composite's and gratitude's. Fee structures, duty of care, regulated advice, and professional liability are the trade and set the format. Whether a production's asker wanted a view or a signature, and whether an adviser was consulted or recruited, are the team's review.

## Knowledge

- Advice is a second judgement placed next to a first, and what the first one does with it. It is not asking's request for discretionary help, not persuasion's attempt on a mind that was not offered, and not expertise's question of whether the adviser knows anything. It wires on a persona at Projection and runs in four movements. The approach settles what the advice will mean before anybody evaluates it: an invitation converts an unsolicited judgement into a requested one and removes almost all of its threat, at the cost of having said in front of a witness that the asker does not know what to do; most advice is sought late, with the options already narrowed and one quietly preferred; and uninvited advice is heard as a claim about the relationship, so being right about it usually makes it land worse. The weighing moves less than it feels like and by less than it should: given somebody else's estimate a persona keeps about seven parts of their own for every three of the adviser's, holds that ratio even when the adviser is better informed, and reports having taken the advice; between two advisers the more confident is followed, though confidence tracks accuracy weakly; and advice that agrees with the persona's existing view is adopted almost whole, which runs the uptake curve backwards to the usefulness curve. The return leaves the adviser better informed about the friendship and worse about the advice, since the report back is edited for its listener and is the only feedback in the system, the thanks are true about the person and false about the advice, and an adviser who has given a judgement is no longer outside the decision. The engine's twist is at the root: a persona takes almost none of the advice and keeps all of the adviser -- the content is discounted and the attachment is produced in full, whether or not a word was used.

## System

- Do keep the adviser competent and the advice sound. The engine needs no fool and no charlatan, and staging one replaces the mechanism with a fable.
- Do decide in advance whether the asker has already chosen. If they have, write the question as a choice between the favourite and something obviously worse, and let nobody in the scene notice.
- Do let the invitation be worked for. A problem described, a pause, a gap left -- the asker manufacturing the permission the adviser needs, or the adviser manufacturing it for them.
- Do make the unbidden advice correct. It should be a good point, and it should still cost the speaker the hearing, because that is the member.
- Do let the revision be small and be described as large. The persona shaves their position, says the conversation completely changed how they were thinking about it, and means both.
- Do reward the confident adviser. The one who hedges is being more informative and is taken less seriously, in the same scene if possible.
- Do write the report back edited. Warm, true, selective, and received by the adviser as evidence about their own judgement.
- Do close on the second name rather than the decision. The outcome is now jointly owned by somebody who supplied none of the reasoning and does not know they own it.
- Do not model the request for help (asking, gift, debt), the attempt to move a mind (persuasion, reactance), whether the adviser knows anything (expertise, overriding), the threat of the remark (face, status-move), being properly heard (heeding, recognition, credibility), or the reception of praise. Do not make the adviser stand for a theme; that is authored key, the play's pitch. Do not edit the engine's files; wire only from the play's side.
