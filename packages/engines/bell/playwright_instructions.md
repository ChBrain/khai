# Instructions: Bell

How a Playwright wires the bell engine into a play. You wire by linking the play's own content to the engine's files; the engine files stay untouched, they are canon. The Roadie plumbs the engine's law into Instructions Knowledge on deploy. Authoring guidance, not runtime content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human lets the service be real before it is a claim: the alarm finds everybody, the hour is free, and the people who paid for the bell were buying exactly that. Keep the ringer ordinary, neither pious nor tyrannical, because the engine needs no tyrant and stops working the moment it gets one. And decide where the earshot ends, since the households on the edge of it are where every interesting scene in this engine happens.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, a piece links under Apparent which movement the object is in, and a place links under Shown whether it is inside the earshot, on the overlap between two ranges, or out of range. Read the engine on what the bell constitutes, never on how it sounds. Keep it the object: the auditory field with its keynote and soundmarks is soundscape's, the institutional record is document's, and the shared clock is social-time's. You link to the engine, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- The keynote ground, the meaningful signal, the soundmark, and the silence of a place are soundscape's, the sensorium composite's, and smellscape's; how a society keeps time and the difference between clock time and the right moment are social-time's, chronos's, and kairos's; the certificate and the state's readable index are document's, register's, and legibility's; the garment that replaces a person with an office and what an insignia collects are uniform's, obedience's, and the yielding composite's; the funeral, the wedding, and the mourning the bell announces are ritual's, grave's, and grief's. Who owns the tower, how a parish is governed, and the law on ringing and noise are the trade and set the format. Whether a production's ringing was a service or an assertion, and whether a household on the overlap belongs to one parish or two, are the team's review.

## Knowledge

- A bell is the instrument that reaches everybody at once: the most expensive object a small community owns, the least used by any individual in it, and the only one that reaches every person in range at the same instant. It is not soundscape's auditory field, not document's institutional record, and not uniform's garment. It wires on a piece at Apparent and a place at Shown. What it holds up is a shared time and a shared alarm -- before anybody owned a clock the bell was the clock, and it remained the clock for whoever could not afford one long after the others could. One position and three processes hang beneath it. The ringer holds the rope and an authorisation somebody granted, can compel everybody within two miles to receive a message and none of them to do anything specific, cannot ring privately, and cannot be wrong quietly, since a bell rung at the incorrect hour is a public error several hundred people notice at once. The casting fixes a voice in an afternoon and issues it for four hundred years, because pitch is settled when the metal cools and a wrong bell is shaved a little or paid for twice. The ringing is a broadcast with no way to switch it off and no channel back: everybody in range interprets a code learned in childhood and never taught, and nobody can query the ring or indicate receipt. The earshot is a real community with an arbitrary edge, drawn by topography, wind, leaf cover, and the height of a tower, coinciding with no administrative line. The engine's twist is in the anchor: nobody can decline a bell, which is why it is a service and why it is a claim -- the reach that makes it useful is the reach that makes it unrefusable, which is why bells were fought over so bitterly and so often.

## System

- Do let the alarm work first. A fire, and every person in the parish knows within seconds, with no equipment and no literacy required.
- Do give the code to somebody who cannot explain it. They know what the pattern means, they have always known, and nobody taught them.
- Do put the bell in the wrong hands only by accident. Nobody in this engine is seizing the tower; the ringer inherited a permission.
- Do let somebody receive it who did not want to. Asleep after a night shift, mourning, or long since finished with the church, and hearing it exactly like everybody else.
- Do stage the edge. A household on the overlap, hearing two parishes, able to tell the voices apart and belonging to neither.
- Do make the object older than everybody. Named, dated, inscribed, cast by somebody nobody can name, and outliving the family that paid for it.
- Do close on an objection rather than a peal. Somebody now has standing to complain about the noise, and their complaint is the first time the unrefusability has ever been named out loud.
- Do not model the auditory field (soundscape, sensorium), the shared clock (social-time, chronos), the institutional record (document, register), the office and its garment (uniform, obedience, yielding), or the rite it announces (ritual, grave, grief). Do not make the peal stand for a theme; that is authored key, the play's pitch. Do not edit the engine's files; wire only from the play's side.
