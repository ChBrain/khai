# Piece: Bell

## Taxonomy

The instrument that reaches everybody at once (no khai file of its own yet).

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: bell

## Place

Above the settlement, in the one structure built tall enough for sound rather than for use. A tower, a belfry, a frame over a school yard, a gantry at a works gate.
It is the most expensive object a small community owns and the least used by any individual in it: nobody touches it, nobody moves it, and everybody is within its reach every hour of their lives.
Its place is the top of whatever the community built highest, which in most settlements is not a coincidence.

## Load Bearing

What it holds up is a shared time and a shared alarm. Before anybody owned a clock the bell was the clock, and it remained the clock for the people who could not afford one long after the others could; the hours it strikes are the hours everybody in range is keeping, and there is no second opinion available.
It also holds up the community's account of itself: it is rung for the dead, for the married, for the invasion, for the fire, and the pattern of ringing is a running commentary on who is being born and buried that nobody has to subscribe to.
Remove it and the settlement keeps its own time in fragments, which is what happened, and which nobody experienced as an emancipation at the time.

## Apparent

Read as an ornament and made as an instrument. What shows is a heavy decorated object, inscribed with a name and a date and often a sentence in Latin, which visitors photograph and residents have not looked at in years.
What is actually apparent is not visual at all. The bell is known by its voice: a pitch and a decay that anybody raised in range can identify against a neighbouring parish's without effort and without ever having been taught to.
So the object presents itself as heritage and operates as a channel.

## Yearbook

The arc runs from the casting to the centuries to the silencing. It outlives every person who ever rang it, is recast rather than replaced when it cracks, and ends not by wearing out but by becoming a complaint -- a noise abatement notice, a neighbour's petition, an insurance premium on the tower.
The engine's twist is here. **Nobody can decline a bell, which is why it is a service and why it is a claim.** The same fact does both jobs. Its reach is what makes it useful -- everybody gets the hour, the alarm, the death, at no cost and with no equipment -- and its reach is also why it cannot be opted out of, so whoever holds the rope has an unrefusable channel into every room in the parish. This is why bells were fought over so bitterly and so often: not because anybody doubted their value, but because the value and the power are the identical property.
It is not the auditory field of a place with its keynote and its soundmarks, which is soundscape's; not the record an institution keeps of a person, which is document's; not the garment that replaces a person with an office, which is uniform's; and a peal staged to **stand for** a theme is authored key, the play's pitch.
It fails by being out of earshot, by being rung wrong, and by becoming a sound somebody has standing to object to.
