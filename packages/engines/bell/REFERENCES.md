# Bell: Reference

## Line of Work

Bell as **piece**: the instrument that reaches everybody at once, and what its reach constitutes.

The engine's subject is an object whose value and whose power are the same property. Corbin's history is
the spine, and its archival core is the part this engine is built on: through the nineteenth century in
rural France, communities fought over bells with a persistence that looks disproportionate until the
instrument is understood. Who might ring, at what hours, on whose authority, whether the tower was the
commune's or the church's, whether a neighbouring parish's peal was an encroachment -- these were not
quarrels about noise. A bell was the only channel that reached every person in a settlement
simultaneously, at no cost to the hearer and with no way to decline, and control of it was control of
the day.

The boundary with what khai already carries is clean. The auditory field of a place -- its keynote
ground, its meaningful signals, its unique soundmark, its silence -- is **soundscape**'s, and a bell is
a soundmark in that engine's terms; this engine reads the object rather than the field, which is to say
who cast it, who may ring it, and what its range makes. The record an institution keeps of a person is
**document**'s. The garment that replaces a person with an office is **uniform**'s, which shares this
engine's structure of a narrow permission carrying a wide authority. The shared time a society keeps is
**social-time**'s and **chronos**'s.

**The casting** fixes a voice in an afternoon and issues it for four hundred years. Pitch is settled
when the metal cools; a bell that comes out wrong is shaved a little or paid for twice, which is why the
pour was a public event and why the object is inscribed with a name and a date like a person.

**The ringing** is a broadcast with no way to switch it off. Everybody in range interprets a code
learned in childhood and never formally taught -- the hour, the fire, the death, the wedding -- and
there is no channel back: no way to query the ring, ask it to repeat, or indicate receipt.

**The earshot** is a real community with an arbitrary edge. The people in the parish, in the sense that
matters here, are the people who can hear it, which is a line drawn by topography, prevailing wind, leaf
cover, and the height of a tower, and which coincides with no administrative boundary. Households on the
overlap between two ranges are in both and in neither, and can tell the voices apart.

Hence the **twist**, in the anchor's Yearbook. **Nobody can decline a bell, which is why it is a service
and why it is a claim.** The same fact does both jobs. Its reach is what makes it useful -- everybody
gets the hour, the alarm, the death, with no equipment and no subscription -- and its reach is why it
cannot be opted out of, so whoever holds the rope has an unrefusable channel into every room in the
parish.

The engine is built so that nobody has to be imposing on anybody for it to run, and a play should keep
it that way. The alarm found everybody. The hour was free. The ringer was neither pious nor tyrannical.

**On the evidence, stated plainly.** This is a history engine with an unusually good archival base and a
correspondingly narrow one, and the shape of that matters. **Corbin's account is documented and local.**
The disputes over ringing rights, the negotiations between communes and clergy, and the sensory world
they belonged to are established from a large body of nineteenth-century French records, which is real
historical evidence and is not a claim about bells in general. **The engine generalises from one century
and one country, and it should be read as doing so.** Bells in a Japanese temple, a Russian belfry, a
New England meeting house, and a South Indian shrine sit in different arrangements of authority, and the
tight fit between the object and the community that Corbin describes is a feature of a particular
settlement pattern rather than of bells. **The regime the engine describes has substantially ended.**
Almost everybody now carries their own clock, alarms arrive by phone, and in much of the world the bell
survives as heritage, as a decorative practice, or as the subject of a noise complaint -- which is the
one place its unrefusability now shows up. A reader should treat the engine as a reading of a
historical arrangement that still runs in some places rather than as a description of the present.
**The acoustic-community idea is conceptual.** That the people in range constitute a unit is Schafer's
framing, it is illuminating, and nobody has measured whether the boundary does the social work the
engine attributes to it. **The unrefusable-channel reading is the engine's own extension.** Corbin
documents the fights; the account here of why the fights were structurally inevitable -- because the
service and the power are one property -- is an inference this engine draws, and a fair one, and not
something he says in those terms. What should not be taken from this engine is that bells are
instruments of domination, that the people who rang them were exercising power deliberately, or that a
community would have been better off without one. The claim is narrow: the voice is fixed once, the
message cannot be refused, and the boundary is acoustic.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The auditory field (soundscape, sensorium, smellscape)**: The keynote ground, the meaningful signal,
  the soundmark, and the silence of a place are the soundscape engine's; a bell is a soundmark in its
  terms. This engine reads the object, its permission, and its range.
- **The shared clock (social-time, chronos, kairos)**: How a society keeps time, what a schedule does,
  and the difference between clock time and the right moment are those engines'. The bell is read as
  the instrument that distributed a time, not as the time itself.
- **The institutional record (document, register, legibility)**: The certificate, the entry, and the
  state's readable index are those engines'. A bell announces a death and records nothing.
- **The office and its garment (uniform, obedience, yielding)**: The clothing that replaces a person
  with an office, compliance with an instruction, and what an insignia collects are those packages'.
  The ringer's authority is over an audience rather than over any individual in it.
- **The rite it accompanies (ritual, meal, grave, grief)**: The funeral, the wedding, the observance,
  and the mourning the bell announces are those packages'. This engine stops at the announcement.
- **The building and the institution (the world, org, hierarchy)**: Who owns the tower, how a parish is
  governed, and the law on bellringing and noise are matters of practice and statute. They set the
  format; this engine reads what the object does to the people in range.
- **A peal as authored symbol (the play's pitch)**: A ringing staged in order to **stand for** a theme
  -- the bell that mirrors a motif of fate -- is refused: that is authored key, the play's pitch. Bell
  sets what was sounded and who received it; the meaning is the play's.

## Encoding

Source to constraint. A piece engine: one anchor, one position, three processes, all flat beneath the
object.

- **[bell](piece_bell.md)** (the anchor): the instrument that reaches everybody at once, read on what it
  constitutes, carrying the twist -- nobody can decline a bell, which is why it is a service and why it
  is a claim. Anchored by Corbin (1994).
- **[the ringer](position_the_ringer.md)** (the position): the holder of the rope and of a narrow
  permission carrying an enormous instrument, who cannot ring privately or be wrong quietly. Anchored by
  Corbin (1994).
- **[the casting](process_the_casting.md)**: how the object is made and what that fixes, in an afternoon
  and for four centuries. Anchored by Price (1983).
- **[the ringing](process_the_ringing.md)**: what a stroke does to everybody in range, with no channel
  back. Anchored by Corbin (1994).
- **[the earshot](process_the_earshot.md)**: the territory the sound makes, real and arbitrarily edged.
  Anchored by Corbin (1994), with Schafer's acoustic community behind it.
