# Position: The Ringer

## Taxonomy

[Bell](piece_bell.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: bell

## Has

The rope, and a right to it that somebody granted.
What they hold is not the bell -- which belongs to a parish, a council, a company, or a trust -- but the authorisation to sound it, which is a narrow permission and an enormous instrument.
They also hold the code: which pattern means fire, which means a death, how many strokes for a man and how many for a woman, and how the pattern differs on the day of the year that everybody local knows and nobody has written down.

## Orders

Everybody in range, at the moment of ringing, without addressing any of them.
The powers are odd. The ringer cannot compel anybody to do anything specific, and can compel every person within two miles to receive a message, interpret it, and adjust -- stop work, count strokes, look up, come.
Historically the ringing was also constrained in the other direction: the ringer was required to ring, at fixed hours, in weather and in grief, which is an obligation attached to a permission.

## Loses

Any ability to ring privately, and any control over what the ring means once it is out.
There is no small version of this act. A single stroke is heard by everybody who is not deaf, including people the ringer would not have chosen to reach, and it is interpreted through a code the ringer did not write.
They also lose the possibility of being wrong quietly: a bell rung at the incorrect hour is a public error that several hundred people notice simultaneously.

## Drives

To be the one who is trusted with it, which is a standing rather than a job.
The motive is usually neither piety nor power. It is the ordinary satisfaction of an accurate practice done in front of everybody -- a skill exercised on a schedule, in a place where the exercise is unmistakable.
The Ringer holds the smallest possible authority over the largest possible audience.
