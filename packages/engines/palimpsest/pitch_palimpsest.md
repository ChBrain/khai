# Pitch: Palimpsest

## Taxonomy

Narratology / hypertextual transformation -- Genette's literature in the second degree.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: palimpsest

## Tenor

The doubled, second-degree key: the run played so that a prior telling shows through it. The events are fixed and the tone is new, but the source is always sensed beneath -- the old writing legible under the scraped-and-reused page, the new tale heard against the one it is written over. A palimpsest tenor is never single; it is the present telling and the remembered one, held at once, and the key is the relation between them.

## Undertow

The pull between homage and subversion. To re-pitch a tale by its debt to another is to honour and to alter it in the same stroke, and the tone stays alive only while both hold: too much reverence and it is mere copy, too much mockery and the source is lost. The counter-current is the source's own dignity resisting the new key -- the pattern pushing back against the hand that re-tunes it -- which is what keeps the doubling from collapsing to one voice.

## Nerve

The doubled recognition: the audience is struck by seeing the old tale through the new key, feeling both at once. The pitch plays for the pleasure and the vertigo of the palimpsest -- the flash of catching what is written under what is written, the same events made to mean otherwise by the telling they are set against. It presses for a reader who knows the source, or comes to sense it, and is moved by the distance the transform opens.

## Echo

The source can no longer be heard quite straight. Once a tale has been re-pitched -- teased, debased, or transplanted -- the prior telling carries the new key as an overtone ever after, re-accented by the transform and by every situation that repeats it. What the palimpsest leaves is a doubling that outlasts the run: the original altered in memory, unable to return to its first, single tone.
