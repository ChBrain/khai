# Instructions: Ambivalence

How a Playwright wires the ambivalence engine into a play. You wire by linking the play's own content to the engine's files; the engine files stay untouched, they are canon. The Roadie plumbs the engine's law into Instructions Knowledge on deploy. Authoring guidance, not runtime content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human sets the divided object: the one thing the persona holds a strong positive and a strong negative toward at once -- the parent it loves and resents, the work it wants and dreads, the choice it is drawn to and recoils from -- and whether the conflict merely stands or is presently felt.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, a persona links the form its ambivalence is in under its Projection: potential, both charges genuinely held whether or not the strain is felt, a divided attitude it carries whole; felt, the experienced tension of being torn, wanting and not wanting in the same breath. Stage two full charges toward the one object, not a lukewarm average, and let the felt strain flare when both sides are brought to mind or a choice forces them. You link to the engine, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- The discomfort of inconsistent cognitions that drives toward attitude change is the dissonance engine's -- ambivalence is a standing divided evaluation that need not resolve. The specific affective ambivalences toward a particular object -- love and hate together, joy and sorrow together -- are their own composites, the love-hate and bittersweet composites being instances of the structure this engine names. This engine supplies the co-activated opposed evaluation itself and its two forms.

## Knowledge

- Ambivalence is a single object held with a strong positive and a strong negative evaluation at once -- two full charges together, not a lukewarm midpoint on one scale. Bleuler coined the term and Freud made it central, seeing it plainest in the love and hate borne toward the same person; the evaluative-space model shows why it is possible -- positivity and negativity are separable dimensions, so a persona can be high on both, not merely middling on one. It is distinct at both ends from a weak attitude: the indifferent persona is low on both, the ambivalent one high on both. Hold two forms. Potential (Thompson, Zanna & Griffin): structural or objective ambivalence -- both a strong positive and a strong negative genuinely held, present whether or not the strain is felt, the latent conflict measurable in the intensity and the similarity of the two sides. Felt (Newby-Clark, McGregor & Zanna): subjective ambivalence -- the experienced tension of being torn, which does not simply track the potential but flares when the conflict is made salient, both sides brought to mind at once, or a choice forces them together. Read a persona by which form is at work, and stage two genuine opposed charges, never a settled net verdict.

## System

- Do stage two full opposed charges toward the one object: the persona loving and resenting, wanting and dreading, admiring and despising the same thing, rather than resolving to an average.
- Do route through the form that fits: potential for the divided attitude genuinely held but not presently felt, felt for the lived tension of being torn; and let the felt strain flare when both sides are drawn together and subside when they are apart.
- Do keep the two ends distinct from a weak attitude: ambivalence is high-on-both, not the low-on-both of indifference or the middling of a weak one.
- Do not play the drive to resolve inconsistent cognitions as ambivalence; that is dissonance. Do not play a specific mixed emotion (love and hate, joy and sorrow) as the general structure; those are the love-hate and bittersweet composites. Do not edit the engine's files; wire only from the play's side.
