# Ambivalence: Reference

## Line of Work

Ambivalence as a **position** engine: the simultaneous holding of a strong positive and a strong negative evaluation toward a single object -- two full opposed charges co-activated, not a lukewarm midpoint on one scale -- taken across the two forms the attitudinal science distinguishes, the potential (structural) and the felt (subjective). The domain does not model the discomfort of inconsistent cognitions that pushes toward attitude change, which is the dissonance engine's; nor the specific affective ambivalences toward a particular object -- love and hate together, joy and sorrow together -- which are their own composites, the love-hate and bittersweet composites being instances of the structure this engine names. It models the evaluative structure itself: opposed charges held whole toward one object, and whether that conflict is merely present or actually felt.

The spine runs from the coining of the term through psychoanalysis to the modern attitudinal science that made it measurable. Bleuler coined "ambivalence" for the co-presence of opposed feelings, wishes, or thoughts in his account of the schizophrenias, and Freud took it up and made it general -- the love and hate borne toward the same person, most sharply in the transference and in mourning, the mixed feeling he found at the root of so much conflict. Twentieth-century social psychology then re-founded the construct on measurable ground: Kaplan reworked the single bipolar attitude scale to separate positive from negative, so that a divided attitude could be told from an indifferent one; Cacioppo and Berntson's evaluative-space model established that positivity and negativity are separable dimensions rather than the two ends of one, which is what makes strong-on-both possible at all; Thompson, Zanna and Griffin formalized potential (structural) ambivalence as a function of the intensity and the similarity of the two sides; and Newby-Clark, McGregor and Zanna showed that felt (subjective) ambivalence does not simply track the potential -- it flares when the two sides are made simultaneously accessible. The engine takes the co-activated opposed evaluation as its anchor and the potential and felt forms as its expressions. The altitudes are those: ambivalence, and its two forms.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The discomfort of inconsistent cognitions (dissonance)**: The engine models opposed evaluations co-held toward one object, not the aversive state that inconsistent cognitions set up and the drive to reduce it. Cognitive dissonance -- the pressure to change an attitude or behaviour so that beliefs and actions cohere -- is owned by the dissonance engine. Felt ambivalence and dissonance are cousins, both uncomfortable and both about inconsistency, but ambivalence is a standing divided evaluation of an object that need not resolve, while dissonance is a motivational drive toward consistency. Ambivalence is the two charges held; dissonance is the push to stop holding them.
- **Love and hate toward the one object (love-hate composite)**: The engine models the general structure of opposed co-activated evaluation; the specific affective ambivalence of love and hate borne toward the same person is owned by the love-hate composite. That composite is an instance of the structure this engine names -- one particular pair of charges -- not a rival account of it. The engine owns the form; the composite owns the case.
- **Joy and sorrow together (bittersweet composite)**: The engine models the evaluative structure; the specific mixed emotion of the poignant, the joy and sorrow felt at once, is owned by the bittersweet composite. Again the composite is an instance -- a named affective pair -- of the co-activation this engine defines, not the definition itself.
- **The indifferent, univalent, and weak attitude (out of domain)**: The engine models the strong-on-both case; it does not own the low-on-both indifferent attitude, the single-signed univalent one, or the weak middling one. These are the contrasts against which ambivalence is defined -- what it is not -- and the evaluative-space science that separates them is cited, not re-modelled: ambivalence is specifically two full opposed charges, and the engine holds only that.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per file.

- **[ambivalence](position_ambivalence.md)** (the anchor, position): The co-activation of a strong positive and a strong negative evaluation toward one object. Anchored by Bleuler (the coining) and Freud (the generalizing), with the evaluative-space model (Cacioppo & Berntson) showing why strong-on-both is possible.
- **[potential](position_potential.md)** (position, form): Structural or objective ambivalence -- both charges genuinely held whether or not the strain is felt, its magnitude a function of intensity and similarity. Anchored by Kaplan (the measurement) and Thompson, Zanna & Griffin (the formalization).
- **[felt](position_felt.md)** (position, form): Subjective ambivalence -- the experienced tension of being torn, flaring when both sides are made simultaneously accessible rather than simply tracking the potential. Anchored by Newby-Clark, McGregor & Zanna (felt vs potential).
