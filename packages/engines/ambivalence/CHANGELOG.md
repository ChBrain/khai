# @chbrain/khai-engine-ambivalence

## 0.1.1

### Patch Changes

- fbfae1d: Add the ambivalence engine (position: anchor + expressions) -- the simultaneous holding of a strong positive and a strong negative evaluation toward one object, two full opposed charges co-activated rather than a lukewarm midpoint on one scale. Fourth engine of the Freud effort, and the one flagged early as "maybe composite" -- resolved as a general **position** engine (see below).

  - **Anchor (position):** `ambivalence` -- opposed charges held whole toward a single object; the persona loves and resents, wants and dreads the one thing, not an averaged net verdict.
  - **Faces (position)** -- the two forms the attitudinal science draws:
    - `potential` -- **structural / objective** ambivalence: both charges genuinely held whether or not the strain is felt, its magnitude a function of the intensity and the similarity of the two sides (Thompson, Zanna & Griffin).
    - `felt` -- **subjective** ambivalence: the experienced tension of being torn, which does not simply track the potential but flares when both sides are made simultaneously accessible or a choice forces them together (Newby-Clark, McGregor & Zanna).

  **Engine, not composite.** The maintainer flagged ambivalence as possibly composite-shaped. It is built as a general **position** engine because a composite fixes its atoms by declaration, whereas ambivalence is the abstract structure of _any_ opposed pair co-activated toward one object. The specific affective ambivalences -- love and hate, joy and sorrow -- are its paradigm _instances_ and already live as the `love-hate` and `bittersweet` composites; this engine owns the structure they instantiate.

  **Warrant is a lineage, not solo work.** Bleuler coined "ambivalence" (1910/1911) and Freud generalized it (_The Dynamics of Transference_, 1912; _Totem and Taboo_, 1913; _Mourning and Melancholia_, 1917), but the measurable warrant is modern social psychology: Kaplan (1972) split the bipolar scale so a divided attitude is distinguished from an indifferent one; Cacioppo & Berntson's evaluative-space model (1994) established positivity and negativity as separable dimensions (why strong-on-both is possible); Thompson, Zanna & Griffin (1995) formalized potential ambivalence; Newby-Clark, McGregor & Zanna (2002) showed felt ambivalence is not a simple readout of potential.

  **Scope note.** The drive to resolve inconsistent cognitions stays with `dissonance` (ambivalence is a standing divided evaluation that need not resolve); the specific mixed emotions stay with the `love-hate` and `bittersweet` composites (instances, not rivals); the indifferent, univalent, and weak attitudes are the contrasts ambivalence is defined against, cited not re-modelled. Set at patch. No whitelist required -- the `ambivalence` / `potential` / `felt` stems are unique.
