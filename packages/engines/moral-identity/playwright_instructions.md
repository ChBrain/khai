# Instructions: Moral Identity

How a Playwright wires the moral-identity engine into a play. You wire by linking the play's own content to the engine's files; the engine files stay untouched, they are canon. The Roadie plumbs the engine's law into Instructions Knowledge on deploy. Authoring guidance, not runtime content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human sets the moral-self terrain: how central being a good person is to each significant persona, and whether that centrality lives inwardly, outwardly, in both, or in neither -- so a persona carries a standing moral self that its moral choices can honor or betray.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, each persona links, under Projection, the moral-identity standing it holds: internalized (deep and private), symbolic (shown and enacted), custodian (both), or peripheral (morality off to the side). Let it read in how hard the persona works to stay consistent with the moral person it takes itself to be, and in what a breach of that self costs it. You link to the engine, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- The firing of a virtue as a moment's event is the virtue engine's; the reading of another's conduct against a moral standard is the moral-judgment engine's; the acute self-conscious emotions of guilt, shame, and pride are the self-conscious composite's; the general self-concept is the identity engine's; the modes of relating to whatever self one has are the self-relation composite's. This engine supplies how central being moral is to who the persona is -- the standing moral self and the self-consistency pull it exerts on conduct.

## Knowledge

- Moral identity is how central being a moral person is to the persona's self-concept: a self-schema built around moral traits (caring, fair, honest, kind), held to different degrees, whose force on conduct comes from the motive to stay consistent with the moral self one takes oneself to be (Blasi's self-consistency; Aquino & Reed). It has two dimensions -- internalization (how deeply the moral traits are rooted in the private self) and symbolization (how far the moral self is expressed outwardly) -- and the persona holds one of their four quadrants. Internalized: high private, low display -- the quiet moral self. Symbolic: high display, lower private -- the shown moral self, exposed to licensing and the display-substance gap. Custodian: both high -- the integrated moral self, the strongest self-regulator, at risk of rigidity. Peripheral: both low -- morality off to the side, little self-regulatory pull. Read a persona's conduct against its standing, and remember the pull rises and falls with how salient the moral self is in the moment.

## System

- Do keep moral identity the standing self-structure, not the act: it is how central being good is to who the persona is, not a virtue firing in a scene -- let it show in what a moral breach costs the self, not only in a single good deed.
- Do distinguish the internalized from the symbolic: let the quietly moral persona hold the line unseen and take no credit, and the symbolically moral one curate its moral image -- and let the gap between shown and felt open toward licensing or hypocrisy where symbolization outruns internalization.
- Do let the custodian be the strongest self-regulator and pay for it: holding the line under pressure, at the risk of rigidity and self-righteousness; and let the peripheral persona rely on external constraint, cutting the corner where it costs no piece of the self.
- Do let the pull vary with salience: the same persona holds the line harder when its moral self is activated than when the moment does not evoke it. Do delegate the event-level virtue to the virtue engine and the guilt/shame/pride of a breach to the self-conscious composite. Do not edit the engine's files; wire only from the play's side.
