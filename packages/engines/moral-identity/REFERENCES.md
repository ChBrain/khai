# Moral Identity: Reference

## Line of Work

Moral identity as a **position** engine: how central being a moral person is to the persona's self-concept, taken across the standings that centrality can hold. The domain does not model the firing of a virtue as a moment's event, which is the virtue engine's; nor the reading of another's conduct against a moral standard, which is the moral-judgment engine's; nor the acute self-conscious emotions of guilt, shame, and pride, which are the self-conscious composite's; nor the general self-concept, which is the identity engine's. It models the standing self-structure: a self built, to some degree, around moral traits, and the pull that structure exerts on conduct through the motive to stay consistent with the moral person one takes oneself to be.

The spine is the social-psychology of the moral self. Blasi's self-model of moral functioning locates the source of moral action not in judgment alone but in the self: when being moral is central to identity, a judgment of moral responsibility follows, and a self-consistency motive presses the person to act on it, because failing to would betray who they are. Aquino and Reed operationalize this as the self-importance of moral identity, a self-schema organized around moral traits (caring, fair, honest, kind, compassionate) that varies across people and has two dimensions: internalization, how deeply those traits are rooted in the private self-concept, and symbolization, how far the moral self is expressed outwardly through action, membership, and self-presentation. Aquino and colleagues' social-cognitive model adds that moral identity is chronically accessible to differing degrees and situationally activated, so its pull on behavior rises and falls with its salience. The standings this engine offers are the quadrants of the two dimensions -- internalized, symbolic, custodian, and peripheral -- read as the resting profiles a persona holds. The altitude is the position: the moral self as a standing part of who the persona is.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **Virtue**: The engine models the standing centrality of the moral self, not the firing of a virtue as an event. The virtue engine reads a virtue as a moment's act and explicitly never classifies the persona; moral identity is the trait it declines -- how central being moral is to who the persona is, and the self-consistency pull that follows. The two compose: virtue fires the good act, moral identity is the standing self the act is (or is not) consistent with.
- **Moral judgment**: The engine models the self's moral centrality, not the appraisal of conduct against a standard. The moral-judgment engine reads whether an act is right or wrong and on which foundation; moral identity turns inward, on how much being moral is part of the self and how hard that self pulls one's own conduct -- the judging is moral-judgment's, the self-centrality is this engine's.
- **The self-conscious composite**: The engine models the standing moral self, not the acute emotions of appraising it. Guilt, shame, and pride -- the emotions that fire when the self is measured against a moral standard -- are read by the self-conscious composite; moral identity is the standing structure whose breach those emotions register, not the emotions themselves. A central moral self makes a lapse hurt; the hurt is the self-conscious composite's.
- **Identity**: The engine models the moral portion's centrality, not the whole self-concept. Who the persona is across all its commitments and its story is owned by the identity engine; moral identity is the specific question of how central the moral traits are within that self, one region of identity, not the whole.
- **Self-relation composite**: The engine models a content of the self (how central morality is), not the modes of relating to the self. Evaluating, caring for, and being true to the self are read by the self-relation composite; moral identity is a distinct axis -- what the self is built around -- orthogonal to how the persona relates to whatever self it has.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per file.

- **[moral identity](position_moral_identity.md)** (the anchor, position): How central being a moral person is to the self-concept, and the self-consistency pull it exerts on conduct. Anchored by Aquino & Reed (the self-importance of moral identity) and Blasi (the self-model).
- **[internalized](position_internalized.md)** (position, standing): High private centrality, low outward display -- the quiet moral self, strongly self-regulating but unbroadcast. Anchored by Aquino & Reed (internalization).
- **[symbolic](position_symbolic.md)** (position, standing): High outward expression, lower private centrality -- the moral self enacted and displayed, exposed to the display-substance gap and licensing. Anchored by Aquino & Reed (symbolization) and Reed & Aquino (licensing).
- **[custodian](position_custodian.md)** (position, standing): Both dimensions high -- the integrated moral self, the strongest self-regulator, at risk of rigidity. Anchored by Blasi (self-consistency) and Aquino & Reed.
- **[peripheral](position_peripheral.md)** (position, standing): Both dimensions low -- morality off to the side of the self, little self-regulatory pull, reliant on external constraint. Anchored by Aquino & Reed (low centrality).
