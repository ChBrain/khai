## Knowledge

- A collection is a series of objects withdrawn from the use they were made for, bounded by a criterion that exists only in its keeper. It is not extended-self's possession experienced as part of the self, not heirloom's object withheld from exchange to carry a lineage, and not the object-cycle composite's material life of wear and mending. It wires at Piece, the persona links at Projection, and the members hang flat beneath the object. Its place is defined by withdrawal from function: nearly everything in it was made for a purpose it is no longer permitted to serve. What it holds up is a persona's account of themselves as somebody with a subject, plus a private jurisdiction in which they alone decide what counts, what is a variant, and what is a fake. What shows to a visitor is quantity and money; what the collector sees is a sequence with holes in it, so the compliment offered is never the one that lands. The collector holds two things and the objects are the smaller -- the other is the criterion -- and the position forecloses using the objects, being finished, and pricing the collection honestly. The hunting is what the collection actually consists of: months of searching punctuated by an afternoon of having, so the satisfaction is delivered by the search and charged to the object, and the next search starts immediately. The completing is where the engine is seen for one thing: as the last items come in the criterion widens, every refinement is genuine and defensible knowledge, and the collector has no way to tell an insight from a reprieve. The dispersal leaves every object intact and mostly at its price, and destroys the criterion, which existed nowhere but in the collector -- so a collection ends by becoming unreadable rather than by being lost. The engine's twist is at the anchor: a collection is made of what is missing from it, so completing it would end it, and the item that would finish the series is the one most wanted and least wanted at once.

## System

- Do decide the gap first. What is missing sets the engine; the shelf is scenery.
- Do give the criterion one sentence and keep it unspoken. Nobody but the collector holds it, which is what makes the dispersal final.
- Do keep the collector competent and good company. This is knowledge and skill, and staging it as compulsion collapses the engine into a diagnosis.
- Do not model hoarding here. The clinical literature separates it from collecting on the criterion, the discarding, and the harm, and this engine models only the first of the two.
- Do stage the acquisition as an anticlimax. The search took months, the having takes an afternoon, and the collector is already looking again by the end of the scene.
- Do let the criterion widen honestly. A variant is discovered, a threshold is revised, a date range extends -- all of it real knowledge, all of it arriving exactly when the end came into view.
- Do put the twist in a near miss. The one item that would finish the series comes available, and watch what the collector does with the fact that they could have it.
- Do close on the dispersal rather than the collection. The objects are fine, the prices hold, the heirs are being reasonable, and the thing itself has become unreadable.
- Do not model the possession as part of the self (extended-self), the lineage object (heirloom), wear and mending (object-cycle, wear, decay, restoration), the group emblem (totem), appetite or compulsion (desire, scarcity, addiction), distinction (capital, status), or the price (money, gift, debt). Do not make the collection stand for a theme; that is authored key, the play's pitch. Do not edit the engine's files; wire only from the play's side.
