# Piece: Collection

## Taxonomy

The series and its missing term (no khai file of its own yet).

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: collection

## Place

Not in use, and not for sale. A collection sits outside the working life of the objects in it: the stamps are not for posting, the records are not the ones played, the cars are not driven, the books are frequently not read.
It occupies a room, a cabinet, a spreadsheet, or a shelf that the rest of the household negotiates around, and its boundary is exact -- an object either belongs to the series or it does not, and the collector can say instantly which.
Its place is defined by withdrawal from function: nearly everything in it was made for a purpose it is no longer permitted to serve.

## Load Bearing

What it holds up is a persona's account of themselves as somebody with a subject. A collection is evidence of sustained attention to one thing across years, which very little else in a life supplies, and it is legible to anybody who walks into the room.
It also holds up a private jurisdiction. Inside its boundaries the collector decides what counts, what is a duplicate, what is a variant, and what is a fake, and that authority is answerable to nobody.
Remove it and the persona keeps the knowledge and loses the standing that made the knowledge worth having.

## Apparent

Displayed to be counted rather than admired, and read by outsiders on the wrong axis. A visitor sees quantity and money; the collector sees a sequence with holes in it.
So the compliment that lands is never the one offered: told the collection is impressive, the collector hears a remark about the part that is finished, and their attention is on the part that is not.
What actually shows is duration -- how long somebody has been at this -- and that reads accurately to everybody.

## Yearbook

The arc runs from the first two objects that were noticed to be a pair. It grows fastest early, when almost anything qualifies, and slows as the field narrows, until what remains is the rare, the expensive, and the possibly nonexistent. It peaks shortly before completion rather than at it, when the series is nearly whole and the hunt still has an object.
The engine's twist is here. **A collection is made of what is missing from it.** The gap is not a defect in the series; it is what makes it a series rather than a pile, and it is where the collector's attention actually rests -- so completing it would end it. The item that would finish the set is therefore the one most wanted and least wanted at once, and collectors resolve that without noticing: a variant is discovered, a threshold is revised, a subseries begins, one entry stays permanently on a list.
It is not the possession experienced as part of the self, which is extended-self's, nor the object withheld from exchange to carry a lineage, which is heirloom's, nor the object's material life of wear and mending, which is the object-cycle composite's; and a collection made to **stand for** a theme is authored key, the play's pitch.
It fails by completion, by dispersal, and by the collector's death, and only one of the three is ever planned for.
