# Process: The Dispersal

## Taxonomy

[Collection](piece_collection.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: collection

## Initiated by

The collection stops being one.
Sold, split among heirs, given to an institution that will keep six items and deaccession the rest, or left in a house that has to be cleared by people who did not build it.

## Direction

Toward a set of objects that are only objects again.
What dispersal destroys is not value; the pieces mostly keep their prices, and some do better apart than together. What it destroys is the criterion -- the thing that made these particular objects a set -- and that existed nowhere but in the collector.
This is why collectors mind dispersal more than loss, and why an institution that accepts a collection and breaks it up is felt to have done something worse than a thief would have.

## Lever

The reasonableness of dispersal against what it dissolves.
The carrying force is that the heirs are not being callous and the institution is not being philistine: the objects are genuinely valuable separately, nobody else holds the criterion, and asking a family to maintain a series they never cared about would be a strange thing to ask of them; the resistance is that the collection was an argument only its maker could state, so it ends not by being destroyed but by becoming unreadable, and there is no version of the ending in which it survives as what it was.
The Dispersal takes nothing and ends everything.

## Echo

Objects with no series.
It shows what the collection actually was, at the point where it has stopped being it.
The Dispersal is dated from the collector's last day of interest and is carried out by somebody else.
