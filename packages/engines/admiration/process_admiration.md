# Process: Admiration

## Taxonomy

Emotion / the psychology of the response to witnessed excellence.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: admiration

## Initiated by

A persona witnesses another's excellence -- a skill, a talent, an achievement, a mastery beyond the ordinary -- and warms upward toward them, moved and lifted rather than diminished.
This is admiration, one of the other-praising emotions (Algoe & Haidt): the response to a witnessed excellence that is non-moral -- competence, not virtue, which is elevation's.
Where envy meets another's superiority with pain and the wish to pull it down or take it, admiration meets it with warmth and the wish to rise toward it.

## Direction

Upward and toward -- toward the admired and toward the excellence itself, to learn from it, be near it, and rise by it.
Admiration is the benign face of upward comparison: it assimilates where envy contrasts, closing the gap by lifting the self rather than lowering the other.
Its motion is centripetal and ascending: the persona drawn toward the excellent, and moved to climb.

## Lever

The appraisal of an excellence that is real, deserved, and worth reaching for -- and the openness to be bettered by it rather than threatened.
What admiration spends is the self's defensiveness before another's superiority: it grants the excellence freely, and takes it as a model rather than a rebuke.
This is admiration's adaptive work -- the emotion of social learning, by which the skilled are marked, watched, and copied (Onu, Kessler & Smith).

## Echo

The excellence is honored and, at its best, propagated -- admiration motivates emulation and spreads skill through a culture, the many rising toward the few who show what is possible.
It runs as [inspiration](process_inspiration.md), the striving it fires; [affiliation](process_affiliation.md), the drawing-near it prompts; and [adoration](process_adoration.md), the reverence it tips into when the excellence is felt beyond reach -- and is tuned by a standing [admiring](position_admiring.md) disposition.
The witnessing of moral excellence is the elevation engine's; the warm response to a benefit received is the gratitude engine's; the accommodation of a vastness that exceeds the frame is the awe engine's; the painful upward comparison and its wish to close the gap by taking is the envy engine's. This engine owns the warm upward response to another's excellence.
