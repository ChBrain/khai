# Admiration: Reference

## Line of Work

Admiration as a **multi-type** engine: the warm upward emotion through which a persona meets another's witnessed excellence -- skill, talent, achievement -- with regard rather than resentment, moved to rise toward it, taken across its forms and the trait that tunes it. The domain does not model the witnessing of moral excellence, which is the elevation engine's; nor the warm response to a benefit received, which is the gratitude engine's; nor the accommodation of a vastness that exceeds the frame, which is the awe engine's; nor the painful upward comparison and its wish to close the gap by taking, which is the envy engine's. It models the emotion itself: the appraisal of another's non-moral excellence and the warm, upward, assimilative response it draws.

The spine is the psychology of the other-praising emotions and of upward social comparison. Admiration is, in Algoe and Haidt's account, one of three other-praising emotions -- gratitude, elevation, and admiration -- distinguished by their elicitor: gratitude answers a benefit to the self, elevation answers witnessed moral excellence, and admiration answers witnessed non-moral excellence, skill and competence and achievement. It is the benign face of upward comparison (van de Ven, Zeelenberg & Pieters): where envy meets another's superiority with pain and a contrastive wish to pull it down or take it, admiration meets it with warmth and an assimilative wish to rise toward it. Its adaptive work is social learning -- the emotion by which the skilled are marked, approached, and copied (Onu, Kessler & Smith), motivating emulation and the transmission of competence. It runs from inspiration, the striving toward a reachable model (Thrash & Elliot), through affiliation, the drawing-near to the admired, to adoration, the reverence that admiration tips into when the excellence is felt beyond reach (Schindler et al.). Individual differences in the disposition to admire tune it all -- the readiness to find models and be inspired rather than to envy. The altitudes are those: the emotion, its three forms, and its trait.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **Elevation**: The engine models admiration of non-moral excellence, not the uplift of witnessed moral excellence. The warm, expansive response to another's virtue -- and the desire to be a better person it prompts -- is owned by the elevation engine; admiration answers skill, talent, and competence, elevation answers goodness, and the two are the other-praising responses to two different excellences (Algoe & Haidt). This engine keeps the competence response; elevation keeps the virtue response.
- **Gratitude**: The engine models admiration of another's excellence, not the warm response to a benefit received. The turn toward a benefactor who has given the self a good is owned by the gratitude engine; admiration needs no benefit to the self -- it answers excellence witnessed at any distance -- while gratitude answers a gift. This engine keeps the response to excellence; gratitude keeps the response to benefit.
- **Awe**: The engine models admiration of an excellent person, not the accommodation of a vastness. The response to a stimulus so vast it exceeds the current frame and forces its revision is owned by the awe engine; adoration's reverence toward a person felt above one borders awe's, but awe answers vastness and need for accommodation, where admiration answers excellence, and the sacred and the natural sublime are awe's. This engine keeps the excellent person; awe keeps the vast.
- **Envy**: The engine models the benign, assimilative response to another's superiority, not the painful, contrastive one. The pain of an upward comparison and the wish to close the gap by pulling the other down or taking what they have is owned by the envy engine; admiration is its mirror -- the same superiority met with warmth and the wish to rise -- and the two are the two faces of upward comparison. This engine keeps the assimilative face; envy keeps the contrastive one.
- **Status and Attachment**: The engine models the emotion, not the rank it confers or the bond it may begin. The standing that excellence earns and that nearness to it borrows is the status engine's, and the durable tie that affiliation may grow into is the attachment engine's; admiration draws toward the excellent and lends them standing, but the rank and the bond themselves are theirs.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per file.

- **[admiration](process_admiration.md)** (the root, process): The warm upward response to witnessed non-moral excellence -- regard rather than resentment, the assimilative face of upward comparison, the emotion of social learning. Anchored by Algoe & Haidt (the other-praising family) and Onu et al. (admiration as social learning).
- **[inspiration](process_inspiration.md)** (process, form): The emulative form -- admiration firing striving toward a reachable model, regard turned into self-improvement. Anchored by Thrash & Elliot (inspiration) and Onu et al. (the emulation tendency).
- **[affiliation](process_affiliation.md)** (process, form): The drawing-near form -- the pull to connect with, be near, and stand in the light of the admired. Anchored by Onu et al. (the affiliation tendency).
- **[adoration](process_adoration.md)** (process, form): The reverent form -- admiration of an unattainable excellence tipping into worship and devotion, the idol and the hero, fragile when it falls. Anchored by Schindler et al. (admiration vs adoration) and the celebrity-worship literature.
- **[admiring](position_admiring.md)** (position, trait): The disposition to meet excellence with admiration rather than envy -- the readiness to find models, be inspired, and be bettered by others. Anchored by van de Ven et al. (the benign pole of upward comparison) and Onu et al.
