# Instructions: Weather

How a Playwright wires the weather engine into a play. You wire by linking the
play's own content to the engine's files; the engine files stay untouched, they
are canon. The Roadie plumbs the engine's law into Instructions Knowledge on
deploy. Authoring guidance, not runtime content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human sets the front on each place: what weather it is under, and where the place sits on its year.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, every weathered place carries under Shown the channel and band the front has brought (wind, water, heat, cold, or sky, each at a band from a load the place tempers without effort to one it cannot temper), and season sets the floor those loads climb from. Show the load in what the place shows; do not narrate it, and never let the weather mirror a persona's feeling. You link to the engine, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- How the weather makes a persona feel is the mood and emotion engines'; the setting the weather moves is space's; the flood the water becomes and the burn the heat feeds are hydrology's and fire's; the weather made to stand for a mood is the play's pitch. Whether the load rings true for the place is the team's review.

## Knowledge

- Weather is a loop a place runs when its sky turns: a front loads it on every face at once, wind and water and heat and cold pressing from outside and the sky deciding how far it can be seen, each at its own band, and the bands need not match. Season runs underneath and sets the floor each transient load climbs from, so the same front numbs a place low on its year and only freshens one high on it. The place shows the load before anyone in it speaks.

## System

- Do give every weathered place its channel and band under Shown (the law admits no exception); do let the channels differ; do set the season floor; do play the load, not a smooth backdrop.
- Do not collapse it to one weather word; the channels are separate loads. Do not make the weather mirror a mood; that is the play's pitch, not the place's force. Do not edit the engine's files; wire only from the play's side.
