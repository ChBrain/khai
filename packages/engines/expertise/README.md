# Expertise

Expertise as process: how a capacity moves out of deliberation and into the hands, so a persona stops working through the rules and starts seeing what the situation requires -- not the response grooved by repetition, which is habit's, and not the held theory of where ability comes from, which is mindset's -- across three movements: the stages (Dreyfus's climb from the novice applying context-free rules, through the advanced beginner noticing what nobody taught, the competent who must choose a plan and thereby owns the outcome, and the proficient who reads the case instantly and still reasons out the response, to the expert in whom situation and response arrive together), the making (deliberate practice at the edge with feedback close enough to correct on, against the plateau where automaticity ends improvement, and the low-validity environment where confidence grows and accuracy does not), and the tacit (apprenticeship, which transmits what cannot be stated, and the blind spot, which opens where a competence has gone under) -- with the twist that becoming an expert destroys the ability to teach it, since the mechanism producing the competence is the mechanism removing it from view.
