# Expertise: Reference

## Line of Work

Expertise as **process**: how a capacity moves out of deliberation and into the hands.

The engine's subject is a transformation rather than an accumulation. A persona who has got good at
something is not doing the same thing faster; they are doing a different thing. They stop working
through the rules and start seeing what the situation requires, and the seeing arrives ahead of any
account of it. That is why the engine sits apart from what khai already carries: the response grooved
by repetition into a stable context is the habit engine's and the ingraining composite's; the held
theory of where ability comes from is mindset's; the belief in one's own capability is self-efficacy's;
the absorption in a matched challenge is flow's. None of them is about a performance changing kind.

**The stages** are Dreyfus and Dreyfus's, and their value is that what changes across them is not
speed but what the persona is looking at. The **novice** applies context-free rules to features they
have been taught to notice -- the right way to begin, and blind to the case the rule was not built
for. The **advanced beginner** starts using situational aspects they can recognise and cannot define,
which produces the engine's first small paradox: being asked to justify a judgement is a demotion, and
a supervisor who demands articulation for everything holds them at the previous stage. The
**competent** must adopt a plan because the accumulated aspects exceed what can be held at once, and
in choosing the plan they acquire ownership -- this is where outcomes start belonging to somebody, and
where the emotional stake that later fuels expertise first becomes painful. The **proficient** reads
the situation whole and immediately, and still has to reason out the response: diagnostic-fast,
decision-slow, which is slower than either neighbouring stage and feels worse than both. The
**expert** has closed the split -- situation and response arrive together, no options present
themselves, and the reasons offered afterwards are reconstructions rather than the process.

**The making** carries the unwelcome findings. Time on task predicts skill poorly; what predicts it is
**deliberate practice** at the edge of the current capacity, with **feedback** close and specific
enough to correct on. Practice without a good loop does not improve performance, it consolidates
whatever was being done. And the **plateau** is the sting: once a capacity is compiled below the level
of attention it stops developing, so the ordinary reward for becoming good is arriving at a level one
will occupy indefinitely, and twenty years of experience becomes two years repeated ten times. The
persona feels themselves improving because they feel themselves getting easier, and those are
different things.

The fourth member of that movement is the engine's most important bound and is deliberately given the
same weight as the others. Kahneman and Klein started from opposite positions -- one from the heuristics
and biases literature, one from naturalistic decision-making -- and agreed: intuitive expertise is real
where the environment has enough regularity to be learnable and feedback prompt enough to learn from,
and where those conditions fail, practitioners accumulate experience, grow more confident, and do not
grow more accurate. **The sense of expertise is produced by fluency and familiarity whether or not
there is anything to know**, and the persona cannot tell the two apart from inside. So the honest
question about any authority is never whether they are experienced but whether their domain is one
that could have taught them.

**The tacit** is Polanyi's -- we know more than we can tell -- and it has two consequences the engine
carries. **Apprenticeship**, in Lave and Wenger's sense, is the only transmission route that works for
the unspeakable part: a newcomer doing peripheral but genuine work near somebody competent, moving
inward as they become able, acquiring judgement and standards as a by-product of taking part. Every
field with unspeakable judgement reinvents this arrangement independently, and it is slow, expensive,
master-dependent, unauditable, and the first thing cut. And the **blind spot**: an expert explaining
omits a large share of the steps a novice needs, underestimates the difficulty, and remembers their own
learning as more direct than it was -- because the compiled version is the only one they still have.

Hence the **twist**, at the root. **Becoming an expert destroys the ability to teach it.** The
mechanism that produces the competence -- chunking and automating what was once effortful and explicit
-- is the same mechanism that removes it from the persona's own sight. The better they are, the worse
their account. Nobody is withholding, and the expert cannot repair it by trying harder or being asked
better questions, because the missing material is not available to introspection. Expertise is bought
with the account of itself.

The engine is careful about two temptations and a play should be too. The first is treating the
expert's silence as gatekeeping: it is not, and the diagnosis matters, because the repairs that work
come from outside the expert -- watching a novice fail, being contradicted by a record, teaching
alongside somebody nearer the beginning. The second is reading the low-validity member as a debunking
of expertise. It is not. Where the conditions hold, expert intuition is fast, correct, and better than
any available alternative.

**On the evidence, stated plainly.** The three movements rest on very different footings. **Deliberate
practice and the feedback requirement are the best supported**: that structured practice at the edge
with correction outperforms accumulated experience is not seriously disputed, and neither is the
plateau. What **is** disputed, sharply, is how much of expert performance deliberate practice accounts
for -- Ericsson's stronger claims have been challenged by meta-analyses finding it explains a
substantial but far smaller share of variance than he argued, with the remainder attributed to
starting age, working memory, physical characteristics, and much else. The engine takes the mechanism
and not the strong claim, and a reader should not take from it that practice is sufficient. **The
Dreyfus stages are a phenomenological model, not a measured taxonomy** -- influential, widely used in
nursing and pilot training, and criticised for being derived from a handful of domains, for treating
the stages as more discrete than performance is, and for describing skills like chess and flying more
convincingly than open-ended professional judgement. **Kahneman and Klein's conditions are the most
robust single result here** and are the reason the engine will not present expertise as uniformly real.
**The tacit material is philosophical and ethnographic**: Polanyi's claim is an argument rather than a
finding, and Lave and Wenger's is fieldwork; the expert blind spot itself has decent experimental
support -- the omission rate and the difficulty underestimation replicate -- but the numbers vary a
great deal by task and should be treated as a robust direction rather than a quantity. What should not
be taken from the engine is that experts cannot teach at all, that experience is worthless, or that
tacit knowledge is a mystification. The claim is narrow: the performance changes kind, the change is
bought by automating what was once explicit, and the automating is what takes it out of view.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The response grooved by repetition (habit, ingraining)**: How a response repeated in a stable
  context comes to run without intention is the habit engine's, and how a response gets installed
  until it runs without consulting why is the ingraining composite's. Those are about a fixed act
  becoming automatic; this engine is about a performance changing kind, so that what the persona
  perceives is different rather than what they do being faster.
- **The theory of where ability comes from (mindset)**: A held belief that ability is fixed or grown
  is the mindset engine's. It bears heavily on whether a persona will do deliberate practice at all,
  and it is not this engine's -- expertise reads the acquisition, not the persona's theory of it.
- **Belief in one's own capability (self-efficacy)**: The judgement a persona makes about what they
  can do is self-efficacy's. The low-validity member reads a confidence that outruns accuracy, and
  delegates the faculty producing the confidence.
- **Absorption in a matched challenge (flow)**: Total absorption where challenge and skill are pulled
  high and matched is the flow engine's. Deliberate practice frequently prevents flow rather than
  producing it, since it is conducted deliberately above the current capacity.
- **Persistence toward a long goal (grit, achievement-motive, discipline)**: Sustained effort over
  years, the motive to attain a standard, and the training of conduct are those engines'. This engine
  reads what the effort builds and says nothing about where the effort comes from.
- **Expert power over another (power, status)**: Influence held because one is believed to know is
  French and Raven's and the power engine's. Expertise reads the competence; whether anybody defers to
  it is power's, and the two come apart frequently in both directions.
- **The expert's sizing of a hazard (risk)**: The count -- expected annual fatalities as the expert's
  reading of risk -- is the risk engine's. That is a specific professional judgement and belongs
  there.
- **Learning a social world (socialization)**: The internalisation of a ready-made objective order
  until it becomes reality itself is the socialization engine's. Apprenticeship transmits membership
  as well as skill, and the membership half is socialization's.
- **Mastery made authored symbol (the play's pitch)**: A skill made to **stand for** a theme -- the
  luthier's hands that mirror a motif of inheritance -- is refused: that is authored key, the play's
  pitch. Expertise sets what the competence is and what it cost; the meaning read into it is the
  play's.

## Encoding

Source to constraint. A process tree: one root, three movements, eleven forms.

- **[expertise](process_expertise.md)** (the root): a capacity moving out of deliberation and into the
  hands, read on what the persona can do and never on what they say they do, carrying the twist --
  becoming an expert destroys the ability to teach it. Anchored by Dreyfus & Dreyfus (1986) and
  Polanyi (1966).
- **[the stages](process_stages.md)**: the climb from rule-following to perception. Anchored by
  Dreyfus & Dreyfus (1986). [the novice](process_novice.md),
  [the advanced beginner](process_advanced_beginner.md), [the competent](process_competent.md),
  [the proficient](process_proficient.md), [the expert](process_expert_stage.md).
- **[the making](process_making_expertise.md)**: what builds skill and what only accumulates hours.
  Anchored by Ericsson et al. (1993), with the bound from Kahneman & Klein (2009) and Macnamara et al.
  (2014). [deliberate practice](process_deliberate_practice.md),
  [the feedback loop](process_feedback_loop.md), [the plateau](process_plateau.md),
  [the low-validity environment](process_low_validity.md).
- **[the tacit](process_tacit.md)**: what was never in words and what has left them. Anchored by
  Polanyi (1966), Lave & Wenger (1991), and Hinds (1999).
  [the apprenticeship](process_apprenticeship.md), [the blind spot](process_expert_blind_spot.md).
