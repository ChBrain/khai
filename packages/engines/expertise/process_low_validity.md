# Process: The Low-Validity Environment

## Taxonomy

[The Making](process_making_expertise.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: expertise

## Initiated by

The world the persona is practising in does not teach.
The Low-Validity Environment starts wherever the domain lacks the two conditions intuitive expertise requires: enough regularity to be learnable, and feedback prompt and clear enough to learn from.

## Direction

Toward confidence that grows while accuracy does not.
In such domains -- long-range prediction, many clinical and political judgements, much of forecasting -- practitioners accumulate experience, become more certain, and do not become more accurate, and the subjective sense of expertise is produced by fluency and familiarity rather than by any improvement in hitting the target.
The feeling of knowing is generated by the same mechanism whether or not there is anything to know.

## Lever

The reality of expertise where the conditions hold against its absence where they do not.
The carrying force is that this is not a debunking of expertise and should never be read as one: where the environment is regular and the feedback good, expert intuition is real, fast, and better than any available alternative, and Kahneman and Klein agreed on that after starting from opposite positions; the resistance is that the persona cannot tell which kind of environment they are in from inside, because the sense of expertise feels identical in both -- so the honest question is never whether somebody is experienced but whether their domain is one that could have taught them.
The Low-Validity Environment is where every other member of this engine keeps running and none of it produces skill.

## Echo

A practised persona who is no better than they were.
It is the engine's most important bound and the one a play is most tempted to skip.
The Low-Validity Environment is dated from nothing, since nothing in it accumulates.
