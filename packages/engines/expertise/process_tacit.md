# Process: The Tacit

## Taxonomy

[Expertise](process_expertise.md), what was never in words and what has left them, and where the engine is seen for one thing.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: expertise

## Initiated by

The persona knows something they cannot state.
The Tacit starts at Polanyi's observation -- we know more than we can tell -- and runs through what follows: knowledge transmitted by [apprenticeship](process_apprenticeship.md) rather than instruction, and the [blind spot](process_expert_blind_spot.md) that opens where a competence has gone under.

## Direction

Toward knowledge that moves only by proximity.
Some of it was never explicit: the feel of the right tension, the sound that means something is wrong, the moment to stop. Some of it was explicit once and has been compiled away.
Either way the route is the same -- being near somebody doing it, over time, on real work -- which is why the guild, the studio, the ward round, and the workshop keep being reinvented in fields that have no historical connection.

## Lever

The efficiency of knowing without telling against the cost of not being able to tell.
The carrying force is that tacit knowledge is not a deficiency or a mystification: compressing a competence below the level of description is what makes it fast, reliable, and available under load, and a persona who had to articulate every judgement would be no use at all; the resistance is that what cannot be stated cannot be written down, audited, argued with, or checked -- so it travels only by contact, is lost when the person leaves, and cannot be corrected by anybody who does not already have it.
The Tacit rides the engine's keystone knife-edge: expertise is bought with the account of itself.

## Echo

A competence that exists and is not on record anywhere.
It shows the three movements complete: a climb that ends in perception, a making that ends in automaticity, and the consequence -- a persona whose knowledge is genuine, effective, and unavailable to anyone who cannot stand next to them.
This is why the Tacit is the hardest of the three to stage and the only one worth closing on: the expert must be genuinely good, genuinely willing to teach, and genuinely bad at it, and the audience must be able to see that the failure is structural rather than a refusal.
A play that stages the expert as guarding their knowledge has replaced the mechanism with a motive.
The Tacit is dated from whenever the persona stopped having to think about it.
