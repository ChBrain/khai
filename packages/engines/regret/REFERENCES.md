# Regret: Reference

## Line of Work

Regret as **process**: how a persona measures a choice they own against a better outcome they can readily imagine having, and wishes they had chosen otherwise -- the agency-plus-counterfactual emotion of decision. The domain does not model a bad outcome merely suffered without a choice behind it, which is disappointment, not regret; nor the moral wrong done to another, which is the guilt engine's; nor the loss of an attachment mourned, which is the grief engine's; nor the global defect of the self exposed, which is the shame engine's. It models the sting of a self-authored, avoidable choice held against its foregone alternative: agency crossed with counterfactual, working through the routes by which the might-have-been bites.

The spine is the cognitive and decision psychology of regret and counterfactual thought. Regret is distinguished from related negative feelings by two ingredients: agency -- a decision the person owns -- and a counterfactual -- a better outcome near enough to imagine. Kahneman and Tversky's norm theory and the simulation heuristic explain the sting's intensity by the ease of undoing the outcome in imagination: abnormal, action-based, near-miss outcomes are more mutable and so more regretted. Gilovich and Medvec mapped the temporal profile: action (commission) regrets are hot and dominate the short term, while inaction (omission) regrets are milder at first but come to dominate over a lifetime, a pattern Roese and Summerville tie to unrealized opportunity. Zeelenberg and colleagues developed the forward-looking side -- anticipated regret and regret regulation -- showing that the imagined future regret of a choice shapes the choice itself. Three routes carry it: commission, inaction, anticipation.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **Disappointment**: The engine models the regret of a choice one owns, not the letdown of a bad outcome one did not bring about. A result that falls short of hopes but follows from no decision of the persona's -- luck, another's act, the world -- is disappointment, which needs no agency; regret requires the persona to be the author of the worse branch. The engine owns only the self-authored case; the agency-free letdown is disappointment's, not regret's.
- **Guilt**: The engine models a suboptimal choice measured against its better alternative, not a moral wrong done to another. The transgression that harms someone and calls for atonement is owned by the guilt engine; regret is self-regarding and comparative -- one can regret a choice that wronged no one, and feel guilt over a wrong one does not otherwise regret -- so the engine delegates the moral-harm case to guilt and keeps the counterfactual-suboptimality case.
- **Grief**: The engine models the counterfactual sting of a controllable choice, not the mourning of a loss. The sorrow at the loss of an attachment -- a person, a bond -- is owned by the grief engine; grief needs no decision and no better branch one could have chosen, only a loss to mourn, where regret needs an owned choice and an imaginable alternative. The engine delegates the mourning of loss to grief.
- **Shame**: The engine models a verdict on a specific choice against its alternative, not a verdict on the whole self. The painful exposure of a global defect -- being, not doing, found wanting -- is owned by the shame engine; regret indicts the decision, shame indicts the person, so one can regret a choice without shame and feel shame without a specific regretted act. The engine delegates the global self-defect to shame.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per file.

- **[regret](process_regret.md)** (the root): The agency-plus-counterfactual sting -- a choice one owns measured against a better outcome one can readily imagine, and wished otherwise; sharper the more mutable the outcome. Anchored by Kahneman & Miller (norm theory) and Gilovich & Medvec (the experience of regret).
- **[commission](process_commission.md)** (form): The action route -- the hot, immediate regret of a deed done, the abnormal act easily mutated in imagination, dominant in the short term. Anchored by Kahneman & Tversky (the simulation heuristic) and Gilovich & Medvec (action regrets short-term).
- **[inaction](process_inaction.md)** (form): The omission route -- the wistful, enduring regret of a chance not seized, the unrealized possibility that never closes, dominant over a lifetime. Anchored by Gilovich & Medvec (inaction regrets long-term) and Roese & Summerville (what we regret most).
- **[anticipation](process_anticipation.md)** (form): The forward-looking route -- the imagined future regret of an option shaping the choice now, the decision bent to forestall a foreseen sting. Anchored by Zeelenberg (anticipated regret) and Connolly & Zeelenberg (regret in decision making).
