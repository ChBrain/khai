# Process: Regret

## Taxonomy

Behavioral science / the psychology of regret, the counterfactual emotion of choice.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: regret

## Initiated by

A persona made a choice, the choice turned out worse than one they can easily imagine having made instead, and they wish they had chosen otherwise.
This is regret: the pain of measuring a decision one owns against a better outcome one can readily picture -- not a bad result merely suffered, but a bad result one feels one brought about and could have avoided.
The persona is not simply unlucky; they are the author of the worse branch, and the better branch stands close enough to imagine.

## Direction

Toward a self measured against its own foregone alternative and found to have chosen wrong.
Regret needs two things a bare bad outcome does not: agency, a choice the persona owns, and a counterfactual, a better outcome near enough to summon -- and the sting grows with both, sharper the more clearly one's own hand made the difference and the more easily the better branch comes to mind.
The direction is comparison, not mere loss: the outcome is weighed not against nothing but against the might-have-been, and the gap between what is and what could have been, had one chosen otherwise, is the wound.

## Lever

The comparison of the chosen outcome against an imagined better one the persona could have had, spent as the wish to have chosen otherwise.
Regret runs on counterfactual thought: the mind, faced with a bad result of its own making, simulates the alternative it did not take, and the closer and easier that simulation, the heavier the regret -- the near miss worse than the far one, the abnormal choice worse than the routine, because each makes the better branch more vivid.
The lever is agency crossed with the counterfactual: because the persona could have chosen the branch they can now so plainly see, the outcome is not just bad but self-authored and avoidable, and that is what regret, uniquely, weighs.

## Echo

The persona holds their own choice against the better one they can imagine, and wishes it undone.
Regret is why the near miss stings more than the clear loss, why "if only I had" outweighs "if only it had," why a decision's cost is counted twice -- once in what happened and once against what might have -- the emotion that makes choosers of us by making us answer for our choices.
It runs as [commission](process_commission.md), [inaction](process_inaction.md), or [anticipation](process_anticipation.md) -- the deed done, the chance missed, or the choice shaped before the fact to forestall a regret foreseen. A bad outcome with no agency behind it is disappointment, not regret; a moral wrong done to another is the guilt engine's; a loss of an attachment mourned is the grief engine's; a global defect of the self exposed is the shame engine's. This engine owns the agency-plus-counterfactual sting of a choice one wishes one had made differently.
