# Mind-Wandering: Reference

## Line of Work

Mind-wandering as a **process** engine: self-generated, task-unrelated thought that decouples attention from the world and carries it inward -- the drift away from the task -- taken across the three modes the drift shows in. The domain does not model the allocation of the attentional spotlight, the selecting of what to attend, which is the attention engine's; nor the repetitive, passive, negative circling that will not resolve, which is the rumination engine's; nor the aversive state of an unengaged mind seeking a target, which is the boredom engine's; nor the total absorption in a task, which is the flow engine's. It models the self-generated drift itself -- its onset off the task, its perceptual decoupling from the world, and whether it is meant and whether it is noticed.

The spine is Smallwood and Schooler's science of mind-wandering. They define it as self-generated thought that is unrelated to the current task and independent of the immediate environment, and identify perceptual decoupling as its signature mechanism: as attention turns to the inner train, processing of the outer world is attenuated, so the eyes rest on the page without reading it. They frame episodes along a small set of dimensions -- how the drift begins (spontaneous versus deliberate), whether the persona is meta-aware of it (Schooler's tuning out, aware, versus zoning out, unaware), and its content (a marked prospective bias toward the future and the self). Seli and colleagues argue intentionality is a central and often-neglected dimension, distinguishing spontaneous from deliberate mind-wandering with different correlates. Killingsworth and Gilbert, sampling experience in daily life, found that people's minds wander a large fraction of the time and that a wandering mind is on average a less happy one -- the affective cost -- set against the documented benefits of autobiographical planning, creative incubation, and relief from monotony. The engine takes the definition and decoupling as its root and the intention-and-awareness modes as its forms. The altitudes are those: the self-generated drift, and its unbidden, zoning-out, and tuning-out modes.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The allocation of attention (attention)**: The engine models the drift away from the task, not the selecting toward it. How a persona allocates a limited spotlight across a field -- selective, divided, sustained, captured, orienting -- is owned by the attention engine; mind-wandering is what happens when that spotlight leaves the task not for another external target but for a self-generated inner train, decoupling from the world. Attention selects among what the world offers; mind-wandering supplies its own object and drifts off. The two are complementary: the wandering is a lapse of the sustained attention the other engine holds.
- **The sticky negative circling (rumination)**: The engine models free-ranging self-generated thought, not the repetitive dwelling on distress. Rumination -- the passive, repetitive circling on one's own suffering and its causes, which will not resolve and stays negative -- is owned by the rumination engine; mind-wandering ranges freely, often to neutral or future or pleasant content, and is not defined by distress or repetition. Rumination is a stuck, dark, narrow current; mind-wandering is a wide and roving one. A drift that locks onto distress and circles is rumination's, not this engine's.
- **The aversive unengaged state (boredom)**: The engine models where the unengaged mind goes, not the aversive signal of being unengaged. Boredom -- the uncomfortable state of a mobilized attention that the situation will not satisfy -- is owned by the boredom engine; mind-wandering is the drift that boredom can trigger and that can relieve it, but the aversive wanting-a-target is boredom's and the inner travelling is this engine's. Boredom is the itch; mind-wandering is the scratching.
- **The total task absorption (flow)**: The engine models decoupling from the task, not full coupling to it. Flow -- the state of complete absorption in a demanding, well-matched task, where self and time fall away into the activity -- is owned by the flow engine; mind-wandering is its near-opposite, attention leaving the task rather than fusing with it, and a fully engaging task is exactly the condition under which the mind does not wander. Flow holds the mind on; mind-wandering lets it off.
