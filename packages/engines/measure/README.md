# Measure

Measure as process: the operation of turning a judgement into a number, and everything that follows from having done so -- not the general equivalent that converts anything into anything, which is money's, and not the readable image of a place, which is legibility's -- across four movements: the cut (commensuration, which produces a relation between things that had none by suppressing everything that would have prevented it, leaving a residue that is not recorded as a caveat but as nothing), the warrant (why a number rather than a judgement: distrust, travel, and answerability), the turn (Goodhart's collapse, the reactivity of institutions rebuilding around an index they do not endorse, and the self-fulfilment of a measure that produces what it claimed to report), and the territory (Scott's simplification, the imposed grid, and the practical knowledge that has no column) -- with the twist that a number is trusted in proportion to how little the people producing it are trusted, so the arrival of metrics is a symptom of collapsed confidence rather than a gain in rigour, and cannot be undone by competence.
