# Process: The Imposed Grid

## Taxonomy

[The Territory](process_territory_measure.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: measure

## Initiated by

The plan is laid down and the ground is made to take it.
The Imposed Grid starts where the abstraction stops describing and starts prescribing: the forest replanted in rows, the plots squared, the village resettled along a road, the schedule that the work must now fit.

## Direction

Toward a world that matches its own record.
This is the point Scott's argument turns on, and it is empirical rather than rhetorical: the simplification does not merely fail to capture the ground -- it succeeds, because the ground is rebuilt.
Afterwards the map is accurate, and its accuracy is evidence of nothing.

## Lever

The efficiency of a designed order against the accumulated fit it replaces.
The carrying force is that designed order frequently works and works spectacularly: the grid yields more per hectare, the resettlement gets people water and schooling, and the old arrangement was not a wise equilibrium in every case -- much of it was simply what nobody had got round to changing; the resistance is that the old arrangement was adapted to conditions nobody had written down, and the grid replaces all of it at once, so its failures arrive together and late, when the knowledge that would have caught them has already gone.
The Imposed Grid succeeds first and fails all at once.

## Echo

A landscape in rows, and a generation before anybody knows whether it worked.
It shows the map winning, which is the movement's whole claim.
The Imposed Grid is dated from the plan and audited by the third harvest.
