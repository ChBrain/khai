# Interpreting: Reference

## Line of Work

Interpreting as **process**: a conversation conducted through a third person, and the work that keeps it running.

The engine's subject is the only conversation in khai with three people in it. Every talk engine the project
already carries is a dyad: two parties establishing that they understood each other is **grounding**, the
trouble and its mending is **repair**, converging on the other's way of speaking is **accommodation**, what a
saying performs and conveys is **speech-act**'s and **implicature**'s, and the specialized variety and the
moving between varieties is **register**'s. None of them has a third body in the room. That is what this
engine is for.

Wadensjö is the spine. Working from recorded Swedish-Russian medical and immigration encounters, she
separates what an interpreter says into two kinds: **relaying**, which renders what somebody else said, and
**coordinating**, which manages the exchange itself -- who speaks next, whether a turn is finished, when to
pause, what a question meant. Both are constant. Neither is optional. Only one of them is what anybody,
including the interpreter, calls the job. Angelelli extends the observation: asked to describe their role,
interpreters overwhelmingly report being invisible -- a channel, a conduit, a voice -- and watched at work,
the same interpreters coordinate continuously. Goffman supplies the frame underneath both, since a
participation framework distinguishes the one who animates an utterance from the one whose words they are,
and the interpreter is an animator for two principals at once.

**The summoning** brings in the person the conversation needs and marks the party who needed them. The
professional booking is the good version and is rationed on the institution's schedule and budget. The
pressing -- a relative, a member of staff, a child -- is how most interpreting in the world actually
happens, and it puts somebody with a stake and no protection inside the encounter. The waiver is the party
who declines an interpreter to avoid being described as the one who needs one, which is a reasonable act
that costs them the conditional clause.

**The relaying** produces an utterance nobody in the room can check: the speaker does not know what was
produced, the listener does not know what was said. Compression is necessary and systematic rather than
random, and what it drops -- hedges, self-corrections, repetitions, colouring -- is what a listener judges
honesty by. First-person voicing is good craft that also manufactures the interpreter's absence, sentence by
sentence. An untranslatable term forces a choice between the word and the work the word was doing, and no
code decides it.

**The coordinating** is roughly half of it and is the one thing the role formally excludes. Turns are held
and sometimes lost. Every side check buys accuracy with a few seconds of exclusion, in both directions,
because holding the check in public would be the rendering. The tempo of the whole encounter is set by the
working memory of the participant who is not in it, and both parties attribute it to each other.

**The invisibility** is the norm that protects the exchange by refusing to describe it. The conduit code is
a good rule -- it is why a damaging answer crosses intact, and it is the protection between a vulnerable
person and an interpreter who has decided what is best for them -- and it describes a job nobody has. The
consequences run downhill: when something goes wrong the error lands on the interpreter, because the
interpreter is the only participant whose contribution can be held against a written standard, and when the
room empties one person carries the whole conversation out with nowhere to put it.

Hence the **twist**, in the root's Echo, carried by the coordinating. **The room requires the interpreter to
add nothing, and what they add is the only reason the conversation runs.** The additions are not lapses.
They are the sequencing, the pacing, the checks, and the choices without which two people blind to each
other's turn-endings produce a collision every second turn. They are made continuously by the one
participant who is not supposed to be making any, and they are the first thing denied when anybody asks what
happened.

The engine is built so that nobody has to be failing at their job for it to run, and a play should keep it
that way. The interpreter is skilled. The official is not obstructive. The party who waived was making a
reasonable decision about themselves.

**On the evidence, stated plainly.** **Wadensjö's finding is close observation of a small corpus.** The
relaying-and-coordinating split comes from detailed transcript work on a limited number of Swedish-Russian
encounters in two institutional settings, which is the right method for the question and is not a survey;
the proportion of interpreter talk that is coordination is a description of those encounters, and the
engine's "roughly half" should be read as an order of magnitude rather than a measured constant.
**Angelelli's invisibility result compares self-report against observation**, and there is more than one way
to read the gap: interpreters may be blind to what they do, or they may simply have no professional
vocabulary for it, since the codes supply none, and the second reading is at least as likely as the first.
**The engine's evidence base is institutional and Western.** Medical, legal, and asylum interpreting in
Europe and North America is where nearly all of this work has been done. Conference interpreting has a
different structure and a different politics, and signed language interpreting has both a substantial
literature of its own and a debate about the interpreter's role -- conduit, helper, ally -- that is older and
sharper than this engine reflects; a reader should not take the four movements as covering it. **The finding is the engine's own extension.** That an encounter's ambiguity converges on the one
participant with an auditable standard is an inference drawn here, it fits what practitioners report, and
nobody has measured it. **One well-known finding is deliberately not the engine's spine.** Flores and
colleagues showed that ad hoc interpreters make substantially more clinically consequential errors than
trained ones, which is real, important, and the strongest available argument for paying for professionals.
It is left out of the mechanism on purpose, because building the engine on it turns the pressing into a
story about incompetence, and the engine's claim is about position rather than skill: the child who
interprets perfectly is still conducting their family's business in front of their family. What should not
be taken from this engine is that interpreters are secretly steering conversations, that the conduit norm is
a fiction to be discarded, or that the arrangement fails. It works, most of the time, remarkably well. The
claim is narrow: half of what makes it work has no name in the room.

## Origin

The interactional study of interpreted encounters, wired as a process on a persona. The twist follows from
Wadensjö's separation of relaying from coordinating and Angelelli's account of what interpreters say they do.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The dyad's own machinery (grounding, repair, accommodation)**: Establishing that two people understood
  each other, locating and mending trouble, and converging on the other's way of speaking are those
  engines'. This engine adds the third participant and reads what that participant does.
- **What a saying carries (speech-act, implicature, tone, the utterance composite)**: The act an utterance
  performs, what it conveys without stating, and the key it is said in are those packages'. Interpreting
  reads what happens to an utterance in transit, not what it was.
- **Varieties and the moving between them (register, legibility)**: The specialized variety that gates who
  can follow, and translation between varieties of one tongue, are register's. This engine is two languages
  and a third body.
- **Whether somebody is believed (credibility, face, backstage)**: The assessment of a speaker's account,
  the standing to be taken at one's word, and the presentation kept up in front of others are those
  engines'. Interpreting stops at what arrived.
- **The institution around the encounter (total-institution, the world, org)**: Clinic scheduling,
  procurement of language services, court rules, and asylum procedure are matters of practice and statute.
  They set the format; this engine reads what the arrangement does to the three people in it.
- **What the interpreter carries away (compassion, upkeep, vigil)**: The occupational cost of
  repeated exposure and the maintenance of a person under it are those packages'. The retention names the
  hand-off and stops there.
- **An interpreted scene as authored symbol (the play's pitch)**: A mistranslation staged in order to
  **stand for** a theme -- the misrendering that mirrors a motif of estrangement -- is refused: that is
  authored key, the play's pitch. Interpreting sets what crossed and what did not; the meaning is the
  play's.

## Encoding

Source to constraint. A process engine: one root, four movements, three forms each.

- **[interpreting](process_interpreting.md)** (the root): a conversation conducted through a third person,
  carrying the twist -- the room requires the interpreter to add nothing, and what they add is the only
  reason the conversation runs. Anchored by Wadensjö (1998).
- **[the summoning](process_the_summoning.md)**: how the third person enters, and on whose terms. Anchored
  by Flores (2005) and Orellana (2009).
  - **[the booking](process_the_booking.md)**: the professional, competent and rationed.
  - **[the pressing](process_the_pressing.md)**: the nearest bilingual person, including the child.
  - **[the waiver](process_the_waiver.md)**: the party who declines, and what falls out.
- **[the relaying](process_the_relaying.md)**: what crosses, and why nobody present can check it. Anchored
  by Wadensjö (1998).
  - **[the compression](process_the_compression.md)**: the cut that keeps content and drops manner.
  - **[the voicing](process_the_voicing.md)**: the first person, lent and taken. Goffman (1981) behind it.
  - **[the untranslatable](process_the_untranslatable.md)**: the word against the work it was doing.
- **[the coordinating](process_the_coordinating.md)**: the management of the exchange, which carries the
  twist. Anchored by Wadensjö (1998).
  - **[the turn holding](process_the_turn_holding.md)**: traffic control described as punctuation.
  - **[the side talk](process_the_side_talk.md)**: accuracy bought with exclusion.
  - **[the pacing](process_the_pacing.md)**: a tempo set by the participant who is not in it.
- **[the invisibility](process_the_invisibility.md)**: the norm that protects by refusing to describe.

  - **[the conduit norm](process_the_conduit_norm.md)**: a good rule for a job nobody has.
  - **[the finding](process_the_finding.md)**: the room's errors at the only address with a standard.
  - **[the retention](process_the_retention.md)**: the whole conversation, carried out by nobody.
