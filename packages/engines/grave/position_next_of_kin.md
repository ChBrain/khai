# Position: Next of Kin

## Taxonomy

[Grave](piece_grave.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: grave

## Has

The right, the invoice, and the phone number on the file.
One person is recorded as holding the burial right, which is to say the standing to decide anything about the plot, the obligation to pay for it, and the call when the stone is judged unsafe. There is rarely a discussion about who this will be; it lands on whoever handled the arrangements, which is usually whoever was nearest.
They also hold the spaces not yet used, which is a strange thing to own: a place where they themselves may go, already bought, already located.

## Orders

Who else may be put in, whose name is added to the stone, what the inscription says, and whether the term is renewed.
The powers are small and absolute. A sibling who wants a different wording has no standing; a cousin who wants to be buried alongside needs permission; a family that has divided cannot divide the plot.
So the position quietly makes one relative the authority on how a shared loss is presented, and the others the people who have to live with it.

## Loses

The privacy of their own mourning, and eventually the right itself.
Everything they do or fail to do at the plot is legible to anybody who walks past, including relatives who will draw conclusions from the state of it. Grief that would otherwise be nobody's business is on display in the form of groundskeeping.
And the holding is not permanent: the term expires, the renewal notice goes to an address from twenty years ago, and the position ends without anybody being told it has.

## Drives

To keep it decent, and not to be the one who let it go.
The motive is rarely about the dead person and is usually about the standard: what the plot looks like against its neighbours, what an aunt would say, what it would mean about the family if the lettering went green.
The Next of Kin is doing maintenance on a relationship in the only currency the relationship still accepts.
