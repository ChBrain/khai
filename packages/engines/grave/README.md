# Grave

Grave as piece: the marked place of the dead, held on a term by the living and read by anybody who walks past -- not the internal bond a mourner carries forward, which is grief's, and not the rite that puts a body in the ground, which is ritual's. The object is the piece; the members are what happens around it. One position names who holds it: a next of kin with the burial right, the invoice, and the standing to decide the wording, who loses the privacy of their own mourning because everything they do at the plot is legible, and whose holding ends when a renewal notice reaches an address twenty years out of date. Three processes name what it does: the burying, which fixes an address in a fortnight and binds it for a century, taken by exhausted people on criteria that look arbitrary long before they stop binding; the visiting, which carries the bond in the only material it has left, since the tending is what makes the visit possible and the flowers die on a schedule; and the lapsing, an ending assembled out of individually innocent weeks that the object records only as a sum. The twist: a grave is the only part of a death anybody can still maintain, which is why it is the part the living are judged on.
