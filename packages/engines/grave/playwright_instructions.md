# Instructions: Grave

How a Playwright wires the grave engine into a play. You wire by linking the play's own content to the engine's files; the engine files stay untouched, they are canon. The Roadie plumbs the engine's law into Instructions Knowledge on deploy. Authoring guidance, not runtime content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human gives the visit something to do: the water, the secateurs, the cloth, the neighbouring plot that has been let go, because a persona who arrives with nothing to do stands at a stone for four minutes and leaves. Keep the lapsing blameless -- nobody neglected it and nobody decided to stop. And decide who holds the right, since that is one relative with small absolute powers and the others living with the result.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, a persona links under Projection where they stand to the plot: whether they hold the burial right, how far they live from it, when they last went, what they take when they go, and what they think the state of it says about them. Read the engine on what the survivors do with the object, never on what the dead person wanted. Keep it the plot: the bond carried forward internally is grief's, the rite that put the body in the ground is ritual's, the object withheld to carry a lineage is heirloom's. You link to the engine, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- The adaptation to loss and the bond carried forward are grief's, memory's, and the lasting composite's; the funeral and the committal are ritual's; the object withheld to carry a lineage, the possession a self has extended into, and the article a collective lodges its we in are heirloom's, extended-self's, totem's, and the investiture composite's; the death certificate, the burial register, and the state's index of who is where are document's, legibility's, and register's; the shared ground and who pays to keep it are the upkeep composite's and commons's; the war memorial, the roadside shrine, and the bench with a plaque are addressed to a public and are not this. Plot pricing, masonry, cemetery regulation, and statutory reuse are the trade and set the format. Whether a production's plot has been let go or is being kept by somebody who cannot afford the travel, and whether an inscription was agreed or imposed, are the team's review.

## Knowledge

- A grave is the marked place of the dead, held on a term by the living and read by anybody who walks past. It is not grief's internal bond, not ritual's rite, not heirloom's object withheld to carry a lineage, and not document's institutional record of a person. It wires on a persona at Projection. The object is somewhere else by design: managed ground at the edge of the settlement, bought as a right of burial for a term rather than as earth, which makes it the only property most households hold that they pay for, visit, maintain, and can neither occupy nor sell. What it holds up is that this person belonged to somebody, since a body in unmarked ground has been disposed of and a body under a name has been kept; what shows is a name, two dates, a line priced by the letter, and a container of flowers, and passers-by read the plot before they read the stone. One position and three processes hang beneath it. The next of kin holds the right, the invoice, and small absolute powers over how a shared loss is presented, and loses the privacy of their own mourning, because grief that would otherwise be nobody's business is on display in the form of groundskeeping. The burying fixes an address at the worst possible moment for choosing one, on cost and on what was available that Thursday, and it cannot afterwards be revised. The visiting is mostly work -- weeding, scrubbing, refilling, talking aloud -- and the tasks are what makes the visit possible, which is real consolation delivered by a chore that reissues itself. The lapsing is an ending arrived at by a sequence of reasonable omissions that the object records only as a sum. The engine's twist is in the anchor: a grave is the only part of a death anybody can still maintain, which is why it is the part the living are judged on.

## System

- Do give the visit tasks. The water, the secateurs, the cloth, the refilling -- the doing is what the visit is made of, and the standing still is the part nobody can sustain.
- Do let somebody talk aloud at the plot while working. It is ordinary, it is not staged as strange, and it is done in the middle of weeding rather than in a silence.
- Do fix the address under duress. The section, the depth, the number of spaces and the term are chosen in a fortnight by exhausted people on cost and availability, and they bind for decades.
- Do put the right in one person's hands. A sibling with a different view of the wording has no standing, and that is the ordinary situation rather than a grievance.
- Do let the state of the plot be read. Somebody notices the lettering, the weeds, the neighbouring grave that is immaculate, and draws a conclusion about a family.
- Do keep the lapsing innocent. Each missed visit had a reason, the distance is real, and the sum is what the object records.
- Do let the object outlast the attention. The term expires, the notice goes to an old address, the stone is laid flat by a groundsman who never heard of anybody in this story.
- Do not model the mourning (grief, memory, lasting), the rite (ritual), the object carrying a lineage or a we (heirloom, extended-self, totem, investiture), the institutional record (document, legibility, register), the shared ground and its upkeep (upkeep, commons), or the public memorial. Do not make the plot stand for a theme; that is authored key, the play's pitch. Do not edit the engine's files; wire only from the play's side.
