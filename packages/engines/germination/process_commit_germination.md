# Process: The Commit

## Taxonomy

[Germination](process_germination.md), the point past which there is no drying back, and where the engine is seen for one thing.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: germination

## Initiated by

Water goes in and does not come out again.
The Commit is what follows: [imbibition](process_the_imbibition.md) past the point of return, [one shot](process_the_one_shot.md) at the surface, and [the after](process_the_after.md), which is no longer a seed.

## Direction

Toward an irreversible act taken on a proxy.
Water uptake runs in phases. The first is physical and reversible: a dry seed wetted and dried again is still a dry seed. The second is a plateau during which the machinery restarts. The third begins when the root breaks the coat, and from that moment desiccation is death rather than a return to storage.
Nothing about the cue guaranteed that the season would hold. The seed committed on a correlate, and the correlate is not answerable for what happens next.

## Lever

The necessity of committing at all against the impossibility of committing safely.
The carrying force is that the commitment has to be made and a seed that never made it would be no better off than one that rotted: the cues are good, the timing is usually right, the species has been doing this for as long as it has existed, and the overwhelming majority of germinations in a normal year succeed at least as far as the surface; the resistance is that the decision is one-way and is made on evidence about the present, so a false spring, a plough turning the soil in autumn, or a dry fortnight after a wet week takes the seed with no route back to the state that was keeping it alive.
The Commit rides the engine's keystone knife-edge: a seed spends nearly all of its life refusing to grow, and the one time it agrees there is no way back.

## Echo

A root through a coat, in whatever season it turns out to be.
It shows the three earlier movements complete -- a pack provisioned once, a hold that refused for years, a cue that certified something adjacent -- and adds the one act none of them can protect.
This is why the Commit is the hardest of the four to stage and the only one worth closing on: nothing has malfunctioned, the cue was read correctly, the seed did exactly what it is built to do, and the audience must be able to watch a well-designed decision be unrecoverable.
A play that stages the germination as a failure has replaced the mechanism with an accident, and one that stages the seed as choosing has replaced it with a fable.
The Commit is dated to the hour the root emerges and cannot be undone.
