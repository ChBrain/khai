# Position: The Repeat Player

## Taxonomy

[By the Gap](position_gap_law.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: law

## Has

Galanter's asymmetry: a party who comes to the system often is differently situated from one who comes once.
The repeat player has expertise, established relationships with the officials, economies of scale on legal cost, and -- decisively -- a stake in the rule rather than in this case, so they can trade an outcome now for a precedent later.
The one-shot party has only this case, and must take what settles it.

## Orders

Play for the rules; settle the cases you would lose.
The repeat player's advantage is not that they win more often but that they can choose which cases are decided, conceding the bad facts and litigating the good ones, so the body of decided law is built from a selected sample.

## Loses

Any presumption that its decided law is representative.
Because the cases that reach judgment are chosen by the party with the long horizon, a legal order's accumulated precedent drifts toward the interests of whoever appears in it repeatedly -- without a single wrong decision being made.
The haves come out ahead through the system working exactly as designed.

## Drives

On the loss of representativeness:
builds counterweights -- class actions, public interest bodies, legal aid, agencies that are themselves repeat players on the other side -- and accepts that this is expensive and permanently contested,
or leaves the asymmetry, and accumulates a precedent that is fair in every case and slanted in aggregate.
