# Position: Law

## Taxonomy

Polity (no khai file of its own yet).

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: law

## Has

A rule system that claims to bind, and a persona living inside it.
Law is not a norm and not a command. A norm binds because it is shared; a command binds because somebody with force behind them issued it; a law binds because **another rule says it does** -- and that second rule is the thing that makes a legal order different in kind from a strong custom or a standing threat.
It is read on four axes: by the [making](position_making_law.md), what turns a rule into law and keeps it one; by the [failing](position_failing_law.md), the eight distinct ways a rule system stops being law without anybody repealing anything; by the [standing](position_standing_law.md), the stance a persona takes toward legality itself; and by the [gap](position_gap_law.md), the distance between the rule as declared and the rule as it actually operates on somebody.
Every legal order has a reading on all four at once, and the fourth is usually the one a persona meets first.

## Orders

Ask what makes this rule a rule, and then ask who it reaches.
The first question is Hart's and it is not a formality: a rule nobody can trace to an authorising rule is somebody's preference with consequences attached, and a persona who cannot tell the two apart cannot tell obedience from submission.
The second question is the one the first cannot answer. A rule identical on the page operates differently on a persona who has met it before, who can afford to contest it, or whom the official in front of them has decided to overlook.

## Loses

The power to forbid something without also pricing it.
A law is enforceable only if the consequence of breaking it is determinate, and a determinate consequence is a tariff: the clearer a rule makes what breaking it costs, the more plainly it publishes the terms on which breaking it is available for purchase.
Gneezy and Rustichini's daycare is the demonstration and it is worse than a paradox. Introducing a fine for collecting a child late made lateness **more** common, not less, because a moral obligation to the staff had been converted into a transaction with the institution -- and when the fine was withdrawn the lateness stayed high, because the obligation did not convert back.
So the conversion runs one way. A legal order can turn a wrong into a price at any time, cannot turn a price back into a wrong, and cannot enforce anything at all without doing some of the converting.

## Drives

On the loss of forbidding without pricing:
leaves the consequence indeterminate where the wrong is one it needs a persona to feel rather than calculate -- unspecified, discretionary, disgraceful -- and pays in every arbitrariness that vagueness licenses,
or names the price precisely, gets the predictability and the fairness that naming it buys, and finds it has quietly opened a counter where the thing it meant to forbid can be bought.
