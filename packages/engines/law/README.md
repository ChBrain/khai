# Law

Law as position: legality as the standing condition a persona's conduct is judged under -- not a norm, which binds by being shared, and not a command, which binds by being issued, but a rule that binds because another rule says it does -- read on four axes: the making (Hart's primary rules of obligation and the secondary rules of recognition, change, and adjudication that turn a set of customs into a legal order), the failing (Fuller's eight distinct routes by which a rule system stops being law without anybody repealing anything), the standing (Ewick & Silbey's three stances an ordinary persona takes toward legality -- before it, with it, against it -- held simultaneously and without discomfort), and the gap (Pound's law in books against law in action, where almost all of a persona's actual experience of law happens) -- with the twist that no law can forbid without also pricing, and the conversion runs only one way (Hart; Fuller; Ewick & Silbey; Pound; Lipsky; Galanter; Merry; Gneezy & Rustichini).
