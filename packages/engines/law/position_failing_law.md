# Position: By the Failing

## Taxonomy

[Law](position_law.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: law

## Has

Fuller's eight distinct routes to failure: the ways a rule system stops being law without anybody repealing anything.
They are not eight kinds of bad law. They are eight kinds of not-law, and a system that goes far enough down any one of them has not made unjust law -- it has failed to make law at all, because there is nothing a persona could do to comply.
The eight: [no rules at all](position_no_rules.md), the [unpublished](position_unpublished_rule.md), the [retroactive](position_retroactive_rule.md), the [unintelligible](position_unintelligible_rule.md), the [contradictory](position_contradictory_rule.md), the [impossible](position_impossible_rule.md), the [unstable](position_unstable_rule.md), and the rule [administered otherwise](position_administered_otherwise.md) than it is declared.

## Orders

Test a rule by asking whether a persona of goodwill could comply with it.
This is the axis's whole method, and it does the work without any appeal to whether the rule is just: a persona who wants to obey and cannot -- because the rule is secret, or unintelligible, or contradicted by another, or was changed after they acted -- is not being governed by law, whatever the rule says.

## Loses

Its own claim to be about morality at all.
Fuller called these the inner morality of law and the objection has never been fully answered: an efficient tyranny can satisfy all eight -- clear, published, prospective, stable, consistently administered rules for wicked ends -- so the eight measure whether a persona is being governed by rules rather than whether the rules are worth obeying.
What they buy is real but narrow: a persona who knows the rule in advance can plan around it, and a regime that must announce what it will do in advance is constrained by having announced it. That constraint is procedural, and a determined regime can meet it and still be monstrous.

## Drives

On the loss of a moral claim:
holds the eight as what they are -- the conditions under which governing by rules is even possible -- and argues the justice of the rules separately,
or overreads them as a guarantee of decency, and is disarmed by the first regime that keeps its paperwork immaculate.
