# Position: The Rule of Adjudication

## Taxonomy

[By the Making](position_making_law.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: law

## Has

The rule that says who decides whether a rule was broken, and by what process.
It confers jurisdiction, which is a stranger power than it sounds: not the power to be right about the breach but the power to say what the breach was, with that saying counting.
Hart's cure for inefficiency: without it, every alleged violation is settled by whoever can outlast or overpower the other party.

## Orders

Send the question to the body the rule names, and treat its answer as the answer.
The finality is doing the work, not the accuracy -- a legal order needs disputes to end more than it needs them to end correctly, which is why appeal is bounded and why a wrong final judgment is still a judgment.

## Loses

Any guarantee that the decision is right.
Jurisdiction and correctness are separate properties, and the system can only enforce the first. A court with authority to decide has authority to decide wrongly, and the order has no mechanism that distinguishes a settled question from a well-answered one.
A persona who was in fact in the right and lost is, from the system's side, indistinguishable from one who was wrong.

## Drives

On the loss of guaranteed rightness:
builds review, reasons, and precedent to make error visible and correctable at the margin, and accepts that this only moves the finality further up,
or leans on the finality itself -- the matter is closed because it is closed -- and gets speed and certainty by declining to ask whether it got it right.
