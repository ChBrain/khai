# Instructions: Law

How a Playwright wires the law engine into a play. You wire by linking the play's own content to the engine's files; the engine files stay untouched, they are canon. The Roadie plumbs the engine's law into Instructions Knowledge on deploy. Authoring guidance, not runtime content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human sets what legality the play happens under, and remembers it is a condition rather than a character trait: the rule reaching a persona is not their belief about rules. Start with the gap, since that is where a persona actually meets law -- who applies it, to whom, how often, with what latitude. Add the failing axis where a play turns on a rule nobody could comply with, and the making axis where it turns on whether something counts as law at all.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, a persona links under Projection the standing they take toward law and whichever gap form is operating on them. Expect the standing to move inside the play: reverence in the abstract, fluency in their own dispute, evasion where something they will not give up is at stake -- the same persona, the same week, no discomfort. Keep it the rule that binds because another rule says so, not the shared norm (tightness-looseness), the peer sanction (altruistic-punishment), or the command obeyed (obedience). You link to the engine, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- Who rules is the regime engine's and the conduct of deciding is politics' -- regime is the arrangement, law is what the deciding produces and how it reaches a persona. Why a persona complies is obedience's; the training that needs no stated rule is discipline's; the strength of a culture's norms is tightness-looseness'; costly peer sanction is altruistic-punishment's; desert and the moral ledgers are deserving's, condemnation's, and moral-account's; the record's authority is speech-act's, document's, and writ's; what being governed makes of a persona is subjection's. Whether a production's rule is genuinely a dead letter or merely unenforced so far, and whether a persona is standing before the law or working it, are the team's review.

## Knowledge

- Law is the legality a persona's conduct is judged under: a rule system that claims to bind them, which they did not consent to and cannot individually revise. It is not a norm and not a command -- a norm binds because it is shared, a command because somebody with force issued it, a law because another rule says it does -- and that second rule is what separates a legal order from a strong custom or a standing threat. It wires on a persona at Projection, and it is read on four axes. The making is Hart's: primary rules of obligation united with secondary rules about them -- recognition, which says which rules count; change, which says how they may be altered; adjudication, which says who decides whether one was broken -- with the rule of recognition resting on nothing but the convergent practice of officials, since there is nothing above it to validate it. The failing is Fuller's eight routes to not-law, tested by asking whether a persona of goodwill could comply: no rules at all, unpublished, retroactive, unintelligible, contradictory, impossible, unstable, and administered otherwise than declared -- the last of which swallows the other seven, since a defective rule administered sensibly can still govern and a perfect rule administered otherwise cannot. The standing is Ewick and Silbey's three stances, held contradictorily and at once: before the law, majestic and remote, respected and not approached; with the law, a game to be played by whoever can afford to; against the law, an arbitrary power to be evaded, joked about, and worked around. The gap is Pound's law in books against law in action, in five standing forms -- discretion, selective enforcement, the dead letter, the repeat player, legal pluralism -- and it is where almost all of a persona's actual experience of law happens. The engine's twist is at the root: a law is enforceable only if the consequence is determinate, and a determinate consequence is a tariff, so no law can forbid without also pricing -- and the conversion runs one way, since a wrong can be made a price at any time and a price cannot be made a wrong again.

## System

- Do put the pressure on the gap rather than on the text: amending the words moves the gap instead of closing it, which is why a reform in a play should disappoint its author and help whoever was already doing well.
- Do let a persona's standing change within the play, and do not treat the change as inconsistency or growth: the three standings are positions available to everybody, not stages of sophistication or types of person.
- Do read the failing axis by its own test -- could a persona of goodwill comply? -- and do not use it to mean the law is unjust; an efficient tyranny can satisfy all eight and still be monstrous, which the axis says of itself.
- Do notice where the failings converge: an impossible or contradictory rule produces universal breach, which is selective enforcement from the other end, and an unintelligible rule hands its content to whoever applies it, which is discretion arrived at by accident.
- Do read the root's twist before staging any penalty: a rule that names precisely what breaking it costs has published the terms on which it may be bought, and a persona who has been shown the price does not go back to seeing a wrong.
- Do not model who rules (regime), the conduct of deciding (politics), why a persona complies (obedience), the training that states no rule (discipline), norm strength (tightness-looseness), peer sanction (altruistic-punishment), desert and the moral ledgers (deserving, condemnation, moral-account), the record's authority (speech-act, document, writ), or what being governed makes of a persona (subjection). Do not make a trial or a statute stand for a theme; that is authored key, the play's pitch. Do not edit the engine's files; wire only from the play's side.
