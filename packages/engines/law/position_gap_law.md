# Position: By the Gap

## Taxonomy

[Law](position_law.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: law

## Has

Pound's distinction, and the whole tradition that came from it: law in books and law in action are two different objects, and only one of them ever reaches a persona.
The gap is not corruption and not failure. It is produced by ordinary features of any working legal order -- officials with caseloads and judgement, resources unevenly held, more than one normative order on the same ground -- and it is where almost all of a persona's actual experience of law happens.
Five of its standing forms: [discretion](position_discretion.md), [selective enforcement](position_selective_enforcement.md), the [dead letter](position_dead_letter.md), the [repeat player](position_repeat_player.md), and [legal pluralism](position_legal_pluralism.md).

## Orders

Ask what happens, not what is provided for.
The operative question about any rule is who applies it, to whom, how often, and with what latitude -- and the answers are found in practice, in caseloads, and in who can afford to contest, never in the text.

## Loses

The ability to reform itself by amendment.
Because the gap is produced by the conditions of application rather than by the words, changing the words moves it rather than closing it: a new rule arrives into the same caseloads, the same discretion, and the same distribution of who can litigate, and is absorbed by them.
Which is why legal reform so reliably disappoints the people who drafted it, and why the same reform helps the parties who were already doing well.

## Drives

On the loss of reform by amendment:
changes the conditions instead -- funding, staffing, representation, review, the cost of contesting -- and accepts that this is slower, duller, and much harder to claim credit for,
or amends the text again, announces the fix, and reproduces the gap in new language.
