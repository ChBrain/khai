# Position: The Rule of Recognition

## Taxonomy

[By the Making](position_making_law.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: law

## Has

The rule that says which rules count.
It is the criterion officials actually use to sort law from everything else -- enacted by this body, decided by that court, written in this text -- and it is almost never written down anywhere itself, because it is the thing every written thing is measured against.
Hart's cure for uncertainty: without it, a society has rules and no way to settle whether something is one.

## Orders

Look at what officials treat as binding, not at what anybody says the criterion is.
The rule of recognition is identified from practice: the test is which sources judges and administrators actually defer to when they disagree, which is frequently narrower and more parochial than the official account.

## Loses

The ability to be validated.
It is the one rule in the system that cannot be shown to be law, because there is nothing above it to do the showing -- it is not valid or invalid, it simply is or is not practised.
So a legal order cannot prove itself legitimate from the inside, and every attempt to do so either assumes the conclusion or steps outside law entirely.

## Drives

On the loss of validation:
rests on the practice openly, treating official convergence as the fact it is and defending it as one,
or dresses the practice as a discovery -- of what the constitution always meant, of what the founders intended -- and buys the appearance of a foundation with a permanent vulnerability to a better reader.
