# Position: Legal Pluralism

## Taxonomy

[By the Gap](position_gap_law.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: law

## Has

More than one normative order operating on the same ground.
State law alongside customary law, religious law, corporate rules, professional codes, a trade's own conventions, or a neighbourhood's settled way of handling things -- each with its own forums, sanctions, and account of what counts.
Merry's point is that this is the normal case rather than the exception, and Ellickson's cattle ranchers are the sharp version: neighbours settling substantial disputes by norms none of them could state, while the state law that formally governs sits unused a few miles away.

## Orders

Find the order a persona is actually in.
The question is never whether state law applies but which forum this persona will use, which sanction they actually fear, and which account of the wrong the people around them will accept.

## Loses

The power to be the only answer.
State law can override a competing order formally and still lose to it practically, because it cannot reach into the relationships that make the other order enforceable -- and a persona who invokes state law against their own order frequently pays more in standing than the claim was worth.
So a right can be perfectly valid and unusable by the person who holds it.

## Drives

On the loss of being the only answer:
recognises the other order -- incorporating custom, deferring to the forum, enforcing its outcomes -- and accepts a legality it does not control,
or asserts exclusivity, and drives the working order underground where it continues to operate and can no longer be seen.
