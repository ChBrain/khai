# Position: The Rule of Change

## Taxonomy

[By the Making](position_making_law.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: law

## Has

The rule that says how the rules may be altered.
It names who may legislate, by what procedure, and within what limits, and it is what makes a legal order capable of deliberate revision rather than only of drift.
Hart's cure for the static: a regime of primary rules alone can change only as slowly as custom does, which is to say not on purpose.

## Orders

Change the rule by the route the rule provides.
An alteration made outside the procedure is not a bad law but a non-law, and the distinction is the whole point: a system that will treat an irregular change as effective has, in that moment, no rule of change.

## Loses

The power to protect anything permanently.
Whatever the rule of change can reach, it can reach later -- so an entrenched right is only as entrenched as the next body's willingness to leave the entrenchment alone, and a rule that could not be altered at all would make the order static again in exactly the way the rule of change exists to cure.
A legal order can be revisable or it can be safe from revision. Not both.

## Drives

On the loss of permanence:
raises the cost of change for the things it most wants kept -- supermajorities, referendums, constitutional courts -- and accepts that it has made itself slower and its errors more durable,
or keeps change cheap, stays able to correct itself, and keeps nothing beyond the reach of whoever holds the procedure next.
