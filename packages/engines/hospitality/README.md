# Hospitality

Hospitality as process: the conversion of a stranger into a guest, and the terms the conversion carries -- not the transfer that binds by naming no price, which is gift's, and not the neutral ground where nobody is host or guest, which is third-place's -- across four movements: the taking in (a stranger is unplaceable and cannot be left so, and there are only two resolutions, guest or enemy, with the conversion performed by an offering rather than agreed in words), the terms (the host's duty to provide unstintingly and unpriced, the guest's duties which are almost all prohibitions protecting the fact that there is one authority here, and the precedence the guest must accept), the clock (the welcome, whose warmth is identical on the last good day and the first, the overstay that changes status without any visible signal, and the leaving that converts the visit into a lasting bond if the term was judged right), and the breach (harming a guest, acting as host, and turning somebody away) -- with the twist that hospitality honours the guest by demonstrating whose house it is, since only somebody who owns a place can extend it.
