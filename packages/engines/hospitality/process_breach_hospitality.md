# Process: The Breach

## Taxonomy

[Hospitality](process_hospitality.md), the violations, and why they are held so grave.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: hospitality

## Initiated by

Somebody has done the one thing that could not be done.
The Breach starts at any of three: [the host who harms a guest](process_host_betrayal.md), [the guest who acts as host](process_guest_usurpation.md), or [the door that does not open](process_the_turning_away.md).

## Direction

Toward a wrong treated as belonging to a different order from ordinary wrongs.
The severity is the striking thing and it is close to universal: societies with very different moralities converge on treating the betrayal of a guest, or by a guest, as among the gravest acts available -- worse than the same harm done to a stranger in the road, and frequently framed as an offence against something beyond the parties.
The reason is structural rather than sentimental. The arrangement runs entirely on the guest's undefended trust, so a breach does not damage a relation, it removes the possibility of the arrangement.

## Lever

The fragility of an unenforced agreement against how much depends on it.
The carrying force is that the gravity is proportionate: nothing enforces hospitality -- no contract, no court, and frequently no shared language -- so its whole strength is that it is unthinkable to break, and the horror attached to a breach is the enforcement mechanism doing its work; the resistance is that a rule held by horror alone cannot be argued with, refined, or applied in degrees, so it also protects the arrangement against every legitimate complaint the parties might have, and a guest with a real grievance has no register in which to raise it.
The Breach is where hospitality's strength and its unaccountability turn out to be the same property.

## Echo

An offence that the parties experience as larger than themselves.
It is the movement that explains the whole arrangement's durability, since a rule this unenforceable should not have survived and has.
The Breach is dated from the act, and it is remembered for generations.
