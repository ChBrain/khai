# Instructions: Hospitality

How a Playwright wires the hospitality engine into a play. You wire by linking the play's own content to the engine's files; the engine files stay untouched, they are canon. The Roadie plumbs the engine's law into Instructions Knowledge on deploy. Authoring guidance, not runtime content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human decides the term before the play opens and never tells the guest, because the clock is the engine's centre and it is unwritten by construction. Give both sides -- the engine is unreadable from one. And keep the host generous and the guest grateful, since the arrangement is at its most interesting when nobody is behaving badly and it goes wrong anyway.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, a persona links under Projection which side of the arrangement they are on and where in it: whether they have been converted, which duties bind them, where the clock stands, and whether anything has been breached. Read the engine on who may say when it ends, which is never the guest. Keep it the conversion and its terms: the transfer that binds by naming no price is gift's, the ground with no host and no guest is third-place's, the kept flame is hearth's. You link to the engine, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- The transfer that binds by naming no price is the gift engine's and the owing composite's; neutral ground where nobody is host or guest is third-place's; the kept flame of a household is hearth's; the person held by a place is the dwelling composite's; territory and personal distance are proxemics's and space's; facework and impression management are face's and presentation's; who counts as one of us is membership's and social-identity's; being made to wait at a door is waiting's. The commercial hospitality trade borrows the word and inverts the mechanism, and is not modelled here. Whether a production's welcome has lapsed, and whether a guest's helpfulness was a usurpation, are the team's review.

## Knowledge

- Hospitality is the conversion of a stranger into a guest and the terms that conversion carries. A stranger is a person with no place in the local order -- not kin, not neighbour, not enemy, not client -- and that condition is unaccountable rather than merely unclassified, so it cannot be left standing: there are two resolutions and only two, guest or threat, and the warmth at the door is the near face of a decision whose far face is hostility. It wires on a persona at Projection and runs in four movements. The taking in is performed rather than agreed: the crossing does the classifying, so the commitment is total and immediate and made on no information, and the offering seals it -- nearly always something ingested or received bodily, indivisible, so the guest cannot accept the shelter and decline the bond. The terms are asymmetric: the host must provide unstintingly and without visible calculation and must protect, sometimes against their own; the guest's duties are almost all prohibitions, and every one protects the fact that there is a single authority here, which is why a guest who is helpful in the wrong way breaches as surely as one who is rude; and the precedence is genuine honour that must be accepted, since only somebody with authority can raise another. The clock is the engine's centre: the welcome is sincere and bounded and those two facts are not visible together, so the guest can calibrate nothing from a manner that is exactly as warm on the last good day as on the first; the overstay changes their status with no visible signal, and the arrangement gives the host no legitimate way to end it, so the only instruments are indirect and a guest reading them correctly must pretend they did not; the leaving, taken in time, converts the visit into a bond that can be claimed by children. The breach explains the durability: harming a guest, acting as host, and turning somebody away are held apart from ordinary wrongs, because nothing enforces the arrangement and its whole strength is that breaking it is unthinkable. The engine's twist is at the root: hospitality honours the guest by demonstrating whose house it is -- to give is to have, so the guest is raised and subordinated in one motion, and the more lavish the host the more complete the demonstration.

## System

- Do set the term before anything else and keep it from everybody, including the host, who knows it only as a feeling.
- Do make the welcome sincere. The host is not counting, does mean the open invitation, and is not performing -- and the guest still cannot tell how long it lasts, which is the whole difficulty.
- Do stage the overstay as invisible: provision continues, honours continue, and what changes is underneath, with the guest the last to know because every visible signal is unchanged.
- Do not let the host ask the guest to leave. That is a breach rather than a clock, and it collapses the movement the engine exists for.
- Do put the twist in the seating. A guest given the best chair, the first serving, and the household's own portion has been honoured by an exercise of the authority they are being kept from -- and may not decline it without insulting the giver.
- Do let a usurpation be a kindness. Most are: the guest tidies, answers the door, offers a visitor a drink, and has claimed an authority they do not hold without meaning anything by it.
- Do keep the refusal free of villainy where you can. A household with nothing to give cannot be obliged to open, and the arrangement supplies no gentle form for saying so, which is why a refusal for the best reason arrives as the same act as one for the worst.
- Do not model the gift (gift, owing), neutral ground (third-place), the household flame (hearth), spacing (proxemics), facework (face, presentation), membership (membership, social-identity), or the commercial trade that shares the word. Do not make the welcome stand for a theme; that is authored key, the play's pitch. Do not edit the engine's files; wire only from the play's side.
