# Process: The Taking In

## Taxonomy

[Hospitality](process_hospitality.md), the conversion at the door.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: hospitality

## Initiated by

A stranger has arrived and cannot be left as one.
The Taking In starts at Pitt-Rivers' problem: [the stranger](process_the_stranger.md) is a person with no place in the local order, and the order has no way to hold them -- so they must be resolved, [across the door](process_the_crossing.md) and by [an offering](process_the_offering.md), into a guest.

## Direction

Toward a status conferred quickly and by act rather than by word.
The conversion is performed rather than agreed. Food, drink, a seat, water for the feet, a share of the fire -- accepting any of them is what makes a persona a guest, and the acceptance is what binds both parties.
Nothing is said, because saying it would make it a transaction.

## Lever

The necessity of resolving the stranger against the fact that only two resolutions exist.
The carrying force is that this is a real solution to a real problem and it is on the whole a merciful one: an unattached person in an unfamiliar place has no standing and no protection, hospitality supplies both immediately and at the host's cost, and societies that keep this arrangement are safer to travel through than societies that do not; the resistance is that the second resolution is always available -- a stranger who is not taken in is not neutral, they are a threat -- so the warmth at the door is the near face of a decision whose far face is hostility, and both parties know it.
The Taking In is generous and it is also a classification, made fast because the alternative is worse.

## Echo

A person who now has a place, conferred by having eaten.
It is the movement that makes the other three possible and the only one that could have gone the other way.
The Taking In is dated from the acceptance, and it is not revocable by either party at will.
