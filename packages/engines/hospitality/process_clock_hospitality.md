# Process: The Clock

## Taxonomy

[Hospitality](process_hospitality.md), the period it holds, and where the engine is seen for one thing.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: hospitality

## Initiated by

The arrangement has a term, and nobody has stated it.
The Clock starts running at the conversion: [the welcome](process_the_welcome.md) is the period in which the guest is honoured, [the overstay](process_the_overstay.md) is what happens past it, and [the leaving](process_the_leaving.md) is the departure that keeps the relation intact.

## Direction

Toward an expiry the guest cannot see and the host cannot announce.
The term is real and unwritten, varying enormously by culture and circumstance, and it is enforced by the one asymmetry that has been present all along: the host decides.
They cannot say so, because saying so would be a breach -- so the guest is left reading a clock they have no access to, and the host is left waiting for a departure they cannot request.

## Lever

The honour of an open welcome against the fact that it is not open.
The carrying force is that the term is what makes generosity possible: a household that had to feed and defend anybody indefinitely could not take anybody in, so the boundary is not meanness but the condition of the whole arrangement -- and the guest who leaves in good time is doing something for the host as much as for themselves; the resistance is that the boundary is unspeakable from both directions, so it can only be discovered by crossing it -- and a guest who has crossed it has changed status without being told, and will be the last to know.
The Clock rides the engine's keystone knife-edge: hospitality honours the guest by demonstrating whose house it is, and the term is where the demonstration becomes visible.

## Echo

A guest who is still being fed and is no longer a guest.
It shows the three other movements complete -- a conversion, a set of terms, and now their expiry -- and it is where the exaltation and the subordination separate, having been one thing until then.
This is why the Clock is the hardest of the four to stage and the only one worth closing on: the host must be genuinely generous, the guest must be genuinely grateful, nobody must be behaving badly, and the audience must be able to see the moment the welcome lapses while neither party can name it.
A play that has the host ask them to leave has staged a breach rather than a clock.
The Clock is dated from the acceptance, and it has never been shown to anybody.
