# Imagination: Reference

## Line of Work

Imagination as a **process** engine: the deliberate forming and manipulation of mental
representations of what is not present -- the voluntary making-present of the absent, taken
across the three modes of the imaginative act. It wires on a persona at Projection, the
persona shown by what it constructs in the mind, because a persona's imagining is read by
what it conjures, supposes, and simulates. The domain does not model the spontaneous,
task-unrelated drift of attention inward, which is the mind-wandering engine's; nor the
future self a persona hopes or dreads to become, which is possible-selves'; nor the
counterfactual undoing of the actual past, which is regret's; nor the reconstruction of what
did happen, which is memory's; nor the pretend stance a persona takes toward a real
activity, which is play-mode's. It models the constructive act itself -- the made
representation, held offline and worked.

The spine is the philosophy and cognitive science of imagination, which carves the faculty
along a small set of modes. Currie and Ravenscroft read imagining as recreation: the offline
running of the very capacities perception and belief use online, so that to imagine is to
recreate a perceptual or doxastic state without its usual cause and quarantined from action.
Kind and others distinguish the imagistic from the propositional -- imagining a sensory
scene (the mind's eye) from imagining that a proposition holds -- and Kosslyn's experiments
established the quasi-perceptual character of the first, the mind scanning and rotating its
own images as if seen. Nichols and Stich modelled the propositional mode as a workspace held
apart from the belief store, where a supposed premise is elaborated by the same inferential
machinery belief uses, without being believed. Schacter and Addis showed the constructive,
combinatorial mode -- assembling a novel scenario from stored elements and running it -- to
be the same machinery that reconstructs the past and pre-experiences the future. So the
engine gives imagination three modes -- conjuring, supposition, simulation -- carrying no
forced twist; one act of imagining may run in more than one at once.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The spontaneous drift inward (mind-wandering)**: The engine models deliberate
  construction, not unbidden drift. Self-generated, task-unrelated thought that decouples
  attention from the task and carries it inward on its own is the mind-wandering engine's;
  imagination is the voluntary act of constructing a representation on purpose. Mind-wandering
  is the drift that happens to a persona; imagination is the constructing the persona does.
  Daydream sits at the border -- when the drift is spontaneous it is mind-wandering's; when the
  persona takes up and works a scenario it is imagination's simulation.
- **The future self (possible-selves)**: The engine models the constructive act, not the
  particular content of a future self-image. What a persona hopes, dreads, expects, or has given
  up becoming -- the future-oriented self-representations -- is the possible-selves engine's;
  imagination is the faculty that constructs any non-present representation, of which a possible
  self is one specific product. Possible-selves owns the future self; imagination owns the
  making of representations as such.
- **The counterfactual past (regret)**: The engine models imagining forward and hypothetically,
  not the undoing of what happened. The counterfactual re-running of the actual past -- if only I
  had, the better outcome that was available -- is the regret engine's; imagination's supposition
  and simulation build hypothetical and novel scenarios, but the specific counterfactual undoing
  of a real prior event, and the emotion it carries, is regret's.
- **The recollection of the actual (memory)**: The engine models construction of the
  non-present, not recovery of the once-present. Reconstructing what did happen is the memory
  engine's -- even though, as Schacter shows, memory and imaginative simulation share machinery;
  the two are distinguished by their object, memory aimed at the real past and imagination at the
  possible. Memory recovers; imagination invents.
- **The pretend stance (play-mode)**: The engine models the cognitive act, not the posture
  toward an activity. Treating a real activity as play, the pretend or make-believe stance a
  persona adopts, is the play-mode engine's; imagination supplies the representations a pretense
  may run on, but the stance of playing is play-mode's. Play-mode is how a persona holds an
  activity; imagination is the representing the holding may use.
- **The mode itself is not engine content**: The engine declares the three modes exist; which
  one a given persona shows lives on that persona, linked under its Projection chapter. The
  engine refuses to fix a persona's imagining for it.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per file.

- **[imagination](process_imagination.md)** (the root, process): the deliberate forming and
  manipulation of mental representations of what is not present, held offline and worked.
  Anchored by Currie & Ravenscroft (imagination as recreation, quarantined from belief).
- **[conjuring](process_conjuring.md)** (process, mode): the sensory, imagistic mode -- a
  quasi-perceptual image summoned before the mind's eye and inspected as if seen. Anchored by
  Kosslyn (mental imagery) and Kind (the imagistic).
- **[supposition](process_supposition.md)** (process, mode): the propositional mode -- an as-if
  premise entertained without belief and elaborated by belief's own inference. Anchored by
  Nichols & Stich (the pretense workspace) and Kind (the propositional).
- **[simulation](process_simulation.md)** (process, mode): the constructive, combinatorial mode
  -- a novel scenario assembled from stored and invented parts and run forward. Anchored by
  Schacter & Addis (constructive episodic simulation).
