# Anger: Reference

## Line of Work

Anger as a **multi-type** engine: the hot approach-emotion through which a persona flares at a wrong done to it or what it cares about -- other-blame, goal-obstruction, and the mobilized urge to set it right -- taken across its forms and the trait that tunes it. The domain does not model the appraisal taxonomy of the basic emotions, which is the emotion engine's; nor the behavior of aggression, which is the aggression engine's; nor the cold downward look at an inferior, which is contempt's, anger's sibling in the CAD triad; nor the recoil from a contaminant, which is disgust's; nor the acute fight-or-flight routing of overwhelming demand, which is stress's. It models the emotion itself: the appraised wrong, the blame, and the approach-oriented flare to confront and correct.

The spine is the psychology of anger, from appraisal to trait. Anger is, in Lazarus's appraisal account, the response to a demeaning offense against me and mine -- keyed on other-blame and on the obstruction of a goal by an agent who could have done otherwise. It is the exception among the negative emotions in its motivational direction: where fear and disgust withdraw, anger approaches, mobilized to confront the offender and right the account. Its oldest studied trigger is frustration -- Dollard's frustration-aggression hypothesis, reformulated by Berkowitz to run through the aversive negative affect of a thwarted goal rather than the thwarting alone. It moralizes into indignation, the justice emotion answering violations of autonomy (Rozin's CAD triad); it banks into grievance, the stored wrong that outlasts its occasion; and it floods into rage, arousal overtopping control. Individual differences in trait anger and cynical hostility (Spielberger's STAXI; the Aggression Questionnaire; the Type-A literature) are stable, predict the hostile attribution bias, and track cardiovascular risk. The altitudes are those: the emotion, its four forms, and its trait.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **Emotion**: The engine models anger in its own depth -- the approach-emotion, its forms, its trait -- not its place in the taxonomy of the basic emotions. The appraisal structure by which an event becomes joy, sadness, anger, fear, or disgust is owned by the emotion engine; this engine unfolds the one node the emotion taxonomy names anger. The two compose: emotion keeps anger as one basic affect, this engine unfolds it.
- **Aggression**: The engine models the emotion, not the behavior. The forms and dynamics of aggression -- hostile, instrumental, displaced, relational, passive -- are owned by the aggression engine; anger is a frequent cause of aggression but neither necessary nor sufficient for it (one can act aggressively cold, or be angry and never act), and the emotion is not the act. This engine keeps the feeling; aggression keeps the doing.
- **Contempt**: The engine models the hot approach at a wrong, not the cold downward look at an inferior. Contempt -- the placing of another beneath the level at which they merit consideration -- is owned by the contempt engine; anger is its sibling in the CAD triad (Autonomy to contempt's Community), and the two differ in temperature and direction: anger opposes an equal who has wronged, contempt dismisses a lesser.
- **Disgust**: The engine models the approach-emotion at a wrong, not the recoil from a contaminant. Disgust -- revulsion and the sense of a spreading taint -- is owned by the disgust engine, the third of the CAD triad (Divinity); anger confronts what is unjust, disgust rejects what is impure, and moralized conflict often recruits both.
- **Stress**: The engine models anger as an appraisal-based emotion, not the acute stress response. The routing of an overwhelming demand into fight, flight, freeze, or fawn is owned by the stress engine; anger may accompany the fight route, but the emotion at a blamed wrong is distinct from the acute physiological triage of threat.
- **Forgiveness and Rumination**: The engine models the holding of a stored wrong (grievance), not its release or its circling rehearsal. The letting-go of an offense is owned by the forgiveness engine, and the repetitive return to it by the rumination engine; grievance is the wrong kept warm, the state those two act upon.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per file.

- **[anger](process_anger.md)** (the root, process): The approach-emotion at a wrong -- other-blame, goal-obstruction, the mobilized urge to confront and correct. Anchored by Lazarus (the appraisal) and Averill (anger as an everyday emotion of perceived wrongs).
- **[frustration](process_frustration.md)** (process, form): Goal-blockage -- anger born of an aversively thwarted goal, the least blame-bound trigger. Anchored by Berkowitz (the reformulated frustration-aggression hypothesis) and Dollard et al.
- **[indignation](process_indignation.md)** (process, form): The moral form -- righteous anger at a norm violated, often on another's behalf, scaling to collective outrage. Anchored by Rozin et al. (anger as the autonomy-violation emotion of the CAD triad).
- **[grievance](process_grievance.md)** (process, form): The stored form -- the wrong nursed and kept rather than discharged, the standing sense of having been wronged that outlasts its occasion. Anchored by the trait-anger and rumination literatures (Spielberger; the grudge as banked anger).
- **[rage](process_rage.md)** (process, form): The flooded form -- arousal overtopping control, judgment and proportion flooded out, the correction abandoned for the discharge. Anchored by the dyscontrol and emotion-regulation literatures.
- **[hostility](position_hostility.md)** (position, trait): Trait anger and cynical hostility -- the standing readiness to read the world as hostile and to anger readily, often, and long, driving the hostile attribution bias and tracking cardiovascular risk. Anchored by Spielberger (the STAXI), Buss & Perry (the Aggression Questionnaire), and the Type-A / cynical-hostility literature.
