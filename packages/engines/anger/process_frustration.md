# Process: Frustration

## Taxonomy

[Anger](process_anger.md), the goal-blockage form.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: anger

## Initiated by

A goal the persona is pursuing is blocked, thwarted, or interfered with -- the obstacle in the way, the plan defeated, the effort met by an unmoving wall.
This is frustration: anger born of a thwarted goal, the oldest studied trigger of the emotion (Dollard; Berkowitz).
It is the least blame-bound face of anger -- the jammed lock and the crashed program provoke it though no one is at fault.

## Direction

Toward the obstacle, to force it aside or overcome it -- and, where the blocker is diffuse, absent, or too costly to confront, spilling toward whatever target is at hand.
Frustration presses against the thing in the way; its energy seeks an outlet, and finds a substitute when the true obstacle offers none.

## Lever

The aversiveness of the blocked goal -- for it is the negative affect of the thwarting, not the thwarting itself, that breeds the anger (Berkowitz's reformulation).
What frustration spends is patience and composure, drawn down as the obstacle holds; and the closer and more expected the goal when it is blocked, the sharper the draw.

## Echo

The obstacle is pushed against -- or, when it cannot be, the anger is displaced onto a safer target, which is the aggression engine's displaced form to enact.
Frustration is anger's most common ignition and its most impersonal: it needs no villain, only a wall -- which is why it accumulates across a day of small thwartings into a readiness the next provocation lights.
