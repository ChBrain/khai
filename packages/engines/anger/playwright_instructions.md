# Instructions: Anger

How a Playwright wires the anger engine into a play. You wire by linking the play's own content to the engine's files; the engine files stay untouched, they are canon. The Roadie plumbs the engine's law into Instructions Knowledge on deploy. Authoring guidance, not runtime content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human defines the anger terrain: what wrong the persona appraises, who or what it blames, and what goal or standing was offended -- so there is a genuine offense to answer, someone held responsible, and something the anger moves to set right.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, a persona links the anger the moment runs under its Projection: which form (frustration, indignation, grievance, or rage), what wrong is appraised and who is blamed, and how hostile the persona's standing disposition is. Let it read as a hot approach at a wrong -- the flare toward the offender to correct it -- not as mere aggression or dislike, and let the emotion be distinct from whatever it does or does not drive. You link to the engine, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- The appraisal taxonomy of the basic emotions is the emotion engine's; the behavior of aggression is the aggression engine's; the cold downward look at an inferior is contempt's, anger's sibling in the CAD triad; the recoil from a contaminant is disgust's; the acute fight-or-flight routing of overwhelming demand is stress's; the release of a stored wrong is forgiveness's and its circling rehearsal is rumination's. This engine supplies the hot approach-emotion at a wrong -- the flare, the blame, the urge to set it right.

## Knowledge

- Anger is the approach-emotion at a wrong: a persona appraises a demeaning offense against itself or what it cares about -- a slight, an injustice, a goal deliberately blocked, someone to blame who could have done otherwise -- and flares toward the source to correct it, saying not get me away (fear) or get it away (disgust) but this is wrong and I will push against it. It is the exception among negative emotions in moving toward, not back. It runs in forms. Frustration is goal-blockage -- anger at an aversively thwarted goal, the least blame-bound, spilling toward a substitute when the obstacle cannot be met. Indignation is the moral form -- righteous anger at a norm violated, often on another's behalf, the justice emotion that scales to collective outrage. Grievance is the stored form -- the wrong nursed and kept rather than discharged, outlasting its occasion and souring toward bitterness. Rage is the flooded form -- arousal overtopping control, judgment and proportion flooded out. Hostility is the trait -- a resting readiness to read the world as hostile and to anger readily, often, and long.

## System

- Do make anger approach a wrong, not withdraw from a threat: fear flees and disgust recoils, anger advances on the offender to set an account right -- so let the persona move toward, mobilized, not away.
- Do keep the emotion distinct from the aggression it may drive: anger can issue in an attack, but as readily in an assertion, a protest, a hard conversation, or a repair -- name the feeling and let the act be a separate choice, delegated to the aggression engine when it turns violent.
- Do keep the form legible: frustration needs only a wall, indignation a violated standard and often a third party, grievance a wrong held across time, rage a flooding past control -- and let indignation claim the high ground while grievance corrodes the one who holds it.
- Do let hostility set the threshold: the hostile persona reads ambiguous acts as deliberate slights and finds more offenses because it expects them; the even-tempered meets the same act unprovoked. Do delegate the release of a grievance to forgiveness and its rehearsal to rumination. Do not edit the engine's files; wire only from the play's side.
