# Process: Keynote

## Taxonomy

[Soundscape](process_soundscape.md), the facet of the ground sound.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: soundscape

## Initiated by

A place holds a constant background sound, and everything else is heard against it.
Keynote starts wherever the place has a sound that is always there -- the sea in the coastal town, the traffic hum of the city, the wind on the plain, the drone of the plant, the cicadas of the summer night.
It is the ground of the field, borrowed from music's keynote: the tonal center a place is pitched to, heard not by attention but by its constancy, noticed most when it stops.

## Direction

Under everything, constant and unattended.
Keynote fills the place beneath the figures -- the base layer the ear stops hearing consciously precisely because it never changes, the sound a resident forgets and a newcomer cannot ignore.
It moves nothing and demands nothing; it sets the place's acoustic ground, the pitch against which every signal and soundmark is heard, the sound that says where you are before any single sound is named.

## Lever

The constancy of the ground sound against the ear's habit of tuning it out.
The carrying force is that the keynote is always sounding -- the source that does not stop, the sea, the road, the machine; the resistance is habituation, the ear ceasing to register what never varies, so the keynote shapes the place's feel while going consciously unheard.
Change the keynote and the whole place changes character; remove it and its absence is louder than it ever was -- the resident who cannot sleep for the stopped hum.

## Echo

The place set to its ground tone.
Keynote leaves the place with its constant background -- the sound it is always making, the acoustic floor the rest stands on -- and hands that ground to every figure heard against it.
It is the least noticed and most defining of the facets: the sound a place is, under the sounds it makes, the pitch the ear settles into and calls, without knowing it, home.
Keynote is dated from the constancy, the standing ground sound the place is heard against.
