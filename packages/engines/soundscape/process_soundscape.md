# Process: Soundscape

## Taxonomy

Acoustics.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: soundscape

## Initiated by

A place is entered, and it is heard as much as seen.
Soundscape starts the moment a place makes sound at all -- the hum, the calls, the footsteps, the machines, the quiet -- and the ear takes in the whole of it before anyone names a single note.
Every place has an auditory field, a way it sounds that is as particular as its look; soundscape is that field as an actor, not the silent scenery a setting is usually taken for.

## Direction

Into the ear, from every source at once.
Four facets compose the field: [Keynote](process_keynote.md) is the ground sound the place is heard against, [Signal](process_signal.md) the foreground sound listened to for its meaning, [Soundmark](process_soundmark.md) the unique sound that marks the place as itself, and [Silence](process_silence.md) the twist -- the quiet read as a presence.
A place is not heard as one flat wall of sound but as a composition: a ground, the figures that rise from it, the one sound that names it, and the silence beneath -- or, where the sound is crowded and masked, a lo-fi din in which the figures and the soundmark are lost.

## Lever

The place's sound against the ear's power to sort and the noise that swamps it.
The carrying force is everything at the place that sounds -- the natural, the human, the mechanical; the resistance is the ear's sorting, which picks the signal from the keynote and the soundmark from the mass, and the crowding of a lo-fi soundscape, where too much sound masks the rest and the place loses its legible acoustic shape.
A hi-fi place, quiet enough that each sound stands clear, is heard in depth and detail; a lo-fi place, packed with masking noise, is heard as an undifferentiated wall, its soundmark drowned.

## Echo

The place known by its sound, and remembered by it.
Soundscape does not close; it is the standing acoustic character of the place, gathering its sounds into a field that is as much the place as its look.
It hands off what it is not: the weather that supplies some of the sound is weather's, the layout and standing mood are space's, the persona's own hearing is theirs, and an authored score laid over the play is the pitch's.
The place is dated from its sound, and read by it -- a setting is not only somewhere seen but somewhere heard, and the ear knows a place the eye has forgotten.
