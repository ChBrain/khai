# Instructions: Soundscape

How a Playwright wires the soundscape engine into a play. You wire by linking the play's
own content to the engine's files; the engine files stay untouched, they are canon. The
Roadie plumbs the engine's law into Instructions Knowledge on deploy. Authoring guidance,
not runtime content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human sets which places are heard as much as seen -- the ground sound a place stands against, the foreground sounds that carry meaning, the one sound that marks it as itself, or the silence it offers -- and whether that sound is the place's quiet background or a live actor in the play.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, a place whose sound is an actor links under Shown the facet of its auditory field: the keynote it is heard against, the signal that rises out of it, the soundmark that names it, or the silence it offers. Play the soundscape as the place's sound present in what it puts before whoever enters -- the sea's constant hush, the church bell over the town, the exact cry of these gulls, the deep quiet of the forest -- heard, never narrated, and never standing in for a persona's own inner noise or quiet. Keep it the sound of the place, not the weather that feeds it (that is weather), the layout and standing mood (space), a persona's own hearing (the persona's engines), or an authored score laid over the play (the pitch). You link to the engine, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- The weather that supplies some of the sound is weather's; the layout and standing mood is space's; a persona's own hearing is theirs; an authored score is the play's pitch. Soundscape is the sensory sibling of weather -- the place heard as weather is the place felt -- and the sound of the place itself, gathered into a field. Whether the facet rings true to the place and its sound is the team's review.

## Knowledge

- Soundscape is the auditory field of a place: the whole of what it sounds like, taken in by the ear before any single sound is named, as particular to a place as its look (Schafer's keynote, signal, and soundmark; the hi-fi field where each sound stands clear against the lo-fi din where they mask; Truax's sound as communication; Thompson's soundscape as historically made). It wires on a place at Shown, and it is the place heard as much as seen. Four facets carry it -- keynote is the ground sound, the constant background heard unconsciously, the tonal center a place is pitched to; signal is the foreground sound listened to for its meaning, the bell and horn and siren that rise out of the ground; soundmark is the place's unique signature, the one sound that identifies it and no other, community-defining and worth keeping like a monument; silence is the twist, the quiet read not as void but as a presence, the refuge from the lo-fi din, one of the field's richest states. Read a place by its sound, and read silence two ways: the absence of sound is not the failure of the soundscape but one of its qualities.

## System

- Do give a place whose sound is an actor its facet under Shown; do play the soundscape as the place's sound shown, present not narrated; do keep the reading the sound of the place -- the keynote, the signal, the soundmark, the silence -- not a feeling and not a symbol.
- Do read silence as the twist: the quiet is not the absence of the soundscape but one of its states, a presence a place can offer -- the hush of the forest, the stillness of the church, the deep quiet fewer and fewer places can give.
- Do read the field two ways: a hi-fi place, quiet enough that each sound stands clear, is heard in depth; a lo-fi place, crowded and masked, is heard as an undifferentiated wall, its soundmark drowned -- and a soundmark, like a species, can go extinct.
- Do not model the weather that feeds the sound (weather), the layout and standing mood (space), a persona's own hearing (the persona's engines), or an authored score laid over the play (the pitch). Do not make the sound stand in for a persona's inner noise or quiet. Do not edit the engine's files; wire only from the play's side.
