# Soundscape: Reference

## Line of Work

Soundscape as **process**: the auditory field of a place -- the whole of what it sounds
like, taken in by the ear before any single sound is named, as particular to a place as
its look and as much an actor as its scenery. It wires on a place at Shown, the place's
self-presentation to whoever enters, because a place is heard as much as seen: the ground
sound it stands against, the sounds that rise out of it to be listened to, the one sound
that marks it as itself, and the silence beneath. The domain does not model the weather
that supplies some of the sound -- the rain, the wind, the thunder -- which is weather's;
nor the layout and standing mood of the setting, which is space's; nor a persona's own
hearing, the inner noise or quiet they carry, which is theirs; nor an authored score laid
over the play, the music the audience hears but the place does not make, which is the
pitch's. It models the sound the place itself makes and is heard by.

The spine is acoustic ecology -- the study of the sonic environment as a composed field
rather than a wall of noise. Schafer named its parts: the keynote, borrowed from music,
the ground tone a place is pitched to and heard against; the signal, the foreground sound
attended to for its meaning; the soundmark, from landmark, the unique sound that
identifies a place and no other. He set the hi-fi soundscape, quiet enough that each sound
stands clear, against the lo-fi one, crowded and masked, where the figures and the
soundmark drown. Truax read the field as acoustic communication -- sound as the exchange
between a place and its listeners. Augoyard and Torgue catalogued the sonic effects by
which a place's sound is shaped and perceived, and Thompson traced how the modern built
environment reengineered what places sound like. So the engine gives soundscape three
sounds and a twist -- keynote, signal, soundmark -- and keeps a fourth, silence, the quiet
read not as the absence of the field but as one of its richest states, the presence a
place can offer.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The weather that feeds the sound (weather)**: The rain on the roof, the wind in the
  wires, the thunder -- weather is one of the soundscape's sources, but the weather itself
  is weather's engine. Soundscape takes the sound the weather makes as part of the field;
  it does not model the storm. The rain is weather's; the sound of the rain, gathered into
  the place's field, is soundscape's.
- **The layout and standing mood (space)**: How a place is arranged, how its size and
  shape and standing feel read -- the room's proportions, the street's enclosure -- is
  space's. Soundscape is the place's sound, not its shape; the two meet in a place (a
  cathedral's vastness and its echo) but are not the same force. Space is how the place is
  laid out; soundscape is how it sounds.
- **A persona's own hearing (the persona's engines)**: The noise in a persona's own head,
  the ringing of their grief, the silence of their numbness, what they hear because of who
  they are -- that is the persona's, not the place's. Soundscape is the place's sound,
  heard by anyone who enters; it never stands in for a persona's inner acoustic state.
- **An authored score (the play's pitch)**: The music laid over the play for the audience,
  the score the place does not make and its personas do not hear -- that is authored key,
  the pitch, not the place's field. Soundscape is the sound within the world; the score is
  the sound over it, and the two are different layers.
- **The facet itself is not engine content**: The engine declares the four facets exist;
  which one a given place shows lives on that place, linked under its Shown chapter. The
  engine refuses to fix a place's sound for it.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per file.

- **[soundscape](process_soundscape.md)** (the root): the auditory field of a place, the
  whole of what it sounds like taken in before any note is named, the place heard as much
  as seen. Anchored by Schafer (the field as composition) and the hi-fi/lo-fi reading.
- **[keynote](process_keynote.md)**: the ground sound -- the constant background heard
  unconsciously, the tonal center a place is pitched to: the sea, the traffic hum, the
  wind, the plant's drone, noticed most when it stops. Anchored by Schafer (the keynote
  from music).
- **[signal](process_signal.md)**: the foreground sound listened to for its meaning -- the
  bell, the horn, the siren, the call, the sound that rises out of the ground and demands
  to be heard. Anchored by Schafer (the signal) and Truax (figure to the keynote's ground).
- **[soundmark](process_soundmark.md)**: the place's unique signature sound -- the one
  sound that identifies this place and no other, community-defining and worth keeping, the
  aural landmark that can go extinct. Anchored by Schafer (the soundmark) and Thompson (the
  endangered particular).
- **[silence](process_silence.md)**: the quiet read as a presence -- the absence of sound
  taken not as void but as one of the field's richest states, the refuge from the lo-fi
  din, the quiet a place offers. The engine's twist; anchored by Schafer's hi-fi ideal and
  the scarcity of true quiet.
