# Anchoring: Reference

## Line of Work

Anchoring as **process**: the estimation through which a persona forming a numeric or magnitude judgment under uncertainty is drawn toward a reference value that entered first, and adjusts away from it too little. The domain does not model the mode by which a choice among options is reached, which is decision's; nor the bargaining a deliberately-set anchor serves, which is negotiation's; nor the shift worked by how an option is described rather than by a number, which is framing's; nor the standing catalogued tilt the whole look leaves on the read of a quantity, which the bias engine holds among the cognitive biases. It models the pull toward the reference itself: a first figure met, the estimate bent toward it, and the correction that stops short.

The spine is the psychology of judgment under uncertainty. Presented with or generating a starting value, a person estimating an uncertain quantity produces a final judgment biased toward that starting value -- the anchor -- because adjustment from it is insufficient. The effect is one of the original three heuristics of judgment, robust across relevant and irrelevant anchors, warned and paid subjects, and expert and novice judges. It runs through more than one mechanism: insufficient adjustment from self-generated anchors, and selective accessibility of anchor-consistent evidence for anchors supplied from outside; and it can be wielded on purpose, a figure planted to move another's estimate. The three forms are those mechanisms and that use: adjustment, accessibility, planting.

## Origin

The psychology of anchoring in judgment under uncertainty, applied directly as process constraints.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **Decision**: The engine models the pull of a reference on an estimate, not the mode by which a choice among options is reached. How a persona decides -- intuitively, deliberately, by heuristic or satisficing -- is owned by the decision engine; anchoring bends a magnitude judgment under uncertainty, and owns that estimation, not the choosing.
- **Negotiation**: The engine models why a first number grips, not the bargaining it serves. The process of preparing, exploring, inventing, and resolving a deal is owned by the negotiation engine; planting supplies the anchor a negotiator deploys, and owns the reference's pull, not the negotiation around it.
- **Framing**: The engine models the shift worked by a numeric reference, not by how an option is described. The gain/loss and valence recasting of the same content is owned by the framing engine; anchoring moves an estimate by a figure, framing by a description, and the two are distinct levers on judgment.
- **Bias**: The engine models the live estimation under a reference, not the static tilt it leaves. The catalogued cognitive tendency -- the standing pull of a number that fixes the scale -- is owned by the bias engine as one node in its codex; anchoring is the process of which that tilt is the residue, and owns the drawing and the insufficient correction, not the entry in the list.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per file.

- **[anchoring](process_anchoring.md)** (the root): The estimate drawn to a reference -- a first figure met, the judgment bent toward it, adjustment away from it stopping short. Anchored by Tversky & Kahneman (anchoring and adjustment as a heuristic of judgment under uncertainty).
- **[adjustment](process_adjustment.md)** (form): The self-generated route -- stepping away from a starting value one holds, but stopping at the near edge of the plausible range. Anchored by Epley & Gilovich (insufficient adjustment) and Tversky & Kahneman (the original adjustment account).
- **[accessibility](process_accessibility.md)** (form): The externally-supplied route -- a provided anchor priming anchor-consistent evidence, so the target is judged through a skewed sample. Anchored by Strack & Mussweiler (selective accessibility) and Mussweiler & Strack's mechanism work.
- **[planting](process_planting.md)** (form): The deliberate route -- a figure set on purpose, a first offer or list price, to drag another's estimate toward it. Anchored by Galinsky & Mussweiler (first offers as anchors) and Northcraft & Neale (anchoring in expert real-estate pricing).
