# @chbrain/khai-engine-moral-conviction

## 0.1.1

### Patch Changes

- 3e3808b: Add the moral-conviction engine (position: anchor + expressions) -- the metacognitive stamp that an attitude is a moral mandate rather than a preference, a taste, or a convention. The gap analysis (Tier-3 #22) found the `moral-judgment` engine owns _which_ concern a verdict runs on (care, fairness, loyalty, authority, sanctity, liberty), but nothing owned the orthogonal mandate _status_ stamped on top of any of them.

  - **Anchor (position):** `moral-conviction` -- the stamp that a position is a moral imperative, marked by two properties Skitka's work isolates: it is held as an objective, universal fact, and it is held as independent of and superior to authority.
  - **Faces (position):**
    - `mobilization` -- the mandate as fuel: moral conviction as the strongest known driver of engagement, the force that moves the persona to act, sacrifice, and refuse to fold or be bought off.
    - `intolerance` -- the mandate as wall: distance from those who disagree, dissent read as bad rather than merely mistaken, compromise refused as complicity, authority and procedure subordinated to the mandate, and at the extreme hard means licensed toward a certain end.

  **Scope note -- the concern beneath the mandate stays with `moral-judgment`.** Moral conviction is orthogonal to which foundation fires: the `moral-judgment` engine owns the foundations a verdict runs on and the right/wrong verdict itself; this engine owns only the felt difference between `I prefer this` and `this is simply right`. Also bounded against plain attitude strength (a categorical metacognition, not a point on the strength continuum) and against `awe`/sanctity (the mandate stamp attaches equally to secular and sacred values; it is not the setting-apart of the sacred).

  Warranted (LORE) on Skitka, Bauman & Sargis (2005, the founding -- moral conviction as something more than attitude strength, marked by objectivity and authority independence), Skitka (2010, the synthesis of the defining features and the double-edged consequences), Skitka & Morgan (2014, the mobilizing edge), and Skitka, Hanson, Morgan & Wisneski (2021, the consolidated review of distancing, resistance to authority, and acceptance of hostile means). Set at patch as the free level. No whitelist required -- the `moral_conviction`/`mobilization`/`intolerance` stems are unique.
