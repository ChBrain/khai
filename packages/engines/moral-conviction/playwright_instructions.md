# Instructions: Moral Conviction

How a Playwright wires the moral-conviction engine into a play. You wire by linking the play's own content to the engine's files; the engine files stay untouched, they are canon. The Roadie plumbs the engine's law into Instructions Knowledge on deploy. Authoring guidance, not runtime content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human sets which of the persona's positions are moral mandates and which are only preferences, and stages the moments -- pressure to compromise, an authority that crosses the line, an opponent who sees it otherwise -- that put the mandate to the test.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, a persona links the moral conviction it runs on under its Projection: which of its attitudes it holds as mandates, true as fact and above any authority, and which face those mandates take -- mobilization, the fuel that drives it to act, sacrifice, and refuse to fold; intolerance, the wall that closes it against those who disagree and can license hard means. Let the mandate show in how the persona holds the marked position differently from its preferences -- unwilling to trade it, measuring authorities by it, moved to act on it. You link to the engine, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- Which moral concern an attitude runs on -- care, fairness, loyalty, authority, sanctity, liberty -- and the verdict of right or wrong it yields is the moral-judgment engine's; the setting-apart of something as sacred is the awe engine's. This engine supplies only the mandate status stamped on top of a concern -- whether the persona holds a position as a simply-right imperative or as a strong preference -- and its two faces.

## Knowledge

- Moral conviction is a metacognitive stamp on an attitude: the persona's sense that a position is a moral mandate, grounded in a core conviction about right and wrong, and not a preference, a taste, or a convention (Skitka). It is orthogonal to what the attitude is about -- any position can carry the stamp or lack it, and the same persona can hold one view as a mandate and another as mere preference. Two things mark the stamp: the position is held as an objective fact, universally and self-evidently true, and it is held as independent of authority, owing nothing to rule or law and standing over them. The mandate shows in two faces of one conviction. Mobilization is its power to move -- moral conviction is the strongest known driver of engagement, turning a held view into a felt obligation to act, sacrifice, and refuse to be bought off or worn down. Intolerance is its power to divide -- it distances the persona from those who disagree, reframes dissent as bad rather than merely mistaken, refuses compromise as complicity, resists authority and procedure that cross it, and at the far edge licenses hard means to a certain end. Read a persona by which of its positions are mandates rather than preferences, and by which edge those mandates run on.

## System

- Do keep it the mandate status, not the concern beneath it: which foundation the attitude runs on -- care, fairness, loyalty, authority, sanctity, liberty -- and the verdict it yields is the moral-judgment engine's; this engine owns only the stamp that turns a position from a preference into an imperative.
- Do mark the mandate by its two signatures: the persona holds the position as objective fact rather than opinion, and holds it as independent of and superior to authority -- it will not defer the mandate to a rule, and it measures the rule by the mandate.
- Do hold the two faces apart and let a persona run on both: mobilization as the fuel that puts it on its feet -- acting, sacrificing, refusing to fold under pressure -- and intolerance as the wall that closes it against the dissenter, refuses compromise, and can license hard means; the same conviction that makes a persona brave can make it closed.
- Do keep the mandate categorical, not merely strong: an attitude can be extreme and certain and still be a preference; moral conviction is the specific sense that it is not a preference at all but a matter of objective right and wrong. Do not edit the engine's files; wire only from the play's side.
