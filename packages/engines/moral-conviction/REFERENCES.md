# Moral Conviction: Reference

## Line of Work

Moral conviction as a **position** engine: a metacognitive property an attitude can carry -- the stamp that a position is a moral mandate rather than a preference, a taste, or a convention -- taken across the two faces the mandate shows in conduct. The domain does not model which moral concern a verdict runs on -- care, fairness, loyalty, authority, sanctity, liberty -- which is owned by the moral-judgment engine; it models only the mandate status stamped on top of a concern, the felt difference between holding a view as opinion and holding it as simply-right, and what that status does to how firmly the position is held and what the persona will do about it.

The spine is Skitka's work on moral conviction. Skitka, Bauman and Sargis asked whether attitudes people describe as strongly moral are merely a stronger version of ordinary attitude strength, or something categorically different, and found the latter: an attitude held with strong moral conviction has properties an equally strong non-moral attitude lacks. Two are definitional. The position is held with **perceived objectivity and universality** -- experienced as a fact about the world, true for everyone, not a matter of personal or cultural preference. And it is held with **authority independence** -- seen as owing nothing to rules, laws, or authorities, and as standing in judgment over them rather than deriving its force from them. Skitka's later syntheses trace the consequences, which run in two directions from the same stamp. Moral conviction is among the strongest predictors of political and civic engagement -- turnout, activism, willingness to sacrifice, and refusal to be bought off -- the mobilizing edge. And it predicts greater social and physical distance from those who disagree, lower trust and goodwill toward them, resistance to legitimate authority and procedure when they cross the mandate, and, at the extreme, greater acceptance of violence and hostile means toward a morally certain end -- the intolerant edge. The altitudes are those: the mandate stamp itself, and its mobilizing and intolerant faces.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The moral foundation a verdict runs on (moral-judgment)**: The engine models the mandate status stamped on an attitude, not the concern the attitude is about. Which foundation fires -- care, fairness, loyalty, authority, sanctity, liberty -- and the verdict of right or wrong it yields is owned by the moral-judgment engine; moral conviction is orthogonal to all of them, the felt difference between holding any such verdict as a preference and holding it as an imperative. The one names what is judged and on what ground; the other names whether the judgment is a mandate at all.
- **Attitude strength as such**: The engine models a categorical property, not a point on the strength continuum. An attitude can be extreme, certain, important, and central without being a moral mandate; moral conviction is the specific metacognition that the position is a matter of objective right and wrong, which Skitka's work shows predicts outcomes that ordinary strength does not. Strength is how hard a view is held; conviction is the sense that it is not a view at all but a fact.
- **The sacralizing of a value (awe / sanctity)**: The engine models the stamp that a position is morally mandated, not the setting-apart of something as sacred and not-to-be-traded. The experience of the sacred is the awe engine's, and the sanctity foundation is the moral-judgment engine's; moral conviction can attach to a sacred value but attaches equally to secular ones, and is the general mandate stamp rather than any particular reverence. The one hallows a thing; the other holds a position as simply-right.
