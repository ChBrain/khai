# Process: Fidelity

## Taxonomy

[Restoration](process_restoration.md), the stance this mend takes.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: restoration

## Initiated by

The mend decides its first duty is to the object's maker, and effaces itself before the original hand.
Fidelity starts from the restorer's restraint: serve the achievement the object embodies, add nothing that claims to be the author's, and keep every intervention honest enough that no one could mistake the new work for the old.
Its target is the maker's truth, guarded against the restorer's own ambition.

## Direction

Onto the Apparent, keeping the mend legible as a mend.
The new work is done well but declared -- distinguishable at close range even where it is invisible at a distance, tratteggio's honest hatching, the documented and reversible touch -- so the object's account of itself is never falsified by the hand that saved it.
Fidelity moves the object forward without letting the restorer become a second author or a forger.

## Lever

The maker's achievement against the restorer's temptation to improve it.
The carrying force is the duty to the original -- an autographic thing's identity is welded to the hand that made it, and a repair that overpaints or completes past the evidence misrepresents that achievement; the resistance is the pull to make the object better, fuller, more saleable than the maker left it.
Hold to the evidence and the mend stays a mend; reach past it, into confident invention or seamless deceit, and fidelity is broken -- the restoration become a forgery of the master's hand.

## Echo

The object saved without being falsified.
It shows a repair that does not lie about who made what, and carries beneath it the ethic the stance keeps -- that the deepest respect a restorer can pay an object is to stay visible as its second, lesser hand and never usurp its first.
Fidelity is dated from the mend that told the truth about itself, and it is the stance that keeps the whole field honest -- the line, along all the others, past which restoration becomes fabrication.
