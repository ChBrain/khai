# Instructions: Restoration

How a Playwright wires the restoration engine into a play. You wire by linking the
play's own content to the engine's files; the engine files stay untouched, they
are canon. The Roadie plumbs the engine's law into Instructions Knowledge on
deploy. Authoring guidance, not runtime content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human sets which objects in the world are being mended, by whom, and what stance the mend takes toward the object's identity and history.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, a mended object links under Apparent the stance its repair takes: erasing the break toward the unbroken original (reversion), gilding the break and honouring it (testimony), arresting the damage and touching no more (stabilization), keeping the thing working through part-turnover (continuation), or serving the maker's hand and staying short of forgery (fidelity). Play it as the object showing what the mend made of its break -- the invisible fill, the golden seam, the arrested wound, the renewed part, the honest touch -- and keep it the intervention on the object, weighing the aesthetic against the historical and how much may be renewed before the thing is lost. Hand the deterioration to the decay family, the mending of a place to reclamation, and the mending of a bond to repair. You link to the engine, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- The breaking and wearing the mend answers are the decay family's; the mending of a place is reclamation's (the same act on a place, not a piece); the mending of a bond is the repair engine's (between persons). The object's standing worth or belonging is authenticity's, heirloom's, extended-self's, sign-value's -- restoration reads only the act of mending. Whether the stance rings true for the object and its history is the team's review.

## Knowledge

- Restoration is the mending of a broken or worn object, arbitrated between the object's aesthetic instance (its form) and its historical instance (its marks of time), and run out along two questions no building faces: how much may be renewed before it is no longer that thing (the ship of Theseus), and whether the mend should show or vanish (Brandi; the constitution and visible/invisible questions). Five stances. Reversion erases the break and returns the object toward its original -- the invisible repair, which pressed too far invents rather than restores. Testimony gilds the break and honours it -- kintsugi's golden seam, the object prized above the unbroken (Benjamin's aura); the twist. Stabilization arrests the damage and does the minimum, keeping the object as-found (minimal intervention). Continuation keeps the thing alive and working through part-turnover, function over fabric (the ship kept sailing though every plank is replaced). Fidelity serves the maker's achievement and keeps the mend honest and distinguishable, short of forgery. Read an object by the stance its mend takes.

## System

- Do give a mended object its stance under Apparent; do play it as the object showing what the mend made of its break; do keep the reading the intervention on the object -- the aesthetic weighed against the historical -- not a feeling about being made whole.
- Do read testimony as break-made-value: the gilded seam adds worth, and the object is more prized for its honoured wound, not less -- restoration's twist, against reversion's erasure.
- Do let continuation ride the constitution question honestly: keeping a thing working spends its original matter, and a fully renewed object raises whether it is still itself.
- Do not model the deterioration (that is the decay family), the mending of a place (that is reclamation), or the mending of a bond (that is repair). Do not make the mended object mirror a persona's own healing (that is the play's pitch). Do not edit the engine's files; wire only from the play's side.
