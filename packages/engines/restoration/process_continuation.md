# Process: Continuation

## Taxonomy

[Restoration](process_restoration.md), the stance this mend takes.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: restoration

## Initiated by

The mend decides the object is saved by being kept alive and in use, not by being frozen.
Continuation starts where a thing must go on working -- the clock kept running, the ship kept sailing, the instrument kept played, the tool kept in the hand -- and accepts the wear and the part-replacement that use demands.
Its target is not a state to preserve but a life to sustain.

## Direction

Onto the Apparent, and through it onto the object's ongoing life.
The worn part is swapped, the failed component renewed, the object kept fit for its work; over time the matter turns over, plank by plank, and yet the thing goes on being the thing.
Continuation moves the object forward in service, its identity carried by the continuity of function and use rather than by the persistence of its original substance.

## Lever

The life kept against the original matter spent to keep it.
The carrying force is that a used thing endures where a frozen one only waits to decay -- identity as the continuity of an organism's life, not of its stuff (the ship of Theseus answered on the side of form and function); the resistance is the authenticity of substance, which every replacement erodes, so that a fully renewed object raises the question of whether it is still itself.
Keep the thing working and it lives; renew it entirely and it may become a replica of itself -- the stance rides the constitution question at its sharpest.

## Echo

The object still in use, its matter quietly turned over.
It shows a working thing, serviceable and whole, and carries beneath it the trade the stance makes -- the original substance spent, part by part, to buy the continued life, until the object is old in identity and new in fabric.
Continuation is dated not from a single mend but from the long succession of them, and it is the stance that keeps a thing alive at the cost of the very originality that reversion and stabilization guard.
