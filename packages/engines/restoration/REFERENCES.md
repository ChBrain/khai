# Restoration: Reference

## Line of Work

Restoration as **process**: the mending of a broken or worn object, and the stance
that mend takes toward the object's identity and history. The domain does not model
the deterioration it answers, which is the decay family's; nor the mending of a
place, which is the reclamation engine's (the same act at the scale of a building or
a site); nor the mending of a bond, which is the repair engine's (rupture and
restitution between persons -- the same English word in a different science). It
models the intervention on the object, and the two questions no building faces: how
much may be renewed before the thing is lost, and whether the mend should show.

The spine is one arbitration worked out along object-specific axes. Brandi set the
arbitration: every treatment answers to an aesthetic instance (the object as form
and image) and a historical instance (the object as a record of its passage through
time), and a mend may commit neither a forgery of the one nor an erasure of the
other. What makes the object case its own, distinct from a building's, are two
pressures a bounded, singular, provenanced thing carries that a site does not: the
**ship-of-Theseus constitution** (Plutarch's ship, Locke's organism -- how far part
may replace part before the thing is a replica), and the **visible-versus-invisible
fork** (the Western seamless ideal against kintsugi's gilded seam, Benjamin's aura
against newness-value). So the engine gives the mend five stances: reversion,
testimony, stabilization, continuation, and the fidelity that keeps the whole field
short of forgery.

## Origin

The theory and practice of object conservation, and the philosophy of repair and
object identity, applied directly as engine constraints across process.

| Source                   | Key Work                                                                              | Scope                                                                                                                                                                               |
| :----------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | :---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Cesare Brandi**        | _Theory of Restoration_ (1963)                                                        | The founding arbitration: the aesthetic instance against the historical instance; minimal intervention, reversibility, and distinguishability. The root, stabilization, fidelity.   |
| **Mark Sagoff**          | _"On Restoring and Reproducing Art"_ (1978)                                           | The purist (arrest only) against the integralist (restore the intended whole), and how over-restoration can destroy the authenticity it means to save. Reversion and stabilization. |
| **Walter Benjamin**      | _"The Work of Art in the Age of Its Technological Reproducibility"_ (1935)            | Aura and authenticity as lodged in the object's material history -- the authority a visible mend can carry and an erasure can spend. Testimony and fidelity.                        |
| **Kintsugi / wabi-sabi** | the golden-joinery tradition (15th-16th c.); Sen no Rikyu; Koren (1994)               | The break honoured and gilded, the mended object prized above the unbroken; beauty in the imperfect and impermanent. The testimony stance.                                          |
| **Plutarch; John Locke** | _Life of Theseus_ (c. 75 CE); _Essay Concerning Human Understanding_, II.xxvii (1694) | The ship of Theseus and identity through part-turnover -- persistence as continuity of organisation, not of matter. The continuation stance and the constitution question.          |

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The deterioration it answers (the decay family)**: Restoration is the response;
  the breaking, wearing, and rotting it reaches in to mend are the decay family's.
  Decay is what happens to the object; restoration is what a hand does about it.
  (The engine's `decay` today is place-typed; an object-decay is charted as the next
  element of the object-lifecycle, and restoration delegates to it once it lands.)
- **The mending of a place (reclamation)**: Restoration is the object case;
  reclamation is the same intervention at the scale of a building or a site.
  Reclamation faces the age-value of a multi-period fabric in place; restoration
  faces what a building mostly dodges -- the singular, provenanced thing's
  constitution (how much may be renewed before it is a replica) and its
  function-to-working (the clock that must run). The two are parallels on different
  types, not one engine.
- **The mending of a bond (repair)**: Restoration mends a thing; the repair engine
  mends a relationship (rupture, accounting, restitution, integration between
  persons). Same English word, different science, different type -- the reason
  restoration takes its own domain rather than a member under repair.
- **The value and identity the object carries (authenticity, heirloom,
  extended-self, sign-value)**: Restoration models the act of mending, not the
  standing worth or belonging of the object. Whether the object is authentic to a
  self, an heirloom, an extension of a persona, or a coded status-sign is those
  engines'; restoration reads only what the mend makes of the break.
- **A persona's feeling about the mend (the persona's engines)**: The consolation,
  the reverence, the unease a mended or over-restored object stirs in a person are
  the persona's own engines' (nostalgia and its kin). Restoration delivers the
  intervention on the object, not the feeling it provokes. And a mended object made
  to **stand for** a character's own healing is refused: that is authored key, the
  play's pitch, not the object's force.
- **The stance itself is not engine content**: The engine declares the five stances
  exist; which one a given object's mend takes lives on the piece, linked under its
  Apparent chapter. The engine refuses to fix an object's restoration for it.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per file.

- **[restoration](process_restoration.md)** (the root): the mending of a broken or
  worn object, arbitrated between the aesthetic and the historical, over the
  constitution question. Anchored by Brandi (the two instances) and Spelman (the
  impulse to repair).
- **[reversion](process_reversion.md)**: erase the break and return the object toward
  its unbroken original, the invisible repair -- risking, pressed too far, the
  forgery of an invented original. Anchored by Sagoff (the integralist).
- **[testimony](process_testimony.md)**: honour the break and make the mend visible,
  the object prized for its marked history. Anchored by kintsugi/wabi-sabi and
  Benjamin (aura); restoration's twist -- the break made value.
- **[stabilization](process_stabilization.md)**: arrest the damage and do the
  minimum, keeping the object as-found. Anchored by Brandi (minimal intervention) and
  Sagoff (the purist).
- **[continuation](process_continuation.md)**: keep the thing alive and working
  through part-turnover, function over fabric. Anchored by the ship of Theseus
  (Plutarch, Locke) -- identity as continuity of use, not of matter.
- **[fidelity](process_fidelity.md)**: serve the maker's achievement and keep the
  mend honest and distinguishable, short of forgery. Anchored by Brandi
  (distinguishability) and the philosophy of forgery (Goodman's autographic art,
  Dutton on the misrepresented achievement).
