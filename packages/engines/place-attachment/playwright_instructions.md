# Instructions: Place Attachment

How a Playwright wires the place-attachment engine into a play. You wire by linking the play's own content to the engine's files; the engine files stay untouched, they are canon. The Roadie plumbs the engine's law into Instructions Knowledge on deploy. Authoring guidance, not runtime content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human sets the place terrain: which particular places matter to each significant persona, and how -- a home, a town, a landscape, a workshop -- and what leaving or losing them would cost, so a persona carries a standing bond to specific ground.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, each persona links, under Projection, the place-attachment standing it holds: rooted (a place woven into the self), dependence (a place needed for what it affords), homesick (displaced from a place still held dear), or placeless (at home nowhere). Let it read in how the persona is oriented by its places -- where it belongs, what a place is worth to it, what its loss would take. You link to the engine, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- The setting read as a mover in the moment -- what a place affords, holds, offers, or withholds -- is the space engine's; the bond to other people is the attachment engine's; the sentimental longing for the past is the nostalgia engine's; the acute grief of a loss is the grief engine's; the sociability of a setting type is the third-place engine's; the whole self-concept is the identity engine's. This engine supplies the enduring bond a persona holds to a particular place -- rooted, functional, displaced, or absent.

## Knowledge

- Place attachment is the enduring affective bond between a persona and a specific meaningful place (Low & Altman; Scannell & Gifford), borrowing the attachment frame -- the place as a secure base and safe haven -- and applying it to place rather than to people. It runs in four standings. Rooted is the identity bond: the place woven into the self, home in the deep sense, belonging and continuity (place-identity; Relph's rootedness). Dependence is the functional bond: the place valued for what it affords, serving goals better than alternatives (place-dependence). Homesick is the severed bond carried: displaced from a place still held dear, the ache for a home left or changed (homesickness; solastalgia). Placeless is the absent bond: at home nowhere, no place woven into the self, mobile and unrooted (Relph's placelessness). Read a persona by which places hold it and how, and let leaving or losing a bonded place cost accordingly.

## System

- Do keep place attachment the person-side bond to a particular place, not the setting's momentary pull: it is the tie the persona carries across time, present or not, where the space engine reads the place acting on whoever is in it now.
- Do distinguish the identity bond from the functional one: let the rooted persona belong to a place as part of itself, unable to leave without leaving a piece of the self, and the dependent one need a place for what it affords, its tie strong but conditional on function.
- Do let displacement cost: the homesick persona half-elsewhere, unable to settle where it stands, keeping faith with a lost or changed ground -- and hand the acute mourning to the grief engine and the longing-for-the-past to nostalgia.
- Do let the placeless be genuinely unanchored -- free and groundless at once -- and let bonds form and transfer over an arc, dependence deepening into rootedness, a lost place slowly replaced or never replaced. Do delegate the setting-as-mover to the space engine and the bond to people to the attachment engine. Do not edit the engine's files; wire only from the play's side.
