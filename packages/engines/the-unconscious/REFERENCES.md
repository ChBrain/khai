# The Unconscious: Reference

## Line of Work

The unconscious as a **position** engine: the region of mental life outside awareness that still shapes what a persona feels, judges, wants, and does -- taken across the three kinds the modern science of the unconscious, building on Freud, distinguishes. The domain does not model the monitor that knows what a persona knows, which is the metacognition engine's; nor the mechanisms that keep the dynamic unconscious repressed, which are the defense engine's; nor the intrapsychic agency the id is largely unconscious of, which is the structural-model engine's; nor the storage and retrieval of latent content, which is the memory engine's. It models the out-of-awareness region itself -- content and process the persona cannot introspect, yet which move it.

The spine runs from Freud through the cognitive revolution's re-founding of the unconscious on experimental ground. Freud made the unconscious the centre of mental life: consciousness the exception, and beneath it a dynamic unconscious of repressed, motivated content -- wishes and memories held out of awareness because they are unbearable, still pressing for expression and returning in disguise as slips, dreams, and symptoms -- with a preconscious antechamber of merely-latent, accessible material between it and awareness. Twentieth-century experimental psychology confirmed his central claim that most of the mind runs outside awareness, while revising his account of what that unconscious is: Kihlstrom's cognitive unconscious and Wilson's adaptive unconscious document a vast, fast, efficient non-conscious machinery that perceives, evaluates, and acts before the conscious self is consulted -- not repressed but never conscious -- and Nisbett and Wilson showed that people, lacking access to these processes, confabulate reasons for behaviour they cannot actually introspect. Contemporary neuropsychoanalysis (Solms) works to reconcile the dynamic and the neuroscientific unconscious. The engine takes the out-of-awareness region as its anchor and the dynamic, adaptive, and preconscious kinds as its expressions. The altitudes are those: the unconscious, and its three kinds.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The awareness monitor (metacognition)**: The engine models what lies outside awareness, not the monitor that tracks what is in it. The knowing-what-one-knows loop -- the feeling of knowing, the judgment of learning, the calibration of confidence -- is owned by the metacognition engine; the unconscious is precisely what that monitor cannot see, and its unreliability about the unconscious (confabulated reasons, false confidence) is a failure of the monitor, not a function of this engine. Metacognition watches the conscious mind; the unconscious is the part it cannot watch.
- **The repressing mechanisms (defense)**: The engine models the region where repressed content lives, not the manoeuvres that put it there. The anxiety-driven distortions by which the ego wards off the unbearable -- repression foremost -- are owned by the defense engine; the dynamic unconscious is the destination of that warding, the place the defended content is held. Defense does the repressing; the unconscious is where the repressed resides.
- **The intrapsychic agencies (structural-model)**: The engine models the descriptive fact of being outside awareness, not the functional agencies of Freud's later theory. The id, ego, and superego -- of which the id is wholly and the ego partly unconscious -- are owned by the structural-model engine; the unconscious cross-cuts them, a property of content and process rather than an agency. The structural model is who is in conflict; the unconscious is that much of that conflict runs unseen.
- **Latent memory (memory)**: The engine models the standing availability of preconscious content, not the storing and retrieving of it. How a persona encodes, consolidates, and recovers memories -- episodic, semantic, procedural -- is owned by the memory engine; the preconscious is the descriptive state of that content when it is available but not attended. Memory is the store and the retrieval; the preconscious is the accessible-but-latent condition of what is stored.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per file.

- **[the-unconscious](position_the_unconscious.md)** (the anchor, position): The region of mind outside awareness that still shapes the persona. Anchored by Freud (the unconscious) and the cognitive science that re-founded it (Kihlstrom; Wilson).
- **[dynamic](position_dynamic.md)** (position, kind): The repressed, motivated unconscious -- content held out of awareness because unbearable, pressing and returning disguised. Anchored by Freud (the dynamic unconscious; the return of the repressed).
- **[adaptive](position_adaptive.md)** (position, kind): The automatic, never-conscious unconscious -- fast efficient processing that judges and acts before awareness. Anchored by Kihlstrom (the cognitive unconscious), Wilson (the adaptive unconscious), and Nisbett & Wilson (confabulation).
- **[preconscious](position_preconscious.md)** (position, kind): The accessible unconscious -- latent content available to awareness on call. Anchored by Freud (the descriptive preconscious).
