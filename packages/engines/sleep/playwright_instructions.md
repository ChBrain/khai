# Instructions: Sleep

How a Playwright wires the sleep engine into a play. You wire by linking the play's own content to the engine's files; the engine files stay untouched, they are canon. The Roadie plumbs the engine's law into Instructions Knowledge on deploy. Authoring guidance, not runtime content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human decides first whether the persona can afford to stop watching, because that decides whether anything else in the engine gets a chance to run. Then decide what they are carrying, and remember that a persona in deficit cannot report it -- so the evidence has to be in what they do rather than in what they say. This engine is not clinical guidance and should not be used as any.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, a persona links under Projection what is happening to their sleep: whether the surrender is available to them, what the debt has come to, whether they have begun to try, and which clock they are keeping. Expect several at once, since the four movements are independent ways the same thing goes wrong rather than stages of a night. Keep it the requirement and its failure: the tiredness pulling on attention is the body engine's, the drift of thought is mind-wandering's, the brooding is rumination's. You link to the engine, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- The physical demand pulling on attention is the body engine's; self-generated task-unrelated thought is mind-wandering's; repetitive dwelling on distress is rumination's; content outside awareness, including dreams, is the-unconscious's and imagination's; the mending of a worn object is restoration's, which shares the word and nothing else; how long the night felt is time-perception's; being required to be available is waiting's and employment's; what a diagnosed condition makes of a persona socially is illness's. Whether a production's persona is genuinely in the effort loop rather than merely tired, and whether their vigilance is warranted or anxious, are the team's review.

## Knowledge

- Sleep is what a persona must set down before the day's largest need will be met, and the setting down is the whole difficulty, because it cannot be performed. It wires on a persona at Projection and runs in four movements. The surrender is the precondition nobody states: sleep is the deliberate suspension of vigilance, so it requires a body willing to stop monitoring -- the letting go has no controls and no first-person account of its middle, safe enough is judged below the level of decision and cannot be overridden by what the persona has concluded about the risk, and the kept watch is the common partial failure, an ear open for a child or a phone, producing hours that look like a night's sleep to anybody counting. The requirement is graded by circumstance rather than by need, so the persona with least reason to feel safe gets the least sleep and is blamed for the deficits. The debt is cumulative and dose-dependent, and its finding is that subjective sleepiness flattens after a few days while performance keeps declining -- so the persona feels much as they did on the third night, is performing very much worse, and will sincerely insist they are fine; the microsleep then takes the seconds without leaving a gap in their account of the interval. The effort is Espie's sequence and the engine's centre: noticing whether one is drifting requires the monitoring that prevents drifting; deciding to sleep converts a state into a target, and a target comes with a deadline that supplies a reason to check; working at a target recruits effort, and effort is arousal. Each step is reasonable and the three compound, so the persona is at their most awake precisely because they are at their most motivated -- and every failure teaches them they did not try hard enough. The schedule is the social face: chronotype is substantially heritable, the timetable is not, and the same hours are comfortable for one body and a permanent deficit for another, with the weekend catch-up giving genuine relief and pushing the clock further out. The engine's twist is at the root: sleep is the only thing a persona cannot obtain by trying to, since attention, intention, and effort are the documented mechanism of its failure -- and the only route in is to stop minding whether it comes, which cannot be done on purpose either.

## System

- Do decide the surrender first. A persona who cannot afford to stop watching is in a different play from one who simply has too much on their mind.
- Do put the debt in what the persona does rather than in what they say: the missed thing, the short answer, the small error, the drive they should not have made -- while they report feeling fine and mean it.
- Do keep the persona competent in the effort movement. Their reasoning is sound, nothing is wrong with them, and the striving is the illness; a play that makes them anxious or foolish has replaced the mechanism with a temperament.
- Do not release them by having them decide to stop trying. Deciding to stop trying is trying, and a scene in which a persona relaxes and drifts off has staged the one move the engine says is unavailable.
- Do let the kept watch be love as often as fear: somebody has to hear the child, and a persona who slept through would be failing at the thing they most care about.
- Do read the schedule as constitutional rather than moral. The late riser is not undisciplined, and knowing that exonerates without helping, since the timetable does not move.
- Do not model the physical demand (body), thought drifting inward (mind-wandering), brooding (rumination), dreams (the-unconscious, imagination), the felt length of the night (time-perception), or the rota as a term of employment (waiting, employment). Do not make the sleepless night stand for a theme; that is authored key, the play's pitch. Do not edit the engine's files; wire only from the play's side.
