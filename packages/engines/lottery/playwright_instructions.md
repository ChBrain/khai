# Instructions: Lottery

How a Playwright wires the lottery engine into a play. You wire by linking the play's own content to the engine's files; the engine files stay untouched, they are canon. The Roadie plumbs the engine's law into Instructions Knowledge on deploy. Authoring guidance, not runtime content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human makes the impasse real before staging any draw: show the criteria being tried and failing, because a lottery reached for lazily is a different and much less interesting thing. Keep the process clean and the draw honest, since the engine needs no fraud and stops working the moment it gets one. And decide what each persona will make of the result afterwards, because that is the movement the design cannot reach.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, a persona links under Projection where they stand to the draw: whether they entered, which side they came out on, what account they have assembled since, and how many times they have been through it. Read the engine on what the mechanism excluded, never on what it produced. Keep it the allocation by chance: the contest that awards to the highest estimate is bidding's, the reading of an outcome as deserved is belief-in-a-just-world's, the sign found in an unrelated event is superstition's. You link to the engine, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- The contest awarding to the highest estimate is the bidding engine's and the exchange of concessions is negotiation's; the reading of an outcome as deserved is belief-in-a-just-world's and attribution's; a sign found in an unrelated event is superstition's; the hazard and its signalling are risk's and warning's; what a fair hearing buys and the credit given to an account are the heeding composite's, recognition's, and credibility's; the good itself and who needs it are needs's, scarcity's, and commons's; a persona buying tickets for the pleasure of the draw is addiction's and reward's, which is nearly the reverse of this. Ballot rules, weighting, and audit standards are the trade and set the format. Whether a production's claimant filled the vacancy with desert or with an omen, and whether an impasse was genuine or a committee avoiding a decision it could have made, are the team's review.

## Knowledge

- A lottery is a good allocated deliberately at random, and what a decision with no reasons in it does to the people it lands on. It is not bidding's contest awarding to the highest estimate, not belief-in-a-just-world's reading of an outcome as deserved, and not superstition's link between an act and an unrelated result. It wires on a persona at Projection and runs in four movements. The impasse is arrived at by trying hardest: a fixed count converts a resource problem into a series of refusals that a person must make to a person; the claims turn out incommensurable rather than equal, so ranking by need, by benefit, by time waited and by contribution produces four different orders and more information makes it worse; and the decider who would be answerable for any criterion is relieved by an instrument that also leaves the claimant with nobody to ask. The drawing is designed to be empty and the emptiness is the product: the ceremony matters more than the algorithm, because what claimants need is an occasion at which nobody could have interfered rather than a guarantee about the mathematics; keeping every reason out is the only reliable way to keep the wrong ones out, so the device holds out the merit with the corruption because it cannot tell them apart; and the single run is binding precisely because a fair mechanism and a rigged one produce indistinguishable single results. The verdict treats everybody equally and lands unequally, since a good with no story is easy to keep and a refusal with no story is very hard to hold -- the loser was spared a judgement, which is a real mercy and is only legible from outside, and the absence of an appeal removes not just the chance of reversal but the occasion on which somebody would have considered them. The engine's twist is at the root: a lottery works by admitting no reasons, which is why everybody it touches invents one -- within a season the allocation has acquired desert, character, and destiny, all of it manufactured and none of it in the mechanism.

## System

- Do make the impasse real. Show the criteria being tried and failing, because a lottery chosen without that is a shrug rather than an instrument.
- Do keep the draw honest and the process clean. The engine needs no fraud, and staging a rigged ballot replaces the mechanism with a swindle.
- Do give the drawing a ceremony. The drum, the sealed envelope, the witness from each side -- the theatre is what is trusted, and the mathematics is not what anybody is watching.
- Do put the relief on the decider. Somebody is glad not to have had to choose, and that gladness is part of why the method was adopted.
- Do stage the loser with nothing to do next. No criterion failed, no gap to close, nobody to write to -- and let the scene stay uncomfortable rather than resolving it.
- Do let the winner go looking for grounds. They cannot hold something four hundred people wanted with no account of why, and the search starts within days.
- Do put the twist in an explanation. A persona says what the result meant, it sounds like ordinary sense, and the mechanism spent all its effort ensuring there was nothing there to mean anything.
- Do close on the next draw rather than the verdict. Somebody decides whether to enter again on a record that contains no information, and the field thins by discouragement.
- Do not model the priced contest (bidding, negotiation), the desert reading (belief-in-a-just-world, attribution), the sign (superstition), the hazard (risk, warning), the fair hearing (heeding, recognition, credibility), the good itself (needs, scarcity, commons), or gambling for pleasure (addiction, reward). Do not make the draw stand for a theme; that is authored key, the play's pitch. Do not edit the engine's files; wire only from the play's side.
