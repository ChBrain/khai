# Lottery: Reference

## Line of Work

Lottery as **process**: a good allocated deliberately at random, and what a decision with no reasons in
it does to the people it lands on.

The engine's subject is a mechanism whose value is an absence. Stone's account is the spine, and it
identifies the thing precisely: a lottery does not produce an allocation that matches the claims, it
produces one with no considerations in it at all -- and that is a narrower and more defensible good.
He calls it the sanitising effect. A rule that admits good reasons has to specify them, a specification
can be argued around, and the arguing is where advantage re-enters; a device that hears nothing cannot
be talked to. Everything anybody admires about lotteries follows from that and nothing else.

The boundary with what khai already carries is clean. The contest in which several parties price one
thing and it goes to the highest is **bidding**'s -- an allocation by estimate, where this is an
allocation by nothing. The reading of an outcome as deserved, and the machinery behind it, is
**belief-in-a-just-world**'s; the link between an act and an unrelated result is **superstition**'s.
Both arrive in the filling movement uninvited, and both mechanisms are theirs: this engine reads only
that a design built to exclude them cannot. The hazard and its estimation are **risk**'s, and the sense
that a procedure treated one properly is **heeding**'s and **recognition**'s.

**The impasse** is arrived at by trying hardest. **The short supply** is a hard count -- beds, seats,
kidneys, a statute -- so the question is never how to satisfy the claims but which to refuse, and the
refusing has to be done by a person to a person. **The tied claims** are not equal but incommensurable:
rank by need and one order appears, by benefit expected another, by time waited a third, and each is
defensible, so more information about the claimants makes the ranking harder rather than easier. **The
exposed decider** would be answerable for whichever criterion they chose, and the lottery's removal of
them from the answer is a real part of why it gets adopted -- which is also the moment the refused
claimant loses anybody to ask.

**The drawing** is designed to be empty, and the emptiness is the product. **The visible chance** is
trusted for its theatre: a cryptographically sound private draw is more random than a man pulling
capsules from a drum and is trusted less, because what claimants need is an occasion at which nobody
could have interfered rather than a guarantee about the mathematics. **The no reasons** rule is a virtue
that cannot be applied selectively, so it holds out the merit along with the corruption because it
cannot tell them apart. **The single run** is binding precisely because it cannot be checked, since a
fair mechanism and a rigged one produce indistinguishable single results.

**The verdict** treats everybody equally and lands on them unequally. A good with no story is easy to
keep; a refusal with no story is very hard to hold. **The winner unearned** is in a place four hundred
people wanted with no account of occupying it, which most find uncomfortable and most resolve quickly.
**The loser unanswered** was spared a judgement -- a genuine mercy, and one only legible from outside --
and is left with an absence that cannot be argued with, improved on, or reported to anybody. **The no
appeal** protects the process and removes the hearing, since an appeal is not only a remedy but the
form in which a person is considered at all.

Hence the **twist**, at the root. **A lottery works by admitting no reasons, which is why everybody it
touches invents one.** The achievement lasts about as long as the announcement, because a persona
cannot hold a large fact about their life with nothing attached to it. Within a season the allocation
has acquired desert, character, and destiny -- all of it manufactured, none of it in the mechanism, and
nobody having done anything wrong.

The engine is built so that nobody has to be cheating for any of it to run, and a play should keep it
that way. The impasse was real. The draw was honest. Nobody was wronged.

**On the evidence, stated plainly.** This is a political-philosophy engine with an empirical apron that
is thinner than the subject deserves, and the proportions should be visible. **Stone's sanitising
effect is an argument, not a finding**, and it is the load-bearing claim here: that a lottery's virtue
consists in excluding reasons rather than in producing a fair match is a normative thesis, well
defended and contested, and no measurement bears on it. **Elster's survey of when societies randomise
is historical and comparative**, and it establishes the pattern -- lotteries appear where the claims are
incommensurable and the decider is exposed -- without establishing that the pattern is a good one.
**The verdict movement's asymmetry is an inference.** That a refusal with no reason is harder to hold
than a good with no reason is highly plausible, it is consistent with the procedural-justice
literature on wanting an account, and nobody has measured it against reasoned refusals in the same
allocation, which would be the study that settles it. **Acceptability varies enormously by domain and
the engine does not explain the variation.** Randomisation is accepted without complaint for draft
numbers, visas, and school places, and is close to intolerable for organ allocation and disaster
triage, and no account here says why -- which is a real gap, since the pattern is one of the more
interesting facts about the subject. **The filling movement borrows two mechanisms it does not own**:
the reading of an outcome as deserved belongs to the belief-in-a-just-world engine and the reading of a
draw as a sign belongs to superstition, both of which have their own evidence, and this engine claims
only that a design built to exclude them fails to. **The next-draw member's selection claim is
speculative.** That repeated bad luck thins the field by discouragement, so that a fair mechanism
produces an unfair population over time, follows from the members above it and has not been
demonstrated. What should not be taken from this engine is that lotteries are bad allocations, that
people who accept them are naive, or that the reasons supplied afterwards are a failure of character.
The claim is narrow: the impasse is genuine, the emptiness is the point, the emptiness is unequally
survivable, and the vacancy fills.

## Origin

The political theory of allocation by chance, wired as one process on a persona. The twist is Stone's;
the account of when societies reach for it is Elster's.

| Source                               | Key Work                                                                    | Scope                                                                                                                                                                       |
| :----------------------------------- | :-------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Peter Stone**                      | _The Luck of the Draw: The Role of Chance in Public Decision-Making_ (2011) | **The twist.** The sanitising effect: a lottery's virtue is that it keeps reasons out, which is why it keeps the bad ones out. The root and the drawing.                    |
| **Jon Elster**                       | _Solomonic Judgements: Studies in the Limitations of Rationality_ (1989)    | When and why societies resort to randomisation: incommensurable claims, and a decider who cannot defend any criterion. The impasse movement. Used here, also cited by meal. |
| **Barbara Goodwin**                  | _Justice by Lottery_ (1992)                                                 | The case for allocating far more by lot than anybody does, and the objections it draws. The engine's framing and the acceptability question it leaves open.                 |
| **John Broome**                      | _"Selecting People Randomly"_ (Ethics, 1984)                                | Fairness as giving each claim a chance proportional to its strength, and why a lottery answers claims that a straight choice extinguishes. The verdict movement.            |
| **Guido Calabresi & Philip Bobbitt** | _Tragic Choices_ (1978)                                                     | Allocation of scarce life-saving goods, and the devices societies use to avoid stating the values behind their choices. The short supply and exposed decider members.       |
| **Melvin Lerner**                    | _The Belief in a Just World_ (1980)                                         | The reading of outcomes as deserved. Owned by the belief-in-a-just-world engine; the filling movement names the arrival and hands the mechanism back.                       |

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The contest that awards to the highest estimate (bidding, negotiation)**: One indivisible thing
  priced separately by several parties, and the exchange of concessions toward terms, are those
  engines'. A lottery is the allocation that runs when the parties cannot be ranked at all, which is
  the opposite arrangement.
- **The reading of an outcome as deserved (belief-in-a-just-world, attribution)**: Why people take
  what happens to them as fitting, and how causes get assigned, are those engines'. The desert-read
  member states that the reading arrives and hands the mechanism back.
- **A sign found in an unrelated event (superstition, meaning)**: The link between an act and a result
  it has no connection to is the superstition engine's. The omen member reads only that a lottery
  attracts it reliably.
- **The hazard and its size (risk, warning)**: What the danger is, how it is estimated, and how it is
  signalled are those engines'. A lottery allocates a good under certainty about the odds, which is a
  different problem from acting under uncertainty about the world.
- **Being properly heard by a procedure (heeding, recognition, credibility)**: What a fair hearing
  buys, what institutional acknowledgement does, and the credit given to an account are those
  packages'. The no-appeal member reads the specific case where the procedure has removed the occasion
  on which anybody would have considered the claimant.
- **The good itself and who needs it (needs, scarcity, commons)**: What is being allocated, why it is
  wanted, and how a shared resource is governed are those engines'. The engine is indifferent to the
  content of the good and reads the method.
- **Gambling (addiction, reward, desire)**: A persona buying tickets for the pleasure of the draw is
  in those engines'. This one models allocation imposed on claimants who did not seek a gamble, which
  is nearly the reverse.
- **The design of the mechanism (the world)**: Ballot rules, weighting, tie-breaks, audit standards,
  and the statutes behind them are matters of practice and law. They set the format; this engine reads
  what any device that decides by chance does to the people in front of it.
- **The draw as authored symbol (the play's pitch)**: A lot cast in order to **stand for** a theme --
  the ballot that mirrors a motif of fate -- is refused: that is authored key, the play's pitch.
  Lottery sets what the mechanism excluded and left; the meaning read into it is the play's.

## Encoding

Source to constraint. A process tree: one root, four movements, twelve forms.

- **[lottery](process_lottery.md)** (the root): a good allocated deliberately at random, read on what
  the mechanism excluded, carrying the twist -- a lottery works by admitting no reasons, which is why
  everybody it touches invents one. Anchored by Stone (2011).
- **[the impasse](process_impasse_lottery.md)**: the situation that makes chance the least bad
  instrument. Anchored by Elster (1989) and Calabresi & Bobbitt (1978).
  [the short supply](process_the_short_supply.md), [the tied claims](process_the_tied_claims.md),
  [the exposed decider](process_the_exposed_decider.md).
- **[the drawing](process_drawing_lottery.md)**: a mechanism whose whole virtue is that it admits
  nothing. Anchored by Stone (2011). [the visible chance](process_the_visible_chance.md),
  [the no reasons](process_the_no_reasons.md), [the single run](process_the_single_run.md).
- **[the verdict](process_verdict_lottery.md)**: what the winner and the loser are each handed.
  Anchored by Broome (1984). [the winner unearned](process_the_winner_unearned.md),
  [the loser unanswered](process_the_loser_unanswered.md), [the no appeal](process_the_no_appeal.md).
- **[the filling](process_filling_lottery.md)**: what goes into the space where the reason should have
  been, and where the engine is seen for one thing. Anchored by Lerner (1980), whose mechanism belongs
  to belief-in-a-just-world. [the desert read](process_the_desert_read.md), [the omen](process_the_omen.md),
  [the next draw](process_the_next_draw.md).
