# Comparison: Reference

## Line of Work

Social comparison as **process**: how a persona evaluates their own opinions and abilities by measuring against others, so the self is known relative to others rather than in the absolute. The domain does not model the pain at another's superiority, which is the envy engine's; nor the persona's rank within an order, which is the status engine's; nor the resting global self-attitude, which is the self-esteem engine's; nor the catalog of comparison tilts held as static positions, which is the bias engine's. It models the evaluative measuring itself: the drive to appraise the self against others, and the choice of target -- upward, downward, or similar -- by which that appraisal is set.

The spine is Festinger's theory of social comparison and the tradition that extended it. Festinger held that people have a drive to evaluate their opinions and abilities, that in the absence of objective, non-social standards they do so by comparing with others, and that they prefer to compare with those similar to themselves, since only a similar other yields a diagnostic reading. Later work articulated the two directions and their functions: upward comparison, with the better-off, informs and can inspire but also threatens (Wheeler); downward comparison, with the worse-off, enhances the self and is recruited under threat (Wills). Suls, Martin, and Wheeler synthesized the field around who compares, with whom, and with what effect. Three routes carry it: upward, downward, similar.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **Envy**: The engine models the evaluative measuring of the self against a superior other, not the pain that measuring can provoke. The distressing awareness of another's advantage and the hostile longing for it are owned by the envy engine; upward comparison is upstream -- it supplies the gap that envy may feed on -- but owns the appraisal, not the emotion, so the engine delegates the pain at another's superiority to envy and keeps the comparison itself.
- **Status**: The engine models the act of comparing the self to others, not the standing that results. A persona's rank within an order -- their position in a hierarchy -- is owned by the status engine; comparison is the evaluative process by which a persona reads their relative standing, status the standing read. The engine delegates the rank itself to status and keeps the measuring.
- **Self-esteem**: The engine models a comparison as an input to self-appraisal, not the resting global attitude toward the self. The persona's standing, positive or negative, self-regard as a durable position is owned by the self-esteem engine; comparison is one of the processes that feed it, an episode of measuring rather than the settled self-attitude. The engine delegates the global self-attitude to self-esteem.
- **Bias**: The engine models the process of social comparison, not the catalog of comparison-related tilts held as static positions. The standing entries a persona carries -- social comparison, assumed similarity, and their kin -- are the bias engine's list of tilts; this engine owns the dynamic beneath them, the measuring of the self against a chosen other. The engine delegates the position-list of tilts to bias and keeps the process.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per file.

- **[comparison](process_comparison.md)** (the root): The drive to evaluate one's opinions and abilities by measuring against others, the self known by the gap where no objective standard is at hand. Anchored by Festinger (a theory of social comparison processes) and Suls, Martin & Wheeler (why, with whom, with what effect).
- **[upward](process_upward.md)** (form): The better-off route -- self-evaluation against a superior other, double-edged as a spur to improve or a wound to the self, turning on whether the higher mark is attainable. Anchored by Wheeler (upward comparison) and Festinger (the abilities drive).
- **[downward](process_downward.md)** (form): The worse-off route -- self-enhancement by contrast with those below, a defense recruited most under threat. Anchored by Wills (downward comparison principles).
- **[similar](process_similar.md)** (form): The target-selection route -- the similar-other principle, a persona comparing with those like themselves because only a like other yields a diagnostic read. Anchored by Festinger (the similar-other principle) and Tesser (self-evaluation maintenance).
