# Process: Comparison

## Taxonomy

Social psychology / the theory of social comparison.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: comparison

## Initiated by

A persona wants to know where they stand -- how able, how right, how well-off -- and, finding no fixed yardstick in the world, turns to other people to measure themselves.
This is social comparison: the drive to evaluate one's own opinions and abilities by measuring against others, so the self is known relative to others rather than in the absolute.
Lacking an objective standard, the persona reads their worth off the gap between themselves and those they compare with.

## Direction

Toward a self appraised by its distance from others -- above, below, or alongside.
Social comparison runs where objective measures fail: one cannot know if an opinion is sound or an ability adequate in a vacuum, so the persona seeks others as a gauge, and the comparison sets the reading -- better than these, worse than those, on a par with the like.
The direction is relative and social: the same performance feels excellent beside the weak and poor beside the strong, so the self a persona arrives at depends less on the absolute fact than on whom they have measured it against.

## Lever

The standing of others, spent as the yardstick by which the self is judged.
Comparison runs on the informativeness of a chosen other: a persona evaluates themselves by selecting a target and reading the gap, so the choice of whom to compare with -- better, worse, or similar -- sets what the self will look like, and the drive to a stable self-estimate keeps the comparing going.
The lever is the relative measure: because worth here has no absolute scale, it is constituted by the comparison, and shifting the target shifts the self-appraisal without any change in the person themselves.

## Echo

The self is measured against others, and what it comes to seem depends on whom it is set beside.
Social comparison is why a raise feels like a cut when a peer got more, why we scan the room to gauge how we are doing, why the same grade elates in a strong class and deflates in a weak one -- the self sized by the company it keeps.
It runs as [upward](process_upward.md), [downward](process_downward.md), or [similar](process_similar.md) -- the look at those better off, the look at those worse off, the choice of a like other as the diagnostic yardstick. The pain at another's superiority is the envy engine's; the persona's rank in an order is the status engine's; the resting global self-attitude is the self-esteem engine's; the catalog of comparison tilts held as static positions is the bias engine's. This engine owns the evaluative measuring of the self against others.
