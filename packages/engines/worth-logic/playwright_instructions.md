# Instructions: Worth-Logic

How a Playwright wires the worth-logic engine into a play. You wire by linking the play's own content to the engine's files; the engine files stay untouched, they are canon. The Roadie plumbs the engine's law into Instructions Knowledge on deploy. Authoring guidance, not runtime content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human sets the worth terrain: where the persona's culture locates its worth, and the affronts and challenges -- an insult, a loss of standing, a breach of the order -- that put that worth to the test.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, a persona links the worth-logic it runs on under its Projection: dignity, worth inherent and inalienable, insults deflected; honor, worth precarious and reputation-staked, a slight answered even by force; face, worth conferred by position, held by propriety and harmony. Read the persona by how it meets an affront -- letting it slide, avenging it, or absorbing it to restore the order. You link to the engine, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- The interactional face-work of claiming a public self-image and softening a face-threatening act -- the politeness strategies of Brown and Levinson -- is the face engine's; worth-logic's face form is the cultural rule beneath it, not the moment's face-management. The felt pride, shame, and anger of worth won, lost, and defended are the pride, shame, and anger engines'; worth-logic sets what they are about. Honor as a personal virtue of integrity is the virtue engine's. The structure of rank itself is the hierarchy engine's. This engine supplies the cultural logic of worth.

## Knowledge

- A worth-logic is a persona's standing cultural rule for how its personal worth is assigned and defended (Leung & Cohen): where worth comes from, and what a threat to it demands. Three logics answer differently. Dignity: worth is inherent, equal, and inalienable, each person's by being a person and not in others' gift -- so it is internally anchored, insults are deflected as powerless, and disputes go to conscience and impartial law. Honor: worth is both self-claimed and socially-conferred, a compound of self-respect and reputation, and therefore precarious -- it must be actively defended, an insult or challenge answered, often by retaliation or even violence, or it is forfeit (Nisbett & Cohen). Face: worth is conferred by others according to one's position in a stable hierarchy and held by fulfilling one's role -- maintained by humility, propriety, and harmony, and lost by disrupting the order or failing one's place. The same insult lands differently in each: nothing to a dignity-holder, a wound to be avenged for an honor-holder, a tear in the harmony to be repaired for a face-holder. Read a persona by the worth-logic it runs on.

## System

- Do locate the persona's worth where its logic locates it -- inherent, precarious, or conferred -- and let it meet an affront as the logic prescribes: deflecting, avenging, or restoring harmony.
- Do let each logic carry its cost: the dignity-holder's blindness to real reputational damage, the honor-holder's entrapment in ruinous fights, the face-holder's bondage to an order it cannot breach.
- Do let a clash of logics drive the drama -- a dignity-holder baffled that an insult should cost anything, an honor-holder reading a turned cheek as weakness, a face-holder scandalized by open self-assertion.
- Do not play the interactional softening of a face-threat as worth-logic; that is the face engine. Do not play the felt pride, shame, or anger as worth-logic; those are their own engines. Do not play honor-as-integrity here; that is virtue. Do not edit the engine's files; wire only from the play's side.
