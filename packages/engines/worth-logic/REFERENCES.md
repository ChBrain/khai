# Worth-Logic: Reference

## Line of Work

Worth-logic as a **position** engine: the cultural logic by which a persona's personal worth is assigned and defended -- where its worth comes from, and how it must be protected -- taken across the three logics a culture can run on. The domain does not model the interactional face-work of softening a face-threatening act, which is the face engine's; nor the felt pride and shame of worth won and lost, which are the pride and shame engines'; nor honor as a personal virtue of integrity, which is the virtue engine's. It models the standing cultural rule beneath a persona's sense of its own worth, and how that rule tells it to meet an affront.

The spine is Nisbett and Cohen's culture of honor and Leung and Cohen's typology of cultural logics of self-worth. Nisbett and Cohen documented the culture of honor -- a worth staked on reputation and defended, where necessary, by violence, and traced its persistence in regions whose history rewarded a fierce readiness to retaliate. Leung and Cohen set honor within a three-way typology that also answers, differently, the question of where worth comes from and how it is held. In dignity cultures, worth is inherent, equal, and inalienable: each person has it by virtue of being a person, it is internally anchored and cannot be given or taken by others' opinions, and insults are deflected while disputes go to conscience and impartial law. In honor cultures, worth is both self-claimed and socially-conferred, a compound of self-respect and reputation, and therefore precarious: it must be actively defended, an insult or challenge answered, or it is forfeit. In face cultures, worth is conferred by others according to one's position in a stable social hierarchy and maintained by fulfilling the obligations of one's role: it is held by humility, propriety, and the preserving of harmony, and lost by disrupting the order or failing one's place. The engine takes the cultural logic of worth as its anchor and the three logics as its expressions. The altitudes are those: the worth-logic, and its dignity, honor, and face forms.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **Interactional face-work (face)**: The engine models the cultural logic of worth, not the moment-to-moment management of face in interaction. How a persona claims a public self-image and softens or bares a face-threatening act -- the bald, solidarity, deference, hint, and forbear strategies of Brown and Levinson -- is owned by the face engine; worth-logic's face form is the standing cultural rule (worth conferred by position, held by harmony) that face-culture runs on, not the interactional politeness that plays out under any culture. Same word, two layers: the cultural worth-logic here, the interactional face-work there.
- **The worth emotions (pride, shame, anger)**: The engine models the rule that assigns and defends worth, not the emotions that register its gain and loss. The felt swell of pride at worth affirmed, the sting of shame at worth lost, and the anger that fuels an honor defense are owned by the pride, shame, and anger engines; worth-logic sets what those emotions are about and when they are warranted, but the feelings themselves are theirs. The logic is the rulebook; the emotions are the scoreboard's charge.
- **Honor as personal virtue (virtue)**: The engine models honor as a cultural logic of worth, not honor as a trait of individual integrity. The disposition to be honest, principled, and true to one's word -- honor as a virtue of character -- is owned by the virtue engine; worth-logic's honor form is the reputational, defended worth of the honor culture, which can coexist with or diverge from personal integrity. The culture's honor is a standing to be guarded; the virtue's honor is a goodness to be kept.
- **Status and hierarchy as such (hierarchy)**: The engine models how worth is culturally assigned, not the structure of rank itself. The ordering of persons by standing, dominance, and deference is owned by the hierarchy engine; worth-logic's face form draws on a stable hierarchy but owns the rule that worth is conferred by one's place in it, not the mechanics of the ranking. Hierarchy builds the ladder; worth-logic says whether standing on it is what your worth is made of.
