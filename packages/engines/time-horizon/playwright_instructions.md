# Instructions: Time-horizon

How a Playwright wires the time-horizon engine into a play. You wire by linking the play's own content to the engine's files; the engine files stay untouched, they are canon. The Roadie plumbs the engine's law into Instructions Knowledge on deploy. Authoring guidance, not runtime content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human sets each persona's time-horizon: whether it feels its future open-ended and invests toward it, or foreshortened and invests in the present and its closest ties -- and remembers the horizon tracks perceived time-left, not age, so an ending can foreshorten a young persona's and a reprieve reopen an old one's.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, a persona links under Projection the horizon it runs on: expansive, the open-ended future weighted toward knowledge, new ties, and expansion; or limited, the foreshortened future weighted toward emotional meaning, depth, and the savored present. Read it in what the persona prioritizes and prunes -- who is building toward a later, who is turning to what matters now -- and set it by the time the persona feels it has, not by its years. Keep it the reweighting by perceived time-left, not the stance toward death (mortality), the future self-images pursued (possible-selves), the relishing itself (savoring), or the sense of duration (time-perception). You link to the engine, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- The stance a persona takes toward its own death is the mortality engine's; the future self-images it pursues are possible-selves'; the relishing of a present moment is savoring's; the subjective duration and time-perspective are time-perception's. Time-horizon is the survivable core of the life-course branch -- the motivational reweighting Carstensen's socioemotional selectivity found in perceived time-left. Whether the horizon rings true to the persona, and reads from time-left rather than age, is the team's review.

## Knowledge

- Time-horizon is a persona's resting sense of how much time it has left, and how that perceived horizon reweights its goals (Carstensen's socioemotional selectivity theory). It wires on a persona at Projection, and it is the motivational reweighting perceived time-left produces. Two horizons carry it -- expansive is the future felt as open-ended, so the persona invests in what pays off later: gathering knowledge, meeting new people, expanding its world, deferring the present for future gain; limited is the future felt as foreshortened, so the persona invests in what pays off now: deepening its closest ties, savoring the present, seeking emotional meaning over information, pruning the shallow and the merely useful. The horizon tracks perceived time-left, not age: any ending -- a diagnosis, a departure, a last season -- foreshortens it, and any reopening expands it, and the goals shift with it. Read a persona by the horizon it holds, and read the limited horizon two ways: the foreshortening that reads as loss is also the clarity that turns a persona toward what matters most, so a short horizon is a deeper way of spending time, not only less of it.

## System

- Do give each persona a horizon under Projection; do read it in what it prioritizes and prunes under pressure of time-left -- building toward a later, or turning to what matters now -- shown, not narrated.
- Do set the horizon by perceived time-left, not by age: an ending foreshortens a young persona's horizon and turns it toward depth; a reprieve reopens an old one's and lets it plan and gather again.
- Do read the limited horizon as the engine's twist: the foreshortening is not only loss but a clarifying -- the persona turned toward its closest ties and its most meaningful moments, spending a short time deeply.
- Do not model the stance toward death (mortality), the future self-images (possible-selves), the relishing itself (savoring), or the sense of duration and time-perspective (time-perception). Do not fix a persona's horizon by its years. Do not edit the engine's files; wire only from the play's side.
