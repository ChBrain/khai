# Time-horizon: Reference

## Line of Work

Time-horizon as **position**: a persona's resting sense of how much time it has left, and
how that perceived horizon reweights its goals -- taken across the two horizons a persona
rests in. It wires on a persona at Projection, the standing time-perspective the persona
brings to what it pursues, because the horizon reweights goals before any single choice. The
domain does not model the stance a persona takes toward its own death, which is the mortality
engine's; nor the future self-images it pursues, which are possible-selves'; nor the
relishing of a present moment, which is savoring's; nor the subjective experience of duration
and time-perspective, which is time-perception's. It models the motivational reweighting that
perceived time-left produces.

The spine is Carstensen's socioemotional selectivity theory. Carstensen's finding is that the
perception of time as open-ended or limited is itself a motivator: when a persona sees its
future as expansive, it prioritizes future-oriented goals -- acquiring knowledge, meeting new
people, expanding its horizons, accepting present costs for future gain; when it sees its
future as foreshortened, it prioritizes emotionally meaningful goals in the present --
deepening its closest ties, savoring, seeking felt meaning over information, and pruning the
peripheral. The decisive point is that the reweighting tracks perceived time-left, not age:
the shift is the same for an old person and for a young one whose horizon is foreshortened by
illness or an ending, and it reverses when the horizon reopens. And the limited horizon is
not read only as loss -- Carstensen's work shows it brings emotional gains: the positivity
effect, richer relationships, and a clarity about what matters, the foreshortening that
sharpens rather than only diminishes. So the engine gives time-horizon two poles -- expansive
and limited -- the open and foreshortened futures and the goals each invites, the twist
carried in the limited pole.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **Mortality**: The engine models the reweighting of goals by perceived time-left, not the
  stance toward death. How a persona holds the fact that it ends -- the posture toward its own
  finitude when death is made salient -- is the mortality engine's (and terror management's);
  time-horizon is motivational, not existential: a foreshortened horizon reweights what a persona
  pursues without necessarily invoking death at all, and any ending, not only mortal danger,
  foreshortens it. Mortality is the stance toward the end; time-horizon is what the nearness of an
  end does to the goals.
- **Possible-selves**: The engine models which goals are weighted, not the future self-images
  themselves. What a persona hopes, fears, or expects to become is the possible-selves engine's;
  time-horizon is the reweighting -- an open horizon pursues the expansive possible selves, a
  limited one the present and relational ones -- but the future selves are possible-selves'
  content, and time-horizon is the horizon that selects among them.
- **Savoring**: The engine models why a limited horizon turns a persona toward the present, not
  the relishing itself. The deliberate heightening and prolonging of a present positive experience
  is the savoring engine's; time-horizon is the motivational condition -- a foreshortened horizon
  makes savoring a priority -- but the act of savoring is savoring's.
- **Time-perception**: The engine models the reweighting by perceived time-left, not the
  experience of time. The subjective sense of duration, and the dispositional time-perspective (a
  persona's habitual focus on past, present, or future), are the time-perception engine's;
  time-horizon is specifically Carstensen's socioemotional-selectivity mechanism -- how much
  future a persona feels it has, and what that does to its goals -- not how time feels or which
  time-zone it dwells in.
- **The horizon itself is not engine content, and it is not fixed to age**: The engine declares
  the two horizons exist; which one a persona holds lives on that persona, under Projection, and
  it tracks perceived time-left, not age -- a young persona can hold the limited horizon and an
  old one the expansive. The engine refuses to fix a persona's horizon by its years.
