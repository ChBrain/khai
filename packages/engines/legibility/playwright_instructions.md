# Instructions: Legibility

How a Playwright wires the legibility engine into a play. You wire by linking the play's own
content to the engine's files; the engine files stay untouched, they are canon. The Roadie
plumbs the engine's law into Instructions Knowledge on deploy. Authoring guidance, not
runtime content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human sets which places are read by their image -- the paths one moves along, the edges that bound them, the districts one is inside of, the nodes one enters, or the landmarks one steers by -- and whether that readability is the place's quiet background or a live actor in the play.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, a place whose readability is an actor links under Shown the element of its image: the path one moves along, the edge that bounds it, the district one is inside of, the node one enters, or the landmark one steers by. Play the legibility as the place's readable image present in what it puts before whoever enters -- the street that threads the town, the river that bounds the quarter, the old town one knows one has entered, the square the ways converge on, the tower seen down every street -- shown, never narrated, and never standing in for a persona's own sense of direction. Keep it the place's readability, not its layout and mood (that is space), its sound (soundscape), its social life (street-life), or a persona's own way-finding gift (the persona's engines). You link to the engine, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- The layout and standing mood is space's; the sound is soundscape's; the social life is street-life's; a persona's own orientation is theirs. Legibility is the wayfinding sibling of space and street-life -- the place read not by its shape or its life but by the map it can be held as. Whether the element rings true to the place and its image is the team's review.

## Knowledge

- Legibility is the place read as a navigable image: how clearly a place can be held as a mental map, the quality that lets whoever enters grasp where they are and how to move (Lynch's Image of the City, the public image resolved into five elements, and the imageability a well-formed image depends on). It wires on a place at Shown, and it is the place read as a map one can or cannot hold. Five elements carry it -- path is the channel one moves along, the street and walkway and transit line, the backbone of the image; edge is the linear seam one does not travel, the shore and wall and tracks that bound one section from another; district is the medium-to-large section of common character one enters and is inside of, the quarter and neighborhood; node is the strategic focal point one enters, the junction and square the ways converge on; landmark is the singular external reference point one steers by without entering, the tower and hill and sign held at a distance. Unlike the family's twist-bearing engines, legibility forces no twist: its five elements are equal peers, and the readable-versus-illegible reading -- with even a measure of mystery having its own value -- lives in the whole, not in one element. Read a place by its image: clear in its elements it is held as a map, poor in them it is a blur one is lost in.

## System

- Do give a place whose readability is an actor its element under Shown; do play the legibility as the place's image shown, present not narrated; do keep the reading the readability of the place -- the path, the edge, the district, the node, the landmark -- not a feeling and not a symbol.
- Do read the image two ways: a place clear in its elements is learned fast and moved through with the confidence of knowing where one is; a place poor in them is a daily disorientation, and the same walk never becomes familiar.
- Do let legibility and its opposite both play: a place can be built to be read at a glance, or built to be a maze one is lost in -- and a measure of mystery, the quarter that rewards being explored, is a value and not only a failure.
- Do not model the layout and standing mood (space), the sound (soundscape), the social life (street-life), or a persona's own sense of direction (the persona's engines). Do not make the place's image stand in for a persona's own orientation or its lack. Do not edit the engine's files; wire only from the play's side.
