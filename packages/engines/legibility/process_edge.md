# Process: Edge

## Taxonomy

[Legibility](process_legibility.md), the element of the seam that bounds one section from another.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: legibility

## Initiated by

The place is bounded, and the boundary is read.
Edge starts wherever a place has a linear break that is not itself travelled along -- the shore, the river, the wall, the rail line, the cliff, the ring road -- the seam that runs between two regions and holds them apart.
It is the image's frame: not a channel of movement but a boundary of it, the line the eye reads as the limit of one thing and the start of another, the edge a place is held together at.

## Direction

Between, holding two sections apart and the whole together.
Edge draws the line that bounds -- the waterfront that ends the city and starts the sea, the tracks that split one district from the next, the wall that closes a quarter, the escarpment that cuts the plain -- the seam that gives the map its major joints.
It moves nothing through itself; it organizes by dividing: a strong edge is visible, continuous, and impassable enough to matter, and it lets the mind seam the place into parts, tell this side from that, and hold the boundary as a fixed line of the image.

## Lever

The edge's force as a seam against the break that goes unread.
The carrying force is the edge that reads as a boundary -- continuous, prominent, and clearly the limit of something, the seam a stranger can see and orient by; the resistance is the weak or broken edge, the boundary too faint or too crossed to register, so the sections it should divide bleed together and the joint of the map is lost.
A sharp edge holds the place at its seams and lets the parts be told apart; a blurred one lets them merge, and the place loses the lines the mind would have bounded it by.

## Echo

The place seamed at its boundaries.
Edge leaves the place jointed -- the linear breaks that bound its regions, the seams the whole is held together at -- and hands the mind the lines it divides the place along.
It is the image's frame and its second element: less dominant than the path but the boundary that gives the map its major divisions, the shore and the wall and the rail the place is bounded and joined by.
Edge is read from the bounding, the place known by the seams that hold its sections apart.
