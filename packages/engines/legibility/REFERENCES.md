# Legibility: Reference

## Line of Work

Legibility as **process**: the place read as a navigable image -- how clearly a place can be
held as a mental map, the quality that lets whoever enters grasp where they are and how to
move, as particular to a place as its look and as much an actor as its scenery. It wires on
a place at Shown, the place's self-presentation to whoever enters, because a place is read
to be moved through: the channels one travels along, the seams that bound its sections, the
regions of common character one is inside of, the focal points one enters, and the reference
points one steers by. The domain does not model the layout and standing mood of the setting,
which is space's; nor the sound the place makes, which is soundscape's; nor the social life
it hosts, which is street-life's; nor a persona's own sense of direction, which is theirs.
It models the image the place offers -- the map a place is easy or hard to hold, the
readability it gives the eye or withholds.

The spine is the study of the city image -- the argument that a place is known not as a plan
seen from above but as an image built up in the mind of the person moving through it, and
that a place's form can make that image easy or hard to form. Lynch, working from what
residents of Boston, Jersey City, and Los Angeles actually drew and described of their
cities, found the public image of a place resolves into five kinds of element: paths (the
channels of movement, the dominant element, since people know a place by moving through it),
edges (the linear seams that bound and are not travelled), districts (the medium-to-large
sections of common character one enters and is inside of), nodes (the strategic foci one
enters and pauses at, where paths converge), and landmarks (the singular external points one
steers by without entering). He named the quality a well-formed image depends on
imageability, or legibility -- the apparency that lets a place be vividly and coherently held
-- and showed that a legible place is grasped and moved through with ease while an illegible
one is a standing disorientation. So the engine gives legibility Lynch's five elements as
five equal facets, carrying no forced twist -- the readable-versus-illegible ambivalence, and
even the value of a measure of mystery Lynch himself allowed, lives in the root, not in any
one element.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The layout and standing mood (space)**: How a place is arranged and how it feels to
  stand in -- its size, shape, enclosure, and mood, the actual disposition of its parts -- is
  space's. Legibility is the place's readability, the image the arrangement yields to a mind,
  not the arrangement itself; the two meet in a place (a clear plan is easier to image) but
  are not the same force. Space is how the place is laid out; legibility is how well it can be
  held as a map.
- **The sound the place makes (soundscape)**: The place's auditory field is soundscape's.
  Legibility is the visual and spatial image one navigates by, not the sound; a place can be
  loud and legible or quiet and confusing, and the two are different forces.
- **The social life it hosts (street-life)**: The people out and using the place, the
  contact and the watch and the lingering, is street-life's. Legibility is the readability of
  the place's form, not the life within it; a lively place can be a blur to navigate and a
  legible one can be dead, and the two are different forces.
- **A persona's own sense of direction (the persona's engines)**: Whether a given persona
  reads maps well or gets lost easily, their own way-finding gift or its lack, is the
  persona's, not the place's. Legibility is the image the place offers to anyone; it never
  stands in for a persona's own orientation.
- **The element itself is not engine content**: The engine declares the five elements exist;
  which one a given place shows lives on that place, linked under its Shown chapter. The
  engine refuses to fix a place's image for it.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per file.

- **[legibility](process_legibility.md)** (the root): the place read as a navigable image,
  how clearly it can be held as a mental map, the readability it offers or withholds. Anchored
  by Lynch (the public image and imageability) and the readable-versus-illegible reading.
- **[path](process_path.md)**: the channel one moves along -- the street, the walkway, the
  transit line, the backbone of the image, the way most people know a place by moving through
  it. Anchored by Lynch (paths as the dominant element).
- **[edge](process_edge.md)**: the linear seam that is not travelled -- the shore, the wall,
  the tracks, the break that bounds one section from another and joints the image. Anchored by
  Lynch (edges as boundaries).
- **[district](process_district.md)**: the medium-to-large section of common character one
  enters and is inside of -- the quarter, the neighborhood, the recognizable region. Anchored
  by Lynch (districts as areal regions).
- **[node](process_node.md)**: the strategic focal point one enters -- the junction, the
  square, the interchange, the concentration one pauses at and travels to and from. Anchored by
  Lynch (nodes as strategic foci).
- **[landmark](process_landmark.md)**: the singular external reference point one steers by
  without entering -- the tower, the hill, the sign, the object held at a distance that fixes
  the place. Anchored by Lynch (landmarks as reference points).
