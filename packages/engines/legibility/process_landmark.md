# Process: Landmark

## Taxonomy

[Legibility](process_legibility.md), the element of the reference point one steers by without entering.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: legibility

## Initiated by

The place offers a point held at a distance to steer by.
Landmark starts wherever a place has a singular external object the eye fixes on and orients from without going into it -- the tower, the dome, the hill, the great tree, the sign, the mountain on the horizon -- the reference point picked out from among many and kept in view.
It is the image's fixed star: not entered like a node but sighted from afar, the object a place is located by, the one thing held constant while the traveller moves so that everything else can be placed against it.

## Direction

Outward to a fixed point, sighted and steered by from a distance.
Landmark draws the eye to the singular -- the cathedral seen down every street, the clock tower over the town, the peak that closes the valley, the odd building no other resembles -- the object that stands out from its surroundings and can be seen from many points and angles.
It moves nothing and is not moved through; it orients: a strong landmark is singular, prominent, and visible from far and wide, so the traveller keeps it in the corner of the eye and reads their own position and heading against it, the place fixed and steered by its one held point.

## Lever

The landmark's singularity against the sameness that offers nothing to fix on.
The carrying force is the landmark that stands out -- unique, prominent, sighted from many places, memorable enough to be the thing a stranger is told to steer by and a resident locates home against; the resistance is the surroundings that offer no such point, the uniform skyline and the hidden monument, the place with nothing singular to fix on, so the traveller has no fixed star and every direction reads the same.
A strong landmark is the surest and most-remembered element, the first thing learned and the last forgotten; where none stands out, the place floats unanchored and is held only with difficulty.

## Echo

The place fixed by its steering-points.
Landmark leaves the place anchored to its singular objects -- the external reference points sighted and steered by, the fixed stars the rest of the image is placed against -- and hands the traveller the points they orient and remember by.
It is the image's reference element and often its most vivid: the tower and the hill and the odd building a place is known and navigated by, entered by no one and used by all.
Landmark is read from the sighting, the place known by the singular point it is steered by from a distance.
