# Process: Hydrology

## Taxonomy

Water.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: hydrology

## Initiated by

Water gathers, moves, or fails at the place, and the place's water balance tips.
Hydrology starts wherever the water the place has stops being a given -- the rain gathering to a flood, the dry stretching to a drought, the table falling below the well, the river running or not -- and the place is inside its water balance before anyone gauges it.
Every place stands in a water regime that can drown it, parch it, or sustain it; hydrology is that regime as an actor, not the scenery a river is taken for.

## Direction

Toward the place, in excess, in deficit, in store, and as life.
Four modes work the place's water: [Flood](process_flood.md) is the water in destructive excess, [Drought](process_drought.md) the water in destructive deficit, [Groundwater](process_groundwater.md) the hidden store below, and [Wellspring](process_wellspring.md) the twist -- the same water read as the place's own life.
A place does not hold its water evenly: the surface floods while the aquifer drops, the season of plenty runs to the season of dust, the one river gives the valley both its harvest and its ruin.

## Lever

The water arriving and moving against the place's capacity to hold and shed it.
The carrying force is the water the sky and the ground deliver -- the rain gathered, the snowmelt, the flow upstream, the table's rise; the resistance is the place's own balance -- the soil that soaks or sheds, the channel that carries or overtops, the store that buffers the dry.
When the input exceeds what the place can shed, it floods; when it falls below what the place can draw, it droughts; between the two, the regime is the water the place lives by.
Unlike the rain-event that falls in the hour, this is the balance over the season and the year -- weather delivers the water, hydrology is what the place makes of it over time.

## Echo

The place drowned, parched, watered, or sustained.
Hydrology does not close like a front passing; it is the standing balance the place lives within, tipping toward flood or drought and back, handing the water on to the ground it carves and the life it feeds.
The same water makes and unmakes: the river that floods the town lays the silt that feeds it, the water that fails in drought is the water the aquifer saved, the wetland that drowns the field is the richest ground there is.
The place is dated from its water regime, and every force downstream -- the ground the flow cuts, the green the water raises -- works the water this one gives or withholds.
