# Process: Wellspring

## Taxonomy

[Hydrology](process_hydrology.md), the mode where the water is read as the place's own life.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: hydrology

## Initiated by

The same water that floods and parches, held in balance, is what makes the place livable at all.
Wellspring starts wherever the place's water is a gift rather than a threat -- the perennial river that never fails, the spring that draws the settlement to it, the oasis in the desert, the wetland teeming, the floodplain rich because the water comes and goes.
It is hydrology read the other way: not the water in excess or deficit but the water in its giving, the reason a place is settled where it is.

## Direction

Toward the place made able to live by its water.
Where flood drowns and drought parches, wellspring sustains: the water that waters the crop, fills the well, turns the mill, floats the trade, and gathers the life -- plant, beast, and person -- that a place without water cannot hold.
It moves the place from possible to inhabited: the desert made a garden at the spring, the dry plain made farmland by the river, the settlement grown up around the one reliable water for a hundred miles.

## Lever

The water's gift against the ease of taking it for granted, and losing it.
The carrying force is that reliable water is the rarest and most valued thing a place can offer -- scarce, unfakeable, the difference between a place that lives and one that cannot; the resistance is the neglect that fouls it, drains it, or dams it away, and the flood-and-drought that are the same water turned against the place.
The wellspring rides the knife-edge the whole engine names: the water that is the place's life is the water that, in excess or default, is its ruin.

## Echo

The place alive by its water, and settled for it.
It shows a place made by its water -- the river town, the oasis, the spring-fed valley, the teeming marsh -- and carries beneath it the land's-forces twist, the sibling of the family's ambivalence: that the water which floods and parches is, read the other way and held in balance, the very life of the place.
Wellspring is dated from the settling, the moment the place's water became the reason it was made a place at all.
