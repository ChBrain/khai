# Process: Flood

## Taxonomy

[Hydrology](process_hydrology.md), the mode where the water comes in destructive excess.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: hydrology

## Initiated by

More water arrives than the place can shed, and it rises over the land.
Flood starts wherever the input outruns the outlet -- the river fed faster than its channel can carry, the storm-drain backed up, the snowmelt and the rain together, the sea driven inland by the surge -- and the water leaves its bounds and takes the low ground.
It is water in excess: not the rain falling but the water gathered and risen, standing where the place is meant to be dry.

## Direction

Up and out, over the banks and across the low ground.
Flood spreads to the level the water finds -- the river over its floodplain, the street a channel, the cellar and the field under water, the whole low place a temporary lake.
It moves the place under water: the dry gone wet, the passable gone impassable, the ground the place stood on become the bed the water stands on, until the crest passes and it drains.

## Lever

The volume arriving against the place's power to carry it away.
The carrying force is the water gathered from upstream and above, more than the channel and the soil can take; the resistance is the outlet -- the channel's capacity, the ground's soak, the levee's height -- overwhelmed when the water exceeds it.
Raise the banks and the flood rises with them until it tops them worse; the place cannot temper an excess this large, only delay and steer it.

## Echo

The place drowned, and the water's leavings when it goes.
Flood recedes but leaves the place marked -- the waterline, the silt, the wrecked and the swept-away, the ground saturated and slow to dry -- and hands the low place both its ruin and, in the silt, the richest soil it has.
The flood that drowns is the flood that feeds: the same overflow that wrecks the town lays the fertile mud the valley was settled for.
Flood is dated from the crest, the moment the water stood highest over the place it took.
