# Tipping-point: Reference

## Line of Work

Tipping-point as **process**: the dynamic in which a running process absorbs accumulating
load while appearing stable, crosses a hidden critical threshold, and shifts discontinuously
and irreversibly to a new state -- taken across the phases of the transition. It wires on a
process at Direction, the shape a dynamic's course takes, because the tipping-point is a
shape a process can be in: not the slope it slides down but the edge it jumps from. The
domain does not model the slow, uncorrected slide that loads the system, which is the drift
engine's (drift is all slope and no moment; tipping-point is all moment); nor the loud,
reciprocal spiral of a conflict, which is escalation's; nor the bare inversion of a
situation's direction, which is reversal's; nor the adaptation to the loss once it has
landed, which is grief's. It models the crossing itself -- the accumulation that looks like
safety, and the snap that was never proportional to its trigger. The dynamic is type-generic
-- the same shape stalls a bond, tips a neighborhood, breaks a market, topples a regime --
and is cast here on a process's Direction.

The spine is the mathematics and science of critical transitions -- the study of systems
that change not smoothly but by sudden jumps. Thom's catastrophe theory gave the geometry:
on the cusp, a smooth change in a control variable can carry the system to a fold where its
state leaps discontinuously, and the same control value can correspond to two stable states,
so the path down and the path back do not coincide. Zeeman applied the cusp to human and
social systems -- aggression, prison riots, market panics, anorexia -- and named the
hysteresis by which a system that jumped at one threshold returns only far past it. Bak's
self-organized criticality showed how a slowly loaded system (the sandpile) drives itself to
a critical state where a single grain can trigger an avalanche of any size, cause and effect
decoupled. Scheffer synthesized the modern account of critical transitions in nature and
society: the slow variable eroding resilience unseen, the early-warning signals near the
bifurcation, the regime shift, and the alternative stable states that make the shift hard to
reverse. Schelling and Gladwell carried the tipping idea into social dynamics and popular
understanding. So the engine gives tipping-point four phases -- loading, criticality, snap --
and keeps a fourth, ratchet, the irreversibility read both as the trap and as what makes a
hard-won change durable.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **Drift**: The engine models the sudden crossing, not the slow slide that precedes it. The
  gradual, uncorrected movement of a baseline -- no decision, no event, all slope and no moment
  -- is the drift engine's; tipping-point is all moment. Drift often does the loading that
  brings a system to its edge, and drift's own Echo hands off "after it breaks" -- which is
  exactly where tipping-point begins; the two are complements, the creep and the snap.
- **Escalation**: The engine models the discontinuous jump of one loaded system, not the
  reciprocal spiral of a conflict. The self-amplifying exchange in which each side's move raises
  the other's -- positions hardening, stakes climbing -- is the escalation engine's; escalation
  is loud, mutual, and visible where tipping-point's loading is silent and one-sided, and an
  escalation may itself be the load that tips something, but the spiral is escalation's.
- **Reversal**: The engine models the accumulation-driven, irreversible transition, not the
  bare inversion of direction. The turn by which a situation flips its heading -- the peripeteia
  -- is the reversal engine's; reversal is the abstract about-face, tipping-point the specific
  dynamic of load, threshold, snap, and lock-in that can produce one. A reversal can be gradual
  and undoable; a tipping-point is neither.
- **Grief and the emotions of the aftermath**: The engine models the crossing, not the living
  with what it leaves. How a persona adapts to the irreversible loss the snap produced is grief's;
  the feeling that the trigger was absurdly small is the surprise or confusion engines'.
  Tipping-point delivers the discontinuous fact; the reckoning with it belongs to those engines.
- **The phase itself is not engine content**: The engine declares the four phases exist; which
  one a given process is in lives on that process, linked under its Direction chapter. The engine
  refuses to fix a process's transition for it.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per file.

- **[tipping-point](process_tipping-point.md)** (the root): the critical transition -- a process
  that absorbs load looking stable, crosses a threshold, and shifts discontinuously and
  irreversibly, the trigger the last increment and never the cause. Anchored by Thom (the cusp)
  and Scheffer (critical transitions).
- **[loading](process_loading.md)**: the sub-critical accumulation -- strain taken on unseen, the
  slow variable creeping while the surface holds, resilience spent invisibly. Anchored by Bak (the
  loaded sandpile) and Scheffer (the slow variable).
- **[criticality](process_criticality.md)**: the edge -- the loaded system at maximal sensitivity,
  where a trivial trigger decides everything a large force could not decide before. Anchored by Bak
  (self-organized criticality) and Scheffer (early-warning signals, lost resilience).
- **[snap](process_snap.md)**: the discontinuity -- the sudden jump to a new state, out of all
  proportion to the trigger, the flat curve dropping sheer. Anchored by Thom and Zeeman (the
  catastrophic jump).
- **[ratchet](process_ratchet.md)**: the twist -- the new state locked, the old one unreachable by
  undoing the trigger; the one-way catch that dooms the broken thing and makes the mended thing
  durable. Anchored by Zeeman (hysteresis) and Scheffer (alternative stable states).
