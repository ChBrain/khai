# Instructions: Tipping-point

How a Playwright wires the tipping-point engine into a play. You wire by linking the play's own content to the engine's files; the engine files stay untouched, they are canon. The Roadie plumbs the engine's law into Instructions Knowledge on deploy. Authoring guidance, not runtime content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human sets which processes tip rather than slide: what load has been gathering unseen, what trivial trigger will come to release it, and that the collapse -- a bond, a slope, a market, a regime -- is discontinuous and does not undo when the trigger is undone.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, a process whose sudden transition is an actor links under Direction the phase it is in: loading, the strain taken on while the system still looks stable; criticality, the edge where a trivial trigger looms and the smallest thing will decide everything; snap, the discontinuous fall to a new state out of all proportion to that trigger; or ratchet, the new state locked so that undoing the trigger does not undo the fall. Play the tipping-point as the accumulation that looks like safety and the snap that breaks it -- present in what the process shows, never narrated -- and never let the trigger read as the cause: it is the last increment of a load long since gathered. Keep it the tipping dynamic, not the slow slide before it (that is drift), the mutual spiral (escalation), the abstract turn (reversal), or the aftermath (grief). You link to the engine, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- The slow uncorrected slide that loads the system is the drift engine's -- drift's own Echo hands off "after it breaks", which is where tipping-point begins; the loud reciprocal spiral is escalation's; the bare inversion of direction is reversal's; the adaptation to the loss after is grief's, and the sense that the trigger was absurdly small is surprise's or confusion's. Tipping-point is the F-process dynamic-shape force for the sudden crossing -- the catastrophe on the cusp. Whether the transition rings true -- discontinuous not gradual, the trigger the last increment not the cause, the new state genuinely hard to reverse -- is the team's review.

## Knowledge

- Tipping-point is the dynamic in which a running process absorbs accumulating load while appearing stable, crosses a hidden critical threshold, and shifts discontinuously and irreversibly to a new state (Thom's catastrophe on the cusp; Bak's self-organized criticality; Scheffer's critical transitions; Zeeman's hysteresis). It wires on a process at Direction, and it is the shape of the sudden crossing -- not a slide but a jump. Four phases carry it -- loading is the strain taken on unseen, the slow variable creeping while the surface holds and resilience erodes invisibly, so the system is as stable in appearance the moment before as an hour before; criticality is the edge, the loaded system at maximal sensitivity where a trivial trigger -- the last straw, the dropped mug -- decides everything a large force could not decide before; snap is the discontinuity, the sudden jump to a new state out of all proportion to the trigger; ratchet is the twist, the new state locked in its own stable basin so that reversing the trigger does not reverse the state. Read the trigger as the last increment and never the cause -- the search for a proportionate reason ("over this?") misreads a threshold crossing as an event -- and read ratchet two ways: the irreversibility that dooms the broken thing is what makes a hard-won mend durable, so a system driven up to a better state holds against a dip the way the ruined one holds against repair.

## System

- Do stage the loading before the snap: show the strain gathering unseen, the surface steady, so the collapse lands as a threshold crossed and not a fast slide -- the flat, flat, flat, and then the gone.
- Do route through the phase that fits: loading for the unseen accumulation, criticality for the poised edge and its trivial trigger, snap for the discontinuous fall, ratchet for the locked new state.
- Do keep the trigger the occasion and never the cause: a system at criticality tips on whatever small thing arrives first, so never let a witness's "over such a little thing?!" be the play's own reading -- it was never about the mug.
- Do read ratchet as the engine's twist: the one-way catch that makes the break permanent is the one that makes a recovery, once reached, hold -- irreversibility as trap and guarantee at once.
- Do not model the slow slide that loads the system (drift), the mutual spiral (escalation), the bare inversion (reversal), or the aftermath (grief). Do not edit the engine's files; wire only from the play's side.
