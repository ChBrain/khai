# Process: Schedule

## Taxonomy

[Social-time](process_social-time.md), the facet of the imposed timetable that orders the place.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: social-time

## Initiated by

A place is ordered by the clock, its hours sorted into slots and assignments.
Schedule starts wherever a place's time is disciplined by a timetable -- the opening hours, the shift bell, the class period, the train that leaves at the minute, the appointment that fixes when a thing may happen -- the imposed grid that says not what day but what hour, and what that hour is for.
It is the clock-discipline of the field: the mechanical ordering E. P. Thompson traced to the factory, where time became a currency to be spent and owed, and the day was cut into measured, accountable units the place runs by.

## Direction

Along the timetable, the place's hours sorted and assigned by the clock.
Schedule fills the place with its slots -- the bell that starts and ends the shift, the hours that open and close the shop, the timetable that says when the train comes and the class begins and the meeting is due -- so that being in the place means being on time, or late, or early, measured against a grid.
It moves the place by the clock rather than the sun or the task: not working until the work is done but until the hour strikes, not eating when hungry but at the appointed break -- the timetable overriding the older rhythms of task and daylight with the discipline of the measured hour.

## Lever

The imposed timetable against the older rhythms of task and season it overrides.
The carrying force is the schedule's power to coordinate and to discipline -- to make a place's many people act in concert, the shift changing as one, the train met, the appointment kept -- and, with it, the clock's demand that time be spent well and not wasted (Thompson's work-discipline); the resistance is the human tempo the clock overrides, the body that is not hungry at the break or tired at the bell, and the task that will not fit the slot.
Run the schedule and the place coordinates, its people meeting the same hours; press it too hard and the place is tyrannized by the clock, its life cut to a grid that serves the timetable more than the people kept to it.

## Echo

The place ordered by its timetable.
Schedule leaves the place run by the clock -- its hours slotted, its people timed, its life sorted into the measured units the timetable assigns -- and hands that discipline to everyone who must keep it.
It is the place's collective time at its most explicit and most enforced: the calendar says what day, the schedule says what hour, and the hour is kept whether the body or the task agrees.
Schedule is read from the timetable, the place known by the clock-ordered grid its hours are kept to.
