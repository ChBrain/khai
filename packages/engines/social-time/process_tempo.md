# Process: Tempo

## Taxonomy

[Social-time](process_social-time.md), the facet of the pace the place runs at.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: social-time

## Initiated by

A place runs at a pace, and the pace is felt the moment one enters.
Tempo starts wherever a place has a characteristic speed of life -- the frantic trading floor, the slow village afternoon, the rush-hour street, the hospital at a crawl and then a sprint -- the rate at which a setting's people move, act, and expect things to happen.
It is the pace of the field: not the cycle it repeats or the hours it keeps but how fast it lives, the tempo a community runs at and holds newcomers to.

## Direction

Faster or slower, the place's life set to a speed its people keep and enforce.
Tempo drives the place at its rate -- the city that will not wait, the small town that will not hurry, the office that runs hot, the resort that runs slow -- so that to be in the place is to be swept to its pace or to grate against it, the quick made to dawdle and the slow made to scramble.
It moves the place along a rising or falling speed: the acceleration Rosa named as modernity's signature, the pace of exchange and communication and life quickening until the place demands more done per hour, against the deliberate slowness a place may keep or seek, the town that guards its unhurry as its character.

## Lever

The place's own pace against the pull to speed up and the drag to slow down.
The carrying force is the tempo a place settles into and holds its people to -- the shared sense of how fast is normal here, so that the same act reads as urgent in one place and rude in its haste in another; the resistance is the pressure to accelerate (Rosa's social acceleration, the faster world bearing on the place) and the friction of mismatched paces, the fast newcomer in the slow town, the slow supplicant at the fast counter.
Hold the place's tempo and its people move in a shared rhythm; force it -- the village hurried, the frantic place made to wait -- and the pace turns from the place's own into a strain imposed on it.

## Echo

The place moving at its own speed.
Tempo leaves the place with its pace -- the rate its life runs at, fast or slow and quickening or held -- and hands that speed to everyone who must move at it.
It is the most immediately felt of the place's collective times: before the calendar is learned or the schedule read, the pace is sensed -- the place that rushes or the place that idles, and the demand it makes on the body to match.
Tempo is read from the pace, the place known by the speed of life it runs at and holds its people to.
