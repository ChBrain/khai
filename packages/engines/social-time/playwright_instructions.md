# Instructions: Social-time

How a Playwright wires the social-time engine into a play. You wire by linking the play's own content to the engine's files; the engine files stay untouched, they are canon. The Roadie plumbs the engine's law into Instructions Knowledge on deploy. Authoring guidance, not runtime content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human sets which places are read by the collective time they keep -- the cycle a setting runs on, the timetable that orders it, the pace it moves at, or the moments its people beat as one -- and whether that shared time is the place's quiet background or a live actor in the play.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, a place whose collective time is an actor links under Shown the facet of that time: the calendar it cycles by, the schedule that orders its hours, the tempo it runs at, or the synchrony that binds its people. Play the social-time as the place's shared time present in what it puts before whoever enters -- the market that fills the square each week, the shift bell that sorts the day, the frantic or sleepy pace, the festival the whole town keeps -- shown, never narrated, and never standing in for a persona's own sense of time. Keep it the collective structuring of time, not the measured passage itself (that is chronos), a persona's experience of duration (time-perception), or the place's sound and social life (soundscape, street-life). You link to the engine, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- The measured passage of time is chronos's; a persona's own experience of duration and time-perspective is time-perception's; the place's sound is soundscape's and its social life is street-life's; the authored ordering of the telling is the pitch's. Social-time is the collective-time member of the F-place family and the collective-side branch of the time family -- the shared, socially-made time a place keeps. Whether the collective time rings true to the place and its community is the team's review.

## Knowledge

- Social-time is the collective structuring of time a place keeps: the shared, socially-made order of time a setting runs on and is known by (Zerubavel's sociotemporal order as construction; Thompson's clock-discipline; Rosa's social acceleration; Durkheim's collective effervescence). It wires on a place at Shown, and it is the shared time the place is ordered by, not the neutral clock. Four facets carry it -- calendar is the shared cycle the place runs on, the recurring marked days, the week and the feast and the season and the market day, the socially-made round; schedule is the imposed timetable that orders the place, clock-discipline, the opening hours and the shift bell and the grid that sorts its hours; tempo is the pace the place runs at, fast or slow and quickening, the speed of life it holds its people to; synchrony is the twist, the shared time read as belonging, the festival and the common rest and the moment the whole place beats as one, the collective rhythm that binds a we. Read a place by the collective time it keeps, and read synchrony two ways: the shared time that constrains is also the shared time that belongs.

## System

- Do give a place whose collective time is an actor its facet under Shown; do play the social-time as the place's shared time shown, present not narrated; do keep the reading the collective structuring of time -- the calendar, the schedule, the tempo, the synchrony -- not a persona's feeling and not a symbol.
- Do read synchrony as the twist: the shared time that constrains is also the shared time that belongs -- the festival and the common rest that bind a community by being kept together, not only the clock's demand.
- Do read the collective time against its neighbours: a place can be out of time with those around it -- its holy day their working day, its slow pace grating on a fast newcomer -- and the friction of mismatched sociotemporal orders is the place's own.
- Do not model the measured passage (chronos), a persona's experience of duration (time-perception), the place's sound or social life (soundscape, street-life), or the authored telling (the pitch). Do not make the place's collective time stand in for a persona's own sense of time. Do not edit the engine's files; wire only from the play's side.
