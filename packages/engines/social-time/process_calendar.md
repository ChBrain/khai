# Process: Calendar

## Taxonomy

[Social-time](process_social-time.md), the facet of the shared cycle the place runs on.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: social-time

## Initiated by

A place keeps a round of recurring days, and lives by their return.
Calendar starts wherever a place's time is structured into a repeating cycle of marked days -- the week and its working days and its day of rest, the holy days and the feast days, the market day, the season of harvest or of Lent, the anniversary that comes each year.
It is the shared cycle of the field: not the clock's forward line but the community's recurring round, the socially-made calendar Zerubavel showed is convention and not nature -- the seven-day week answering to nothing in the heavens, chosen and kept.

## Direction

Around, in a cycle that returns, marking the place's time into a repeating shape.
Calendar fills the place's time with its recurrences -- the days that come back, each with its character and its due observance, so the place's people know where they are in the round and what the day asks of them: work or rest, fast or feast, ordinary or holy.
It moves the place through its cycle rather than along a line: the market that fills the square each week, the festival that comes each year, the sabbath that falls each seventh day -- the shared round that tells the community not what time it is but what day it is, and what that day is for.

## Lever

The shared cycle against the flat, undifferentiated line of mere days.
The carrying force is the calendar's power to mark time into meaning -- to make one day holy and another ordinary, to gather the community around a common round so that all keep the same feast on the same day; the resistance is the abstract, uniform time that would make every day the same, and the clash of rival calendars, the place whose holy day is a working day two towns over, the reform that moves the feast and unsettles the round.
Keep the calendar and the place's time has shape and shared meaning; lose it -- the round broken, the days flattened to a uniform grid -- and the place keeps time without keeping days, its recurrences gone.

## Echo

The place cycling through its marked round.
Calendar leaves the place structured by its recurring days -- the week, the feast, the season, the market day, the round the community turns through together -- and hands that cycle to everyone who lives by it.
It is the deepest layer of the place's collective time: the shared round beneath the day's schedule and the moment's tempo, the calendar a community keeps as the frame its whole life in time is hung on.
Calendar is read from the recurrence, the place known by the cycle of marked days it returns through.
