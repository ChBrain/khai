# Social-time: Reference

## Line of Work

Social-time as **process**: the collective structuring of time a place keeps -- the shared,
socially-made order of time a setting runs on and puts before whoever enters, taken across
the facets that order compose. It wires on a place at Shown, the place's self-presentation,
because a setting is read by the time it keeps as much as by its look: the cycle it runs on,
the timetable that orders it, the pace it moves at, and the moments its people beat as one.
The domain does not model the measured passage of time itself -- duration flowing, the clock
running -- which is chronos's; nor a persona's own experience of duration and time-
perspective, which is time-perception's; nor the place's sound, which is soundscape's, or its
social life, which is street-life's; nor the authored ordering of the play's telling, which
is the pitch's. It models the shared time the place is ordered and known by.

The spine is the sociology of time -- the argument that time is not only a natural given but
a social institution, built and kept by communities. Zerubavel showed the sociotemporal
order to be a construction: the seven-day week answers to nothing in nature, calendars are
conventions, and a community's rhythms -- its holy days, its schedules, its private and
public times -- are socially made and socially enforced. E. P. Thompson traced the historic
imposition of clock-discipline: how the mechanical clock and the factory turned time into a
currency to be spent, owed, and measured, displacing the older task- and daylight-rhythms
with the timetable. Rosa named the modern signature of social acceleration -- the quickening
tempo of technology, social change, and the pace of life itself. And Durkheim saw in the
gathered rite the collective effervescence by which shared time binds a community. So the
engine gives social-time three facets of the ordered collective time -- calendar, schedule,
tempo -- and keeps a fourth, synchrony, the shared time read not as constraint but as
belonging.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **Chronos**: The engine models the collective structuring of time, not the measured passage
  itself. The sequential flow of duration -- the clock running, the age accruing, time passing
  over the cargo -- is the chronos engine's; social-time is the socially-made order a community
  imposes on that passage, the calendar and schedule and tempo built over the neutral flow.
  Chronos is time passing; social-time is the shared shape a community gives it.
- **Time-perception**: The engine models the place's collective time, not a persona's private
  experience of it. The subjective sense of duration, and the dispositional time-perspective a
  persona carries, are the time-perception engine's; social-time is the shared, public order the
  place keeps, read by anyone who enters, not one persona's felt time.
- **The sound and the life of the place (soundscape, street-life)**: The engine models the
  place's time, not its sound or its social use. The auditory field is soundscape's; the human
  life the place hosts is street-life's; social-time may coincide with both -- the festival is
  loud and peopled -- but the collective time is its own force, the shared ordering of when,
  distinct from the sound and the life that fill it.
- **The authored telling (the pitch)**: The engine models the collective time within the world,
  not the ordering of the play's own telling. The arrangement of story-time by the discourse --
  the flashback, the ellipsis, the pacing of the narration -- is the pitch's; social-time is the
  time the place keeps inside the world, not the time the play takes to tell it.
- **The facet itself is not engine content**: The engine declares the four facets exist; which
  one a given place shows lives on that place, linked under its Shown chapter. The engine refuses
  to fix a place's collective time for it.
