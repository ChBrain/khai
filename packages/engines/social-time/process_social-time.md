# Process: Social-time

## Taxonomy

Sociology of time.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: social-time

## Initiated by

A place is entered, and it is found to keep a time of its own -- not the clock's, but the community's.
Social-time starts wherever a place runs on a shared, socially-made ordering of time -- the market day, the shift bell, the rush hour, the holy day, the festival -- the collective rhythm a setting lives by and puts before whoever enters.
Every inhabited place keeps a sociotemporal order as particular as its look: a way its people have agreed, mostly without deciding, to divide and fill and pace their time; social-time is that order as an actor, not the neutral clock a setting is usually taken to run on.

## Direction

Into the shared round, the place ordered by a time its people hold in common.
Four facets compose the order: [Calendar](process_calendar.md) is the shared cycle the place runs on, [Schedule](process_schedule.md) the imposed timetable that sorts its hours, [Tempo](process_tempo.md) the pace it runs at, and [Synchrony](process_synchrony.md) the twist -- the shared time read as belonging.
A place is not read as standing in neutral clock-time but as keeping a collective one: a round of recurring days, a grid of ordered hours, a pace fast or slow, and the moments the whole place moves as one -- or, where the order is broken, a place out of time with those around it, its market day nobody's, its bell rung to an empty yard.

## Lever

The place's collective time against the clock's neutral measure and the times of other places.
The carrying force is that a place's time is made, not given -- Zerubavel's point that the week is convention and the calendar a social construction, so a community builds the round it lives by; the resistance is the abstract clock that would flatten it to mere hours, and the friction of other places on other times, the traveller jet-lagged between sociotemporal orders, the town whose sabbath is the next town's market day.
Hold to the shared order and the place coheres in time, its people moving together; lose it -- the clock imposed over the custom, the tempo forced past the place's own -- and the collective time frays, and the place keeps a time its people no longer feel as theirs.

## Echo

The place known by the time it keeps.
Social-time does not close; it is the standing sociotemporal order of the place, gathering its people into a shared round that is as much the place as its look.
It hands off what it is not: the measured passage itself -- duration flowing, the clock running -- is chronos's; a persona's own experience of duration and its time-perspective is time-perception's; the place's sound is soundscape's and its social life is street-life's; the authored ordering of the play's own telling is the pitch's.
The place is read by its collective time -- a setting is not only somewhere seen but sometime kept, and a place is known by the calendar it cycles, the schedule it runs, the tempo it moves at, and the moments it beats as one.
