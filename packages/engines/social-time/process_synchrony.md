# Process: Synchrony

## Taxonomy

[Social-time](process_social-time.md), the facet where shared time is read as belonging.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: social-time

## Initiated by

The place moves as one, and the moving-together is felt as belonging.
Synchrony starts wherever a place's whole community keeps a moment in common -- the festival all attend, the sabbath all rest on, the meal-hour all sit to, the new year all count down, the anthem all rise for -- the shared time in which the many act as one.
It is social-time read the other way: not the calendar's constraint or the schedule's discipline but the belonging their commonness produces -- the being-in-time-together that makes a scattered people feel, for the length of the shared moment, a single we.

## Direction

Toward oneness, the place's people beating as a single rhythm.
Synchrony gathers the place into a common beat -- the square full for the festival, the town quiet for the sabbath, the stadium risen as one, the household round the table at the hour -- so that the shared time is not experienced as a rule obeyed but as a communion entered, each person's participation confirming everyone else's.
It moves the place from many times into one: the collective effervescence Durkheim found in the gathered rite, the warmth of the crowd counting down together, the felt solidarity of a people keeping the same moment for no reason but that it is kept together.

## Lever

The belonging of shared time against the constraint the same time can be.
The carrying force is that keeping time together binds -- the festival and the common rest and the shared hour make a community of those who keep them, so that the very calendar and schedule that constrain also gather; the resistance is the reading that sees only the constraint, the imposed holiday as a day one may not work, the forced festival, the synchrony demanded rather than felt -- and the loneliness of the one outside the shared moment, kept to the place's time but not gathered by it.
Enter the shared moment and the place's time becomes belonging; stand outside it -- excluded, or made to keep it without feeling it -- and the same synchrony that binds the others marks the one apart.

## Echo

The place gathered into a we by the time it keeps together.
Synchrony leaves the place bound by its shared moments -- the festivals and rests and common hours that turn a calendar into a communion -- and carries beneath it the social-time twist, the sibling of the family's ambivalence: that the shared time which constrains is also the shared time that belongs, the collective rhythm read not only as the clock's demand but as the gift of being in time with one's own.
Synchrony is dated from the gathering, the moment the place's many times became, for its people, one.
