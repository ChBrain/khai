# Kairos: Reference

## Line of Work

Kairos as **process**: the qualitative, opportune moment cut from the even flow of
time -- the fitting instant when an act can be done that a moment before or after it
cannot. It is the second engine of the time family and the family's multi-cargo
force, wiring on the three unfolding types at once -- a plot at its Cue, a plan at
its Implementation, a process at its Initiated by -- because the fittingness of a
moment is one phenomenon whatever unfolds. It reads only the moment's fittingness:
the window that opens and shuts, the juncture the outcome pivots on, the timing that
seizes, and the ripeness that reads the pressing moment as full. The domain does not
model the measured passage the moment is cut from, which is chronos's; nor the
suspense of a depleting clock raising stakes, which is the ticking-clock engine's;
nor the imposed deadline compressing execution, which is the deadline engine's; nor
the regime-crossing itself, which is the phase-shift engine's; nor a persona's choice
to seize the moment, which is agency's and decision's. It models the opportune moment
as a force on the unfolding, and reads it two ways -- as the fleeting window pressing
to be caught, and as the ripe time come full.

The spine is the classical distinction between _kairos_ and _chronos_: the qualitative
right moment against quantitative measured duration. Onians traced _kairos_ to the
weaver's opening, the shed through which the shuttle must pass, and to the archer's
opportune aperture -- the origin image of the moment as a passing gap that must be
hit. Kinneavy and the essays gathered by Sipiora and Baumlin put the fitting word at
the fitting time at the centre of rhetoric, timeliness as a skill (_phronesis_ applied
to the "when"). Kermode read the difference in narrative terms: _chronos_ as mere
"passing time," _kairos_ as the moment "charged with significance" in relation to an
end. And Tillich named the fullness the moment can hold -- the _pleroma tou chronou_,
"the time is ripe." So the engine gives the opportune moment three faces -- the window
that opens, the juncture that decides, the timing that seizes -- and keeps a fourth,
ripeness, the same pressing moment read not as the window closing but as the occasion
come full.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The measured passage the moment is cut from (chronos)**: Chronos runs the even,
  quantitative flow -- how old, in what order, how long; kairos is the one moment on
  that flow charged with the chance to act. The two are the classical pair: chronos
  the measure, kairos the fitting moment. Kairos never re-owns the passage; it reads
  only whether this instant fits, and chronos never reads the fittingness. They meet
  where a window opens at a point on chronos's sequence, and part there.
- **The suspense of a depleting clock (ticking-clock)**: The charted ticking-clock
  force is a depleting external time-window raising stakes -- a countdown, a device of
  tension. Kairos is the qualitative fittingness of the moment, not the suspense of a
  timer running down. A ticking clock may make a window feel urgent, but the window's
  openness and the clock's countdown are different things: kairos owns the fitting
  instant, ticking-clock the dread of the closing count.
- **The imposed deadline (deadline)**: The charted deadline force is an external clock
  compressing execution as it nears -- a term imposed on a plan from outside. Kairos on
  a plan is the opportune moment to act, the window to strike, not the compelled term.
  The boundary is imposed versus opportune: a deadline is when the plan must be done;
  kairos is when it is ripe to do. The two can pull against each other -- the ripe
  moment before the deadline, or the deadline forcing action before the moment is ripe.
- **The regime-crossing itself (phase-shift)**: The charted phase-shift force is a
  dynamic crossing a threshold into a new regime -- the ignition, the tipping. Kairos on
  a process is the opportune moment to act on the dynamic, the fitting instant, whether
  or not a regime crosses. Phase-shift is that the crossing happens; kairos is that the
  window to act is open. A juncture may coincide with a phase-shift, but kairos owns the
  fittingness of the moment, phase-shift the regime it tips into.
- **The persona's choice to seize it (agency, decision)**: Kairos delivers the moment
  as a force on the unfolding -- that the window is open, the juncture live, the time
  ripe. Whether a persona reads and seizes it is agency's and decision's, on the
  persona; kairos is the occasion, not the choosing. The moment is there for anyone in
  the scene; who takes it, and how well, is theirs.
- **The moment as authored symbol**: The opportune moment made to **stand for** a
  persona's one chance at life, the missed window that mirrors a wasted life, is
  refused: that is authored key, the play's pitch, not the force on the unfolding.
  Kairos delivers the fitting moment; the meaning read into a taken or missed one is
  the play's.
- **The facet itself is not engine content**: The engine declares the four facets
  exist; which one a given plot, plan, or process turns on lives on that unfolding,
  linked under its moment-chapter. The engine refuses to fix a moment's facet for it.
