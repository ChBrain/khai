# Schadenfreude: Reference

## Line of Work

Schadenfreude as **process**: the pleasure a persona takes in another's misfortune, taken without having caused it -- the fall of the deserving, the envied, or the disliked felt as a small, often guilty joy. The domain does not model the upward pain at another's good, which is envy's; nor the deservingness judgment that licenses the just case, which is moral-judgment's; nor the standing cold down-look on another, which is contempt's; nor the active wish to inflict the harm, which is aggression's; nor the between-group enmity the tribal case feeds on, which the intergroup composite holds. It models the pleasure itself: another's loss converted into the persona's gain, and the grounds -- desert, envy, antipathy -- that make the misfortune sweet.

The spine is the social psychology of schadenfreude. It is the mirror of envy: where envy is pain at another's superior good, schadenfreude is pleasure at another's ill, and the two are linked -- the fall of an envied other is especially enjoyed. Research identifies several grounds on which the pleasure is licensed: the misfortune is deserved, so its enjoyment feels like justice; the fallen was envied, so their descent relieves a painful comparison; or the fallen was disliked or a rival, so their harm is enjoyed as an opponent's setback. These map to a tripartite motivational account of the emotion. The three forms are those: comeuppance, leveling, grudge.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **Envy**: The engine models the pleasure at another's ill, not the pain at another's good. The upward comparison that aches at a superior's advantage -- and its benign and malicious poles -- is owned by the envy engine; schadenfreude is its mirror, the pleasure when that superior falls, and its leveling form is the discharge of envy's ache, but the ache itself, and the wish to close the gap by rising, are envy's.
- **Moral judgment**: The engine models the pleasure in a deserved fall, not the judgment of desert that licenses it. Whether a misfortune is earned -- the reading of a transgression through a moral foundation -- is owned by the moral-judgment engine; comeuppance takes that verdict of deservingness and enjoys the fall it warrants, but owns the pleasure, not the judging.
- **Contempt and Aggression**: The engine models pleasure at a misfortune, not the standing devaluation of another nor the wish to cause them harm. The cold down-look that holds another beneath is owned by the contempt engine; the active drive to inflict injury is owned by the aggression engine; schadenfreude enjoys a fall it did not cause and need not have wished, distinct from looking down and from striking.
- **Intergroup**: The engine models one persona's pleasure at a disliked target's fall, not the between-group dynamic that supplies the enmity. The division, enmity, and contact between groups are held by the intergroup composite; grudge's tribal pleasure feeds on that enmity but is the individual's felt joy at the rival's loss, not the group relation itself.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per file.

- **[schadenfreude](process_schadenfreude.md)** (the root): The pleasure taken in another's misfortune -- a fall not caused by the persona, enjoyed because the fallen was deserving, envied, or disliked, the mirror of envy. Anchored by Smith (the joy of pain) and van Dijk & Ouwerkerk (the appraisal antecedents).
- **[comeuppance](process_comeuppance.md)** (form): The justice route -- a deserved fall enjoyed as balance restored, the cheat caught and the arrogant humbled. Anchored by Feather & Sherman (deservingness) and van Dijk & Ouwerkerk (the deservingness route).
- **[leveling](process_leveling.md)** (form): The envy route -- an envied superior brought low, the painful comparison eased by the loss. Anchored by Smith et al. (envy and schadenfreude) and the neuroscience of reward at an envied other's misfortune.
- **[grudge](process_grudge.md)** (form): The antipathy route -- a disliked other's or rival group's fall enjoyed for its own sake, an opponent's setback as a partisan gain. Anchored by Leach & Spears (intergroup schadenfreude) and Wang, Lilienfeld & Rochat (the aggression subform).
