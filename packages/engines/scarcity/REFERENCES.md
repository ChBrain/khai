# Scarcity: Reference

## Line of Work

Scarcity as **process**: how the felt lack of a resource -- money, time, food, company -- captures the mind and reorganizes it around the shortfall, sharpening attention on the pressing lack while taxing the mental capacity left for everything else. The domain does not model the resource that is short, which is the money and capital engines'; nor the mental faculties the lack taxes -- attention, planning, impulse control -- which are the attention and executive-function engines'; nor the demand-exceeds-resources strain, which is the stress engine's. It models the capture and taxing of the mind by the felt lack itself: the subjective sense of not-enough, working through the mechanisms by which a shortfall reorganizes cognition.

The spine is Mullainathan and Shafir's behavioral science of scarcity. Having less than one feels one needs is not merely a condition of the world but a distinctive psychology: scarcity captures the mind, focusing it on the pressing lack, and this capture has a cost -- it taxes cognitive capacity and self-control, so that the same person reasons and chooses worse while short. Three mechanisms carry it: tunneling, the shortfall seizing attention so that a focus dividend is won inside the tunnel and the important-but-not-urgent neglected outside it; the bandwidth tax, the nagging lack consuming mental capacity so that fluid intelligence and executive function measurably fall; and the scarcity trap, the neglect and the tax compounding as the persona borrows against the future to meet the present, a spiral that keeps the scarce scarce. The three forms are those: tunneling, bandwidth, trap.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **Money and Capital**: The engine models the mind's capture and taxing by a felt lack, not the resource that is short. The money a persona has or owes, and the standing stock of resource a persona commands, are owned by the money and capital engines; scarcity is the cognition of their felt insufficiency, not the resource itself -- one can be short of time or company with no money in question, and the engine models the psychology of the lack whatever the resource.
- **Attention and Executive function**: The engine models the shortfall's seizure and taxing of the mind, not the faculties seized and taxed. The beam of attention and its direction are the attention engine's; planning, impulse control, and the holding of goals are the executive-function engine's; scarcity owns what the lack does to them -- tunneling commandeers the beam, bandwidth consumes the capacity -- but the faculties themselves belong to those engines, and the engine delegates their mechanics to them.
- **Stress**: The engine models the cognitive reorganization by a felt lack, not the strain of demand exceeding resources. The alarm-and-adaptation response to a load too great is the stress engine's; scarcity and stress often ride together, but scarcity owns the specific cognitive signature -- capture, tunnel, tax, trap -- and not the physiological or affective strain, which is stress's to model.
- **Goal and Drive**: The engine models the lack of a resource as it captures cognition, not the wanting that motivates pursuit. The need that orients a persona toward an end is the goal engine's, and the push of an appetite is the drive engine's; scarcity is downstream of the need -- it models what having too little of the needed thing does to the mind, not the needing itself.
