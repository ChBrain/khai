# Process: Moral Licensing

## Taxonomy

Morality / the self-regulation of conduct against a moral set-point.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: moral-licensing

## Initiated by

A persona reads its recent moral record and adjusts what it permits itself now.
This is moral licensing in the broad sense (Sachdeva; Monin & Miller): moral conduct self-regulated against a set-point, not maximized, so that the persona's standing sense of itself as good enough governs its next act.
A stretch of good behavior loosens the reins -- the persona feels it has earned some latitude -- and a lapse or a threat to its moral self tightens them, driving it to make the balance good again; the record is a running account the present act is charged to or credited from.

## Direction

Toward the moral balance, and back to its set-point.
Moral licensing points the persona's recent record at its present choice: above the set-point, having lately been good, it licenses itself a shortcut it would otherwise refuse; below it, having lapsed or been made to doubt its goodness, it moves to restore the balance with compensatory good.
Its direction is regulation, not accumulation -- the system pulls conduct back toward a felt-sufficient middle from either side, sinning after saintliness and turning saintly after sin.

## Lever

What moral licensing spends -- the persona's need to be moral enough rather than as moral as possible.
Because the persona regulates to a set-point, being well above it feels like surplus it can draw down, and being below it feels like a debt it must pay; the licensing spends the assumption that recent good and recent bad are commensurable, that virtue banked can offset a later lapse and a lapse can be worked off by later virtue.
A production holds this lever by letting the persona keep a rough moral account and act on the balance -- the good deed that quietly buys the later shortcut, the wrong that provokes a burst of compensating virtue -- so conduct reads as regulated against a standing sense of sufficiency, not as steady character.

## Echo

The persona's next act is loosed or checked by its last -- and the standard itself never moved.
This is what distinguishes licensing from justification: the moral rule is held intact, the bad act still counts as bad; what changed is that the persona feels it has the credit to afford it, or the deficit it must repair.
It runs as [indulgence](process_indulgence.md), the spending of banked credit on a lapse, and [cleansing](process_cleansing.md), the repair of a deficit with compensatory good. The switching-off of self-sanction for a specific harm by reinterpreting it is the moral-disengagement engine's; the credentialing reframe that makes an ambiguous act read as already cleared is the bias engine's moral-credential member; the standing centrality of morality to the self is the moral-identity engine's; the felt guilt that often drives the cleansing is the guilt engine's; the general reduction of inconsistency is the dissonance engine's. This engine owns the regulation of conduct against a moral set-point.
