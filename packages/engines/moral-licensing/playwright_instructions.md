# Instructions: Moral Licensing

How a Playwright wires the moral-licensing engine into a play. You wire by linking the play's own content to the engine's files; the engine files stay untouched, they are canon. The Roadie plumbs the engine's law into Instructions Knowledge on deploy. Authoring guidance, not runtime content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human sets the moral terrain: what the persona treats as a good deed and what as a lapse, and what recent record it is carrying -- so a moment can run on a genuine moral balance the persona reads and acts against.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, a persona links the moral-licensing move a moment runs under its Projection: whether it is spending banked moral credit on a lapse (indulgence) or repairing a moral deficit with compensatory good (cleansing). Let the recent record do quiet work on the present act -- the good deed that buys the later shortcut, the transgression that provokes a burst of virtue -- and keep the standard intact: the lapse still counts as a lapse, it is only afforded. You link to the engine, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- The switching-off of self-sanction for a specific harm by reinterpreting it is the moral-disengagement engine's; the credentialing reframe that makes an ambiguous act read as already cleared is the bias engine's moral-credential member; the standing centrality of morality to the self is the moral-identity engine's; the felt guilt that drives the reach for repair is the guilt engine's; the bodily disgust the Macbeth effect borrows is the disgust engine's; the general reduction of inconsistency is the dissonance engine's. This engine supplies the trade against a moral balance -- the indulgence that spends a surplus and the cleansing that repairs a deficit.

## Knowledge

- Moral licensing is the self-regulation of conduct against a moral set-point: a persona reads its recent moral record and adjusts what it permits itself now, so a run of good behavior licenses a later lapse and a transgression drives compensatory good (Sachdeva; Monin & Miller). It runs in two directions. Indulgence is the licensing arm -- a prior good deed banks moral credit, and the persona spends it, feeling entitled to a lapse it would otherwise refuse; the good record does not make the bad act good, it makes the persona feel it has earned the latitude. Cleansing is the compensatory arm -- a transgression, or a threat to the persona's sense of itself as moral, opens a deficit it moves to close with compensatory good, or even the literal washing that stands in for moral repair (the Macbeth effect). Read a persona by what recent record it carries and whether it is spending a surplus or repairing a deficit.

## System

- Do keep moral licensing the trade against a balance, not the reinterpretation of the act: the moral rule stays intact and the bad act still counts as bad -- the persona feels it has the credit to afford it, or the deficit it must repair. Let the switching-off of self-sanction that makes a harm feel not-wrong be the moral-disengagement engine's; that is a different move.
- Do stage the two directions: indulgence as the good deed quietly buying a later shortcut (the persona more willing to cut a corner just after being visibly good), and cleansing as a lapse or moral threat provoking a burst of virtue or a reach for the clean.
- Do keep the regulation to a set-point, not a maximizing: the persona acts to be moral enough, drawing down a surplus and paying off a deficit, so conduct pulls back toward a felt-sufficient middle from either side rather than climbing steadily.
- Do let the indulgence be the spending of a genuine balance (delegate the credentialing reframe -- one clean act as a standing permit -- to the bias engine), and let the cleansing be the compensatory move itself (delegate the felt guilt to the guilt engine and the bodily disgust to the disgust engine). Do not edit the engine's files; wire only from the play's side.
