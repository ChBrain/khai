# Process: Cleansing

## Taxonomy

[Moral Licensing](process_moral_licensing.md), the compensatory direction -- a moral deficit repaired with compensatory good.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: moral-licensing

## Initiated by

The persona moves to repair a deficit in its moral balance.
This is the compensatory direction (Zhong & Liljenquist's Macbeth effect; Sachdeva's saintly sinners): a transgression, or even a mere threat to the persona's sense of itself as moral, opens a shortfall the persona is pressed to close, and it does so with compensatory good -- helping, giving, affirming the right value, or even the literal washing that stands in for moral repair.
It opens whenever the persona has lapsed, or been made to feel unclean or in the wrong, and reaches for an act that will make the balance good again.

## Direction

Upward, from a moral deficit to a restoring good.
Cleansing draws the balance back toward its set-point from below: having fallen short, the persona does a compensating good -- disproportionate to the lapse, aimed less at the specific wrong than at the general sense of being moral again -- so the recent record is brought back to sufficiency.
Its direction is the paying-down of a moral debt, the deficit worked off with virtue or with the symbolic clean.

## Lever

What cleansing spends -- the discomfort of a self that has fallen below its own moral standard.
The threat to the moral self is aversive, and the persona will act to relieve it, so the lever is the felt debt: a shortfall demanding repayment, which a compensating good -- or the bodily cleansing that substitutes for it -- can settle.
A production holds this lever by letting a lapse or a moral threat provoke a burst of virtue or a reach for the clean -- the wrongdoer suddenly generous, the accused scrubbing at hands or record -- so the good reads as restoration of a threatened self, not as steady goodness.

## Echo

The persona restores its balance -- and the compensating good is spent on the deficit, not stored as new credit.
Made moral again, the persona returns to its set-point; the debt paid, the pressure that drove the good is relieved, and the account is level rather than in surplus.
The felt guilt that often drives the reach for repair is the guilt engine's, and the bodily disgust that the moral threat borrows is the disgust engine's; cleansing here is the compensatory move itself, the deficit-repair that restores the moral self. Its opposite number is [indulgence](process_indulgence.md), the spending of a surplus rather than the repair of a deficit.
