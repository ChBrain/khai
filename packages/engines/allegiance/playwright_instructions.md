# Instructions: Allegiance

How a Playwright wires the allegiance engine into a play. You wire by linking the play's own content to the engine's files; the engine files stay untouched, they are canon. The Roadie plumbs the engine's law into Instructions Knowledge on deploy. Authoring guidance, not runtime content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human sets the character terrain the audience will judge: what the story's people do that reads as worthy or unworthy, and whose fortunes the plot will raise and lower -- so an audience has characters it can sort and stakes it can care about.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, the audience-persona links the allegiance a moment runs under its Projection: which characters it has sorted liked and disliked, on what moral grounds, and how its enjoyment rides on their fortunes -- the hopes for the loved, the wish for the loathed to fall. Let it read as the taking of sides and the pull toward poetic justice. You link to the engine, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- A persona's own exit-cost bond to a group inside the story is the loyalty engine's; the general mechanism of judging conduct against a moral standard is the moral-judgment engine's; the entry into the story world as a whole is the transportation engine's; the joy at a disliked character's misfortune is the schadenfreude engine's, which the payoff recruits; the dread and pity of the loved lost is the tragic composite's; the capacity to feel into a character is the empathy engine's. This engine supplies the audience's moral bond to the people of a tale -- the verdict that sorts them, the rooting that takes sides, and the payoff that rides on their fortunes.

## Knowledge

- Allegiance is the audience's moral bond to the characters of a story (Zillmann's affective disposition theory; Raney; Murray Smith): the spectator morally monitors the people of the tale, sorts them into liked and disliked, and thereafter enjoys the story as their fortunes match the disposition -- the engine of rooting interest. It runs in three forms. Verdict is the moral monitoring -- the audience as an untiring moral monitor, judging conduct and sorting characters into the deserving and the undeserving. Rooting is the disposition itself -- sympathy and the wish for the liked to prosper, antipathy and the wish for the disliked to fall. Payoff is the outcome-contingent enjoyment -- satisfaction when the liked triumph and the disliked get their comeuppance, dissatisfaction when the deserving lose and the undeserving win. Read a production for whom the audience is for and against, and let the plot's outcomes land by the sides taken.

## System

- Do keep allegiance the audience's bond to characters, not a character's own tie: it is formed from outside the story, by the spectator, toward the people in it -- distinct from a character's loyalty to a cause, which can run opposite to the audience's rooting.
- Do stage the verdict before the rooting: let the characters do things the audience can weigh, so the sorting into liked and disliked is earned by conduct, and remember a character's morality, more than its charm, sets which way the audience leans.
- Do let the disposition make the stakes: once the audience is for someone and against someone, let the plot's turns land as hope and dread, and anchor the whole on at least one character worth rooting for.
- Do use the payoff deliberately: grant poetic justice to satisfy, or withhold it -- the loved lost, the loathed triumphant -- to unsettle on purpose, and let the villain's fall recruit schadenfreude and the loved one's ruin the tragic composite. Do delegate the general moral mechanism to the moral-judgment engine and the entry into the world to the transportation engine. Do not edit the engine's files; wire only from the play's side.
