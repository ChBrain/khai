# Allegiance: Reference

## Line of Work

Allegiance as a **process** engine: the audience's moral bond to the characters of a story, taken across the moral monitoring that forms it, the sympathy and antipathy that follow, and the outcome-contingent enjoyment it pays out. The domain does not model a persona's own exit-cost bond to a group inside the story, which is the loyalty engine's; nor the general mechanism of judging conduct against a moral standard, which is the moral-judgment engine's; nor the entry into the story world as a whole, which is the transportation engine's; nor the joy at a disliked character's misfortune, which is the schadenfreude engine's. It models the spectator-to-character relation: the audience morally monitoring characters, taking sides, and enjoying the tale as the characters' fortunes match the sides taken -- the engine of rooting interest.

The spine is the media-psychology of affective disposition. Zillmann and Cantor's affective disposition theory casts the audience as an untiring moral monitor: spectators continuously judge characters' conduct, form dispositions of liking and disliking, and then enjoy narrative outcomes to the degree that liked characters meet good fortune and disliked characters meet bad -- enjoyment is contingent on the fit between outcome and disposition, which is why poetic justice satisfies and its violation frustrates. Raney extended the model, showing that moral judgment and disposition are reciprocal, and that audiences will bend their moral monitoring to sustain a liking for a protagonist. Murray Smith's structure of sympathy distinguishes three levels of engagement with characters -- recognition (constructing the character), alignment (access to their actions and subjectivity), and allegiance (moral evaluation into sympathy or antipathy) -- locating allegiance as the specifically moral-evaluative bond. Cohen's work on identification marks the related but distinct experience of merging with a character rather than evaluating them from outside. The altitudes are those: the bond, its moral verdict, its rooting disposition, and its outcome payoff.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **Loyalty**: The engine models the audience's evaluative bond to characters, not a persona's own bond to a group. Loyalty -- a persona's exit-cost tie to a group or cause inside the story, and its voice-and-exit dynamics -- is owned by the loyalty engine; allegiance is formed from outside the tale, by the spectator, toward the characters, and the two run at different altitudes: a character can be loyal to a cause the audience is against, and the audience roots by its own morality, not the character's ties.
- **Moral judgment**: The engine models the audience's sorting of characters and its payoff, not the general mechanism of moral appraisal. The moral-judgment engine reads whether an act is right or wrong and on which foundation; allegiance's verdict is that mechanism turned by an audience onto a story's characters, adding the disposition and the outcome-contingent enjoyment that moral judgment alone does not carry.
- **Transportation**: The engine models the bond to characters, not entry into the story world. Transportation -- being carried into the narrative as a whole -- is owned by the transportation engine; allegiance is the taking of sides among the people found there, and the two are distinct: one can be transported into a world before sorting its characters, and root hard for a character in a story one is not deeply transported by.
- **Schadenfreude**: The engine models the disposition-outcome contingency, not the pleasure at misfortune itself. The joy at a disliked party's suffering is owned by the schadenfreude engine, which the payoff recruits when the villain falls; allegiance owns the structure -- that enjoyment tracks the fit of fortune to disposition -- and hands the specific pleasure-at-misfortune across.
- **Empathy**: The engine models evaluative allegiance, not the capacity to feel into a character. Empathy -- the channel for taking in another's inner state -- is owned by the empathy engine, and it is distinct from allegiance: one can empathize with a disliked character (feel their state while wishing them ill) or root for a liked one without feeling into them; allegiance is the moral taking-of-sides, not the feeling-into.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per file.

- **[allegiance](process_allegiance.md)** (the root, process): The audience's moral bond to the characters of a story, on which enjoyment of their fortunes rides. Anchored by Zillmann & Cantor (affective disposition theory) and Smith (the structure of sympathy).
- **[verdict](process_verdict.md)** (process, form): The moral monitoring -- the audience continuously judging characters' conduct and sorting them into liked and disliked. Anchored by Zillmann (the moral monitor) and Raney (moral judgment and disposition).
- **[rooting](process_rooting.md)** (process, form): The disposition itself -- sympathy and the wish for the liked to prosper, antipathy and the wish for the disliked to fall. Anchored by Zillmann and Smith (allegiance).
- **[payoff](process_payoff.md)** (process, form): The outcome-contingent enjoyment -- satisfaction when fortunes match the disposition, dissatisfaction when they violate it. Anchored by Zillmann (disposition-outcome congruence) and Raney & Bryant (enjoyment).
