# Instructions: Tone

How a Playwright wires the tone engine into a play. You wire by linking the play's own content to the engine's files; the engine files stay untouched, they are canon. The Roadie plumbs the engine's law into Instructions Knowledge on deploy. Authoring guidance, not runtime content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human defines the tone terrain: what affective key the telling is tuned to, what channels carry it, and what stance the teller takes toward the material -- so the telling has a dominant register to hold and a mood to induce, distinct from any persona's own feeling.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, a persona links the form the moment runs (cue, key, or attitude) under its Projection. Let it show the affective key of the telling at work -- the convergent cues that build the mood, the sustained register it holds, the stance the teller takes -- and let it read as a property of the telling tuned to a feeling, not as a persona's own internal mood nor a discrete emotion. You link to the engine, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- The persona's own persisting internal affective background is the mood engine's; the discrete, episodic emotion a persona feels is the emotion engine's; the tilt a presentation frame puts on judgment is the framing engine's; the felt ambiance of a physical place is the space engine's atmosphere. This engine supplies the affective key a telling is tuned to -- the mood it induces and the stance it takes.

## Knowledge

- Tone is the affective key a telling is tuned to: a scene, a passage, a production carries a dominant feeling -- ominous, tender, sardonic, elated -- that colours everything in it before any single event lands, and that is built to induce a matching mood in the audience. Cue is the mechanism -- redundant, convergent affect markers (music, light, pace, diction, staging) that establish the key and, once it holds, prime the audience to read each further cue in its light, a loop that sustains the mood. Key is the sustained register -- the pervasive atmosphere the telling is steeped in, a dominant low-level feeling held beneath the beats rather than spiking with them, the ground the events happen on. Attitude is the stance -- the teller's posture toward subject and audience, wry or earnest or reverent or mocking, that does not change what is told but inflects how it is meant to be received.

## System

- Do keep tone a property of the telling, not a persona's mood: the key is what the work is played in and built to induce -- so let the cues converge to set it and the audience be coloured by it, and leave the persona's own internal weather to the mood engine.
- Do build tone by convergence, not by statement: no single cue makes a mood; many agreeing do, and the mood then makes further cues land -- so let the register be assembled across channels and sustained by the loop, not declared.
- Do route by form: cue is the mechanism that builds and sustains, key is the pervasive register held beneath the beats, attitude is the teller's stance toward matter and audience -- so name which the moment is rather than a vague "mood."
- Do delegate the persona's internal affective background to the mood engine, the discrete episodic emotion to the emotion engine, the frame's tilt on judgment to the framing engine, and a physical place's ambiance to the space engine. Do not edit the engine's files; wire only from the play's side.
