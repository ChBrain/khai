# Tone: Reference

## Line of Work

Tone as **process**: how a telling -- a scene, a passage, a production -- is tuned to a dominant affective key, the mood it is built to induce and the stance it takes. The domain does not model a persona's own internal affective background, which is the mood engine's; nor the discrete, episodic emotion a persona feels, which is the emotion engine's; nor the tilt a presentation frame puts on judgment, which is the framing engine's; nor the felt ambiance of a physical place, which is the space engine's atmosphere. It models the affective register of the telling itself: a property of the work, not a state of a persona -- the key it is played in, the mood it sets in an audience, and the posture it takes toward what it tells, working through the routes by which that key is set and carried.

The spine is the media-aesthetic and literary study of tone, mood, and atmosphere. Greg M. Smith's mood-cue approach holds that the primary emotive work of a film is to create mood: redundant, convergent emotion cues across every channel establish a low-level affective orientation, and once established the mood primes the audience to respond to further cues, a cyclical loop punctuated by brief bursts of intense emotion. Hans Ulrich Gumbrecht recovers Stimmung -- the pervasive atmosphere a work is steeped in, moods placed "on a continuum akin to a musical scale" -- as reading's experience of being present to a tone. I.A. Richards named tone the speaker's attitude toward the audience, one of the four kinds of meaning. Carl Plantinga and Ed S. Tan distinguish the diffuse, sustained mood of a narrative work from the discrete emotions it also arouses. Three routes carry it: cue, key, attitude.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **Mood**: The engine models the affective key a telling is tuned to, not the persona's own internal affective background. The persisting mood a persona carries -- the weather beneath the moment's feeling, on the axes of pleasantness and activation -- is owned by the mood engine; tone is a property of the telling, mood a state of the persona, and though a telling's tone is often built precisely to induce a mood in an audience-persona, the induced state is the mood engine's and the inducing key is tone's. The engine delegates the persona's internal affective state to mood.
- **Emotion**: The engine models the diffuse, sustained affective register of a telling, not the discrete emotion a persona feels. The episodic, appraisal-driven feeling -- joy, sadness, anger, fear, disgust -- that spikes at an event and subsides is owned by the emotion engine; tone is low-level and durable where emotion is intense and brief, a property of the whole telling rather than a persona's response to a moment. The engine delegates the discrete episodic emotion to emotion.
- **Framing**: The engine models the tuning of a telling's feeling, not the tilt a presentation puts on judgment. How the same content, framed as a gain or a loss or through a metaphor, shifts a persona's judgment is owned by the framing engine; tone works on affect and atmosphere, framing on the evaluation of content -- one sets how it feels, the other how it is judged. The engine delegates the presentation-frame's tilt on judgment to framing.
- **Space (atmosphere)**: The engine models the affective key of a telling, not the felt ambiance of a physical place. The atmosphere a persona senses in a room or a landscape -- the ambiance of an inhabited place -- is owned by the space engine's atmosphere; tone is the register of a telling as told, which may evoke a place but is not the place's own felt quality. The engine delegates the ambiance of a physical place to space.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per file.

- **[tone](process_tone.md)** (the root): The affective key a telling is tuned to -- the mood it is built to induce and the stance it takes, a property of the telling colouring all it contains before any event lands. Anchored by Smith (the mood-cue approach) and Gumbrecht (Stimmung).
- **[cue](process_cue.md)** (form): The mechanism route -- redundant, convergent affect markers that establish the key and, in a loop with the mood they create, sustain it. Anchored by Smith (mood-cue theory) and Tan (film as an emotion machine).
- **[key](process_key.md)** (form): The sustained-register route -- the pervasive atmosphere the telling is steeped in, a dominant feeling held beneath the beats rather than spiking with them. Anchored by Gumbrecht (Stimmung) and Plantinga (sustained mood vs discrete emotion).
- **[attitude](process_attitude.md)** (form): The stance route -- the teller's posture toward subject and audience, ironic or earnest or tender, that inflects how the content is meant to be received. Anchored by Richards (tone as the speaker's attitude to the audience).
