# Self-Monitoring: Reference

## Line of Work

Self-monitoring as **position**: the persona's characteristic degree of watching and tuning its own self-presentation to fit the situation, versus letting the presented self flow from inner states held constant across situations. The domain does not model the image a persona defends, which is face's; nor the managing of feeling to meet a role's display, which is emotional-labor's; nor the misleading of another for gain, which is deception's; nor the stable self-concept the constancy expresses, which is identity's. It models the trait itself: how much a persona tailors its self-presentation to the audience, and the positions -- reading the room, tuning the self, holding one self -- that the trait takes.

The spine is Snyder's self-monitoring construct. People differ, as a stable individual difference, in the extent to which they observe and control their expressive behavior and self-presentation. High self-monitors are attentive to social and situational cues and skilled at adjusting their behavior to fit them -- pragmatic, other-directed, variable across situations. Low self-monitors present a self that flows from their own attitudes and feelings and stays consistent across situations -- principled, inner-directed, the same in every room. The high pole divides into a sensitivity to cues and an ability to modify presentation; the low pole is the inner-directed consistency. The three positions are those: scanning, tuning, consistency.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **Face**: The engine models the trait of tailoring self-presentation, not the image a persona defends. The honor, competence, and standing a persona claims and guards in interaction are owned by the face engine; self-monitoring is the standing tendency to watch and adjust the presentation, of which face is one thing managed -- the trait, not the image.
- **Emotional labor**: The engine models the general trait of adjusting self-presentation to situations, not the managing of feeling to meet a role's display for a wage. The surface and deep acting a job's display rules demand are owned by the emotional-labor engine; self-monitoring is the broader dispositional tendency, not tied to a role or to feeling specifically, and the high self-monitor is simply better equipped for the labor the other engine names.
- **Deception**: The engine models fitting the self to the situation, not misleading another for gain. The strategic falsehood that exploits a target is owned by the deception engine; the high self-monitor's tuning is the sanctioned social adjusting of presentation to context, not a con -- the same fluency can serve deceit, but the trait is not the deceit.
- **Identity**: The engine models how consistently a self is expressed across situations, not the self-concept being expressed. The stable sense of who one is is owned by the identity engine; self-monitoring's consistency position expresses that identity steadily and its tuning position varies its display, but the identity itself, and the authenticity the low monitor prizes, are identity's.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per file.

- **[self-monitoring](position_self_monitoring.md)** (the anchor): The trait dimension -- how much a persona watches and tunes its self-presentation to fit the situation versus holding one self from within. Anchored by Snyder (self-monitoring of expressive behavior) and Snyder (public appearances, private realities).
- **[scanning](position_scanning.md)** (position): The high-monitor's outward attention -- a sensitivity to social cues, reading the room for the presentation it calls for. Anchored by Lennox & Wolfe (sensitivity to others' expressive behavior) and Snyder (attentiveness to situational cues).
- **[tuning](position_tuning.md)** (position): The high-monitor's expressive control -- the ability and habit of adjusting the presented self to fit the read, the chameleon across situations. Anchored by Lennox & Wolfe (ability to modify self-presentation) and Snyder (expressive control).
- **[consistency](position_consistency.md)** (position): The low-monitor's inner direction -- a presentation flowing from one's own attitudes and feelings, held steady across situations. Anchored by Snyder & Gangestad (the inner-directed, consistent self) and Snyder (the low self-monitor).
