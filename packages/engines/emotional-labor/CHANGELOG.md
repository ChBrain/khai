# @chbrain/khai-engine-emotional-labor

## 0.1.1

### Patch Changes

- 4738578: Add the emotional-labor engine: the effort a persona spends to feel or feign the emotion a role demands they display -- managing the heart to meet a display rule, for a wage or a part. The root (`process_emotional_labor.md`) declares Hochschild's emotional labor: a role that owes a particular emotion makes its production work, and the width of the gap between what the persona feels and what they must show sets the cost. Three forms carry it, the canonical strategy triad: `masking` (surface acting -- the display painted on over an unfelt or contrary emotion, felt and shown held apart by effort, the costliest form to the self), `method` (deep acting -- the persona works themselves into actually feeling the required emotion, à la method acting, so the display rides out on a feeling induced to order), and `genuine` (the spontaneous case -- the required emotion already felt, so the display is sincere and the labor vanishes into ordinary emotion).

  An unusually good fit for khai's dramaturgical core -- personas performing feeling, the very stuff of a play. Bounded against `emotion` (the raw feelings themselves vs their management for a display), `regulation` (Gross's self-directed regulation -- emotional labor recruits its reappraisal and suppression but bends them to a role's prescribed display for an audience), `backstage` (the hidden region where the performance is dropped), `face` (the self-presentation of competence and image), and `deception` (a sanctioned institutional display vs manipulation for gain). No homonym gate: `emotional-labor` and the form stems `masking`/`method`/`genuine` are all free. Warranted (LORE) on Hochschild (_The Managed Heart_, 1983), Ashforth & Humphrey (service-role reconceptualization, 1993), Grandey (labor-as-regulation, 2000), Diefendorff et al. (the three strategies, 2005), and Ekman & Friesen (display rules, 1969). Set at patch as the free level; a new engine may warrant a minor at the maintainer's `bump:minor` label.
