# Possible Selves: Reference

## Line of Work

Possible selves as a **position** engine: the future-oriented components of the self-concept -- cognitive representations of what a persona could become -- taken across the standings a persona holds toward its own future self. The domain does not model the present self-story, who the persona takes itself to be now, which is the identity engine's; nor the present gap between the actual self and the ideal or ought self-guides a persona is measured against, which is the self-discrepancy engine's; nor a specific external end-state a persona pursues, which is the goal engine's; nor the counterfactual ache over a past choice already made, which is the regret engine's. It models the forward face of the self: the self projected into a future the persona moves toward or away from, and the pull and push that future exerts on the present.

The spine is the possible-selves literature. Markus and Nurius's founding work casts possible selves as the cognitive bridge between self-concept and motivation -- the selves we would like to become (hoped-for), are afraid of becoming (feared), and expect to become (probable) -- giving specific cognitive form to hopes, fears, and goals, and so lending them the power to organize and energize action. Oyserman's program develops the motivational mechanism: possible selves regulate behavior most when a hoped-for self is balanced by a corresponding feared self in the same domain, when they feel congruent with the persona's current and social identity, and when they are linked to concrete strategies -- a free-floating aspiration moves little, an identity-congruent and strategy-linked one moves much (identity-based motivation; the school-to-jobs findings). King's work adds the lost possible self -- a once-live future relinquished -- and shows that the capacity to elaborate such a self and then let it go tracks maturity and well-being, a developed relation to one's own foreclosed becoming. The altitudes are those: the repertoire of future selves, and the hoped, feared, expected, and lost standings a persona holds within it.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **Identity**: The engine models the future self, not the present one. Identity -- the persona's present self-story, who it takes itself to be now, its standing sense of self across roles and time -- is owned by the identity engine; possible selves are strictly the forward projections, the selves the persona could yet become, which draw on the present identity but face the future. The two compose: identity is who the persona is, possible selves are who it might be.
- **Self-discrepancy**: The engine models the future self-representation, not the present gap against a standard. Self-discrepancy -- the felt distance between the actual self and the ideal or ought self-guides a persona holds itself to now, and the dejection or agitation that gap produces (Higgins) -- is owned by the self-discrepancy engine; possible selves are the temporally forward selves a persona moves toward or away from, not the present measurement of the self against a guide. The hoped-for self can supply the content a discrepancy is later measured against, but the possible self is the future picture, the discrepancy the present gap.
- **Goal**: The engine models the self-representation, not the end-state pursued. A goal -- a specific outcome a persona commits to and pursues, with its own structure of intention, planning, and attainment -- is owned by the goal engine; a possible self is the self-relevant picture that lends a goal its motivating personal form, the who-I-would-become rather than the what-I-would-achieve. The two compose: the possible self personalizes the goal, the goal engine drives its pursuit.
- **Regret**: The engine models the relinquished future self, not the counterfactual emotion over a past choice. Regret -- the aversive feeling on comparing an actual past outcome to a better one that a different choice would have brought -- is owned by the regret engine; the lost possible self is the self-representation left behind, the person one could have been, held as a branch of identity rather than as the emotion of a specific bad decision. The lost self can be an object regret attaches to, but the possible self is the foreclosed identity, the regret the feeling about the turn that foreclosed it.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per file.

- **[possible-selves](position_possible_selves.md)** (the anchor, position): The repertoire of future self-representations -- the selves a persona could become, the bridge between self-concept and motivation. Anchored by Markus & Nurius (the founding) and Oyserman (the motivational mechanism).
- **[hoped](position_hoped.md)** (position, standing): The desired future self -- what the persona would like to become, drawing present effort toward it. Anchored by Markus & Nurius and Markus & Ruvolo.
- **[feared](position_feared.md)** (position, standing): The dreaded future self -- what the persona is afraid of becoming, pushing present effort away, often the sharper motivator and strongest paired with its hope. Anchored by Markus & Nurius and Oyserman (balance).
- **[expected](position_expected.md)** (position, standing): The probable future self -- what the persona realistically anticipates becoming, the reality anchor between hope and fear. Anchored by Markus & Nurius.
- **[lost](position_lost.md)** (position, standing): The relinquished future self -- a once-live future given up, carried as a branch of identity, its letting-go a mark of maturity. Anchored by King (lost possible selves).
