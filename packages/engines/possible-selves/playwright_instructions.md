# Instructions: Possible Selves

How a Playwright wires the possible-selves engine into a play. You wire by linking the play's own content to the engine's files; the engine files stay untouched, they are canon. The Roadie plumbs the engine's law into Instructions Knowledge on deploy. Authoring guidance, not runtime content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human sets the possible-selves terrain: what futures are live for the persona -- what it could become, hopes to, dreads, or has had to give up -- so a moment can run on a genuine future self that pulls the present toward it or pushes it away.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, a persona links the possible self a moment runs under its Projection: which future self it is reaching for, fleeing, expecting, or mourning -- and let it steer the present, the hope drawing effort toward it, the fear driving effort clear of it, the expected setting the realistic anchor, the lost sitting as a road closed. Read the pull and push as the future self acting on the now. You link to the engine, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- The present self-story, who the persona takes itself to be now, is the identity engine's; the present gap between the actual self and its ideal or ought guides is the self-discrepancy engine's; a specific external end-state pursued is the goal engine's; the counterfactual ache over a past choice already made is the regret engine's. This engine supplies the future selves -- the hoped, the feared, the expected, and the lost -- and the pull and push they exert on the present.

## Knowledge

- Possible selves are the future-oriented components of the self-concept -- cognitive representations of what a persona could become, the bridge between self-concept and motivation (Markus & Nurius). They run in standings. Hoped is the desired future self -- what the persona would like to become, drawing effort toward it. Feared is the dreaded future self -- what it is afraid of becoming, pushing effort away, often the sharper motivator. Expected is the probable self -- what it realistically anticipates becoming, the anchor between hope and fear. Lost is the relinquished self -- a once-live future given up, the person it could have been, carried as a branch of identity. A possible self motivates most when a hope is balanced by its feared counterpart in the same domain and linked to a strategy the persona can actually walk (Oyserman). Read a persona by which future selves it holds and how they steer its present conduct.

## System

- Do keep the possible self the future self-representation, not the present one: it is who the persona could yet become, drawn on from the present identity but facing forward -- let the present self-story be identity's and the present gap against a standard be self-discrepancy's, and keep the possible self the temporally forward picture.
- Do stage the pull and the push: the hoped-for self drawing effort toward it, the feared self driving effort clear of what it dreads, and let the feared self often be the sharper mover -- strongest, both of them, when a hope is paired with the fear of its opposite so the persona has both a destination and a cliff.
- Do let the expected self anchor the two in reality: the likely course the persona plans by, against which the hope is measured for reach and the fear for danger -- and let the gap between the expected and the hoped-for be much of what the persona is working to close.
- Do make the possible self motivate only when it is real to the persona -- congruent with its current and social identity and linked to a path it can see; a free-floating fantasy moves little (Oyserman). Do let the lost self be held with maturity -- elaborated and then relinquished, the letting-go a developed relation to one's own becoming, distinct from clinging or from never having faced it. Do not edit the engine's files; wire only from the play's side.
