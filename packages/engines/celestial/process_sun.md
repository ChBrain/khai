# Process: Sun

## Taxonomy

[Celestial](process_celestial.md), the mode where the sun is the place's governor -- the day's arc, the seasons' swing, the light that the place's stone and fields face toward.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: celestial

## Initiated by

The sun rises at the place's horizon and will rise tomorrow at a different angle, then angle again through the seasons until the year turns and the same arc repeats.
The Sun starts where the place sits on the Earth and the solar track crosses it -- the day given over to the sun's path from false dawn through noon to the evening, then the season swinging from winter's low arc to summer's high, solstices and equinoxes as the place's own hinges and the light that will not be stopped.
Every place knows the sun by the angle it takes; no two places see the same day.

## Direction

Along the place's own latitude and the year's own turning.
The sun measures time on three scales at once: the day, the track from dawn to dusk on this one rising and setting; the season, the swing from the winter solstice when the sun hangs low all day to the summer solstice when it climbs high and the dusk lasts long, turning back toward dark again on the equinoxes when the day and night balance; and the year, the full turn that repeats -- the same solstice alignment that marked the place's founding and will mark it again in three hundred years, the place as calendar and the sun as the hand that marks it.
The direction is the calendar itself: the place keeping time by an arc it cannot alter, only answer.

## Lever

The sun's certain arc against the place's own making, which has bent itself toward light.
The carrying force is the sun's path, fixed by the place's latitude and the season, giving the place its day-length and its light-angle; the resistance is the place's own buildings and fields that face toward it, that have been aligned to it, that live by its arc and cannot ignore it -- the architecture that catches or shades the light, the fields that turn to face the sun's noon, the monuments aligned to solstice, the shadow cast at a time that marks the year.
No place can hide from the sun's arc, and every place has shaped itself to answer it.

## Echo

The place read by the sun's angle and the season that swings it.
The solstice-aligned monument casts its shadow through the chamber where it will not come again for a year; the field knows when to plant because the sun's angle tells it; the shade cast by the place's stone moves through the day and gives the dweller its time, read from the ground itself.
The sun's path is dated from the place's own latitude and the day's own turn, and the place is known by the geometry of light it makes.
