# Process: Starfield

## Taxonomy

[Celestial](process_celestial.md), the mode where the night sky's fixed stars are the place's own unchanging ceiling and the oldest navigation the place carries.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: celestial

## Initiated by

When the sun sets and the sky darkens enough for the stars to emerge, the night sky appears the same year to year and generation to generation, the constellations and bright stars rising and setting in the same patterns that marked the night for thousands of years before anyone named them.
The Starfield starts where the place's darkness allows the stars to be seen -- not the darkness of a place that has its own light source burning in the night but the true dark where the only light is the light that falls from the stars themselves and the reflections of the Milky Way's own abundance.
The stars have not moved in any human timescale; they are the one Shown that does not change, and the place that can see them carries a ceiling as old as the Earth itself.

## Direction

Above the place from sunset to dawn, a map fixed in the dark.
The stars trace the constellations the place uses to mark the seasons and the passage of the night, the bright stars standing as navigation points and the Milky Way itself the river that runs across the sky from north to south, visible from most places on the Earth that still have a dark enough night to see it.
For most of human history, the starfield was the place's own map and calendar, Polaris marking north and the bright stars rising in sequence through the seasons, the night sky as the oldest instrument of navigation and the one that still works in places where darkness remains.

## Lever

The stars' own fixed light against the place's own making of artificial light that blinds the eye to them.
The carrying force is the stars themselves, fixed in their positions and shining for thousands of years at the same brightness from the Earth's perspective; the resistance is the light the place makes at night -- streetlamps, building lights, the glow of cities burning the night bright enough that the Milky Way is invisible and the stars are few enough to count.
Light pollution is not the weathering of the stars but the blinding of the place that stands beneath them, the substitution of the place's own light for the light the sky offers.

## Echo

The place darkened or blinded, its ceiling visible or hidden.
A place in true darkness shows a Milky Way so bright that it casts shadows on the ground at night, the river of stars running across the sky in such abundance that the constellation names are all but lost in the profusion; a place in the light pollution of a city shows perhaps the brightest stars and the Moon if it is bright enough, the vast majority of the sky dark and empty because the artificial light blinds out the stars before the eye can gather their light.
More than one-third of humanity now lives beneath light-polluted skies where the Milky Way is invisible, most of Europe and North America unable to see the fixed ceiling that once marked the night as surely as the sun marked the day.
The starfield is dated from the night's own darkness and the place's own making of light, and the place is known by whether its sky shows the stars at all.
