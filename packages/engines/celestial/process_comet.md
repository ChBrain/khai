# Process: Comet

## Taxonomy

[Celestial](process_celestial.md), the mode where the visitor hangs in the sky on a clock measured in lifetimes, returning on a schedule no human witness will see complete.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: celestial

## Initiated by

A point of light appears in the sky and begins to brighten, drawing a tail across the night that grows longer over weeks as the comet swings toward its closest approach and then recedes again.
The Comet starts where a comet enters the visible sky -- the apparition that is not a fixed star but a moving light that stays in the same small corner of the sky for weeks, growing bright enough to see by day and bright enough to cast shadows at night, then fading back to invisibility as it swings away on an orbit that will not return within any human lifetime, or returns on a schedule written in generations.
A comet is a visitor in the most literal sense: it arrives according to a rhythm older than empires and departs according to that same mathematics.

## Direction

Along the arc of its own orbit, visible from the place only when it swings close enough to the sun to heat and brighten.
Halley's comet returns every 76 years, close enough to the rhythms of recorded history that a human can measure one return against another; other comets swing in orbits centuries or millennia long, appearing once in a place's recorded time and disappearing back into the deep dark beyond any generation's reach, their return readable in the mathematics only after the fact.
The comet itself does not change for the place; the place only sees it when its mathematics bring it near enough to shine.

## Lever

The apparition against the place's inability to call it back or speed its going.
The carrying force is the comet's own orbit, fixed and knowable only after it has already passed through the sky and astronomers have measured its arc; the resistance is the place's own watching and the meaning read into the visitor, the awe or fear it provokes, the records made of it because it is rarer than the daily sun and the nightly stars.
The comet comes and goes by mathematics older than prophecy, and no place can hold it or name it into staying.

## Echo

The place marked by the apparition, recorded against the day it will return.
In 1066, the comet Halley swung close to the sun and appeared in the sky above the Normans and Saxons at Hastings, its image woven into the tapestry that records the year, a visitor placed in the weaving not because it caused the battle but because it marked the year, a sky-witness to the place's own event.
The comet is dated from the year it appeared and the mathematics that predict its return, and the place remembers the visitor and the night the sky put it on display.
