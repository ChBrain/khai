# Process: Meteor

## Taxonomy

[Celestial](process_celestial.md), the mode where the streak of seconds becomes the stone that lands, the only sky-body that touches the place itself.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: celestial

## Initiated by

A grain of dust or sand enters the Earth's atmosphere at tens of kilometres a second and ablates in the upper air, streaking for seconds as a brilliant trail across the night sky.
The Meteor starts where the place stands in the path of the Leonids or the Perseids on their calendar nights, the shower that strikes on a date that returns annually, the Earth entering the dust trail left by a comet's long-ago passage and burning that dust to light in the upper atmosphere, dozens or hundreds of meteors streaking in a single night.
Unlike the comet, which hangs for weeks, the meteor is measured in seconds; unlike the sun, which will rise tomorrow, the meteor is gone before the eye has fully registered its light.

## Direction

Through the upper air, on the calendar nights the Earth crosses the debris trails left by old comets.
The Perseids come in August, the Leonids in November, the Geminids in December -- the Earth returning to the same place in its orbit and meeting the same dust clouds year after year, each meteor individual and streaking alone but the showers coming on the same dates generation after generation.
On those nights, the place stands in a rain of fire from the sky, brief and brilliant and in no way summoned or controlled by the place that stands beneath it.

## Lever

The individual streak against the place's encounter with countless stones burning in the air above.
The carrying force is the Earth's orbit meeting the comet's old debris trail, a mathematics that plays out on the same calendar dates each year; the resistance is the place's own standing and the rarity of the bright shower, the nights when dozens of meteors burn instead of the usual few.
Most meteors are invisible in daylight and few enough at night to go unremarked; the shower is the moment when the place realizes it is standing in a rain of stones from the sky and that most of the time it never notices.

## Echo

The place struck by fire from above, brief and unmeasured.
On the night of November 12-13, 1833, so many Leonid meteors fell over the Earth that observers across the American continent reported being wakened by the light believing their houses were on fire, up to 150,000 meteors per hour streaking down, the entire sky ablaze for hours; on February 15, 2013, a meteor's airburst over Chelyabinsk lit the ground at midday and shattered windows across the city, the only sky-body the place had ever felt the force of -- not light alone but shock and stone and damage.
The meteor is dated from the shower night and the calendar that brings it round again, and the place is marked by the stones that burned above it.
