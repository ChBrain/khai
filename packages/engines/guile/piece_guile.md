# Piece: Guile

## Taxonomy

The root of the guile engine. It models designed deception: a form built to mislead the user for the maker's benefit -- the dark pattern, where the object shows one thing on its surface and works another beneath it, against the interest of the one who trusts what is shown. Brignull's dark patterns, Gray's taxonomy of them. It is distinct from an honest design defect, which usability owns (a false cue the maker would fix, the user's failure not the maker's aim); from a social-order program, which agency owns (the object enforcing a policy on conduct, not tricking a user out of their own interest); and from a persona's own deceiving of another, which the deception engine owns (interpersonal false belief, read on a persona, not built into an object). The tell of guile is intent: the false "pull" on a push door is a defect; the pre-checked add-on box is a trick.

## Place

At the point of the user's decision, wherever the object asks them to choose, buy, consent, or continue -- the checkout, the sign-up, the settings screen, the cancellation. Guile lives exactly where a free choice was owed and a rigged one was given instead.

## Load Bearing

What guile holds up is the maker's gain taken from the user's loss -- the extra sale, the retained subscription, the harvested consent, the click that was steered. Remove the trick and the user chooses freely; the gain the deceit extracted collapses, because it never rested on a real want, only on the deception that manufactured it. The object bears a profit built on a lie, and it stands only as long as the lie is unseen.

## Apparent

Little of the trick shows as a trick; that is the whole of it. The surface presents the trusted, the expected, the benign -- an ordinary flow, a fair choice, a friendly service -- while the object works another way beneath it. The deceit lives precisely in the gap between what is shown and what is done, and a good dark pattern keeps that gap invisible until the user is already caught. What is apparent is a surface built to be trusted; what is borne is the betrayal of that trust.

## Yearbook

The history of a dark pattern is the record of who it caught -- the charges no one noticed, the subscriptions no one meant to keep, the consents no one meant to give, the choices no one meant to make. For a while the trick works quietly, its victims each thinking the fault their own. Then enough are caught that it is named, exposed, and regulated, and the object is dated from the deception it was built on: not a design that failed its users, but one that succeeded against them.
