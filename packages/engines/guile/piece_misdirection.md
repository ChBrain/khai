# Piece: Misdirection

## Taxonomy

[Guile](piece_guile.md), the facet this is a part of. Misdirection models the trick of steering the eye and the hand by a rigged presentation -- the confirm button bright and the decline greyed to near-invisibility, the pre-checked opt-in, the confirmshaming "no thanks, I like paying full price," the question worded to trip. Brignull's misdirection (Gray's interface interference). It is distinct from sneaking, which hides the thing: misdirection shows the choice plainly but rigs how it is presented, so the user picks the maker's way believing it their own.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: guile

## Place

At the point of choice -- the consent screen, the checkout option, the settings toggle -- wherever two paths are offered and the presentation can tilt the field between them.

## Load Bearing

What misdirection holds up is the choice made by design rather than by intent. Remove the rigging -- present the options with equal weight -- and the user's real preference shows; the maker's-way outcomes rest on the tilt, not the want. The trick bears a decision the interface made and dressed as the user's own, and it holds only while the scale stays hidden under the appearance of a fair choice.

## Apparent

What shows is a fair choice; what is built is the thumb on the scale -- the visual weight, the default already set, the shaming decline, the trick wording. Each option is technically present, and the presentation is the deceit: the user is steered while believing they were free, which is why the misdirection is felt, if at all, only in hindsight.

## Yearbook

The history of a misdirection is the record of the choices it stole -- the opt-ins never meant, the boxes left checked, the upgrades taken by a bright button and a dim refusal. The object is dated from the scales it tipped while looking level.
