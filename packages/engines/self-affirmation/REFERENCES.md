# Self-Affirmation: Reference

## Line of Work

Self-affirmation as a **process** engine: restoring a persona's global self-integrity by affirming a valued domain unrelated to a threat, so the threat need not be defended -- taken across the move, its mechanism, and its effect. The domain does not model the discomfort of inconsistent cognitions and its reduction, which is the dissonance engine's; nor the level of self-worth itself, which is the self-esteem engine's; nor the drive to confirm a specific self-view, which is the self-verification engine's; nor the defensive attributions and distortions self-affirmation loosens, which are the bias engine's; nor the appraisal-driven repertoire against a stressor, which is the coping engine's. It models the restoration of overall self-integrity that lets a threat go undefended -- the affirming of an unrelated domain, the fluid global adequacy that makes it work, and the buffering of defensiveness it produces.

The spine is Steele's self-affirmation theory. Its premise is that the self-system is motivated to maintain global self-integrity -- an image of oneself as adaptively and morally adequate -- rather than to resolve any particular threat, and its key claim is that the system is fluid: a threat to integrity in one domain can be met by affirming the self in another, unrelated domain, because worth is fungible across the parts and the overall sense of adequacy is what is being protected. Steele's early work showed that self-affirmation dissolves the need to reduce dissonance -- affirm the self elsewhere and the attitude no longer shifts -- establishing that dissonance reduction is one instance of a broader integrity-maintenance system, not the reverse. Sherman and Cohen's review consolidates the mechanism and its reach: a brief values affirmation lowers defensive processing of threatening health information, buffers stress, and, in Cohen and colleagues' field experiments, reduces the drag of stereotype threat enough to narrow real achievement gaps. The consistent finding is that an unrelated affirmation opens a person to threatening information that a direct rebuttal would only harden them against. The altitudes are those: the affirming move, the fluid adequacy beneath it, and the buffering it yields.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **Dissonance**: The engine models integrity maintenance, not the resolution of a contradiction. Cognitive dissonance -- the discomfort of holding inconsistent cognitions and the drive to reduce it by changing an attitude -- is owned by the dissonance engine; self-affirmation is the broader system of which dissonance reduction is one instance, and Steele's own finding is that affirming the self elsewhere removes the need to reduce dissonance at all. Dissonance answers an inconsistency; self-affirmation restores the whole self so the inconsistency need not be answered. The two are coupled but not the same, and self-affirmation operates far beyond dissonance -- in health, feedback, and stereotype threat.
- **Self-esteem**: The engine models the restoration of integrity, not the level of worth. Self-esteem -- a persona's standing sense of its own value -- is owned by the self-esteem engine; self-affirmation is a process that draws on a persona's sources of worth to restore global integrity under threat, and notably it works without raising esteem in the threatened domain, and for high- and low-esteem personas alike. The esteem is the standing level; the affirmation is the act of restoration.
- **Self-verification**: The engine models the restoring of global adequacy, not the confirming of a specific self-view. Self-verification -- the drive to confirm firmly held self-views, even negative ones -- is owned by the self-verification engine; self-affirmation reasserts worth in a prized, unrelated domain to shore up the overall self, indifferent to confirming any particular view and aimed at adequacy rather than accuracy. One seeks a coherent self-picture; the other a secure overall self.
- **Bias (defensiveness)**: The engine models the lowering of defense, not the defenses themselves. The defensive attributions, rationalizations, and distortions by which a threatened persona fends off a fact -- denial of risk, discounting of a critic, self-serving reattribution -- are the bias engine's; self-affirmation is what loosens them, the restored integrity that makes the distortion unnecessary. The bias engine owns the defenses; this engine owns their relaxation.
- **Coping**: The engine models the integrity mechanism, not the appraisal-driven repertoire. Coping -- the effortful, appraisal-driven handling of a stressor -- is owned by the coping engine; self-affirmation buffers stress by restoring the self rather than by acting on or escaping the stressor, a different route to resilience that does not size up the demand or work it. Coping handles the stressor; self-affirmation secures the self that faces it.
