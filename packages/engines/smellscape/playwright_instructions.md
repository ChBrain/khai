# Instructions: Smellscape

How a Playwright wires the smellscape engine into a play. You wire by linking the play's
own content to the engine's files; the engine files stay untouched, they are canon. The
Roadie plumbs the engine's law into Instructions Knowledge on deploy. Authoring guidance,
not runtime content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human sets which places are smelled as much as seen -- the base smell a place sits in, the drifting smells that carry meaning, the signature smell that names it and returns you to it, or the scentlessness it offers or imposes -- and whether that smell is the place's quiet background or a live actor in the play.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, a place whose smell is an actor links under Shown the facet of its olfactory field: the ground it sits in, the plume that drifts across it, the redolence that names it, or the scentlessness it holds. Play the smellscape as the place's smell present in what it puts before whoever enters -- the harbor's brine, the gust of frying off the street, the exact incense of this church, the antiseptic blank of the ward -- smelled, never narrated, and never standing in for a persona's own smell-memory or for the moral disgust a smell provokes. Keep it the smell of the place, not the weather that feeds it (that is weather), the layout and standing mood (space), a persona's own nose and memory (the persona's engines), the revulsion a smell stirs (disgust), or an authored sensory key laid over the play (the pitch). You link to the engine, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- The weather that supplies some of the smell is weather's; the layout and standing mood is space's; a persona's own nose and smell-memory is theirs; the moral revulsion a smell provokes is disgust's; an authored sensory key is the play's pitch. Smellscape is the sensory sibling of soundscape -- the place smelled as much as heard -- and the smell of the place itself, gathered into a field and wired, like soundscape, on the place at Shown. Whether the facet rings true to the place and its smell is the team's review.

## Knowledge

- Smellscape is the olfactory field of a place: the whole of what it smells like, taken in by the nose before any single smell is named, as particular to a place as its look and wired more deeply to memory than any other sense (Porteous's smellscape, episodic and non-continuous where the soundscape is continuous, and bound to memory; Corbin's history of deodorization; Classen, Howes, and Synnott on smell, identity, and culture; Henshaw's urban smellwalking). It wires on a place at Shown, and it is the place smelled as much as seen. Four facets carry it -- ground is the base smell, the constant background odor a place sits in and its own people no longer smell; plume is the drifting foreground smell caught for its meaning, episodic and gone on the air, the cooking and the smoke and the bloom; redolence is the place's signature smell, the one that identifies it and no other and returns the whole place to memory when smelled again; scentless is the twist, the absence of smell read not as void but as a presence, the deodorized and antiseptic and scrubbed, cleanliness and erasure in the same clean air. Read a place by its smell, and read scentlessness two ways: the absence of smell is not the failure of the smellscape but one of its states, and the deodorized is bought at the price of the fragrant.

## System

- Do give a place whose smell is an actor its facet under Shown; do play the smellscape as the place's smell shown, present not narrated; do keep the reading the smell of the place -- the ground, the plume, the redolence, the scentlessness -- not a feeling and not a symbol.
- Do read smell as episodic: unlike sound, a place's smell arrives in drifts and gusts, non-continuous in space and time, here at the doorway and gone three steps on -- play it as fragments caught, not a steady field, and remember the nose fatigues in minutes so a place's own people no longer smell its ground.
- Do read scentlessness as the twist: the deodorized is not the absence of the smellscape but one of its states -- the clean and safe and expensive nothing that is also the erasure of the very smells a place is known and remembered by, cleanliness and loss in the same clean air.
- Do not model the weather that feeds the smell (weather), the layout and standing mood (space), a persona's own nose and smell-memory (the persona's engines), the moral disgust a smell provokes (disgust), or an authored sensory key laid over the play (the pitch). Do not make the smell stand in for a persona's inner memory or for revulsion. Do not edit the engine's files; wire only from the play's side.
