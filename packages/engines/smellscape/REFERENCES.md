# Smellscape: Reference

## Line of Work

Smellscape as **process**: the olfactory field of a place -- the whole of what it smells
like, taken in by the nose before any single smell is named, as particular to a place as
its look, wired more deeply to memory than any other sense, and as much an actor as its
scenery. It wires on a place at Shown, the place's self-presentation to whoever enters,
because a place is smelled as much as seen: the base it sits in, the smells that drift
across it to be read, the one that marks it as itself and returns it to memory, and the
scentlessness beneath. The domain does not model the weather that supplies some of the
smell -- the rain, the wet earth, the sea-wind -- which is weather's; nor the layout and
standing mood of the setting, which is space's; nor a persona's own nose and smell-memory,
the aromas that move them because of who they are, which is theirs; nor the moral revulsion
a smell provokes, the disgust and the othering, which is disgust's; nor an authored sensory
key laid over the play, which is the pitch's. It models the smell the place itself gives
off and is smelled by.

The spine is the geography and cultural history of smell -- the study of the smell
environment as a composed and remembered field rather than a mere nuisance to be scrubbed.
Porteous named the smellscape by deliberate analogy to Schafer's soundscape, and drew its
defining difference: where sound is continuous, smell is episodic and non-continuous,
arriving in fragments across space and time, and it is bound to memory and emotion as no
other sense is -- the involuntary Proustian return. Corbin traced the long history of
deodorization: how the modern world grew intolerant of odor and worked its places toward a
clean scentlessness, and what the fragrant and the foul once meant. Classen, Howes, and
Synnott read smell as culturally coded -- how a place and a people are known, judged, and
remembered by how they smell. Henshaw walked the modern city by nose, mapping its
background and episodic smells and the deodorizing pressures on them. So the engine gives
smellscape three smells and a twist -- ground, plume, redolence -- and keeps a fourth,
scentless, the absence of smell read not as the failure of the field but as one of its
states, the deodorized presence a place can offer or impose.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The weather that feeds the smell (weather)**: The rain on hot stone, the wet earth
  after a storm, the sea-wind -- weather is one of the smellscape's sources, but the weather
  itself is weather's engine. Smellscape takes the smell the weather makes as part of the
  field; it does not model the storm. The rain is weather's; the smell of the rain, gathered
  into the place's field, is smellscape's.
- **The layout and standing mood (space)**: How a place is arranged, how its size and shape
  and standing feel read -- the room's proportions, the street's enclosure -- is space's.
  Smellscape is the place's smell, not its shape; the two meet in a place (a cellar's
  closeness and its damp) but are not the same force. Space is how the place is laid out;
  smellscape is how it smells.
- **A persona's own nose and smell-memory (the persona's engines)**: The aroma that undoes a
  particular persona, the smell that carries their private past, what they smell because of
  who they are and what they have lived -- that is the persona's, not the place's. Smellscape
  is the place's smell, smelled by anyone who enters; it never stands in for a persona's own
  involuntary memory, though the place's redolence is exactly what such memory catches on.
- **The moral revulsion a smell provokes (disgust)**: The recoil from the stench, the
  judgment carried in calling a person or a place foul, the othering by smell -- that moral
  emotion is disgust's engine. Smellscape is the place's smell as a sensory field, the reek
  and the fragrance as facts of the place; the revulsion or the craving it stirs in a
  persona is disgust's and the persona's, not the place's own.
- **An authored sensory key (the play's pitch)**: A sensory register the author lays over
  the whole play for the audience, a key the place does not itself give off -- that is
  authored key, the pitch, not the place's field. Smellscape is the smell within the world,
  the smell the place makes; an authored overlay is over it, and the two are different
  layers.
- **The facet itself is not engine content**: The engine declares the four facets exist;
  which one a given place shows lives on that place, linked under its Shown chapter. The
  engine refuses to fix a place's smell for it.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per file.

- **[smellscape](process_smellscape.md)** (the root): the olfactory field of a place, the
  whole of what it smells like taken in before any smell is named, the place smelled as much
  as seen and bound to memory. Anchored by Porteous (the smellscape by analogy to the
  soundscape, episodic and memory-bound).
- **[ground](process_ground.md)**: the base smell -- the constant background odor a place
  sits in, smelled by constancy and forgotten by its own people: the brine, the bakery, the
  pine, the tannery, noticed most on arrival or return. Anchored by Porteous (the base of
  the field) and Corbin (the standing odor of a place).
- **[plume](process_plume.md)**: the drifting foreground smell caught for its meaning -- the
  episodic, non-continuous smell borne on the air, the cooking and the smoke and the bloom
  and the rot, here and gone. Anchored by Porteous (smell as episodic and fragmentary) and
  Henshaw (the city's episodic smells).
- **[redolence](process_redolence.md)**: the place's signature smell -- the one that
  identifies it and no other and returns the whole place to memory when smelled again, the
  aroma worth keeping. Anchored by Porteous (smell and involuntary memory) and Classen,
  Howes, and Synnott (smell as identity).
- **[scentless](process_scentless.md)**: the absence of smell read as a presence -- the
  deodorized, antiseptic, scrubbed nothing, cleanliness and erasure in the same clean air.
  The engine's twist; anchored by Corbin (deodorization) and the modern scarcity of the
  fragrant.
