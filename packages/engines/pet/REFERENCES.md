# Pet: Reference

## Line of Work

Pet as **piece**: an animal kept for company at total dependency, and the keeper who arranged it.

This is khai's first engine with an animal in it. Across the catalogue animals have appeared only as
material inside human-subject engines -- Hardin's herder adding one more beast in **commons**,
animal-reminder revulsion in **disgust**, a carved animal in **totem**. Nothing has read the relation
itself, which is distinctive enough to want its own engine: the other party has interests, cannot be
reasoned with, cannot consent, and cannot reciprocate in the terms the arrangement runs on.

Tuan is the spine, and his title is the argument. _Dominance and Affection: The Making of Pets_ holds
that the two words are not opposites but a compound: pet-making is the exercise of power in its most
palatable form, and affection is what power looks like when nothing in the arrangement resists it. He
takes the claim well past animals -- to topiary, fountains, dwarfs, and servants -- which is what makes
it usable here, because it means the engine is about a **relation** rather than a species. Serpell
supplies the empirical side, and Berger explains why the arrangement is as common as it now is: animals
have been withdrawn from ordinary life, and the pet is the remnant, a creature of its owner's way of
living.

**The acquiring** is a decision about a life taken entirely on one side. The criteria are the keeper's,
and breed animals are shaped over generations toward exactly those criteria, which is why so many carry
the illnesses that come with the shape. Rescue inverts the moral story and leaves the structure
untouched: the animal is still chosen, still by somebody, still from a room of others who were not.

**The arranging** protects the animal by removing its capacity to do without protection. Every removal
is defensible alone and most are veterinary orthodoxy -- neutering, confinement, the schedule, the
training -- and the numbers are not close, since an indoor cat outlives an outdoor one by years. Their
sum is an organism that would die in weeks if somebody stopped bothering, and that dependence is not a
side effect of the care but the thing the care is organised around.

**The keeper** holds every route to everything the animal needs, and none of those routes existed before
they opened them. They order the whole of another creature's day without ever issuing an order it could
refuse, and they cannot take a neutral position: a keeper who stops deciding has neglected rather than
freed. The authority is total and is almost never felt as authority, because the keeper experiences the
arrangement as looking after something, and that description is accurate.

**The ending** is the one act with no equivalent in the keeper's other relationships. Nobody schedules a
parent's death; here it is the responsible act and declining it is the failure. It is a genuine mercy,
and its timing turns on the keeper's judgement, finances, and tolerance for watching.

Hence the **twist**, in the anchor's Yearbook. **A pet is kept alive by a dependence its keeper arranged,
and arranging it is how the keeper came to love it.** The causation runs the way round nobody expects.
It is not that affection came first and care followed; the taking on of a creature that can do nothing
for itself -- the feeding, the letting out, the vet bill, the noticing -- is the activity out of which
the attachment is built.

The engine is built so that nobody has to be cruel for it to run, and a play should keep it that way.
The keeper is good. Every removal was the right call. The affection is real.

**On the evidence, stated plainly.** **Haraway is the standing objection and is kept rather than
answered.** _When Species Meet_ and _The Companion Species Manifesto_ argue directly against the reading
this engine is built on: companion species co-constitute each other, the traffic runs both ways, and
describing the animal as a recipient of arrangement misses that it is also an agent shaping the person.
She is not a footnote here. If she is right, the engine reads one half of a two-way relation as though it
were the whole, and a reader should hold that possibility open while using it. The engine's defence is
narrow -- that the asymmetries it names (who chooses, who is altered, who decides the ending) are real
whatever else is also true -- and that defence does not dispose of her case. **Tuan's book is an essay,
not a study.** It is a wide-ranging humanistic argument across gardens, fountains, animals and servants,
and it is persuasive rather than tested; nobody has measured whether affection follows caretaking in the
order he claims, and the engine's twist inherits that status. **The engine cannot speak for the animal.**
Every claim here about what the arrangement is like from the other side is unavailable, and the engine
says so rather than filling the gap: distress can be read, preferences cannot be converted into consent,
and a keeper's confidence about what their animal wants is not evidence. **The piece grammar is the
engine's own argument and is uncomfortable on purpose.** Running a living participant through Place,
Load Bearing, Apparent and Yearbook is a claim, not an oversight -- a pet is talked about as kin and kept
as a ward, and the form states the second half. A reader who takes the shape for an endorsement of it has
read the engine backwards. What should not be taken from this engine is that keeping a pet is wrong, that
keepers are exercising power deliberately, or that an animal would be better off without one. Most of
them plainly would not be. The claim is narrow: the dependence was made, and the affection grew out of
making it.

## Origin

The humanistic geography of pet-keeping and the study of human-animal relations, wired as a piece on a
persona. The twist follows from Tuan's account of dominance and affection as one compound rather than two
opposed things.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The bond under threat (attachment, tending, compassion)**: Seeking closeness to a secure base, what
  happens inside a persona who meets another's need, and the feeling toward suffering are those
  packages'. This engine reads the arrangement, not the carer's interior.
- **The possession that carries a self (extended-self, heirloom, investiture)**: An object a persona's
  identity extends into, one kept out of exchange across generations, and how a thing comes to carry an
  identity are those packages'. Their subject is an object; this one's is not, which is the seam.
- **The designed delegate (agency, usability)**: A made thing that prescribes and forbids conduct is
  agency's. A pet was not designed and does not enforce; it depends.
- **The loss afterwards (grief, mortality, vigil)**: The adaptation to an irreversible absence, the
  stance toward finitude, and the keeping of watch are those engines'. This engine stops at the
  decision.
- **The animal as resource (commons, meal, collection)**: The grazed pasture, eating together, and the
  series withdrawn from use are those engines'. A pet is kept for company and is not consumed.
- **Veterinary and legal practice (the world, org)**: Welfare law, breed regulation, veterinary ethics,
  and what an animal is in property law are matters of statute and profession. They set the format;
  this engine reads what the arrangement does to the two in it.
- **An animal as authored symbol (the play's pitch)**: A creature staged in order to **stand for** a
  theme -- the dog that mirrors a motif of loyalty -- is refused: that is authored key, the play's
  pitch. Pet sets what was kept and what was decided; the meaning is the play's.

## Encoding

Source to constraint. A piece engine: one anchor, one position, three processes, all flat beneath the
animal.

- **[pet](piece_pet.md)** (the anchor): the animal kept for company at total dependency, carrying the
  twist -- a pet is kept alive by a dependence its keeper arranged, and arranging it is how the keeper
  came to love it. Anchored by Tuan (1984).
- **[the keeper](position_the_keeper.md)** (the position): the holder of every route to everything the
  animal needs, with complete authority experienced as being chosen. Anchored by Tuan (1984).
- **[the acquiring](process_the_acquiring.md)**: how the animal arrives, chosen entirely on one side.
  Anchored by Serpell (1986), with Berger (1980) on why the arrangement is common.
- **[the arranging](process_the_arranging.md)**: the removals that make the keeping possible, each one
  right and their sum a creature that cannot be released. Anchored by Tuan (1984).
- **[the ending](process_the_ending.md)**: the decision nobody else gets to make, and the only act with
  no version the animal survives.
