# Piece: Pet

## Taxonomy

An animal kept for company, at total dependency (no khai file of its own yet).

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: pet

## Place

Inside the home, in a space arranged around it that it cannot leave and did not choose.
It has a bed somebody bought, a bowl somebody fills, a door it cannot open, and a territory whose boundary is a decision rather than a fence it could test.
Where it sleeps is a compromise between what it would do and what is permitted, and the compromise was not negotiated.

## Load Bearing

What it holds up is the household's account of itself as a place where something is looked after.
A household with a pet is one that feeds something, notices when something is off its food, and rearranges a holiday around a creature that will never know what a holiday is. That is not a small claim for a household to be able to make about itself, and it is not a false one.
It also holds up a particular kind of company: presence without conversation, attention without assessment, and a greeting that does not depend on how the day went.

## Apparent

Read as a member of the family and kept as a dependent whose alternatives were removed.
What shows is the relationship: the name, the routines, the preferences everybody in the house can recite, the grief that will come. What is equally present and rarely counted is the arrangement underneath -- the animal cannot feed itself, cannot leave, cannot breed, and in most cases has been altered so that it never could.
So it is described in the vocabulary of kinship and lives in the conditions of a ward.

## Yearbook

The arc runs from the choosing to the years to the decision. It arrives as somebody's choice, occupies a decade or two of a household's life at a scale nobody plans for, and ends at a time and by a means its keeper selects -- which is the plainest fact in the whole arrangement and the one least often said out loud.
The engine's twist is here. **A pet is kept alive by a dependence its keeper arranged, and arranging it is how the keeper came to love it.** The direction runs the way round nobody expects. It is not that affection came first and care followed; it is that the taking on of a creature that can do nothing for itself -- the feeding, the letting out, the vet bill, the noticing -- is the activity out of which the attachment is built. Dominance and affection are not opposites here and never were: the affection is what dominance looks like when nothing in the arrangement resists it.
It is not the bond a persona seeks under threat, which is attachment's; not what happens inside somebody who meets a need, which is the tending composite's; not a possession carrying an identity, which is extended-self's and heirloom's; not a designed thing prescribing conduct, which is agency's; and a creature staged to **stand for** a theme is authored key, the play's pitch.
It fails by outliving its keeper, by becoming a cost the household cannot carry, and by the decision arriving early.
