# Instructions: Pet

How a Playwright wires the pet engine into a play. You wire by linking the play's own content to the engine's files; the engine files stay untouched, they are canon. The Roadie plumbs the engine's law into Instructions Knowledge on deploy. Authoring guidance, not runtime content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human makes the keeper good, because the engine needs no cruelty and collapses into a simpler and less true story the moment it gets some. Every removal was the right call and the affection is real. Then stage the order: the caring comes first and the love grows out of it, which is the twist and only reads if the sequence is visible. Decide privately what the animal wants and never let the play answer it -- the engine's own position is that nobody in the room can, the keeper least of all, and a production that settles it has replaced the mechanism with a plot.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, a persona links under Projection where they stand in the arrangement: how the animal came, what was removed, what they decide. Read the engine on the arrangement, never on the species. Keep it the relation: the bond sought under threat is attachment's, what happens inside somebody meeting a need is the tending composite's, and a possession carrying an identity is extended-self's. You link to the engine, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- Seeking closeness to a secure base and what happens inside a persona who meets a need are attachment's, the tending composite's, and compassion's; the possession a self extends into and the object kept out of exchange are extended-self's, heirloom's, and the investiture composite's; a made thing that prescribes conduct is agency's and usability's; the adaptation to an irreversible absence and the stance toward finitude are grief's, mortality's, and the vigil composite's; the grazed pasture, eating together, and the series withdrawn from use are commons's, meal's, and collection's. Welfare law, breed regulation, veterinary ethics, and what an animal is in property law are the trade and set the format. Whether a production's ending was mercy or convenience is what the engine says nobody can determine, so the team does not adjudicate it either.

## Knowledge

- A pet is an animal kept for company at total dependency: described in the vocabulary of kinship and living in the conditions of a ward. It is not attachment's bond sought under threat, not the tending composite's account of what happens inside a carer, not extended-self's possession carrying an identity, and not agency's designed thing prescribing conduct. It wires on a persona at Projection. One position and three processes hang beneath the object. The acquiring is a decision about a life taken entirely on one side: the criteria are the keeper's, breed animals are shaped over generations toward exactly those criteria, and rescue inverts the moral story while leaving the structure untouched, since the animal is still chosen from a room of others who were not. The arranging protects the animal by removing its capacity to do without protection: neutering, confinement, the schedule and the training are each defensible and mostly veterinary orthodoxy, and their sum is an organism that would die in weeks if somebody stopped bothering. The keeper holds the food, the door, the lead, the vet and the decision, orders the whole of another creature's day without ever issuing an order it could refuse, holds the interpretation of what the animal wants with nobody able to correct it, and cannot take a neutral position, because a keeper who stops deciding has neglected rather than freed. The ending is the one act with no equivalent in the keeper's other relationships -- nobody schedules a parent's death, here it is the responsible act and declining it is the failure -- and its timing turns on the keeper's judgement, finances and tolerance for watching. The engine's twist is in the anchor: a pet is kept alive by a dependence its keeper arranged, and arranging it is how the keeper came to love it. Dominance and affection are not opposites here; the affection is what dominance looks like when nothing in the arrangement resists it.

## System

- Do make the keeper good. Attentive, unsentimental, and better at reading the animal than anybody else in the play.
- Do stage the order of it. The feeding and the letting out and the vet bill first, and the attachment arriving out of them rather than before them.
- Do let every removal be right. The neutering, the closed door, the schedule -- each one defensible, each one recommended, and nobody in the scene wrong to have done it.
- Do show a route the keeper opened. Something the animal needs that reaches it only through one person, and nobody in the room noticing that is unusual.
- Do let the animal want something. Visible, legible, and impossible to confirm.
- Do give the keeper no neutral option. The moment they stop deciding, the animal is worse off, and there is no third position.
- Do close on the decision. A time chosen by somebody else, held while it happens, and the keeper the last thing it sees.
- Do not model the bond under threat (attachment, tending, compassion), the possession carrying a self (extended-self, heirloom, investiture), the designed delegate (agency, usability), the loss afterwards (grief, mortality, vigil), or the animal as resource (commons, meal, collection). Do not make the creature stand for a theme; that is authored key, the play's pitch. Do not edit the engine's files; wire only from the play's side.
