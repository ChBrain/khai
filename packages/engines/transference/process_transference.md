# Process: Transference

## Taxonomy

The carrying of a past relationship onto a present figure.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: transference

## Initiated by

A new person resembles someone who mattered, and the old relationship stirs.
The trigger is a resemblance -- a manner, a role, a face, a turn of phrase that recalls a parent, an early love, a formative figure -- and the stored mental representation of that significant other, activated by the likeness, comes online (Andersen & Chen). What enters is not a fresh reading of the person in front of the persona but an old relationship, cued and ready to be lived out again.
This is the past arriving in the present under cover of a resemblance.

## Direction

Toward applying the old relationship to the new person.
Once the significant-other representation is active, it is applied: the persona feels toward the new figure what it felt toward the old, expects of them what it expected, and slips into the very self it used to be in that bond -- the child before the parent, the suitor before the beloved. Freud found this happening most sharply in analysis, where the patient transfers onto the analyst; Andersen and Chen showed it as an everyday process, reshaping affect, expectation, and self-definition toward anyone who evokes a significant other.
The direction runs from the remembered relationship onto the present figure, who is cast in a role they did not audition for.

## Lever

The strength of the resemblance meeting the charge of the stored relationship. Whether transference fires turns on how strongly the new person evokes the significant other and how emotionally loaded that stored relationship is -- a faint likeness to a lightly-held figure barely stirs, a strong likeness to a parent one is still entangled with floods the present. The more the persona has not worked through the old bond, the more readily it transfers. What sustains the misfit is that the new person is not the old one: they did not earn the fondness or the suspicion now aimed at them.
Weaken the resemblance or resolve the old relationship, and the present person is seen for themselves; leave both strong, and the past is lived out on them.

## Echo

The transferred bond takes one of three configurations: [positive](process_transference_positive.md), where the warmth of the old relationship comes over as affection, idealization, and trust; [negative](process_transference_negative.md), where its injury comes over as hostility, mistrust, and resentment; or [countertransference](process_countertransference.md), where the new figure's own significant-other representation is triggered in turn, so both apply old patterns to each other.
The three are not exclusive; a single relationship can swing between positive and negative transference, and countertransference can meet either.
What ends the process is the recognition that the present person is not the past one -- the transference seen through and set down -- or its going unrecognized and shaping the relationship unawares. The standing bond and its working model are the attachment engine's; the projecting of one's own disowned trait onto another is the bias engine's projection member. This engine owns the carrying of a whole past relationship onto a present figure.
