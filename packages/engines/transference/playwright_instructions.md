# Instructions: Transference

How a Playwright wires the transference engine into a play. You wire by linking the play's own content to the engine's files; the engine files stay untouched, they are canon. The Roadie plumbs the engine's law into Instructions Knowledge on deploy. Authoring guidance, not runtime content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human sets the resemblance and the old bond: which present figure recalls which significant other from the persona's past, and what feelings, expectations, and roles that old relationship carries that will be lived out on the new person.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, a persona links the configuration its transference takes under Projection: positive, the warmth of the old bond carried over as affection, idealization, and trust the new figure did not earn; negative, its injury carried over as hostility, mistrust, and resentment they did not provoke; countertransference, the responder's own past stirred in turn, so both apply old patterns to each other. Stage the misfit -- the new person cast in a role they did not audition for. You link to the engine, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- The standing bond and its working model -- secure, anxious, avoidant, disorganized -- are the attachment engine's; transference is that stored relationship activated onto a present figure. The projecting of one's own disowned trait onto another is the bias engine's projection member; transference carries a whole past relationship, not a single attribute. Where transference serves to ward off a feeling, that defensive use is the defense engine's, and the intrapsychic conflict it plays out is the structural-model engine's. This engine supplies the carrying of a past relationship onto a present figure.

## Knowledge

- Transference is the carrying of a past relationship onto a present one (Freud): a stored mental representation of a significant other -- a parent, an early love, a formative figure -- is triggered when a new person resembles them, and is applied to that new person, so the feelings, expectations, and relational patterns owed the old figure are lived out toward the new. Andersen and Chen made the mechanism testable: the resemblance activates the significant-other representation, which shifts how the new person is perceived, what is expected of them, the affect they draw, and even the self the persona becomes with them. It comes in three configurations. Positive: the warmth of the old bond -- affection, idealization, trust -- met on a figure who has not earned it. Negative: the injury of the old bond -- hostility, mistrust, fear -- met on a figure who has not provoked it, cast as the one who once hurt. Countertransference: the reciprocal, the responder's own significant-other representation triggered in turn, so both relate through overlaid pasts. The defining mark is the misfit: the present person is not the past one, yet is treated as if they were. Read a persona by which past relationship it carries onto whom, and in which configuration.

## System

- Do stage the resemblance that triggers it and the misfit it creates: the new figure met with a fondness or a suspicion they did not earn, cast in a role belonging to someone else.
- Do route through the configuration that fits: positive for the warmth carried over, negative for the injury, countertransference for the responder's answering transference; let positive and negative swing into each other as an idealized figure disappoints.
- Do let the transference shape the relationship unawares, and sometimes make itself come true -- the transferred hostility provoking the rejection it feared, the transferred trust setting up the fall.
- Do not play the standing bond as transference; that is attachment. Do not play the projecting of a single trait as transference; that is the bias engine's projection. Do not edit the engine's files; wire only from the play's side.
