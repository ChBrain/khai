# Employment: Reference

## Line of Work

Employment as **process**: the effort bargain. The engine is read on the gap in the agreement rather
than on the agreement, because the gap is where all the interesting behaviour is.

The founding observation is old and unglamorous. What an employer buys is a persona's **time and
capacity** for a period, not a quantity of work: for most jobs the amount cannot be specified in
advance and cannot be verified afterwards. So the deal is struck with its central term left open, and
everything that follows -- supervision, rules, targets, pay above the market, culture, the shift going
quickly -- is an answer to a question the contract could not settle. That is why the engine exists,
and why it sits apart from what khai already carries: the organisation's culture is org's, the feeling
a role demands be displayed is emotional-labor's, the norm-training that makes a subject is
discipline's, and the ladder is hierarchy's. None of them is about how much work is in a day's work.

**The indeterminacy** states the problem and supplies its first answer. The hours are bought and the
effort is unnamed, so the persona is holding an obligation whose size is decided after the fact, by
watching what is praised and what is queried -- learned the way an accent is learned. The important
consequence is that an unbounded obligation cannot be discharged: there is no amount of work that
constitutes having done enough, and the persona who wants certainty has only one strategy, which is
more. Into that gap comes the **customary rate**, the floor's own unwritten sense of a fair day, which
is a genuine collective achievement -- without it the term is settled by whoever is most desperate --
and which holds by sanction, so the fast worker is constrained by the people they eat lunch with
rather than by their employer.

**The control** is Edwards' contested terrain, and its shape is the argument: each form answers the
previous one's failure and becomes a new terrain of contest rather than an end to it. **Simple**
control is adaptable and personalises everything, so a structural problem acquires a face. **Technical**
control removes the face by putting the pace in the plant -- and binds management to the equipment,
which is how it hands the persona a lever exactly as impersonal as the one used on them. **Bureaucratic**
control recruits rather than merely constrains, giving the persona a grade, a criterion, and a career,
and the engine insists this was a **real improvement in working life**; its failure mode is that once
the rule is the authority, the rule is what gets worked. **Metric** control is the newest and repeats
the pattern precisely: continuous, impersonal, evaluated without an evaluator, and reliably producing
exactly what it scores while destroying what sits adjacent -- and the persona gaming it is not
cheating, they are responding correctly to the instruction they were actually given.

**The gift** is Akerlof's partial gift exchange, and it is the best answer to the gap and the least
coercive. Discretionary effort -- noticing what was about to go wrong, staying to finish, saying the
awkward thing -- is where almost all the value of skilled work lives and is enforceable nowhere. The
efficiency wage is the employer's side, given without an enforceable return and effective for exactly
that reason. And **work-to-rule is the proof the engine rests on**: when the gift is withdrawn while
every term is honoured, the work stops. An organisation running only on its enforceable terms does not
run. The vulnerability is that a gift is priced by **perceived fairness rather than absolute level**,
so it can collapse over a change that costs almost nothing, and no contract records what was lost.

**The consent** is Burawoy's, and it is the engine's centre. He went to a shop floor expecting to
document coercion and found personas playing a game: **making out**, the daily project of hitting the
rate and handling the awkward job, played hard, argued about constantly, and cared about. The game
supplies what the work does not -- goals, immediate feedback, a difficulty met with skill, a running
score -- and the day, which is otherwise long, goes. The small autonomies of method and sequence are
real, and the experience of deciding inside a frame is indistinguishable from within from the
experience of deciding. And the frictions that result run **sideways**: against the previous shift,
the storeman, the machine, the rate on this particular job -- all of them genuine obstacles, none of
them the arrangement.

Hence the **twist**, which is at the root. The cheapest and most durable answer to the open term is
the persona's own interest in doing the job well, because it costs the employer nothing and cannot be
withdrawn -- it belongs to the persona. But playing a game means taking its rules as given, and there
is no way to play well while disputing the scoring. So **the thing that makes the day bearable is the
thing that ratifies its terms**. Consent is not produced by ideology and not by force. It is produced
by the ordinary experience of trying to do well.

The engine takes some care not to sneer at this, and a play should take the same care. The absorption
is not false consciousness, the skill is real, the pride in a hard job handled well is earned, and a
day spent inside the game is enormously better than a day spent resenting a supervisor. This is the
good part of work. It is also the part doing the employer's work, and both halves have to be staged
or the mechanism is gone.

**On the evidence, stated plainly.** This is an engine built from industrial sociology and labour
economics, and the three legs are supported very differently. **Akerlof's gift-exchange account and
the efficiency-wage literature are the best-evidenced**, with experimental and field support, though
the size of reciprocity effects in real labour markets is argued about and some laboratory results
attenuate under field conditions. **Edwards' three forms of control are a historical typology**, not a
measured taxonomy -- persuasive, widely used, and open to the objection that the forms did not
succeed each other as tidily as the account implies and coexist far more than it suggests; metric
control is khai's addition to his three, drawn from a much newer and still-unsettled literature on
algorithmic management, and it should be read as the least established member of that movement.
**Burawoy's** _Manufacturing Consent_ is a single participant-observation study of one machine shop in
the 1970s, and its reach is the honest limit: it is a brilliant and widely accepted account of a
mechanism, not a demonstration that the mechanism operates everywhere at the strength he found it. The
customary rate rests on older shop-floor ethnography of a similar kind. What none of this rests on is
a claim that employment is essentially exploitative or essentially benign; the engine deliberately
gives control, gift, and consent each their genuine case, and a reader who finds only the critical
half has read selectively. What should not be taken from the engine is that workers who enjoy their
jobs are deceived, that discretionary effort is naive, or that rules and measurement are inherently
oppressive. The claim is narrow: the term is open, something closes it, and which something is closing
it is the most informative fact about a workplace.

## Origin

Industrial sociology and the economics of the labour contract, wired as one process on a persona. Each
movement is separately sourced; the twist is Burawoy's.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **How an organisation operates (org)**: The competing-values stance -- clan, adhocracy, market,
  hierarchy -- and the four processes an organisation runs are the org engine's. That is the culture;
  this engine is the bargain, and the two vary independently. A clan-cultured workplace can run on
  metric control and frequently does.
- **The feeling a role demands (emotional-labor)**: Managing the heart for a wage, in its masking,
  method, and genuine forms, is the emotional-labor engine's. Employment reads how much work is got
  out of a persona, not which feelings the job requires them to produce.
- **The training that makes a subject (discipline)**: Foucault's productive, continuous, impersonal
  power -- the surveillance, the norm, the examination -- is the discipline engine's. Metric control
  is its close cousin and belongs here because it is an answer to the effort bargain specifically;
  what watching does to a persona's relationship with themselves is discipline's.
- **The ladder and its rungs (hierarchy, status, org)**: Rank, deference order, and formal position
  are those engines'. Bureaucratic control uses a ladder and does not own one.
- **Field position by capital (capital)**: The configuration of economic, cultural, social, and
  symbolic capital that places a persona in a field is Bourdieu's and the capital engine's. This
  engine reads one relation, not a position in a field.
- **Staying, speaking, or leaving (loyalty)**: Exit, voice, and loyalty when an organisation declines
  are the loyalty engine's. Work-to-rule sits here rather than there because it is a mode of working
  rather than a mode of leaving, but a persona doing it may well be exercising voice, and the play
  should hold both.
- **Fair process and what it buys (heeding, recognition)**: How a persona is treated by a procedure
  and what compliance that purchases is the heeding composite's. The gift movement's dependence on
  perceived fairness is the same territory seen from the employer's side, and a play running both
  should keep them straight: heeding reads an encounter, employment reads a standing exchange.
- **Absorption itself (flow)**: Total absorption in a challenge-skill balanced activity is the flow
  engine's. Making out frequently produces it, and the engine's claim is about what the absorption
  underwrites rather than about the absorption.
- **What is owed between persons (owing, gift as engine)**: The transfer that binds by naming no
  price, the debt, and the settling are the gift engine's and the owing composite's. The employment
  gift is a specific institutional case and does not restate them.
- **The job as authored symbol (the play's pitch)**: Work made to **stand for** a theme -- the
  assembly line that mirrors a motif of repetition -- is refused: that is authored key, the play's
  pitch. Employment sets what the bargain costs; the meaning read into it is the play's.

## Encoding

Source to constraint. A process tree: one root, four movements, thirteen forms.

- **[employment](process_employment.md)** (the root): the effort bargain, read on the gap and never on
  the contract, carrying the twist -- the thing that makes the day bearable is the thing that ratifies
  its terms. Anchored by Braverman (1974) and Burawoy (1979).
- **[the indeterminacy](process_indeterminacy.md)**: the open term and the rate that quietly settles
  it. Anchored by Braverman (1974) and Roy (1952). [the hours bought](process_hours_bought.md),
  [the effort unnamed](process_effort_unnamed.md), [the customary rate](process_customary_rate.md).
- **[the control](process_control_work.md)**: effort got by direction, in escalating forms. Anchored by
  Edwards (1979), with the fourth form from the algorithmic-management literature.
  [simple](process_simple_control.md), [technical](process_technical_control.md),
  [bureaucratic](process_bureaucratic_control.md), [metric](process_metric_control.md).
- **[the gift](process_gift_work.md)**: effort given rather than got, and the proof by its withdrawal.
  Anchored by Akerlof (1982) and the gift-exchange experiments.
  [discretionary effort](process_discretionary_effort.md),
  [the efficiency wage](process_efficiency_wage.md), [work to rule](process_work_to_rule.md).
- **[the consent](process_consent_work.md)**: effort produced by the persona's own absorption.
  Anchored by Burawoy (1979). [making out](process_making_out.md),
  [choosing within](process_choosing_within.md), [the horizontal turn](process_horizontal_conflict.md).
