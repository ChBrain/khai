# Process: The Consent

## Taxonomy

[Employment](process_employment.md), effort produced by absorption, and where the whole engine is seen for one thing.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: employment

## Initiated by

The persona is trying to make the number, and the shift is going quickly.
The Consent starts where Burawoy found it, on a shop floor he expected to be a site of coercion and found to be a site of games: [making out](process_making_out.md), the daily project of hitting the rate and beating the difficult job, played hard, discussed constantly, and cared about.
It is the face of the engine where nobody is being made to do anything.

## Direction

Toward effort nobody had to extract, and agreement nobody had to ask for.
The Consent reads how the game does its work: [choosing within](process_choosing_within.md), where the small real autonomies of sequencing and method make the day the persona's own; and [the horizontal turn](process_horizontal_conflict.md), where the contest that results runs sideways -- against the rate, the machine, the previous shift, the storeman, the other operator -- rather than upward.
Playing requires taking the rules as given. There is no way to play well while disputing the scoring.

## Lever

The genuine satisfaction of doing the job well against what that satisfaction underwrites.
The carrying force is that none of this is false consciousness and the persona is not a dupe: the absorption is real, the skill is real, the pride in a hard job well handled is earned, and a day spent in it is enormously better than a day spent resenting a supervisor -- this is the good part of work and the engine should not sneer at it; the resistance is that the game is played on a board somebody else set, and every hour of skilled, willing, absorbed effort is also an hour of practical agreement that the board is the board.
The Consent rides the engine's keystone knife-edge: the thing that makes the day bearable is the thing that ratifies its terms.

## Echo

A persona working hard, well, and willingly, at a rate they never agreed.
It shows the three earlier movements complete: an open term, a control system that could not close it, a gift that could be withdrawn -- and finally an answer that costs the employer nothing and cannot be withdrawn, because it belongs to the persona.
This is why the Consent is the hardest of the four to stage and the only one worth closing on: the satisfaction must be real, the persona must be good at the job, nobody must be lying to them, and the audience must be able to see that the agreement was produced by the absorption rather than by any argument.
A play that stages the worker as deceived has replaced the mechanism with a villain, and a play that stages the game as merely pleasant has staged half of it.
The Consent is dated from whenever the game got interesting, and it holds for as long as it stays interesting.
