# Self-Verification: Reference

## Line of Work

Self-verification as a **process** engine: a persona's drive to confirm its existing self-concept -- to seek, elicit, and hold onto feedback that matches how it already sees itself, even when that self-view is negative -- taken across the routes the drive runs. The domain does not model the level of a persona's self-worth, which is the self-esteem engine's; nor the content of the self-story, which is the identity engine's; nor the opposing pull toward flattering feedback, which is the self-serving member of the bias engine's; nor the passive cognitive slant toward self-confirming information, which the bias engine owns; nor the general drive to reconcile inconsistent cognitions, which is the dissonance engine's. It models the active motive to be known as one knows oneself, and its interpersonal enactment.

The spine is Swann's self-verification theory. Its claim is that people are motivated to confirm their firmly held self-views, because a stable, coherent self-concept lets them predict their world and be predicted within it, and it makes the counterintuitive prediction that this drive runs on the content of the self-view rather than its favorability: people with negative self-views seek, elicit, and prefer feedback that confirms those negative views, choosing partners who appraise them unfavorably over partners who appraise them well, so self-verification and self-enhancement diverge whenever a self-view is negative. Swann's marriage and roommate studies show the effect where it is sharpest -- people with low self-esteem grow more intimate with spouses who see them unfavorably -- and his account lays out the routes: developing self-confirming social environments and soliciting confirming feedback, systematically eliciting self-confirming reactions through identity cues and self-presentation (identity negotiation), and processing information so as to see more confirmation than is there. This engine owns the active motive and its first two, interpersonal routes plus the resistance to disconfirmation; the passive cognitive-processing route belongs to the bias engine. The altitudes are those: the drive, its soliciting, its signaling, and its resistance.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **Self-esteem**: The engine models the drive to confirm a self-view, not the favorability of that view. Self-esteem -- the level of a persona's sense of its own worth -- is owned by the self-esteem engine; self-verification runs on whatever the self-view is, positive or negative, and its signature cases are precisely those where a low self-esteem is verified rather than raised. The esteem is the content the drive confirms; the drive is this engine's.
- **Identity**: The engine models the motive to confirm the self-concept, not the self-concept itself. Identity -- the content of the persona's self-story, who it takes itself to be -- is owned by the identity engine; self-verification is the pressure to have that story ratified by the world, not the story's substance. Identity supplies the self-view; self-verification defends it.
- **Self-enhancement (bias, self-serving)**: The engine models the drive for confirmation, not the drive for flattery. The opposing motive -- to seek and believe the most favorable verdict on oneself -- is owned as the self-serving member of the bias engine; self-verification is its counterweight, and the two align when the self-view is positive but pull apart when it is negative, verification then keeping the low self-view against enhancement's correction. This engine owns the confirmation drive; the bias engine owns the enhancement one.
- **Cognitive self-confirming bias**: The engine models the active, interpersonal routes, not the passive cognitive slant. The tendency to attend to, believe, and recall self-confirming information over self-discrepant, as a processing bias, is owned by the bias engine (its coherence-seeking and self-consistency family); self-verification owns the motive and its enactment in the world -- soliciting, signaling, and resisting -- and hands the internal information-processing slant across. The two compose: the bias tilts the reading, the motive works the world.
- **Dissonance**: The engine models the specifically self-confirming drive, not the general one for consistency. Cognitive dissonance -- the discomfort of holding any inconsistent cognitions and the drive to reduce it -- is owned by the dissonance engine; self-verification is the narrower drive to keep the self-concept in particular confirmed by feedback and environment, which may feel dissonance-like when disconfirmed but is defined by its object, the self-view, not by inconsistency as such.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per file.

- **[self-verification](process_self_verification.md)** (the root, process): The drive to confirm the existing self-concept, running on the self-view's content rather than its favorability. Anchored by Swann (1983, 2012).
- **[soliciting](process_soliciting.md)** (process, route): Seeking self-confirming feedback and building a self-confirming world -- selecting partners, roles, and settings that verify the self-view. Anchored by Swann (selective interaction; the marriage studies).
- **[signaling](process_signaling.md)** (process, route): Eliciting self-confirming reactions -- identity cues and self-presentation that pull confirming responses from others. Anchored by Swann, Rentfrow & Guinn (identity negotiation).
- **[resistance](process_resistance.md)** (process, route): Rejecting self-discrepant feedback, even when favorable -- discounting, avoiding, and withdrawing from appraisals that do not fit. Anchored by Swann & Pelham (leaving over-favorable relationships).
