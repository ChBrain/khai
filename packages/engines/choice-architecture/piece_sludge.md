# Piece: Sludge

## Taxonomy

[Choice architecture](piece_choice_architecture.md), the facet this is a part of. Sludge models what each path costs to take -- the steps, the screens, the waits, the forms, the proofs, the phone call at set hours, the document that must be found before anything can proceed. Sunstein's sludge, and the administrative-burden literature behind it (Herd and Moynihan), which splits the cost into what the user must learn, what they must comply with, and what it takes out of them to do it. It is distinct from guile's obstruction, which is friction raised on the exit so that leaving costs more than staying -- there the intent is the trick. Sludge here is neutral in intent and set in both directions: friction lowered so a path can actually be taken, or raised on purpose so an irreversible act is considered before it happens, or -- most often -- accumulated by nobody in particular over years of additions. The tell is that every path costs something to take, and someone set that cost whether or not they meant to.

## Place

On the distance between wanting a thing and having it -- the sign-up, the claim, the appeal, the renewal, the verification, the checkout, the deletion. Wherever the object stands between an intention and its completion, sludge is the length of that stand.

## Load Bearing

What sludge holds up is who got through. Takeup of a thing people are entitled to tracks the paperwork far more closely than it tracks eligibility: lower the cost and the people at the margin arrive, raise it and they fall away. And they do not fall away evenly. The first to drop are the ones with the least time, the least documentation, and the least practice at processes, which means the cost is a sorting mechanism that nobody wrote down and everybody can deny. Sludge bears a selected population dressed as a self-selected one.

## Apparent

It shows as procedure, and each step shows as reasonable. Nobody meets the total; each user meets only the next screen, and the sum is never displayed anywhere -- not to the user, and frequently not to the maker either, since the steps were added one at a time by different people for defensible reasons. What is apparent is a sequence of small asks; what is borne is their product.

## Yearbook

The record of a sludge is the record of the abandoned -- the half-filled forms, the claims never made, the appeals dropped at the third letter, the carts left at the delivery-address page. Its dates are the removals: the day a step came out and the takeup jumped, which is the only moment the step's real work becomes visible, and always after the fact.
