# Choice Architecture: Reference

## Line of Work

Choice architecture as **piece**: the arrangement of the options an object offers, read on the
object and not on the person choosing. The domain does not model the persona's own tilt toward
whatever is standing, which the bias engine owns (the default effect and status-quo bias, read on
a person); nor an arrangement built to deceive, which guile owns (the dark pattern, where the
intent is the trick); nor a form that fails to communicate its own use, which usability owns (a
defect in the signal); nor the reinforcement loop that conditions a return, which captology owns.
It models the setting: what was in place before the user arrived, and the fact that some version
of it had to be.

The spine is Thaler and Sunstein's choice architecture, and specifically its structural claim
rather than its policy programme: there is no neutral arrangement, because leaving a box unticked
is a setting, putting the options in no particular order is an order, and offering the whole
catalogue is a decision about the set exactly as much as offering three is. The engine takes the
arrangement four ways: what stands if the user does nothing (preset), what each path costs to take
(sludge), where the options sit and which is met first (ordering), and how many are offered at all
(assortment) -- so a piece can declare not only that it arranges but which part of the arrangement
is bearing the weight.

**On effect sizes, stated plainly, because the four facets are not equal and the engine does not
pretend they are.** The structural claim above is true by construction and needs no experiment: an
object either ships with a box ticked or it does not, and either way it shipped with a state. How
much each facet moves behaviour is a separate question with four different answers. Presets have
the strongest evidence, and it is field evidence rather than laboratory evidence: automatic
enrolment reproduces wherever an employer has flipped it, and presumed consent shows the same
pattern across national donor registers. Sludge is likewise well supported and largely
administrative -- takeup of an entitlement tracks its paperwork more closely than its eligibility
rules. Ordering has solid position-effect data at the shelf and on the menu. Assortment is the
weak leg: the choice-overload finding does not replicate cleanly, and its meta-analytic mean sits
near zero, which is why the assortment facet claims the arrangement and explicitly not the effect.
And the umbrella term itself does not survive scrutiny: the headline meta-analysis reporting
"nudging" as a broadly effective category (Mertens and colleagues) was reanalysed by Maier and
colleagues with publication-bias correction and the pooled effect fell to approximately zero. khai
therefore does not carry "nudging" as a construct at all. It carries the arrangement, which is a
structural fact about objects, and it hands each facet's magnitude to the literature below rather
than asserting one.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The person's own pull (the bias engine)**: The default effect and status-quo bias are the bias
  engine's, read on a **persona** -- the tendency to take whatever is standing. That is the reason
  a preset works; it is not the preset. Same phenomenon from two poles: the arrangement sits on
  the object, the tilt sits in the one who meets it. A play that puts the pull on the object, or
  the setting on the person, has crossed them.
- **The arrangement built to deceive (guile)**: Guile owns the arrangement whose point is a trick
  -- the pre-ticked box that hides a charge (sneaking), the exit made harder than staying
  (obstruction), the layout rigged to steer the click (misdirection). This engine owns the same
  surfaces with the intent removed: every object has a preset, a friction, an order, and a count,
  and most were set by default, inheritance, or nobody. The two share the mechanism and split on
  intent, the same way usability and guile do.
- **The form communicating its use (usability)**: Whether the control reads as pressable, whether
  the label says what it does, whether the affordance is honest -- usability's. An arrangement can
  be perfectly legible and still be doing all the work this engine reads.
- **The reward loop (captology)**: Captology conditions a return over time -- variable reward,
  trigger, investment. Choice architecture shapes one decision as it is met. They co-occur
  constantly and the mechanisms are different: an arrangement makes something easy, a loop makes
  something habitual.
- **The social-order program (agency)**: The object enforcing a policy on conduct -- the bench that
  cannot be slept on -- is agency's. Agency governs who may do what; this reads what the
  arrangement made likely, including where the maker wanted nothing in particular.
- **The size of the effect**: The engine claims the arrangement exists and had to be set. It does
  not claim a magnitude, and it does not carry "nudging" as a construct at all -- see the paragraph
  above and the last three rows of the table. A play that treats the four facets as equally
  powerful has overclaimed; the references are there so that it does not have to.
- **The facet is not engine content**: The engine declares the four facets exist; which one a given
  piece turns on lives on the piece, linked under its Apparent chapter. The engine refuses to set a
  piece's arrangement for it.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per file.

- **[choice architecture](piece_choice_architecture.md)** (the root): the arrangement of the
  options an object offers, which the maker could not decline to make. Anchored by Thaler and
  Sunstein (the coinage and the no-neutral-arrangement claim).
- **[preset](piece_preset.md)**: what stands if the user does nothing. Anchored by Madrian and Shea
  (automatic enrolment) and Johnson and Goldstein (presumed consent) -- the facet with the
  strongest field evidence.
- **[sludge](piece_sludge.md)**: what each path costs to take, and who that cost sorts out.
  Anchored by Sunstein (sludge, sludge audits) and Herd and Moynihan (administrative burden).
- **[ordering](piece_ordering.md)**: where the options sit and which is met first. Anchored by
  Bar-Hillel (position effects in displays and menus) and Chandon and colleagues (shelf position).
- **[assortment](piece_assortment.md)**: how many options are offered at all. Anchored by Iyengar
  and Lepper as its source, and bounded by Scheibehenne, Greifeneder and Todd -- the facet claims
  the arrangement, never the overload effect.
