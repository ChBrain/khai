# Piece: Choice Architecture

## Taxonomy

The root of the choice-architecture engine. It models the arrangement of the options an object offers: what stands if the user does nothing, what each path costs to take, where the options sit and which is met first, and how many are offered at all. Thaler and Sunstein's choice architecture, with the claim that carries it -- there is no neutral arrangement, because leaving a box unticked is a setting and putting the options in no particular order is an order. It is distinct from a persona's own pull toward whatever is standing, which the bias engine owns (the default effect, read on a person, not on the object); from an arrangement built to deceive, which guile owns (the intent is the trick, and the surface lies); from a form that fails to communicate its own use, which usability owns (a defect in the signal, not a setting of the options); and from the reward loop that conditions a return, which captology owns (training over time, not the shape of one decision). The tell of choice architecture is that the maker could not abstain: whatever they did or failed to do, the user met an arrangement.

## Place

Wherever the object presents more than one option -- the form, the enrolment, the settings screen, the plan table, the menu, the shelf, the search result. It sits before the user arrives, in the state the object was in when it shipped. A choice architecture is not something the object does at the moment of choosing; it is the condition the choosing happens inside.

## Load Bearing

What the arrangement holds up is the distribution of outcomes across everyone who met it -- the enrolment rate, the consent rate, the share who ended on the standing plan, the share who never finished. It cannot be removed, only replaced with another, and that is the whole of the engine's claim: the load is borne whether or not anyone decided to bear it. Each user experienced a free choice and most of them will describe it that way; the aggregate is what the arrangement was actually doing. How much any one facet moves that aggregate is a separate and very unequal question -- the arrangement always exists, and the size of its pull is per-facet and empirical.

## Apparent

Almost nothing shows as an arrangement. A pre-ticked box shows as a form, an order shows as a list, a required step shows as procedure, a shortlist shows as the options. The surface presents the way things are, and the fact that somebody set it -- or failed to, which sets it too -- is exactly what is not visible. What is apparent is a set of choices; what is borne is that the set arrived already shaped, and that no version of the object could have handed it over unshaped.

## Yearbook

The history of a choice architecture is the aggregate it produced and the day it changed. The record is not of any user persuaded -- none were -- but of the numbers before and after: the participation rate that moved when the enrolment flipped, the takeup that jumped when a step came out, the shares that shifted when the same options were listed the other way round. The object is dated from the distribution it made likely, and the most telling entries are the ones where nothing was argued and everything moved.
