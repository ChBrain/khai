# Piece: Assortment

## Taxonomy

[Choice architecture](piece_choice_architecture.md), the facet this is a part of. Assortment models how many options are offered at all -- the shortlist against the catalogue, three plans against thirty, the curated set against everything in stock. Iyengar and Lepper's jam study is where the facet comes from, and the contest over it is part of the facet: the choice-overload result does not replicate cleanly, and the meta-analytic mean across the literature sits near zero (Scheibehenne, Greifeneder and Todd), so this facet claims the arrangement and not the effect. What the count does reliably is set the comparison the user is asked to perform and change what "having chosen" can honestly mean. It is distinct from ordering, which arranges a set that is already fixed, and from sludge, which prices a path that is already offered. Assortment sets the set.

## Place

On the menu, the plan table, the catalogue, the settings page, the shortlist -- wherever somebody decided which options to show and, in the same act, which to leave out.

## Load Bearing

What the count holds up is the work of comparing, and the responsibility for the result. A short list is a set of judgements already made on the user's behalf, and it holds up their confidence at the cost of their reach; a long list hands the judgements back, along with the labour, and holds up the maker's defence that everything was available. Both are the maker's doing and neither is neutral. Change the count and you change what a user would have to know to choose well, and therefore how well they can be said to have chosen. Whether the count also changes how many choose at all is precisely the contested part, and nothing in this facet rests on it.

## Apparent

It shows as generosity or as curation, depending on which way it was set, and in both cases what was left out is never listed. Nobody can see the options that were not offered; the set presents as the field. What is apparent is what is there; what is borne is the excluding, which leaves no trace on the surface at all.

## Yearbook

The record of an assortment is the shape of what was excluded and what was never asked for. Its dates are the additions and the prunings -- the day the catalogue opened, the day the plans went from thirty to three -- and, where the list ran long, the record of users who took the first tolerable option rather than the best one and never learned the difference.
