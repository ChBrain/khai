# Instructions: Choice Architecture

How a Playwright wires the choice-architecture engine into a play. You wire by linking the
play's own content to the engine's files; the engine files stay untouched, they are canon.
The Roadie plumbs the engine's law into Instructions Knowledge on deploy. Authoring
guidance, not runtime content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human sets which objects in the world offer options, and which part of their arrangement is the one doing the work.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, a piece that offers a user options links under Apparent the facet of its arrangement that is bearing the weight: what stands if the user does nothing (preset), what each path costs to take (sludge), where the options sit and which is met first (ordering), or how many are offered at all (assortment). Play the arrangement as the condition the choosing happened inside, not as something the object urged -- nobody was persuaded, and every user will describe the choice as free. Keep it the object's arrangement and not the user's pull, which is the bias engine's on a persona; keep it neutral in intent, since an arrangement built to trick is guile's. You link to the engine, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- The user's own tilt toward the standing option is the bias engine's, read on a persona; the arrangement built to deceive is guile's, where the intent is the trick; the form that fails to communicate its own use is usability's; the reward loop that conditions a return is captology's; the object enforcing a policy on conduct is agency's. Whether the arrangement rings true for the object is the team's review -- and how strongly it moves anyone is not the engine's to assert: the four facets are not equal, and the references carry each one's evidence.

## Knowledge

- Choice architecture is the arrangement of the options an object offers, read on the object rather than the person choosing (Thaler and Sunstein). Its claim is structural and needs no experiment: there is no neutral arrangement, because leaving a box unticked is a setting, putting the options in no particular order is an order, and choosing which options to show is a decision either way -- the maker could not abstain. Four facets over an arrangement root. The preset is what stands if the user does nothing, the strongest of the four in the field: change it and the share who end on it moves further and faster than argument moves it, with no mind changed, because doing nothing is the most common thing a user does (Madrian and Shea on automatic enrolment; Johnson and Goldstein on presumed consent). Sludge is what each path costs to take -- steps, waits, forms, proofs -- lowered so people can act, raised so an irreversible act is considered, or accumulated by nobody in particular; takeup tracks paperwork more closely than eligibility, and the ones who fall away first are those with the least time, so the cost sorts a population and denies having done so (Sunstein; Herd and Moynihan). Ordering is where the options sit and which is met first: reorder the same set and the shares move without a single option changing, because a set is never met as a set but one item at a time under attention that runs out (Bar-Hillel; Chandon and colleagues). Assortment is how many options are offered at all, which sets the comparison the user must perform and what having chosen can honestly mean -- carried with its caveat, since the choice-overload result behind it does not replicate cleanly and its meta-analytic mean sits near zero, so the facet claims the arrangement and not the effect (Iyengar and Lepper as source; Scheibehenne, Greifeneder and Todd as the bound). The arrangement always exists; the size of its pull is a separate, per-facet, empirical question, and khai does not carry "nudging" as a construct, its pooled effect having collapsed under publication-bias correction (Maier and colleagues).

## System

- Do give an option-offering piece its facet under Apparent: what stands if nothing is done, what each path costs, where the options sit, how many there are.
- Do play the arrangement as invisible: a preset shows as the state of the world, sludge shows as procedure, an order shows as a list, an assortment shows as the field. The user meets a free choice and is right to say so.
- Do read the load in the aggregate, not in any one user: the arrangement's work is the distribution it produced -- the enrolment rate, the takeup, the shares by position -- and it is visible mostly at the moment it changes.
- Do keep the facets apart: usability asks whether the form communicates, guile asks whether it lies, and this asks what the arrangement made likely.
- Do keep the four unequal: the preset and the friction carry field evidence, the ordering carries position data, and the assortment carries a contested one. A play that gives them equal force has overclaimed.
- Do not model the user's pull toward the standing option (that is the bias engine, on a persona), an arrangement built to trick (that is guile), a form that fails to communicate (that is usability), a reward loop that forms a habit (that is captology), or a policy enforced on conduct (that is agency). Do not edit the engine's files; wire only from the play's side.
