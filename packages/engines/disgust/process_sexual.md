# Process: Sexual

## Taxonomy

[Disgust](process_disgust.md), the mate-choice domain.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: disgust

## Initiated by

The persona meets a sexual prospect or act it appraises as reproductively costly -- a kin member, the coercive, the disease-signalling, the badly matched -- and recoils.
This is sexual disgust (Tybur, Lieberman, Griskevicius): the emotion turned to the second great adaptive problem, not what to eat but whom to mate with, steering away from partners and acts that would carry a fitness cost.
It is the same rejection machinery reading the body of another for signals of a poor sexual choice.

## Direction

Away from the costly liaison, and toward its avoidance.
Sexual disgust does not attack or shame from above; it withdraws desire and interposes revulsion, making the wrong partner or act aversive rather than merely declined.
For kin the direction is fixed early by the incest-avoidance system (Westermarck; Lieberman): co-rearing tunes the recoil, so proximity in childhood, not knowledge of relatedness, sets it.

## Lever

The perceived reproductive cost, weighed against one's own mate value.
Sexual disgust is calibrated: it spends the openness to a partner in proportion to the fitness threat they pose and the alternatives one commands, and it is sex-differentiated -- higher on average in women, for whom the minimum cost of a bad sexual choice is greater.
What it withdraws is arousal and approach, replaced by aversion.

## Echo

The fitness-threatening choice is avoided -- and, turned outward, the emotion becomes a weapon of judgment.
Sexual disgust is readily moralized: much of the condemnation of others' sexual behavior borrows its force, the "impure" act cast as contaminating rather than merely disapproved, which carries it toward the sociomoral domain.
This engine keeps the mate-choice recoil; the moral condemnation it fuels runs on into [sociomoral](process_sociomoral.md) disgust.
