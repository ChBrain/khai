# Instructions: Hatred

How a Playwright wires the hatred engine into a play. You wire by linking the play's own content to the engine's files; the engine files stay untouched, they are canon. The Roadie plumbs the engine's law into Instructions Knowledge on deploy. Authoring guidance, not runtime content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human sets the hatred terrain: whom the persona holds as an enemy, the history or threat that fed the sentiment, and what makes the target read as bad to the core -- so a moment can run on a durable, eliminatory sentiment rather than a passing flare.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, a persona links the hatred a moment runs under its Projection: repulsion, distancing from and recoiling at the target; malice, wishing it harmed or undone; and essentializing, fixing its badness as its nature. Let the sentiment hold across scenes and generalize from a deed to the person -- sustained, not spent like anger. You link to the engine, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- The incident-level reaction to a provocation is the anger engine's; the cold disengaged looking-down is the contempt engine's; the visceral revulsion reflex is the disgust engine's; the behavioral infliction of harm is the aggression engine's; the moral licensing of that harm is the moral-disengagement engine's. This engine supplies the durable sentiment of hate -- the repulsion, the malice, and the essentializing.

## Knowledge

- Hatred is a durable, eliminatory sentiment toward a devalued other: a standing orientation against a target seen as bad in its very nature, aimed at distancing from, harming, or being rid of it (Sternberg; Fischer et al.). It differs from anger by lasting and generalizing, and from contempt by leaning in rather than disengaging. It runs on three components. Repulsion is the negation of intimacy -- distancing and revulsion that push the target away and deny it closeness. Malice is the passion -- active hostility, the wish to see the target harmed or destroyed. Essentializing is the commitment -- attributing the target's badness to its stable, malevolent essence, so the sentiment persists past any act and can pass from a person to a category. Read a persona by whom it hates and how the three components move it against them.

## System

- Do keep hatred a durable sentiment, not an incident: it holds its object across time and generalizes from an act to the person's nature -- let the flare at a single provocation be the anger engine's, and hatred the settled stance that outlasts it.
- Do keep it hot and engaged, not cold and disengaged: hatred wants the target's undoing and leans in -- distinct from contempt, which looks down and turns away. Let the malice be an active wish, not mere dismissal.
- Do stage the essentializing as the durable core: the persona reading the target's badness as its essence ("that is what they are"), so the hatred is licensed to persist and to generalize to the target's kind -- its most dangerous reach.
- Do keep it the sentiment, not the deed or its excuse: delegate the actual infliction of harm to the aggression engine and the moral licensing of that harm (dehumanization, self-exculpation) to the moral-disengagement engine, and let the disgust engine supply the visceral recoil the repulsion borrows. Do not edit the engine's files; wire only from the play's side.
