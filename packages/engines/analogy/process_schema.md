# Process: Schema

## Taxonomy

[Analogy](process_analogy.md), the subprocess where the shared structure is abstracted.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: analogy

## Initiated by

The persona has aligned two cases, and sees the structure they share standing free of either.
Having mapped a source onto a target, the persona can lift out what the two have in common -- not the sun or the nucleus but the central-force structure both instantiate -- as a pattern in its own right. What enters is a completed alignment ready to be generalized, the relational skeleton pulled loose from the specific domains that wore it.
This is analogy's most durable product: comparing two cases to abstract the schema they both are.

## Direction

Toward a general relational structure that outlives the pair.
The persona distils the aligned commonality into a schema -- a domain-general pattern of relations it can hold apart from any single instance -- so that what was a one-off comparison becomes a reusable template. Gick and Holyoak showed the power of this: solvers who compared two analogs and abstracted their shared schema transferred to a new problem far better than those who saw either case alone.
The direction is from the concrete pair to the abstract pattern, the structure freed to be applied again.

## Lever

Whether the comparison is carried through to abstraction, or left as a one-off match.
Whether a schema forms turns on the persona generalizing across the aligned cases rather than stopping at the single mapping -- two examples compared and their common structure named, versus one example understood and set down. The abstraction is effortful and easy to skip, which is why so much hard-won analogical insight fails to transfer: the schema was never lifted out.
Compare and abstract, and the structure becomes portable; map once and stop, and the insight stays trapped in its case.

## Echo

A domain-general schema, freed from the pair and ready to apply to the next case.
Because the schema is held apart from any instance, it transforms later reasoning: it makes [retrieval](process_retrieval.md) reliable by giving memory a structural index, and it makes [mapping](process_mapping.md) fast by supplying the alignment ready-made -- the expert's power is largely a stock of such schemas.
It is the abstracting end of the [inference](process_inference.md) that transfers and the whole [analogy](process_analogy.md) that maps; the relational abstraction here is structure-mapping's own, distinct from the construal engine's high-level, distance-driven abstraction.
