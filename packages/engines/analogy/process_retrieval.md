# Process: Retrieval

## Taxonomy

[Analogy](process_analogy.md), the subprocess where a source is called to mind.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: analogy

## Initiated by

The persona faces a target and needs a source, and searches memory for one built the same way.
Before any mapping can happen, an analog must be found: the target cues memory, and some prior case comes back as a candidate to reason from. What enters is a target in search of a base, and the access process that answers it.
This is the gate on every analogy -- no source retrieved, no mapping to run.

## Direction

Toward a source analog surfacing from memory.
The target's features probe stored knowledge and pull back whatever they most resemble, and the persona receives a candidate base to align. But the direction has a known bias: retrieval is driven largely by surface similarity -- shared objects, shared wording, shared setting -- even though the sound analogy the persona needs turns on relational similarity instead.
The direction is from target to remembered source, and it does not, on its own, reliably deliver the structurally right one.

## Lever

The mismatch between what cues retrieval and what makes an analogy sound.
Whether the useful source comes back turns on the gap between surface and structure: memory hands over the case that looks like the target, but the case that is built like the target -- the one worth mapping -- may share no surface and stay buried. This is why a solver given a story with the same deep structure as their problem so often fails to think of it unless prompted (Gick & Holyoak's transfer failures).
Cue retrieval on surface and the shallow match returns; the deep one waits on a hint, an expert's indexed memory, or a schema already abstracted.

## Echo

A candidate source surfaces, apt or merely superficial, ready to be mapped or to mislead.
Because retrieval leans on surface while mapping demands structure, this subprocess is where good analogies are missed and false ones are handed up -- the reasoning is only as good as what memory offered it to work with.
It feeds the [mapping](process_mapping.md) that tests the retrieved source's structure and the [inference](process_inference.md) that would transfer from it; a [schema](process_schema.md) already held makes retrieval far more reliable. That the whole runs on relational structure and not surface is the [root's](process_analogy.md); the general retrieval of memories as such is the memory engine's.
