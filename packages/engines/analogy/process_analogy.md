# Process: Analogy

## Taxonomy

Reasoning by relational structure-mapping.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: analogy

## Initiated by

A persona meets a target it does not fully understand, and reaches for a source it does.
The trigger is a problem, a situation, or a system whose workings are unclear, and the sense that something already known is built the same way. What enters the process is a pairing: a base or source domain the persona understands, held against a target it wants to understand, on the bet that the two share a structure.
This is not a claim that the two things resemble each other on the surface; it is the move to understand one by the shape of the other.

## Direction

Toward an alignment of relational structure, and a transfer across it.
Analogy does not compare features; it maps relations. The persona sets the elements of the source in correspondence with the elements of the target so that the relations among them line up -- the roles matched, the connections parallel -- and then carries what the source knows across the alignment onto the target (Gentner's structure-mapping). The direction runs from a structure understood in one place to the same structure understood in another.
What moves across is not the things but the relations between them.

## Lever

Systematicity: whether the match is of deep, interconnected relations or of shallow surface features.
Whether the analogy is sound turns on what it aligns. A good analogy maps a system of relations -- above all higher-order relations like cause -- so that the correspondences hold together and the transfer is warranted; a bad one rides on surface resemblance, matching things that look alike but are not built alike. The systematicity principle prizes the connected relational structure and discounts the isolated attribute.
The lever is depth of structure: align the causal skeleton and the analogy carries; align the surface and it misleads.

## Echo

The reasoning runs by four subprocesses: [retrieval](process_retrieval.md), where a source is called to mind from the target; [mapping](process_mapping.md), where the two structures are aligned into correspondences; [inference](process_inference.md), where the source's unmatched relations are projected onto the target as new predictions; and [schema](process_schema.md), where the shared structure is abstracted into a general schema that outlives the pair.
The four run in order but each can be the one a scene turns on -- a failure of retrieval, a forced mapping, an overreaching inference, a hard-won schema.
What ends the process is the transfer made and evaluated -- the target understood, or the analogy found unsound and dropped -- and what it can leave is a schema that eases the next transfer (Gick & Holyoak). The persuasive framing of a claim, including framing by metaphor, is the framing engine's; this engine owns the structure-mapping and transfer beneath it.
