# Face: Reference

## Line of Work

Face as **process**: the public self-image a persona claims in interaction, and how they manage an act that threatens it -- their own or another's. The domain does not model the act being softened -- the request, the refusal, the correction, which are the speech-act engine's; nor the rank behind the weight of a threat, which is the status engine's; nor the felt collapse when face is lost, which the shame engine holds. It models the face-work itself: the two wants that make up face, the acts that threaten them, the weighing of the threat, and the strategies by which a persona does the act with more redress or less, or does not do it at all.

The spine is the finding that face has two sides -- positive face, the want to be approved of and included, and negative face, the want to be unimpeded and autonomous -- and that many ordinary acts threaten one or both, so speakers pay redress in proportion to the threat's weight (social distance, relative power, the ranking of the imposition). The five strategies are the ordered ways of doing a face-threatening act: bald (no redress), solidarity (redress to positive face), deference (redress to negative face), hint (off the record), and forbear (do not do it).

The sociology and pragmatics of face and politeness, applied directly as process constraints.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **Speech act**: The engine models the redress wrapped around an act, not the act itself. The illocutionary deed being softened -- the directive that asks, the assertive that corrects, the commissive that refuses -- is owned by the speech-act engine; this engine governs how much face that deed is made to cost, not the deed.
- **Status and Power**: The engine models the weighing and management of face, not the rank that feeds it. The place in a deference order and the base from which a persona moves others are owned by the status and power engines; relative power is one input to a threat's weight, but this engine governs the face-work, not the standing behind it.
- **Shame**: The engine models the interactional handling of face before and during the act, not the felt collapse when face is lost. The appraisal of exposure against a standard is owned by the shame engine; this engine governs the work to keep face, and hands the emotion of losing it to shame.
- **Recognition**: The engine models the want for approval that is positive face, not the act of granting it. The social acknowledgment of who a persona is or what they have done is owned by the recognition engine; this engine governs the face-want that such acknowledgment satisfies, not the granting.
- **Self-esteem**: The engine models the public self-image claimed in interaction, not the private global attitude toward the self. The resting valuation a persona holds of themselves is owned by the self-esteem engine; this engine governs the face put out to the room, which can be defended even as the private esteem is low.
- **Status-move**: The engine models the redress of a face-threat, not the tactical raising or lowering of standing. A discrete move in status space is owned by the status-move engine; the two often ride together -- a bald act can lower another's standing -- but this engine governs the face-work, that one the status play.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per file.

- **[face](process_face.md)** (the root): The public self-image with its two wants, the acts that threaten it, and the weighing of a threat by distance, power, and imposition. Anchored by Goffman (face and face-work) and Brown & Levinson (the two faces and the threat weighting).
- **[bald](process_bald.md)** (strategy): The act done on the record without redress -- chosen for low threat, high power, or urgent need, and read as frankness, command, or rudeness by the weight it ignores. Anchored by Brown & Levinson (bald on-record).
- **[solidarity](process_solidarity.md)** (strategy): Redress to the other's positive face -- draw near, claim common ground, do the act inside a gesture of belonging. Anchored by Brown & Levinson (positive politeness), Lakoff (make the other feel good), and Leech (approbation).
- **[deference](process_deference.md)** (strategy): Redress to the other's negative face -- hedge, minimise, apologise, give an out, do the act without presuming. Anchored by Brown & Levinson (negative politeness), Lakoff (don't impose, give options), and Leech (tact).
- **[hint](process_hint.md)** (strategy): Off the record -- imply the want so it can be denied, and leave the uptake to the hearer; safest for the speaker, least sure of landing. Anchored by Brown & Levinson (off-record).
- **[forbear](process_forbear.md)** (strategy): Do not do the act -- the threat weighed too great to risk in any form, the want set aside and the unsaid thing left to accumulate. Anchored by Brown & Levinson (don't do the FTA).
