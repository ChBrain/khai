# Anxiety: Reference

## Line of Work

Anxiety as a **multi-type** engine: the diffuse, anticipatory apprehension of an uncertain future threat, taken across its cognitive, attentional, and anticipatory forms and the trait that tunes them. The domain does not model the sharp, cued response to a present and identified threat, which is the fear engine's; nor the physiological fight/flight/freeze/fawn mobilization to a present stressor, which is the stress engine's; nor the repetitive dwelling on a past or present loss, which is the rumination engine's; nor the perceptual-attentional baseline that filters what a persona notices, which is the apprehension engine's. It models the state itself: the bracing against a danger that has no clear object, is not yet present, and may never come.

The spine is the affective science of the fear/anxiety distinction. Where fear is phasic and cued -- a rapid response to an imminent, identified threat -- anxiety is sustained and objectless, a readiness for the possible rather than a reaction to the actual, and the two run on partly distinct neural systems: the amygdala for phasic fear, the bed nucleus of the stria terminalis (BNST) for sustained anxiety (Davis, Walker, Miles & Grillon), and Gray and McNaughton's behavioral inhibition system, which halts and scans when threat is possible but unconfirmed. Barlow frames anxiety as a future-oriented mood state of preparation for possible threat, marked by a sense of uncontrollability; LeDoux distinguishes the defensive survival circuits from the cognitively assembled feeling. The state runs in forms: Borkovec's account of worry as a verbal-linguistic stream that both engages and avoids the feared; the hypervigilance and attentional bias toward threat documented across the anxiety literature (Eysenck; Mathews & MacLeod); and Riskind's looming vulnerability, the appraisal of threat as approaching. Beneath them, intolerance of uncertainty (Dugas & Ladouceur; Carleton's "fear of the unknown") is argued to be the core cognitive vulnerability. The altitudes are those: the state, its three forms, and its trait.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **Fear**: The engine models the diffuse, anticipatory apprehension of an uncertain threat, not the sharp response to a present one. Fear -- the phasic, cued alarm at an imminent, identified danger, and its panic, phobia, and terror forms -- is owned by the fear engine, which explicitly disclaims anxiety at its boundary; anxiety is the sustained, objectless bracing that runs on a distinct system (BNST vs amygdala). The two compose: fear reacts to the actual, anxiety braces for the possible.
- **Stress**: The engine models the anticipatory apprehension, not the physiological response to a present demand. The fight/flight/freeze/fawn mobilization to a present stressor is owned by the stress engine; anxiety may recruit that arousal, but its object is an uncertain future threat, not a present demand exceeding resources, and the apprehension is the emotion, the mobilization the body's answer to it.
- **Rumination**: The engine models the future-facing worry, not the past-facing brood. Rumination -- the repetitive, self-focused dwelling on a loss or wrong that has already happened -- is owned by the rumination engine; worry, anxiety's cognitive form, shares its repetitive verbal shape but runs at the future, not the past, and this engine owns that forward-aimed case.
- **Apprehension**: The engine models the affective state of anxiety, not the perceptual-attentional baseline. The apprehension engine models a persona's characteristic perceptual filter -- what it is set to notice; anxiety is the emotion of uncertain future threat, which biases attention toward danger (vigilance) but is the felt apprehension itself, not the standing perceptual style.
- **Hope (dread)**: The engine models the sustained emotional state, not the prospect-appraisal stance. The pessimistic expectation that the future holds bad with no agency over it -- dread as a resting orientation -- is the hope engine's position; anxiety is the live, felt apprehension with its worry, vigilance, and looming, of which a dread stance may be the standing colour.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per file.

- **[anxiety](process_anxiety.md)** (the root, process): The diffuse, anticipatory apprehension of an uncertain future threat -- objectless where fear is cued, sustained where fear is phasic. Anchored by Barlow (anxious apprehension) and Gray & McNaughton / Davis (the BIS and the fear/anxiety dissociation).
- **[worry](process_worry.md)** (process, form): The verbal-cognitive stream of future what-ifs that both engages and avoids the feared. Anchored by Borkovec (worry as verbal-linguistic avoidance).
- **[vigilance](process_vigilance.md)** (process, form): The hypervigilant scanning, attentional bias toward threat, and readied body. Anchored by Gray & McNaughton (the BIS) and the attentional-bias literature (Eysenck; Mathews & MacLeod).
- **[looming](process_looming.md)** (process, form): The threat appraised as approaching, mounting, closing in -- the anticipatory core. Anchored by Riskind (looming vulnerability) and Grupe & Nitschke (anticipation of uncertain threat).
- **[intolerance of uncertainty](position_uncertainty.md)** (position, trait): A standing inability to bear the not-knowing, the core cognitive vulnerability that turns open questions into threats. Anchored by Dugas & Ladouceur and Carleton (fear of the unknown).
