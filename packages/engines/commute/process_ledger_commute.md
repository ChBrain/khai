# Process: The Ledger

## Taxonomy

[Commute](process_commute.md), why the account is never settled, and where the engine is seen for one thing.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: commute

## Initiated by

The persona is worse off and cannot produce the entry that says so.
The Ledger is why: [an invisible gain](process_the_invisible_gain.md) that cannot be re-experienced, [an unattributed cost](process_the_unattributed_cost.md) charged to something else, and [a next move](process_the_next_move.md) decided the same way as the last one.

## Direction

Toward an arrangement that cannot be evaluated by the person inside it.
Evaluating the trade would require holding both halves at once: what the distance bought, felt as it was felt at the beginning, against what the journey takes, summed across six years. Neither figure is available. The first has stopped producing anything and the second was never charged to this account.
So the persona has an accurate general sense that something is heavy and no way to locate it.

## Lever

The persona's competence at their own life against an arrangement built to be unauditable.
The carrying force is that they are not deceived and would resent the suggestion: they know exactly how long the journey is, they chose it, they can list what it bought, and they are managing a complicated life about as well as anybody does; the resistance is that the two quantities that would settle the question are held in different currencies at different times, one of which has stopped being issued, so no amount of competence assembles them into a comparison.
The Ledger rides the engine's keystone knife-edge: everything the commute was traded for stops being felt, and the commute does not.

## Echo

A tiredness with no line item.
It shows the three earlier movements complete -- a trade struck on unequal information, a passage costing most on its ordinary days, a settling that erased one side -- and adds the reason none of it gets corrected.
This is why the Ledger is the hardest of the four to stage and the only one worth closing on: the trade must have been sensible, the house must be genuinely good, the journey must be ordinary, nobody must be complaining, and the audience must be able to watch a reasonable arrangement quietly become a bad one with nothing going wrong in it.
A play that stages the commute as misery has replaced the mechanism with a complaint, and one that stages the persona as regretting the move has replaced it with a mistake.
The Ledger is dated to no particular day and is felt on all of them.
