# @chbrain/khai-engine-rumination

## 0.1.1

### Patch Changes

- 0b55676: Add the rumination engine: the persona's repetitive, passive dwelling on its own distress and its causes and consequences, circling without resolving. The root (`process_rumination.md`) declares Nolen-Hoeksema's response-styles rumination: the mind returns to its own pain again and again without moving to act, so the thought circles rather than resolves and the distress is deepened by the dwelling -- why low mood outlasts its cause. Three forms carry the circling: `brooding` (the moody pole -- passive, evaluative comparison of the self to an unmet standard, the why-me, the most harmful component), `replaying` (the rewind -- a specific unresolved event, slight, or mistake run through again and again, kept raw by its recurrence), and `reflection` (the analytic pole -- the purposeful turn inward to understand the distress, less toxic than brooding and constructive when kept concrete but corrosive when it abstracts away from what to do).

  A clean cognitive-clinical gap from the audit. Bounded against `mood` (the affective state the loop feeds vs the loop itself -- the two drive each other), `apprehension` (the future-tilted anxiety of worry vs the retrospective dwelling), `regulation` (the adaptive repertoire the ruminator fails to reach for vs the perseverative response style that replaces it), and `executive-function`/`goal` (the attentional control whose lapse permits the loop and the blocked goal whose recurrence feeds a replay). No homonym gate: `rumination` and the form stems `brooding`/`replaying`/`reflection` are all free. Warranted (LORE) on Nolen-Hoeksema (response-styles theory, 1991), Treynor, Gonzalez & Nolen-Hoeksema (the brooding/reflection two-factor split, 2003), Nolen-Hoeksema, Wisco & Lyubomirsky (rethinking rumination, 2008), Watkins (constructive vs unconstructive repetitive thought, 2008), and Martin & Tesser (rumination as recurrent goal-related thought, 1996). Set at patch as the free level; a new engine may warrant a minor at the maintainer's `bump:minor` label.
