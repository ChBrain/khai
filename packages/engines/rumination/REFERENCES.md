# Rumination: Reference

## Line of Work

Rumination as **process**: the persona's repetitive, passive dwelling on its own distress and its causes and consequences, circling without resolving. The domain does not model the affective state the loop feeds, which is mood's; nor the future-tilted anxiety of worry, which is apprehension's; nor the adaptive repertoire of emotion regulation the ruminator fails to reach for, which is regulation's; nor the attentional control whose lapse lets the loop run, which is executive-function's; nor the recurrent pull of a blocked goal, which is goal's. It models the dwelling itself: the return of thought to its own pain, the modes that return takes, and the way the circling deepens what it examines.

The spine is Nolen-Hoeksema's response-styles account of rumination. Faced with distress, some people respond by repetitively and passively focusing on their symptoms and on the causes, meanings, and consequences of those symptoms, without moving to active problem-solving -- a response style that prolongs and deepens low mood rather than relieving it. The construct divides: a maladaptive, moody brooding that compares the self to an unmet standard; a specific replaying of unresolved events; and a more purposeful, analytic reflection whose value turns on whether it is processed abstractly or concretely. The three forms are those: brooding, replaying, reflection.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **Mood**: The engine models the repetitive dwelling on distress, not the affective state it feeds. The persisting affective background a persona carries is owned by the mood engine; rumination is the cognitive loop that maintains and deepens low mood, and owns the dwelling, not the state dwelt in -- though the two drive each other, low mood recruiting rumination and rumination worsening low mood.
- **Apprehension**: The engine models the retrospective dwelling on distress, not the future-tilted anxiety of worry. The anxious baseline and the anticipation of threat are owned by the apprehension engine; worry looks forward to what may go wrong, rumination looks back and inward at what has gone wrong and how it feels, and the two are distinct forms of repetitive negative thought.
- **Regulation**: The engine models a perseverative response style that maintains distress, not the adaptive repertoire that would relieve it. Gross's regulation -- reappraisal, situation change, expression control -- is owned by the regulation engine; rumination is what happens instead of effective regulation, a passive dwelling rather than an active intervention, and its reflection form sits at the border but hands the constructive reappraisal itself to regulation.
- **Executive function and Goal**: The engine models the perseverative content-loop, not the attentional control that fails to stop it nor the goal that drives its recurrence. The capacity to disengage attention is owned by the executive-function engine; the pull of a blocked goal that makes an event recur is owned by the goal engine; rumination owns the dwelling that impaired control permits and unfinished goals feed, not the control or the goal.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per file.

- **[rumination](process_rumination.md)** (the root): The repetitive, passive return of thought to its own distress -- circling the pain without resolving it, and deepening it by the circling. Anchored by Nolen-Hoeksema (response-styles theory) and Nolen-Hoeksema, Wisco & Lyubomirsky (rethinking rumination).
- **[brooding](process_brooding.md)** (form): The moody pole -- passive, evaluative comparison of the self to an unmet standard, gloom pondered without progress, the most harmful component. Anchored by Treynor, Gonzalez & Nolen-Hoeksema (brooding) and Nolen-Hoeksema (response styles).
- **[replaying](process_replaying.md)** (form): The rewind -- a specific unresolved event run through again and again, kept raw by its recurrence. Anchored by Martin & Tesser (recurrent thought about an unattained goal).
- **[reflection](process_reflection.md)** (form): The analytic pole -- the purposeful turn inward to understand the distress, constructive when concrete and corrosive when abstract. Anchored by Treynor, Gonzalez & Nolen-Hoeksema (reflection) and Watkins (constructive versus unconstructive repetitive thought).
