# References

## Line of Work

Social anxiety as **process**: the chronic, anticipatory fear of negative evaluation by others. The engine's core claim rests on the distinction between objective social danger and perceived social hazard. The persona enters an interpersonal arena convinced that other people are critical, judgmental, and observant monitors who expect high performance standards, while appraising themselves as deficient, vulnerable, and prone to catastrophic failure.

**The cognitive model of Clark & Wells (1995)** decides the engine's movement structure across three sequential phases of social exposure. Before the encounter, **anticipatory processing** sets in: the persona engages in detailed mental rehearsal of anticipated failure, reviewing past humiliations and priming anxious imagery. During the encounter, attention shifts inward into **self-focused attention**: the persona constructs an observer-perspective mental representation of the self as a social object, taking interoceptive sensations (heart rate, trembling, blushing) as visible proof that they are failing publicly. To cope, they deploy subtle safety behaviors that impair social performance and prevent the disconfirmation of their fears. After the encounter, **post-event processing** consolidates the failure: a ruminative post-mortem review selectively recalls perceived flaws, filtering out neutral or positive feedback and cementing the narrative of disaster, which directly seeds the next anticipatory cycle.

**Rapee & Heimberg (1997)** formalize the dual attentional allocation: the persona concurrently scans an internalized mental representation of their appearance and behavior alongside external threat cues in the room, constantly computing a perceived discrepancy between expected standards and perceived personal adequacy. **Leary's sociometer theory** grounds the evolutionary stakes: social anxiety is an adaptive alarm monitoring relational value and potential exclusion from the group, which explains why the perceived cost of a social slip feels catastrophic rather than minor. **Hofmann (2007)** clarifies the maintaining factors: the persona systematically overestimates the probability and the social cost of a perceived mistake while underestimating their own coping capacity.

**The twist is that the safety behaviors deployed to prevent disaster are precisely what guarantee awkwardness, misinterpretation, or distance.** The persona avoids eye contact, rehearses sentences silently before speaking, monitors their posture, or stays at the room's periphery to avoid drawing attention. The room does not read this as terror; it reads it as aloofness, disinterest, coldness, or hostility. The very defenses intended to avert rejection evoke the cool reception the persona feared, confirming the internal thesis that people are unwelcoming and that the persona does not belong.

**On the evidence, stated plainly.** The tripartite cognitive model is well validated experimentally across clinical and non-clinical populations; the link between self-focused attention, safety behaviors, and impaired social performance is robust. The engine takes **no position on etiology or diagnosis**: whether the anxiety stems from temperament, past humiliation, developmental conditioning, or high-stakes contexts is left to the author. It models the maintaining mechanism of evaluative dread, authored as character architecture for fiction, never as a clinical assessment of a real person.

## Restrictions

- **Fear and Phobia**: The conditioned terror of an external object, creature, or physical setting belongs to `fear` (and specific phobia). Social anxiety is an evaluative and interpersonal appraisal, not an object-fear.
- **Embarrassment**: The acute, transient mortification that follows an actual social breach or exposure belongs to `embarrassment`. Social anxiety is chronic, anticipatory, and perseverative surveillance, occurring before and long after any event, often without any real breach.
- **Shame**: The global painful feeling of being defective, worthless, or exposed in one's core self belongs to `shame`. Social anxiety is specifically concerned with negative evaluation by an audience, even when the persona intellectually values their own worth.
- **Diagnosis**: This is authored character architecture for a play. It is never a clinical assessment or diagnosis of a real person.

## Encoding

- `process_social_anxiety.md` is the root, declaring the chronic evaluative threat model and the maintaining cycle.
- `process_anticipatory_processing.md` models the pre-exposure phase: mental rehearsal of failure, catastrophic prediction, and pre-event dread.
- `process_self_focused_attention.md` models the in-event phase: the shift to internal self-monitoring, the self as a social object, and self-defeating safety behaviors.
- `process_post_event_processing.md` models the post-exposure phase: the ruminative post-mortem that filters reality to confirm catastrophe and feed the next cycle.
