# Instructions: Social Anxiety

How a Playwright wires the social anxiety engine into a play. You wire by linking the play's own content to the engine's files; every file stays untouched, they are canon. The Roadie plumbs the law into Instructions Knowledge on deploy. Authoring guidance, not runtime content, and it does not go on tour. This engine authors a character whose social life is bounded by the ongoing fear of negative evaluation; it is not a diagnosis of any real person.

## Human

- The human sets which movements are in play and how severe the maintaining cycle runs. Choosing all three by default can clutter a scene; often a persona is defined primarily by the dread days before ([anticipatory processing](process_anticipatory_processing.md)), the rigid self-protective stiffness during the encounter ([self-focused attention](process_self_focused_attention.md)), or the agonizing midnight autopsy after returning home ([post-event processing](process_post_event_processing.md)). The human also sets the audience and the setting: whether the evaluative gaze is an authority figure, peers, an audience, or an intimate dinner.

## Agent

- Cast the persona along one or more movements: anticipatory processing, where the upcoming event is predicted to end in humiliation and safety plans are obsessively rehearsed; self-focused attention, where the persona turns their scrutiny inward during interaction, monitors physical anxiety signals as proof of visible failure, and uses safety behaviors that strangle spontaneous connection; or post-event processing, where the persona reconstructs the encounter as a disaster regardless of how well it went. A persona holding a movement may link it under Projection; the audit surfaces the ones left unnamed. You link to the engine, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- The acute, conditioned panic in response to a specific object or physical setting belongs to `fear`. The transient, immediate flushing that follows an actual exposed blunder belongs to `embarrassment`. The deep, global conviction of personal unworthiness belongs to `shame`. The evolutionary concern for tribal standing belongs to `status`. This engine owns only the chronic, anticipatory, and maintaining machinery of perceived negative evaluation: an imagined critical audience, an internalized observer self-image, and the self-protective behaviors that bring about the very estrangement they attempt to avoid.

## Knowledge

- Social anxiety is a cognitive and interpersonal maintenance cycle, not a lack of social skill. Clark & Wells (1995) establish the three core temporal phases: anticipatory processing, in-situation self-focused attention, and post-event processing. During an interaction, Rapee & Heimberg (1997) demonstrate that attention splits: the persona monitors an internalized mental representation of how they look and sound to others, taking inner sensations (heart pounding, voice tremor, blushing) as visible evidence of inadequacy. To avoid catastrophe, the persona executes safety behaviors (averting gaze, holding a glass tightly, rehearsing words mentally, speaking minimally), which prevent them from disconfirming their fears and cause others to perceive them as cold or distant. Leary's sociometer theory explains why the threat feels existential: humans evolved to monitor inclusionary status, and negative evaluation signals lethal ostracism. Hofmann demonstrates that the persona systematically overestimates both the probability and the social cost of negative evaluation.

## System

- Do stage the discrepancy between internal turmoil and external reality: the room is usually indifferent or friendly, while the persona is enduring an internal trial.
- Do stage safety behaviors clearly: show the persona holding a cup with two hands to hide shaking, looking at notes to avoid eye contact, or staying beside the exit.
- Do let the twist land: the persona's defensive guardedness is misinterpreted by others as snobbery, coldness, or boredom, creating the social distance the persona feared.
- Do choose the movements deliberately: a persona who enjoys an evening while there but destroys it in retrospect during post-event processing is a distinct and compelling character.
- Do not make the persona genuinely incompetent: the power of social anxiety is that the persona often performs acceptably, yet feels completely exposed and defeated.
- Do not resolve it with superficial cheer: telling the character "nobody is looking at you" or "just be yourself" fails to touch the cognitive machinery and only invalidates them.
- Do not use this as a diagnosis or clinical evaluation of a real human: it is authored character architecture for a play.
