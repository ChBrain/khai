# Process: Categorization

## Taxonomy

The sorting of instances into kinds, and the forming of the kinds.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: categorization

## Initiated by

A persona meets a particular, and treats it as a case of a kind.
The trigger is an instance -- a thing, a person, an event -- that the persona does not respond to in its bare particularity but as a member of a class it already holds, or as the seed of a class it is forming. What enters is the move from the individual to the kind: this is a bird, a betrayal, a chair.
Categorization is the most basic cognitive economy there is, letting the persona bring everything it knows about a kind to bear on a case it has never seen before.

## Direction

Toward the instance placed in a kind, and the kind itself given structure.
Two things run together: sorting a case into a category, and building the category it is sorted into. And the categories the persona builds have a shape Rosch found universal -- membership is graded rather than all-or-none, so a robin is a better example of bird than a penguin (typicality); categories cohere by family resemblance, their members sharing overlapping features rather than a set of defining ones; and they settle at a privileged basic level, the middle rung of abstraction, most informative and first to mind.
The direction is from particulars to a structured system of kinds, and back to the next particular judged by it.

## Lever

Similarity and coherence, not a checklist of necessary-and-sufficient features.
Whether an instance is taken as a member turns not on its meeting strict defining criteria -- most natural categories have none -- but on its resemblance to the category's structure and its coherence with what the persona knows. The classical view, that a category is a definition and membership is all-or-none, fails against the graded, family-resemblant categories people actually use; the lever is degree of fit, and its threshold is soft.
Raise the resemblance and the coherence, and the instance is admitted as a good member; lower them, and it becomes a borderline or a non-member, but rarely by a bright line.

## Echo

The membership is decided on one of three bases (Murphy): [prototype](process_prototype.md), by similarity to an abstracted central tendency; [exemplar](process_exemplars.md), by similarity to stored individual instances; or [theory](process_theory.md), by fit to an intuitive account of why the features cohere.
The three are not exclusive; a persona may judge a clear case by its prototype, a borderline one by a remembered exemplar, and a puzzling one by theory.
What ends the process is the instance placed and the kind updated -- and what it leaves is a category the persona will bring to the next case. The heuristic that judges a probability by a category's similarity is the representativeness engine's; the social category applied to persons is the bias engine's stereotype member; the mapping of relational structure across domains is the analogy engine's. This engine owns the formation and structure of the kinds.
