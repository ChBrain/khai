# Authenticity: Reference

## Line of Work

Authenticity as a **multi-type** engine: living and acting from one's true self -- one's own values, feelings, and convictions -- rather than from external pressure or a false front, taken across its three components and the trait that tunes them. The domain does not model the situational tuning of self-presentation to fit the room, which is the self-monitoring engine's; nor the revealing of personal information, which is the self-disclosure engine's; nor the content of who one is, the self-concept, which is the identity engine's; nor the managed emotional display of a work role, which is the emotional-labor engine's. It models the alignment itself: the outward life -- what the persona says, chooses, and shows -- matched to the inward truth of what it actually is.

The spine is the psychology of authenticity as alignment between the true self and its expression. Rogers gave it its root in congruence -- the fully functioning person whose experience, awareness, and expression match, against the incongruence that breeds distress -- and Deci and Ryan set it in self-determination, the true self acting from its own reasons rather than another's. Kernis and Goldman gave it a multicomponent structure: awareness (knowing one's own motives and feelings), unbiased processing (not denying or distorting self-relevant information), behavior (acting in accord with one's values and needs rather than to please or gain reward), and relational orientation (valuing openness and truthfulness in close relationships). Wood and colleagues built the person-centered Authenticity Scale from three factors -- authentic living, self-alienation, and accepting external influence -- naming the failure mode as sharply as the achievement. The construct is bounded against self-monitoring: the high self-monitor tunes presentation to the situation, but tuning how one comes across is not the same as betraying the self beneath, and one can do the first while remaining congruent in the second. The altitudes are those: the alignment, its three components, and its trait.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **Self-monitoring**: The engine models the alignment of the shown self with the felt one, not the tuning of self-presentation to the situation. Self-monitoring -- the characteristic degree to which a persona watches and adjusts how it comes across to fit the room -- is owned by the self-monitoring engine; authenticity is the deeper match of the presented self to the true self, and the two are distinct, since a persona can tune its presentation while remaining congruent beneath, or hold one style everywhere while alienated from its own values. The two compose: self-monitoring tunes the surface, authenticity aligns the depth.
- **Self-disclosure**: The engine models relational genuineness, not the revealing of personal information. Self-disclosure -- the act of telling others private facts about oneself -- is owned by the self-disclosure engine; authenticity's transparency is the wider thing, being genuinely oneself in a close tie, which disclosure serves but does not exhaust: one can disclose facts while performing, or be transparently genuine while private.
- **Identity**: The engine models living in accord with the true self, not the content or construction of that self. Who the persona is -- the self-concept, its commitments and its story -- is owned by the identity engine; authenticity presupposes an identity and asks only whether the persona lives from it, not what it is.
- **Emotional labor**: The engine models authenticity as a general alignment, not the managed emotional display of a work role. The surface and deep acting a job's feeling rules demand -- and the strain of their mismatch with felt emotion -- are owned by the emotional-labor engine; inauthentic display at work is one instance of the gap this engine names, but the labor of managing it for a role is the emotional-labor engine's.
- **Self-esteem and self-compassion**: The engine models truth to the self, not evaluation or care of it. The judgment of the self's worth is the self-esteem engine's, and the compassionate stance toward the suffering self is the self-compassion engine's; authenticity is orthogonal to both, the alignment of the shown and felt self whether that self is judged well or badly, met kindly or harshly.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per file.

- **[authenticity](process_authenticity.md)** (the root, process): Living and acting from the true self -- the outward life aligned with the inward truth, against the false front. Anchored by Kernis & Goldman (the conceptualization) and Rogers (congruence).
- **[awareness](process_awareness.md)** (process, component): Self-knowledge, clear and undistorted -- knowing one's own motives, feelings, and values without denial or self-serving distortion. Anchored by Kernis & Goldman (awareness and unbiased processing).
- **[congruence](process_congruence.md)** (process, component): Authentic action -- behaving in accord with the true self rather than to please or gain reward. Anchored by Rogers (congruence) and Kernis & Goldman (the behavioral component).
- **[transparency](process_transparency.md)** (process, component): Relational authenticity -- being genuine and open in close relationships, the real self shown to intimates. Anchored by Kernis & Goldman (relational orientation).
- **[authentic](position_authentic.md)** (position, trait): A standing disposition to live from the true self, against self-alienation and accepting external influence. Anchored by Wood et al. (the Authenticity Scale) and Harter/Winnicott (the false self).
