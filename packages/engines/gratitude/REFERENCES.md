# Gratitude: Reference

## Line of Work

Gratitude as **process**: how a persona appraises a benefit received as freely and benevolently given, and the warm, prosocial impulse that follows -- the turn back toward the source of a good, to acknowledge it and to answer it. The domain does not model the structure of exchange or the obligation to return -- that is the gift engine's territory -- nor the acknowledgment of who another is or what they did -- that is recognition's. It models the receiver's felt appraisal: what a good becomes to the one who receives it, once they read whether it was a gift or a due.

The spine is that gratitude is an appraisal, not a reflex of receiving. The same benefit warms the persona toward its source and binds them closer, or lands as a debt to be discharged, depending on whether it is read as freely, costlessly, and benevolently given. So the modes are the turns the received good can take: toward an identified benefactor (benefaction), into an obligation felt as weight (indebtedness), toward a good with no giver (appreciation), or beyond persons entirely (transcendence). Gratitude is the moral memory of a benefit -- the tie that keeps working after the transaction is closed.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **Gift**: The engine models the receiver's felt appreciation of a benefit, not the structure of the exchange or the obligation to return. How a gift creates obligation, binds the parties, and requires a return is owned by the gift engine; this engine governs what the good becomes to the one who receives it.
- **Debt**: The engine models indebtedness as the receiver's felt weight of obligation, not the quantified obligation as an object. The ledgered debt that binds creditor to debtor is owned by the debt engine; this engine governs the felt sense a benefit creates, not its accounting.
- **Recognition**: The engine models the receiver's gratitude for a benefit, not the social acknowledgment of who a person is or what they have done. The act of acknowledging another is owned by the recognition engine; this engine governs the warm appraisal of a good received.
- **Virtue**: The engine models gratitude as an emotion-process -- the state, its appraisal, and the turns it takes in the moment. Gratitude as a dispositional character pole, the settled disposition fired under pressure against its axis-partner, is owned by the virtue engine; this engine is the same phenomenon at the altitude of the state, as pride and envy are each both a virtue pole and their own engine.
- **Forgiveness**: The engine models the response to a good received; the release of a grievance owed -- the negative counterpart of the account -- is owned by the forgiveness engine.
- **Awe**: The engine models directed thanks toward a source, including one beyond persons; the accommodation of a vastness that exceeds the persona's frame is owned by the awe engine. Transcendent gratitude may ride with awe but is the giving of thanks, not the enlarging of the frame.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per file.

- **[gratitude](process_gratitude.md)** (the root): A benefit is read as freely and benevolently given, and the persona turns toward its source to answer it. Anchored by Emmons & McCullough (gratitude as state and disposition) and Simmel (the moral memory that outlasts the exchange).
- **[benefaction](process_benefaction.md)** (mode): An identified benefactor's costly, responsive gift reveals their regard; gratitude finds, reminds, and binds, and the persona draws closer. Anchored by Algoe (find-remind-bind).
- **[indebtedness](process_indebtedness.md)** (mode): The same benefit read as an obligation not a gift; discomfort and the pull to repay and be free, holding the persona at the length of what they owe. Anchored by Greenberg (the theory of indebtedness, distinct from gratitude).
- **[appreciation](process_appreciation.md)** (mode): Gratitude with no benefactor -- fortune, a near-miss, the ordinary good; the outlook that counts what is there and is broadened by it. Anchored by Emmons & McCullough (counting blessings) and Fredrickson (broaden-and-build).
- **[transcendence](process_transcendence.md)** (mode): Gratitude directed beyond persons -- to the sacred, nature, or life itself -- where no human giver is adequate to the good. Anchored by Emmons & McCullough (transpersonal gratitude).
