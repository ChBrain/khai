# Gossip: Reference

## Line of Work

Gossip as **process**: evaluative talk about absent third parties, circulated through a
community as its reputational memory, its social bond, and its decentralized enforcement.
It wires on a persona at Projection, how a persona speaks and is spoken of in the group's
talk, because gossip is legible in what its members do -- the reputation carried ahead of a
newcomer, the confidence shared to bind a friend, the shirker's name passed around, the
cruel tale relished. The domain does not model the exclusion the talk may bring, which is
ostracism's; nor the felt exposure of being talked about, which is shame's; nor the vertical
command of an authority, which is obedience's, or its impersonal training, which is
discipline's; nor the direct pull of a present peer majority, which is conformity's; nor the
spread of unverified news about events rather than persons, which is a distinct force. It
models the evaluative talk about the absent and what that talk does.

The spine is the anthropology and evolution of gossip -- the recovery of gossip from its
reputation as idle vice to its place as a load-bearing social mechanism. Dunbar argued that
language itself may have evolved to gossip: that talk about who is doing what with whom does
at conversational scale what mutual grooming does for other primates, binding groups too
large to hold together by grooming alone, and pooling the social information a member needs
to navigate the group. Gluckman showed gossip and scandal to be the maintenance of a group's
unity and morality -- the talk that polices its norms and marks its boundaries, the right to
gossip about a group's members itself a badge of belonging. Boehm found in gossip, ridicule,
and ostracism the tools of a reverse-dominance hierarchy: the means by which egalitarian
groups level the upstart and hold the powerful in check, social control from below carried on
talk. And Spacks traced gossip's moral ambivalence -- condemned as prurient and malicious,
yet intimate, knowing, and inseparable from the way people actually understand one another.
So the engine gives gossip three functions and a twist -- ledger, grooming, policing -- and
keeps a fourth, venom, the condemned malice read not only as a vice but as the sting that
gives the talk its teeth.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The exclusion the talk may bring (ostracism)**: When the group turns from talking about a
  persona to shutting them out -- the silent treatment, the cut, the exclusion -- that acute
  event and its felt threat is the ostracism engine's. Gossip is the talk that may inform or
  precede the exclusion, not the exclusion itself; the murmur is gossip's, the shut door is
  ostracism's.
- **The felt exposure of being talked about (shame)**: The hot, sinking experience of being
  seen as diminished, the wish to hide when one learns one is discussed, is the shame engine's.
  Gossip is the talk that circulates in the group; the emotion it lands in the one talked about
  is shame's, a distinct force the talk may cause but is not.
- **The vertical command and the impersonal training (obedience, discipline)**: Enforcement
  from above -- a legitimate authority's command obeyed against conscience, or the productive
  disciplinary power that trains a self-regulating subject -- is obedience's and discipline's.
  Gossip is enforcement from the side and below: decentralized, horizontal, carried on everyone's
  talk and no one's authority, the group policing itself rather than being policed.
- **The present peer majority (conformity)**: The pull of a group of equals whose visible
  standard bends what a persona shows or believes, there in the room, is the conformity engine's.
  Gossip works on the absent and through reputation, not on the present through the felt pressure
  of the crowd; conformity is the majority before the persona, gossip the talk behind them.
- **Unverified news about events rather than persons (a distinct force)**: The spread of
  unconfirmed information about happenings and situations under ambiguity -- rumor -- is a
  distinct phenomenon from gossip's evaluative talk about absent persons. Gossip weighs people
  and their conduct; the circulation of unverified news about events is its own force, and this
  engine does not model it.
- **The facet itself is not engine content**: The engine declares the four facets exist; which
  one a given persona shows lives on that persona, linked under its Projection chapter. The
  engine refuses to fix a persona's gossip for them.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per file.

- **[gossip](process_gossip.md)** (the root): evaluative talk about absent third parties,
  circulated as a group's reputational memory, bond, and enforcement. Anchored by Dunbar (gossip
  as social grooming and the evolution of language).
- **[ledger](process_ledger.md)**: the reputational record -- the running account of who did
  what and whom to trust, the distributed database that lets cooperation reach past the people
  one has personally tested. Anchored by Dunbar (gossip as information) and the logic of
  reputation and indirect reciprocity.
- **[grooming](process_grooming.md)**: the bond -- the alliance and intimacy built by sharing
  talk about a third, the human form of social grooming, friendship carried on talk about
  others. Anchored by Dunbar (gossip as grooming).
- **[policing](process_policing.md)**: the enforcement -- the threat of being talked about that
  keeps a persona in line and the ridicule that levels an upstart, norms held with no office to
  hold them. Anchored by Gluckman (gossip as group morality) and Boehm (reverse-dominance
  leveling).
- **[venom](process_venom.md)**: the condemned malice and relish in the wounding word, read not
  only as a vice but as the sting that gives the record and bond and policing their teeth. The
  engine's twist; anchored by Spacks (gossip's moral ambivalence).
