# Instructions: Risk

How a Playwright wires the risk engine into a play. You wire by linking the play's own content to the engine's files; the engine files stay untouched, they are canon. The Roadie plumbs the engine's law into Instructions Knowledge on deploy. Authoring guidance, not runtime content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human sets what makes the play's hazard large, and sets it separately for each persona near it, since the same hazard is a different size to the person at the controls and the person beside them. Start with the dread axis, above all with control and consent, because those decide most real objections. Remember the engine sizes a hazard and does not say whether it is dangerous; a play that uses it to settle that has used it backwards.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, a persona links under Projection the features doing the sizing for them -- ordinarily one or two from the dread axis and one from the unknown, since the features load together and a play does not need all eleven to show the shape. Read the sizing as a judgment with content rather than as an error to be corrected: a persona who says a thing is risky and can also tell you roughly how many it kills has not contradicted themselves. Keep it the sizing, not the feeling (anxiety, fear), not the frequency estimate (availability), and not a judgment tilt (bias). You link to the engine, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- The diffuse apprehension of an uncertain threat is anxiety's; the cued response is fear's; the baseline filter is apprehension's; dread as an emotional state is hope's. Judging how often something happens by ease of recall is availability's, which owns the lethal-events work. Zero-risk bias, risk compensation, and the identifiable victim are the bias engine's. Choosing between options is decision's. Group membership is social-identity's and the mandate stamp is moral-conviction's. Whether a production's hazard is genuinely uncontrollable or merely feels so, and whether a disagreement is genuinely an identity reading rather than an ordinary difference of view, are the team's review.

## Knowledge

- Risk is the size a hazard has for a persona, which is not its death rate. Ask somebody how many a thing kills each year and the answer is roughly right; ask how risky it is and the answer moves, because risk for a persona means how dreadful and how unfamiliar the harm is. They are not estimating badly, they are estimating something else. It wires on a persona at Projection and is read on three axes. The dread is the larger: six features that arrive together and move as one -- uncontrollable, so no care on the persona's part changes their exposure; catastrophic, arriving at once to many rather than spread thin; involuntary, which carries an asymmetry of roughly three orders of magnitude, so consent does more work than magnitude; inequitable, borne by one set of people and enjoyed by another; irreversible, where being wrong is permanent rather than expensive; and inherited, handed to people who cannot be asked. The unknown is the second: unobservable, with no sensory signature, so the persona depends on an institution for a fact about their own body; delayed, so attribution never arrives; novel, so familiarity lowers the sizing whether or not anything was learned; unsettled, where visible expert disagreement is itself the information; and unwitting, where the exposed were not told, and the concealment disqualifies every later assurance. The divergence reads what follows: the affect heuristic, which links risk and benefit inversely in the head though they run together in the world; signal value, where a small event stands for a large one and costs far more than it damaged; amplification, the ripple through reporting and agencies that lands costs on people the hazard never touched; the identity reading, where a hazard recruited into a standing division is sized by which side the persona is on; and the count, the expert's sizing, which is a different quantity rather than a corrected one. The engine's twist is at the root: a hazard's size cannot be corrected by its own numbers, and on an identity-marked hazard greater literacy and numeracy widen the disagreement rather than narrowing it.

## System

- Do give the same hazard different sizes for different personas, and let the difference come from control, consent, and who benefits rather than from how much anybody knows.
- Do stage the argument as a disagreement about what risk means, not about how big it is: two personas who size a hazard differently are usually disagreeing about who chose, who gains, and whether the source can be trusted, and evidence aimed at frequency lands on none of it.
- Do let the count be a real position with a real case -- it is the only reading that divides and compares, and it is what any large protective decision must be made in -- rather than a straw man for the persona to defeat.
- Do read the root's twist before writing a scene of persuasion: handing a persona the fatality rate answers a question they were not asking and arrives as an evasion, and on a hazard that marks allegiance the better-informed persona is the more polarised one, with nobody acting in bad faith.
- Do use the identity reading sparingly. Most hazards are argued on their merits, and reading every disagreement as tribal is the misuse this material invites.
- Do not model the feeling (anxiety, fear, apprehension, hope), the frequency estimate (availability), judgment tilts (bias), the choosing (decision), the shortfall (scarcity), or the spiral (escalation). Do not use the engine to settle whether a thing is actually dangerous; it models the sizing and not the hazard. Do not make the hazard stand for a theme; that is authored key, the play's pitch. Do not edit the engine's files; wire only from the play's side.
