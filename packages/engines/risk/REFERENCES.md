# Risk: Reference

## Line of Work

Risk as **position**: the size a hazard has for a persona, which is not its death rate.

The engine's founding observation is Slovic's and it is easy to state and hard to absorb. Ask people
how many die each year from a given cause and their answers track the statistics reasonably well. Ask
them how **risky** it is and the answers move somewhere else entirely, and the somewhere else is
lawful: two factors recovered from ratings of dozens of hazards account for most of the variance. The
persona is not producing a bad estimate of frequency. They are producing a good estimate of something
else, and the whole engine depends on reading it that way.

**The dread** is the larger factor and the one that decides most disputes. Six features load together
and move as one: the hazard is uncontrollable, catastrophic rather than spread thin, involuntary,
inequitable in who bears it against who gains, irreversible, and inherited by people not yet born.
Starr's asymmetry sits inside it and is very large -- a persona will tolerate a voluntarily accepted
risk hundreds of times greater than an imposed one at the same benefit -- which means **consent is
doing more work than magnitude** in most real objections. Because dread is a quality rather than a
quantity it does not divide, so it cannot be traded off against anything, and a society allocating
protection by dread will protect most thoroughly against what frightens rather than against what
kills, while feeling that it has the priorities right.

**The unknown** is the second factor: unobservable, delayed, novel, unsettled in the science, and
unwitting in those exposed. Where dread asks what the harm would be like, this asks whether the
persona would ever find out. Its consequences are the awkward ones. On an unobservable hazard the
persona's own senses stop being an instrument, so a fact about their own body becomes something they
must take from an institution -- which is the actual grievance in most disputes about invisible
hazards, and is not a dispute about parts per million. On a delayed hazard attribution is permanently
unavailable, so the exposed can never be told what happened to them. On a novel one, the sizing falls
with elapsed time whether or not anything was learned, so having been safe becomes indistinguishable
from having been lucky. And where the hazard was concealed, the concealment is evidence about the
discloser rather than about the hazard, which disqualifies the instrument that would have been used to
lower any future estimate.

**The divergence** reads what happens where the sizing and the count part company. The **affect
heuristic** (Finucane, Alhakami, Slovic and Johnson) links risk and benefit inversely in judgment
though they run together in the world, and the link is causal in both directions -- told a
technology's benefits are greater, personas lower their estimate of its risk, having been told nothing
about its risk. **Signal value** is Slovic's: a mishap is not only what it did but what it says about
what could happen, so a minor failure in a poorly understood domain carries costs orders of magnitude
above its damage. **Amplification** is Kasperson's ripple, the signal passing through reporting,
agencies, professional bodies and conversation, with each station magnifying or muffling it -- and its
secondary costs settling on places and industries adjacent to the hazard rather than on the exposed.
The **identity reading** is Kahan's, and it applies to the handful of hazards recruited into a
standing division rather than to hazards generally. And **the count** is the expert's own sizing, held
here as a member of the engine rather than as its correction: it is the right instrument for
allocating a budget and the only reading that divides and compares, and it cannot see consent,
control, equity, or reversibility.

The **twist** is at the root. A hazard's size **cannot be corrected by its own numbers**, because the
size was never made of numbers: the fatality rate answers a question the persona was not asking and
arrives as an evasion, which is how it is generally received. And on a hazard that has become a marker
of allegiance, the correction runs backwards -- greater science literacy and numeracy go with **wider**
polarisation, not narrower, because a persona better equipped to work with evidence is better equipped
to reach the answer their side already holds. Nobody in this has to be acting in bad faith. A
persona's ability to affect a national hazard is nil and their stake in their own community is total,
so holding their people's position is individually sensible and being persuaded is individually
expensive. The instrument that ought to close the gap is the instrument that widens it, and it widens
it most in the personas best able to use it.

The engine **does not** model the feeling. Anxiety, dread as an emotional state, and fear are those
engines'; risk is a sizing, and a persona can size a hazard very large while perfectly calm. Nor does
it model the estimating of frequency by ease of recall, which is availability's -- and the boundary
matters, because Lichtenstein and Slovic's lethal-events work belongs there, on the frequency side of
the same authors' research programme. Nor is it a catalogue of judgment tilts: the bias engine owns
zero-risk bias, risk compensation, and the identifiable victim, and this engine's members are not
biases at all but the content of an alternative and defensible definition.

**On the evidence, stated plainly.** The core finding is solid and the edges are not, and they should
not be read at the same confidence. The psychometric paradigm has been replicated across countries,
hazard sets, and four decades, and the two-factor structure is about as robust as anything in
judgment research. But it is **factor-analytic work on aggregated mean ratings**, and that matters in
two ways the engine should not paper over: the structure is much cleaner in group means than at the
individual level, where personas are considerably messier than the maps suggest; and "dread" and
"unknown" are **interpretive labels placed on loadings**, so the eleven features here are the field's
reading of what clusters together, not eleven separately demonstrated mechanisms. Starr's revealed-
preference asymmetry is a real and repeatedly observed pattern, though his original method of
inferring acceptance from historical data is not well regarded. The affect heuristic is
experimentally demonstrated including the causal manipulation, and holds up. Social amplification is
a **framework rather than a theory** -- it organises cases well and predicts poorly in advance, and
its authors have said so. The **weakest and most contested leg is the identity reading**: Kahan's
polarising-literacy result is real, has replicated on several issues, and is genuinely surprising, but
it is drawn from a small number of highly politicised hazards in one country, the effect sizes are
modest, and whether it generalises beyond that is disputed, with some replications finding weaker or
conditional effects. The engine treats it as a distinct member for exactly this reason: most hazards
are not identity-marked, and reading every disagreement as tribal is the misuse this material invites.
What should not be taken from the engine is that lay risk judgment is superior to expert judgment, or
that the count is a mistake. The claim is narrower: the two are measuring different quantities, both
of which are real, and treating either as the correction of the other is what makes the argument
unresolvable.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The feeling of a threat (anxiety, fear, apprehension, hope)**: The diffuse anticipatory
  apprehension of an uncertain future threat is the anxiety engine's; the cued response is fear's; the
  baseline nervous-system filter is apprehension's; and dread as an emotional state, the awaiting of a
  bad thing, belongs to the hope engine, which owns that member. Risk is a **sizing** and not a
  feeling: a persona can size a hazard very large while perfectly calm, and can be terrified of
  something they know to be trivial.
- **Judging how often something happens (availability)**: The estimation of frequency by ease of
  recall -- including Lichtenstein and Slovic's own work on judged frequency of lethal events -- is
  the availability engine's. That is the frequency side of the same research programme, and this
  engine begins where a persona stops estimating how often and starts sizing how bad.
- **Tilts in judgment (bias)**: Zero-risk bias, risk compensation, proportionality bias, and the
  identifiable victim are the bias engine's, which owns them as tilts. This engine's members are not
  biases -- Slovic's argument is that they are the content of an alternative and defensible definition
  of risk -- and the difference is the whole reason the two packages are separate.
- **Choosing between options under uncertainty (decision)**: How a persona resolves between competing
  options and commits is the decision engine's. Risk sets the size of what is on one side of the
  choice and says nothing about how the choosing is done.
- **The felt lack of a resource (scarcity)**: The tunnelling and bandwidth tax of going without is
  the scarcity engine's. A hazard is not a shortfall.
- **A conflict spiralling (escalation)**: Positions hardening and each retaliation raising the stakes
  is the escalation engine's. A dispute about a hazard may escalate, and the escalating is not this
  engine's.
- **Which side a persona is on (social-identity, moral-conviction, bias)**: Group membership and its
  pull are the social-identity engine's; the stamp that turns a position into a mandate is
  moral-conviction's; the tilts serving belonging and the stake are the bias engine's. This engine
  owns only the narrow consequence -- that on identity-marked hazards, better reasoning widens the
  disagreement.
- **What the hazard actually does (the world)**: The engine models the sizing and not the hazard.
  Nothing in it says whether a thing is dangerous, and a play that uses it to settle that question has
  used it backwards.
- **The hazard as authored symbol (the play's pitch)**: A danger made to **stand for** a theme -- the
  invisible contaminant that mirrors a motif of secrecy -- is refused: that is authored key, the
  play's pitch. Risk sets the size; the meaning read into it is the play's.

## Encoding

Source to constraint. A position tree: one root, three axis heads, sixteen features.

- **[risk](position_risk.md)** (the root): the size a hazard has for a persona, carrying the twist --
  it cannot be corrected by its own numbers, and on a contested hazard the correction runs backwards.
  Anchored by Slovic (1987) and Kahan et al. (2012).
- **[by the dread](position_dread_risk.md)**: how terrible the harm would be, and how little the
  persona could do about it. Anchored by Fischhoff et al. (1978) and Starr (1969).
  [uncontrollable](position_uncontrollable.md), [catastrophic](position_catastrophic.md),
  [involuntary](position_involuntary.md), [inequitable](position_inequitable.md),
  [irreversible](position_irreversible.md), [inherited](position_inherited_hazard.md).
- **[by the unknown](position_unknown_risk.md)**: how far the hazard lies outside what can be seen,
  felt, or settled. Anchored by Fischhoff et al. (1978).
  [unobservable](position_unobservable.md), [delayed](position_delayed_harm.md),
  [novel](position_novel_hazard.md), [unsettled](position_unsettled_science.md),
  [unwitting](position_unwitting.md).
- **[by the divergence](position_divergence_risk.md)**: what happens where the sizing and the count
  part company. Anchored by Finucane et al. (2000), Kasperson et al. (1988), Kahan et al. (2012), and
  Slovic (1987). [the affect heuristic](position_affect_heuristic.md),
  [signal value](position_signal_value.md), [amplification](position_amplification.md),
  [the identity reading](position_identity_reading.md), [the count](position_expert_count.md).
