# Instructions: Chronos

How a Playwright wires the chronos engine into a play. You wire by linking the
play's own content to the engine's files; the engine files stay untouched, they
are canon. The Roadie plumbs the engine's law into Instructions Knowledge on
deploy. Authoring guidance, not runtime content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human sets which objects in the world carry a measured past that matters -- how old they are, the order of what befell them, how long they have lasted, and whether their years read as wear toward an end or as a fullness worth prizing.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, an object with a past links under Yearbook the facet of measured time its history shows: the sheer age gathered from its origin (age), the ordered run of its dated events and provenance (sequence), its having lasted where its cohort is gone (endurance), or the fullness elapsed time brought it (maturation). Play the chronos as the object simply carrying its time -- the datemark, the ordered provenance, the survivor's rarity, the matured worth -- present in the Yearbook, never narrated, and never made to mirror a persona's own ageing. Keep it the measure of time, not its matter: hand the material the years work to decay and wear, the status-career to biography, the felt duration to time-perception, and the opportune moment to kairos. You link to the engine, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- The material the years work in the fabric or substance is decay's and wear's; the object's career between commodity and singular status is biography's; the object kept and passed across generations is the heirloom engine's; a persona's felt duration is time-perception's; the opportune moment cut from the flow is kairos's. Maturation is the shared seam with decay's patina, and they are kept distinct: patina is the surface time marks on the matter, maturation is the fullness the years grow into. Whether the facet rings true for the object and its years is the team's review.

## Knowledge

- Chronos is the measured, sequential passage of time as it bears on a made thing: the one-way flow a piece gathers from its origin, read under its Yearbook, the object's history told as a story in time (Newton's absolute time that flows equably; Kermode's chronos, mere passing time, against kairos). It is the first force to wire on a piece at Yearbook, and it reads the measure, not the matter. Four facets carry it -- age is the sheer quantity of time gathered from the origin, the one figure that only rises; sequence is the ordered, unreorderable run of the thing's dated events, its provenance; endurance is the thing's having lasted, the years survived where its cohort is gone; maturation is the same elapsed time read as fullness rather than loss, the vintage the years alone confer, the engine's twist (distinct from decay's patina: fullness grown into, not a mark on the surface). Chronos is one-way and use-independent -- the drawer-forgotten thing ages exactly as the treasured one does. Read the object by the facet of time its history shows.

## System

- Do give an object with a past its facet under Yearbook; do play the chronos as the object carrying its own measured time, present not narrated; do keep the reading the measure of time -- how old, in what order, how long endured, how full -- not a feeling and not a symbol.
- Do read maturation as worth, not only loss: the same years that spend one thing toward its end bring another to its fullness, and that fullness is distinct from decay's patina -- the state grown into, not the mark on the surface.
- Do not model the material the years work (that is decay and wear), the object's status-career (that is biography), the object kept and passed on (that is heirloom), a persona's felt duration (that is time-perception), or the opportune moment (that is kairos). Do not make the object's age mirror a persona's own ageing or mortality (that is the play's pitch). Do not edit the engine's files; wire only from the play's side.
