# Process: Sequence

## Taxonomy

[Chronos](process_chronos.md), the facet of order.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: chronos

## Initiated by

More than one thing has happened to the object, and an order is fixed between them.
Sequence begins as soon as there are two entries in the thing's history -- a before and an after -- and time's one-way arrow locks which is which; the making came before the sale, the sale before the break, the break before the mend.
It is the Yearbook read as a chain: not how old the thing is but in what order its dated events fell.

## Direction

Along the arrow, earlier to later, unreorderable.
Sequence runs the run of what befell the thing in the order it befell -- made, then owned, then damaged, then repaired, then handed on -- each entry pinned to its place and none of it movable, because the arrow does not reverse.
It moves toward provenance: the ordered record of the thing's passage from hand to hand and state to state, the line of its history laid straight.

## Lever

The fixed order against the loss of its record.
The carrying force is that the arrow of time is one-way, so the entries have a true order whether or not anyone holds it; the resistance is the breaking of the record -- when provenance is lost, the order still happened but can no longer be read, and the chain goes dark between two known points.
Sequence owns the order alone, not the meaning of it: what the ordered history means for the thing's worth or identity is biography's and memory's to read; chronos fixes only that this came before that.

## Echo

The thing's history as an ordered chain.
Sequence leaves the piece with a provenance -- the run of its dated events in their true order, the line a later force reads for a story.
It is the ordering the whole Yearbook rests on: age counts the distance, endurance measures the lasting, but sequence is the spine that puts every entry in its place, first to last.
