# Dissonance: Reference

## Line of Work

Cognitive dissonance as **process**: how the discomfort of holding inconsistent cognitions -- a belief and a discrepant act, a choice and its forgone rival, an effort and a poor return -- drives a persona to change an attitude to restore consistency. The domain does not model the catalog of dissonance-driven tilts held as static positions, which is the bias engine's; nor the exemption of a harm one commits, which is the moral-disengagement engine's; nor the persistence in a failing course of action, which is the escalation engine's. It models the arousal of inconsistency and its reduction: the aversive drive state and the change of the cheapest cognition -- usually the attitude -- by which the persona restores coherence, working through the paradigms in which it arises.

The spine is Festinger's theory of cognitive dissonance and the experimental tradition that established it. Festinger held that inconsistency among cognitions produces an uncomfortable drive state proportional to the importance of the cognitions and the ratio of dissonant to consonant ones, and that the person is motivated to reduce it -- by changing a cognition, adding consonant ones, or trivializing the matter. Because behavior once enacted is fixed, the attitude typically moves instead. Three canonical paradigms demonstrate it: induced compliance (Festinger & Carlsmith's forced-compliance study, where too little reward for a counter-attitudinal act produced the most attitude change), free choice (Brehm's post-decisional spreading of alternatives), and effort justification (Aronson & Mills's severity-of-initiation effect). Aronson later recast dissonance as a threat to a consistent, positive self-concept, and Steele's self-affirmation showed the need to reduce it dissolves when the self is affirmed elsewhere. Three routes carry it: induced compliance, free choice, earning.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **Bias**: The engine models the process of dissonance -- the arousal of inconsistency and its reduction by a change of attitude -- not the catalog of dissonance-driven tilts held as standing positions. The static entries a persona carries, cognitive dissonance and effort justification and system justification among them, are the bias engine's list of tilts; this engine owns the dynamic beneath them, the drive state and the attitude change over time. The engine delegates the position-list of tilts to bias and keeps the process.
- **Moral disengagement**: The engine models the reduction of inconsistency in general, not the specific exemption of a harm one commits. The mechanisms by which a persona does wrong yet feels no self-censure -- moral justification, euphemism, displacement -- are the moral-disengagement engine's; dissonance reduction is broader and need involve no moral harm at all, so the engine delegates the excusing of a transgression to moral-disengagement and keeps the general restoration of consistency.
- **Attribution**: The engine models the change of attitude that resolves an inconsistency, not the causal explanation of an outcome. Assigning a result to internal or external, stable or unstable causes is the attribution engine's; dissonance reduction may recruit an attribution but owns the motivated shift toward consistency, not the causal reasoning itself. The engine delegates the explanation of causes to attribution.
- **Escalation**: The engine models the attitude and value change by which dissonance is resolved, not the persistence in a failing course of action. Throwing good resources after bad to justify a prior investment -- the sunk-cost commitment -- is the escalation engine's; effort justification shares the theme of honoring a cost, but earning revalues the goal already attained while escalation continues to pour into a course not yet abandoned. The engine delegates the persistence in a failing course to escalation.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per file.

- **[dissonance](process_dissonance.md)** (the root): The aversive drive state of inconsistent cognitions and its reduction by moving the cheapest cognition -- usually the attitude, the belief bending to the committed deed. Anchored by Festinger (_A Theory of Cognitive Dissonance_).
- **[induced compliance](process_induced_compliance.md)** (form): The counter-attitudinal route -- an act against one's belief with too little external justification, resolved by changing the belief; the less the inducement, the more the change. Anchored by Festinger & Carlsmith (forced compliance, the \$1/\$20 study).
- **[free choice](process_free_choice.md)** (form): The post-decisional route -- the spreading of alternatives after a hard choice, the chosen upgraded and the rejected downgraded to justify the commitment. Anchored by Brehm (postdecision changes in desirability).
- **[earning](process_earning.md)** (form): The effort-justification route -- the value of a goal raised to match the effort or suffering spent to reach it, the severe initiation ennobling the prize. Anchored by Aronson & Mills (severity of initiation).
