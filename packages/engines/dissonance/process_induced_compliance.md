# Process: Induced Compliance

## Taxonomy

[Dissonance](process_dissonance.md), the counter-attitudinal form.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: dissonance

## Initiated by

The persona acts or speaks against what they believe -- praises a dull task, argues a position they reject -- and cannot point to a large reward or a strong threat that made them do it.
This is induced compliance: a counter-attitudinal act performed with insufficient external justification, so the persona is left holding a deed their belief does not cover.
Having too little outside reason to excuse the act, they are pushed to find the reason inside -- by changing the belief.

## Direction

Toward an attitude shifted to match the act, and shifted more the smaller the inducement.
Induced compliance turns on the size of the justification: a large reward or a real coercion supplies an external reason for the act, leaving the belief untouched; a small one does not, so the dissonance is greater and the persona resolves it by coming to believe the act was right after all.
The direction is inverse to the payment: the less the persona was paid or pressed to act against their attitude, the more that attitude moves -- the one dollar changing the mind that the twenty leaves alone.

## Lever

The insufficiency of the external justification, spent as a change of the attitude to cover the act.
Induced compliance runs on the missing excuse: because the act is done and cannot be undone, and because there is no adequate outside reason to explain it away, the only cheap cognition left to move is the attitude -- so it shifts until the act looks consistent with what the persona now believes.
The lever is the felt need to own the act: lacking a "they made me" or "they paid me," the persona supplies an "I meant it," and the counter-attitudinal deed becomes an attitude-consistent one after the fact.

## Echo

The deed done for too little reason is made right by a belief that comes to fit it.
It is why the person who argues a side for a pittance drifts toward it, why mild pressure changes minds that heavy pressure does not, why "I wouldn't have done it if I didn't mean it" is the shape the mind takes to cover an act -- the smallest push producing the largest turn.
It is [free choice](process_free_choice.md) and [earning](process_earning.md)'s counterpart, the counter-attitudinal act where they are the post-decisional spread and the justified cost. The classic $1/$20 result is its paradigm; induced compliance owns the attitude change that follows a counter-attitudinal act done without enough justification to excuse it.
