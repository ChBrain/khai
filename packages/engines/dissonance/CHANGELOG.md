# @chbrain/khai-engine-dissonance

## 0.1.1

### Patch Changes

- c14a681: Add the dissonance engine: how the discomfort of holding inconsistent cognitions -- a belief and a discrepant act, a choice and its forgone rival, an effort and a poor return -- drives a persona to change an attitude to restore consistency. The root (`process_dissonance.md`) declares Festinger's theory: inconsistency among cognitions produces an aversive drive state proportional to their importance and the ratio of dissonant to consonant ones, and because a committed behavior is fixed, the persona reduces it by moving the cheapest cognition -- usually the attitude, the belief bending to the deed. Three canonical paradigms carry it: `induced_compliance` (a counter-attitudinal act with too little external justification, resolved by changing the belief -- the less the inducement, the more the change), `free_choice` (post-decisional spreading of alternatives, the chosen upgraded and the rejected downgraded after a hard decision), and `earning` (effort justification -- the value of a goal raised to match the effort or suffering spent to reach it).

  Promotes cognitive dissonance from a single `bias/position_cognitive_dissonance.md` entry to a full process engine -- the same move made for `anchoring`, `availability`, and `representativeness`, each of which coexists with its `bias` position-members. Bounded against `bias` (the static catalog of dissonance-driven tilts vs the arousal-and-reduction process), `moral-disengagement` (the exemption of a committed harm vs the general restoration of consistency), `attribution` (the causal explanation of an outcome vs the motivated shift toward consistency), and `escalation` (sunk-cost persistence in a failing course vs the attitude/value change that resolves dissonance). No homonym gate: the root stem `dissonance` and the form stems `induced_compliance`/`free_choice`/`earning` are all free (the bias member's stem is `cognitive_dissonance`). Warranted (LORE) on Festinger (_A Theory of Cognitive Dissonance_, 1957), Festinger & Carlsmith (forced compliance, the \$1/\$20 study, 1959), Brehm (postdecision changes in desirability, 1956), and Aronson & Mills (severity of initiation, 1959), with Aronson's self-consistency revision and Steele's self-affirmation for the root's modern nuance. Set at patch as the free level; a new engine may warrant a minor at the maintainer's `bump:minor` label.
