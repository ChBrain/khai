# Process: The Outcome

## Taxonomy

[Complaint](process_complaint.md), how it ends and what has changed, and where the engine is seen for one thing.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: complaint

## Initiated by

A letter arrives with a finding in it.
The Outcome is what the letter and the months produced: [a partial upheld](process_the_partial_upheld.md), [a settlement](process_the_settlement.md), and [a next complainer](process_the_next_complainer.md) who has been watching.

## Direction

Toward a conclusion that closes the matter and leaves the conduct where it was.
Findings in these cases are usually mixed, remedies are usually training or a reminder of expectations, and the most common practical resolution is that the complainer leaves -- sometimes with a payment, often with a clause about what may be said.
The institution's file records a complaint received, investigated, and concluded, which is an accurate description of a process that worked.

## Lever

The genuine closure of the process against the distribution of what it changed.
The carrying force is that an ending is owed to everybody and endless cases are their own cruelty: the respondent cannot live under an open allegation forever, findings have to be made on what could be established, a partial finding is often the honest answer, and a settlement can be the best available outcome for a persona who needs to be somewhere else; the resistance is that at the end of it the conduct is unchanged, the respondent is in post, the file is closed, and the one thing measurably different is the position of the person who complained.
The Outcome rides the engine's keystone knife-edge: a complaint changes the complainer more reliably than it changes anything they complained about.

## Echo

A closed file and a vacancy.
It shows the three earlier movements complete -- a grievance narrowed into a case, a procedure correct in every part, a cost carried on one side -- and adds the arithmetic nobody performs.
This is why the Outcome is the hardest of the four to stage and the only one worth closing on: the investigator must be conscientious, the safeguards must be real, the finding must be defensible, and the audience must be able to watch a fair process produce a result that only one person paid for.
A play that stages the institution as covering it up has replaced the mechanism with a conspiracy, and one that stages the complainer as vindicated at the end has replaced it with a courtroom.
The Outcome is dated to the letter and is read differently by every party.
