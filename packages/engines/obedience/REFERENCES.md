# Obedience: Reference

## Line of Work

Obedience as **process**: how a persona complies with the command of an authority they take to be legitimate even against their own conscience -- Milgram's destructive obedience. The domain does not model the matching of a peer majority, which is the conformity engine's; nor the capacity to compel by controlling resources and rewards, which is the power engine's; nor the moral self-exemption by which a wrongdoer silences their own conscience for a harm they still own, which is the moral-disengagement engine's. It models the vertical override of conscience by a legitimate command: the pressure of a superior's directive obeyed against what the persona would otherwise do, working through the agentic shift, the authority's perceived right, and the factors that hold the persona in.

The spine is Milgram's experimental study of obedience to authority. Milgram found that a majority of ordinary people, instructed by an experimenter to deliver what they believed were dangerous electric shocks to another person, continued to the maximum voltage despite the victim's protests -- not from cruelty but under the command of a legitimate authority. He explained it by the agentic state: the person shifts from an autonomous mode, owning their acts, to an agentic mode in which they see themselves as the instrument of the authority and relocate responsibility upward. Obedience depended on the authority's perceived legitimacy, and was held in place by binding factors -- the sequential nature of the demands, the awkwardness of breaking off -- while it varied sharply with proximity: obedience fell as the victim was brought closer and the authority made more distant. Three routes carry it: instrument, legitimacy, binding.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **Conformity**: The engine models compliance with a vertical command from a legitimate authority, not the matching of a horizontal peer majority. Yielding to the group -- adjusting one's judgment or behavior to match equals, as in the line-judgment paradigm -- is owned by the conformity engine; obedience is to a superior's order and conformity to a peer consensus, distinct pressures Milgram himself set apart, one hierarchical and acknowledged, the other lateral and often denied. The engine delegates peer-majority influence to conformity and keeps the authority's command.
- **Power**: The engine models the override of conscience by a legitimate command, not the capacity to compel by control of resources. The ability to reward, punish, or coerce through command of what others need is owned by the power engine; obedience runs on the authority's perceived legitimacy -- its accepted right to command -- rather than on its raw leverage, so a legitimate authority is obeyed without any threat and an illegitimate one commanding by force is a matter of power, not obedience. The engine delegates coercive leverage to power.
- **Moral disengagement**: The engine models the shift by which the persona ceases to feel the author of the commanded act, not the self-exemption by which a wrongdoer silences conscience for a harm they still own. The mechanisms -- moral justification, euphemism, displacement of responsibility -- by which a person does wrong yet feels no self-censure are the moral-disengagement engine's; obedience's agentic shift is upstream, a prior divestment of authorship under command, though the two often work together. The engine delegates the moral self-exemption to moral-disengagement.
- **Escalation**: The engine models the binding factors and proximity gradient that hold a persona in an obedient course, not the sunk-cost persistence in a failing venture. The commitment creep by which a small first step eases a larger later one is elaborated by the escalation engine; binding recruits that sequential creep but owns the wider set of holds -- awkwardness of defiance, the authority's presence, the victim's distance -- that keep obedience going. The engine delegates the sunk-cost commitment to escalation.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per file.

- **[obedience](process_obedience.md)** (the root): The override of conscience by the command of a legitimate authority -- a persona doing on order what they would refuse on their own account, responsibility relocated upward. Anchored by Milgram (behavioral study of obedience; _Obedience to Authority_).
- **[instrument](process_instrument.md)** (form): The agentic route -- the shift from autonomous author to felt instrument of the authority, by which responsibility passes upward and the deed ceases to feel one's own. Anchored by Milgram (the agentic state).
- **[legitimacy](process_legitimacy.md)** (form): The authority route -- the perceived right of the authority to command, backed by role, setting, and symbol, that gives the order its binding force. Anchored by Milgram (conditions of legitimacy) and Kelman & Hamilton (authorization).
- **[binding](process_binding.md)** (form): The maintenance route -- the binding factors that hold the persona in the obedient course and the proximity gradient that raises or lowers the obedience. Anchored by Milgram (binding factors; the proximity and immediacy variations).
