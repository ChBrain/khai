# Process: Obedience

## Taxonomy

Social psychology / the study of obedience to authority.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: obedience

## Initiated by

A persona is told by someone they take to be a legitimate authority to do a thing their own conscience refuses, and they do it -- harming another because they were ordered to.
This is obedience to authority: compliance with the command of a superior taken as legitimate, carried out even against the persona's own moral restraints.
The force is vertical: not the pull of equals nor a bribe nor a threat of one's own, but a directive from above obeyed because it came from above.

## Direction

Toward an act the persona would not choose, performed because a legitimate authority commanded it.
Obedience overrides conscience by relocating its ground: under a legitimate command, the persona's own sense of responsibility for the deed is displaced onto the authority, and the moral question shifts from "is this right?" to "am I doing as I am told?"
The direction is compliance despite objection: the persona may protest, may suffer visibly, may believe the act wrong -- and still continue, because the authority's directive, once accepted as legitimate, carries a weight their own scruples do not overturn.

## Lever

The command of a legitimate authority, spent as the override of the persona's own restraints.
Obedience runs on the authority's perceived right to command crossed with the persona's shift into feeling a mere instrument of that command: the order has force because the authority is legitimate, and the persona can carry it out because they no longer feel its author, only its channel.
The lever is the transfer of responsibility upward: because the persona acts as the authority's agent rather than their own, the deed's moral cost is charged to the one who ordered it, and the persona is freed to do what, on their own account, they could not.

## Echo

The order is obeyed against conscience, and the deed is done by a persona who feels they did not author it.
Obedience to authority is why ordinary people carry out cruelties on command, why "I was only following orders" is both an evasion and a real description of a felt state, why the majority of Milgram's subjects went to the end though they believed they were harming a man -- the legitimate command overriding the restraints that would have stopped them acting alone.
It runs as [instrument](process_instrument.md), [legitimacy](process_legitimacy.md), or [binding](process_binding.md) -- the agentic shift that displaces responsibility, the perceived right that licenses the order, the factors that hold the persona in. The matching of a peer majority is the conformity engine's; the capacity to compel by control of resources is the power engine's; the moral self-exemption for a harm is the moral-disengagement engine's. This engine owns the override of conscience by a legitimate authority's command.
