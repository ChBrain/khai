# Asking: Reference

## Line of Work

Asking as **process**: a request for discretionary help, put to somebody free to refuse.

The engine's subject is an estimation error with social consequences. A persona needs something they
cannot get alone, somebody else could supply it, and between those two facts sits a calculation the
persona runs quickly and gets wrong in a consistent direction. Flynn and Bohns' work is the spine:
across favours, donations, and requests for help at work, people asked to predict how many others will
agree substantially underestimate, and the underestimate is directional rather than noisy. What makes
this an engine rather than a finding is the second half -- the error cannot correct itself, because
the evidence that would revise it is produced only by asking.

The boundary with what khai already carries is clean. The transfer that binds precisely by naming no
price is **gift**'s, and the obligation it creates, along with the settling of it, is **debt**'s and
the **owing** composite's -- this engine stops at the request and hands the rest across. The
engineering of compliance, the levers by which a request is made harder to refuse, is **persuasion**'s
and the **swaying** composite's; the difference is the direction of interest, since this engine reads
a persona trying to get something they need rather than a persona trying to move somebody. Why nobody
moves when a stranger collapses is **bystander-effect**'s, which is about non-response to an emergency
nobody asked about. The sustained care of somebody who needs it is **caregiving**'s and
**compassion**'s.

**The reluctance** is the largest movement and leaves no trace. The **imposition** is imagined at high
resolution from one side of the table -- the asker sees the interruption, the hour, the thing that
would have to move, and cannot see how small it is against the helper's day. The **workaround** is
what gets done instead, and it is not cowardice: it feels like competence, it usually succeeds, and a
workaround that succeeds is never audited against the request that was never priced. The **hedging**
wraps whatever finally gets asked -- _no worries if not_, _only if you have a minute_ -- which is
working machinery, because it hands the other person an exit, and is also a discount applied before
anybody has looked at the request.

**The misestimate** is the engine's evidential core. The **no expected** is a forecast rather than a
fear: the asker reasons from the helper's costs, which are visible, and not from the helper's
position, which is not -- that declining a direct request to somebody's face is socially expensive and
that yes is frequently the path of least resistance. The **ask unmade** is where nearly all the volume
sits and the only part with no evidence trail at all, since a decision closed on a forecast produces
no result to learn from. The **channel** carries the error into the mechanics: asking in person is far
more likely to be granted than asking in writing, and the property that makes writing considerate --
that it can be declined at no cost, in one's own time, without a scene -- is exactly the property that
makes it fail.

**The asking** is mostly form. The **request put** has to name the thing, the size, and the deadline
and be addressed to a person, all of which are uncomfortable to supply because each converts a hope
into something refusable -- so requests degrade toward the general, and the general cannot be granted.
The **reason given** raises compliance sharply even when it carries no information, and stops doing so
once the favour is large, which is the half of that finding almost nobody quotes. The **free refusal**
is a load-bearing fiction: remove it and the request is an instruction and the yes means nothing, and
it is not equally available, because standing does not switch off for the length of a favour.

**The granting** is what the operation produces. The **helping** leaves the helper in a better mood
than they started in, and tends to warm their view of the person who asked. The **standing conferred**
is the request's real content -- a public estimate of somebody's competence, issued by a person with no
incentive to flatter, which is why being left out of a request is felt more sharply than being left
out of a thank-you. And because standing is conserved, the same act marks the asker as the one who
could not, which is Blau's finding read from the other side and the reason the engine's cheerful
arithmetic is not available to everybody equally. The **next ask** starts from the same number: the
evidence was delivered and filed as an occasion rather than as a fact.

Hence the **twist**, at the root. **Asking feels like taking and lands like giving.** The asker
experiences the request as incurring a debt; the receiver experiences it as being handed something --
a judgement that they were capable, trusted, and worth approaching, followed by the satisfaction of
having been useful. The two readings of the same act never meet, and the asker's is the one that
decides whether it happens.

The engine is built so that nobody has to be behaving badly for any of it to run, and a play should
keep it that way. The helper is pleased. Nothing is owed. The asker apologises anyway.

**On the evidence, stated plainly.** The legs here are of very unequal strength and the engine should
be read accordingly. **The underestimation effect is the strongest thing on this page.** Flynn and
Bohns and colleagues have shown it repeatedly, across request types, with the error running the same
way each time, and it is one of the better-replicated results in the compliance literature. Its limit
matters: the studies are overwhelmingly **small favours asked of strangers in campus and laboratory
settings**, and the extrapolation to large, repeated, or high-stakes requests is not supported by
them. The engine claims that the error is directional, not that any given request will be granted.
**The channel difference is one striking number from a small set of studies** and should be read as
"in person beats writing by a lot", not as a coefficient anybody should quote. **Langer's placebic
"because" is among the most over-quoted findings in psychology**: in the original paper the empty
reason worked for the small favour and failed for the large one, and the engine states the limit
because most retellings drop it. **The Ben Franklin effect is the weakest leg here.** It rests mainly
on a single 1969 study with a small sample, the dissonance explanation for it is contested, and the
engine uses it for the direction of the effect rather than its size. **Blau's analysis cuts against
the engine's own good news and is kept for that reason**: consultation transfers status to the person
consulted, so asking can cost the asker precisely because it raises the helper -- which means whether
asking is cheap depends on the asker's position, and the samples behind the cheerful findings
systematically exclude the personas for whom it is not. **Adam Grant's synthesis is a trade book**,
used here for the framing that help is undersupplied, not as evidence. There is also a finding in this
area that asking for advice makes the asker look more competent; it is not used here, because one of
its authors has since had multiple papers retracted for data fabrication, and the engine will not rest
a member on work that has not been independently re-established. What should not be taken from this
engine is "just ask" as advice, or that reluctance is irrational, or that any particular request will
be granted. The claim is narrow: the estimate runs low, the unmade asks are the bulk of the
phenomenon, the act is mostly form, and the person who says yes comes out ahead.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The transfer that binds by naming no price (gift, debt, owing)**: How a transfer creates
  obligation, what the receiver then owes, and how it is settled are the gift and debt engines' and the
  owing composite's. This engine stops at the request and hands the rest across; the asker's fear of
  owing is read here, and the owing itself is not.
- **The engineering of compliance (persuasion, swaying, captology)**: Reciprocity, commitment,
  liking, scarcity and the rest of the influence levers are those packages'. The difference is the
  direction of interest: this engine reads a persona trying to get something they need, not a persona
  trying to move somebody who does not.
- **Nobody moving when a stranger collapses (bystander-effect)**: Diffusion of responsibility and
  non-response to an emergency are the bystander-effect engine's, and no request has been made there.
  Asking begins where somebody addresses a person.
- **The care of somebody who needs it (caregiving, compassion, empathy)**: Sustained care, the
  feeling for another's suffering, and the taking of their perspective are those engines'. The helping
  member reads a discrete favour and its effect on the helper, not a relationship of care.
- **What the helper's yes is worth to them later (gratitude, reciprocity in the owing composite)**:
  Thanks, its expression, and the return that follows are those packages'. The engine ends at the
  granting and at the fact that the asker does not update.
- **Being unable to reach anybody at all (loneliness, ostracism)**: Having no one to ask is those
  engines'. This one models a persona who has somebody and does not ask them.
- **The account required rather than offered (confession)**: Telling under a relation of authority is
  the confession engine's. A question put to a persona there is a demand; a request put by a persona
  here is an ask, and the direction of the standing is reversed.
- **The request as authored symbol (the play's pitch)**: A favour asked in order to **stand for** a
  theme -- the hand extended that mirrors a motif of grace -- is refused: that is authored key, the
  play's pitch. Asking sets what the request cost and produced; the meaning read into it is the play's.

## Encoding

Source to constraint. A process tree: one root, four movements, twelve forms.

- **[asking](process_asking.md)** (the root): the request for discretionary help, read on what was not
  asked for, carrying the twist -- asking feels like taking and lands like giving. Anchored by Flynn &
  Bohns (2008).
- **[the reluctance](process_reluctance_asking.md)**: everything that happens instead of asking.
  Anchored by Nadler & Fisher (1986). [the imposition](process_the_imposition.md),
  [the workaround](process_the_workaround.md), [the hedging](process_the_hedging.md).
- **[the misestimate](process_misestimate_asking.md)**: where the arithmetic goes wrong, and in which
  direction. Anchored by Flynn & Bohns (2008) and Bohns & Roghanizad (2017).
  [the no expected](process_the_no_expected.md), [the ask unmade](process_the_ask_unmade.md),
  [the channel](process_the_channel.md).
- **[the asking](process_act_asking.md)**: the act and its form. Anchored by Langer, Blank &
  Chanowitz (1978). [the request put](process_the_request_put.md),
  [the reason given](process_the_reason_given.md), [the free refusal](process_the_free_refusal.md).
- **[the granting](process_granting_asking.md)**: what a request does to the person who receives it,
  and where the engine is seen for one thing. Anchored by Jecker & Landy (1969) and Blau (1964).
  [the helping](process_the_helping.md),
  [the standing conferred](process_the_standing_conferred.md), [the next ask](process_the_next_ask.md).
