# Instructions: Asking

How a Playwright wires the asking engine into a play. You wire by linking the play's own content to the engine's files; the engine files stay untouched, they are canon. The Roadie plumbs the engine's law into Instructions Knowledge on deploy. Authoring guidance, not runtime content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human decides what each persona needs and is not asking for, and keeps it off the page, because the unmade ask is the engine's largest term and leaves no trace in any scene. Give both sides -- the helper is a position in the engine, not a bystander to it. And keep the helper pleased, since the arrangement is at its most interesting when nobody is imposed upon and the asker still cannot believe it.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, a persona links under Projection where they stand in the request: what they need, what they expect the answer to be, whether they are working around it instead, and what they have been asked for and granted. Read the engine on what was not asked for, never on what was. Keep it the request: the transfer that binds by naming no price is gift's, the obligation it creates is debt's, the engineering of compliance is persuasion's. You link to the engine, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- The transfer that binds by naming no price is the gift engine's; the obligation it creates and its settling are debt's and the owing composite's; the levers by which compliance is engineered are persuasion's and the swaying composite's; non-response to a stranger's emergency is bystander-effect's; sustained care is caregiving's and compassion's; thanks and the return that follows are gratitude's and owing's; having nobody to ask at all is loneliness's and ostracism's; the account required rather than offered is confession's. Whether a production's request was hedged into uselessness, and whether a refusal was genuinely free, are the team's review.

## Knowledge

- Asking is a request for discretionary help put to somebody free to refuse. It is not gift's transfer that binds by naming no price, not debt's obligation, and not persuasion's engineering of compliance -- the direction of interest is a persona trying to get something they need. It wires on a persona at Projection and runs in four movements, the first much the largest. The reluctance is decent rather than neurotic and runs on bad numbers: the imposition is imagined at high resolution from one side of the table, where the interruption is visible and how small it is against the helper's day is not; the workaround feels like competence, usually succeeds, and is therefore never audited against the request that was never priced; the hedging hands the other person an exit, which is what makes a request askable, and discounts it before anybody has looked. The misestimate is the engine's core and is self-sealing: the no expected is a forecast rather than a fear, because the asker reasons from the helper's costs and not from the helper's position; the ask unmade closes on a forecast with no result, so it can never revise the forecast; the channel chosen for the asker's comfort is the one least likely to work, since what makes writing considerate is exactly what makes it declinable at no cost. The asking is mostly form: a request has to name the thing, the size, and the deadline to be grantable, and therefore has to be refusable; a stated reason raises compliance even carrying no information and stops working once the favour is large; the free refusal is a load-bearing fiction and is not equally available, because standing does not switch off for the length of a favour. The granting is the product: the helper is genuinely better off and warmer toward the asker, the standing conferred is the request's real content and is transferred one way -- which is why a persona under evaluation pays for a request that costs a secure persona nothing -- and the next ask starts from the same number. The engine's twist is at the root: asking feels like taking and lands like giving, and the asker cannot see it.

## System

- Do decide what each persona is not asking for before anything else. It never appears in a scene and it sets everything that does.
- Do let the workaround succeed. The persona solves it alone, expensively, and comes away with no evidence that a sentence would have done it.
- Do make the helper pleased and mean it. The person who says it was no trouble at all is reporting accurately, and staging them as put upon replaces the mechanism with an imposition.
- Do not stage the asker as working an angle. That replaces the mechanism with a technique, and the engine is about somebody who needs something, not somebody applying a lever.
- Do put the twist in the discount. The asker thanks the helper for a favour while the helper is quietly pleased to have been the one asked, and neither says the true thing.
- Do let a hedge cost somebody. A serious need wrapped in no worries if not can be declined by a person who would have moved a great deal had they understood the size of it.
- Do make standing decide who pays. A persona whose competence is under evaluation is not in the same engine as one who is secure, and the request costs them differently for the same words.
- Do close on the next ask rather than the favour. The persona has been helped, thanked, and told it was nothing, and approaches the next request from exactly the same number.
- Do not model the gift, the debt owed (debt, owing), compliance technique (persuasion, swaying), the unhelped stranger (bystander-effect), sustained care (caregiving, compassion), thanks (gratitude), or having nobody to ask (loneliness, ostracism). Do not make the request stand for a theme; that is authored key, the play's pitch. Do not edit the engine's files; wire only from the play's side.
