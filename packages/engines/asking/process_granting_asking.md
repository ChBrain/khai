# Process: The Granting

## Taxonomy

[Asking](process_asking.md), what a request does to the person who receives it, and where the engine is seen for one thing.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: asking

## Initiated by

Somebody says yes, and is glad to have been asked.
The Granting starts on the far side of the request: [the helping](process_the_helping.md) and what it does to the helper, [a standing conferred](process_the_standing_conferred.md) by having been the one approached, and [the next ask](process_the_next_ask.md), which is exactly as hard as the last.

## Direction

Toward a person better off for having been asked a favour.
Granting a request reliably produces a good mood, a warmer view of the person who asked, and a sense of having been useful, and the effect is out of proportion to the size of what was actually done.
The helper is not being generous against their own interest. They are being handed something.

## Lever

The reality of what the asker gains against the fact that the helper gains too.
The carrying force is that this is the good news of the engine and should be read that way: the request costs less than the asker fears, it is usually granted, and the person granting it comes out ahead as well, which makes asking one of the few social acts with no obvious loser; the resistance is that the asker can see none of it -- they experience the yes as a debt taken on and the helper's pleasure as good manners -- so the evidence that would correct the reluctance arrives in the one form the asker discounts.
The Granting rides the engine's keystone knife-edge: asking feels like taking and lands like giving.

## Echo

Two people better off and one of them unaware.
It shows the three earlier movements complete -- a reluctance that did most of the work, an estimate wrong in a knowable direction, an act that took a sentence -- and adds what the whole thing was for, which was never the object requested.
This is why the Granting is the hardest of the four to stage and the only one worth closing on: the helper must be genuinely pleased, nothing must be owed, the asker must still not believe it, and the audience must be able to watch two people leave better off while one of them apologises.
A play that stages the helper as put upon has replaced the mechanism with an imposition, and one that stages the asker as working an angle has replaced it with a technique.
The Granting is dated from the yes and does not update the asker.
