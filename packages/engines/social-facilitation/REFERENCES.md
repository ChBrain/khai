# Social Facilitation: Reference

## Line of Work

Social facilitation as **process**: how the presence of others changes a persona's own arousal, effort, and performance -- lifting an easy or well-learned task, spoiling a hard or novel one, and slackening a pooled and unaccountable one. The domain does not model the mass of bodies as an environment, which is crowd's; nor the matching of one's position to others', which is conformity's; nor the group's development and shared pattern over time, which is group's; nor the raw arousal the presence produces, which is body's; nor the anxiety that evaluation recruits, which is apprehension's. It models the effect of others' mere presence on the individual's performance: the lift, the choke, and the slack, and the arousal and accountability that drive them.

The spine is the social psychology of the audience and co-action effects. The presence of others -- as an observing audience or a co-acting partner -- raises a person's arousal, which strengthens the dominant response: on a simple or well-learned task, where the dominant response is correct, performance improves; on a complex or novel task, where it is not, performance suffers. The effect is heightened when the others can evaluate the performer. And it reverses when the individual's contribution is pooled into a group where no single effort can be identified: then accountability diffuses and effort drops, the phenomenon of social loafing. The three forms are those: enhancement, choking, loafing.

## Origin

The social psychology of presence effects on performance, applied directly as process constraints.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **Crowd**: The engine models the presence of others as a force on individual performance, not the mass of bodies as an environment. The massed throng that presses on everyone inside it -- density, growth, discharge -- is owned by the crowd engine; social facilitation works from as little as a single observer or co-actor and lands on the individual's effort and skill, not on the collective.
- **Conformity**: The engine models arousal-and-accountability effects on performance, not the matching of one's position to others'. The pull of the group's standard on what a persona thinks or says is owned by the conformity engine; social facilitation changes how well a persona performs, not what stance they take, and works without any norm to match.
- **Group**: The engine models the immediate effect of others' presence on the individual, not the group's development over time. The forming, storming, and norming of a group's shared pattern is owned by the group engine; social facilitation's loafing form is one member's effort-drop within a group, not the group's own trajectory.
- **Body and Apprehension**: The engine models the presence-to-performance link, not the arousal or the anxiety that mediate it. The raw arousal a persona's body produces is owned by the body engine; the anxiety that the prospect of evaluation stirs is owned by the apprehension engine; social facilitation recruits both -- arousal driving the dominant response, evaluation apprehension sharpening the effect -- but owns the performance consequence, not the arousal or the fear.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per file.

- **[social facilitation](process_social_facilitation.md)** (the root): The effect of others' presence on the individual's performance -- arousal strengthening the dominant response, and accountability driving or diffusing effort. Anchored by Triplett (co-action) and Zajonc (the drive theory).
- **[enhancement](process_enhancement.md)** (form): The lift -- presence sharpening a dominant, well-learned, or simple task, the aroused strong response being the right one. Anchored by Triplett (faster in competition) and Zajonc (facilitation of the dominant response).
- **[choking](process_choking.md)** (form): The spoiling -- presence impairing a non-dominant, complex, or novel task, the aroused strong response being wrong and the scrutiny disrupting the skill. Anchored by Zajonc (impairment of the non-dominant response), Cottrell (evaluation apprehension), and Baumeister (choking under pressure).
- **[loafing](process_loafing.md)** (form): The slackening -- effort dropping when the contribution is pooled and unaccountable in a group, accountability diffused. Anchored by Latané, Williams & Harkins (social loafing and social impact theory).
