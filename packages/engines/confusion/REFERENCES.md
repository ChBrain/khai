# Confusion: Reference

## Line of Work

Confusion as a **multi-type** engine: the knowledge emotion of the not-yet-comprehended, taken across its trigger, its two forks, and the trait that tunes which fork it tends to take. The domain does not model the brief alarm of a violated expectation, which is the surprise engine's; nor the flat, unengaged mind seeking any target, which is the boredom engine's; nor the pull toward the new-yet-graspable, which is the interest engine's; nor the hot response to a blocked goal, which is anger's frustration form. It models the state itself: the cognitive disequilibrium struck when information will not integrate with what is already understood, and what becomes of it -- worked through to a deeper understanding, or abandoned into frustration and disengagement.

The spine is the psychology of confusion as a knowledge emotion. Silvia places confusion alongside interest, surprise, and awe as an emotion about knowledge, elicited when comprehension is appraised as blocked. D'Mello and Graesser give it a mechanism and a dynamics: confusion arises from cognitive disequilibrium -- an impasse where incoming information contradicts, is anomalous, or cannot be assimilated into existing schemas (a lineage running back to Piaget's disequilibrium and equilibration) -- and from that impasse it forks, either driving the effortful sensemaking that resolves it or, unresolved, decaying through frustration into disengagement and boredom. Crucially, D'Mello, Lehman, Pekrun and Graesser show that confusion, when induced and then resolved, is beneficial for learning: the impasse that is worked through deepens understanding past where fluent processing would have left it. The trait that governs which fork is likelier is tolerance of ambiguity and its inverse, the need for cognitive closure (Frenkel-Brunswik; Budner; Kruglanski). The altitudes are those: the emotion, its impasse trigger, its productive and unproductive forks, and its trait.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **Surprise**: The engine models the held state of blocked comprehension, not the brief alarm of a violated expectation. Surprise -- the interrupt when a specific expectation is disconfirmed, and the reorienting sensemaking that follows -- is owned by the surprise engine; confusion is the sustained impasse of not being able to make sense at all, which may follow a surprise but need not, and which persists where surprise is momentary. The two compose: surprise flags the mismatch, confusion holds the failure to resolve it.
- **Boredom**: The engine models the impasse and its decay, not the flat state past the decay. Boredom -- the aversive, unengaged mind whose mobilized attention seeks a target the situation will not supply -- is owned by the boredom engine; confusion's unproductive stall decays toward boredom but is not it, being the effortful, still-engaged wrestling with a block, not the disengaged search for another target. Confusion hands off to boredom at the point the problem is abandoned.
- **Interest**: The engine models the not-yet-comprehended, the other side of interest's coping-potential appraisal. Interest -- the emotion of engagement with the new-yet-graspable -- is owned by the interest engine; where the coping appraisal reads high the same complexity draws interest, where it reads low it draws confusion, and this engine owns that second case. The two are the fork of one appraisal, split into their own domains.
- **Anger (frustration)**: The engine models the confusion that may sour into frustration, not the frustration itself. The hot response to a blocked goal -- the goal-obstruction form of anger -- is owned by the anger engine; confusion's stall passes through frustration on its way to disengagement, but the frustration is anger's, and this engine owns the blocked comprehension that precedes it, not the heat.
- **Problem-solving and reasoning**: The engine models the emotion of the impasse, not the cognition that resolves it. The actual mechanisms of sensemaking, inference, and insight are owned by the reasoning and problem-solving engines where those exist; confusion supplies the felt disequilibrium and the motivation to work it, not the machinery of the working.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per file.

- **[confusion](process_confusion.md)** (the root, process): The knowledge emotion of blocked comprehension -- cognitive disequilibrium when the incoming will not integrate, held until worked or abandoned. Anchored by Silvia (the knowledge emotion) and D'Mello & Graesser (cognitive disequilibrium).
- **[impasse](process_confusion_impasse.md)** (process, trigger): The cognitive disequilibrium itself -- a contradiction, anomaly, or gap the frame cannot assimilate. Anchored by D'Mello & Graesser and Piaget (disequilibrium).
- **[resolve](process_confusion_resolve.md)** (process, productive fork): The impasse held and worked, driving effortful sensemaking that deepens learning when the block breaks. Anchored by D'Mello, Lehman, Pekrun & Graesser (confusion beneficial for learning).
- **[stall](process_confusion_stall.md)** (process, unproductive fork): The impasse unresolved, hardening through frustration into disengagement. Anchored by D'Mello & Graesser (the confusion→frustration→boredom dynamics).
- **[ambiguity tolerance](position_ambiguity.md)** (position, trait): A standing threshold for the unresolved -- from the need for closure that seizes an answer fast to the ease that can sit in an open impasse. Anchored by Frenkel-Brunswik and Budner (ambiguity tolerance) and Kruglanski (need for closure).
