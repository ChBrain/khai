# Process: Climate

## Taxonomy

Macroclimate.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: climate

## Initiated by

The weather of a place, averaged over the decades, settles into a standing regime the place is set in.
Climate starts not with any one day but with the long run of them -- the temperature and moisture a place holds year on year, the pattern its seasons keep, the extreme it tends to -- the regime that was there before this weather and will be there after it.
Every place is set in a climate whether or not it storms today; climate is that standing regime as an actor, the odds behind the weather, not the weather itself.

## Direction

Over the place, over the decades, governing what its weather can be.
Four modes work the standing regime: [Regime](process_regime.md) is the place's baseline type, [Seasonality](process_seasonality.md) its reliable seasonal structure, [Extremity](process_extremity.md) the extreme it is built for, and [Shift](process_shift.md) the slow moving of the regime itself.
A place does not wear its climate as an event but as a setting: the desert is not having a dry day, it is dry as a rule; the tropics are not warm this afternoon, they are warm as a standing fact the whole place is built around.

## Lever

The standing energy balance of the place against nothing that shifts it in the hour.
The carrying force is the long-run drivers -- the latitude, the altitude, the sea and the mountains, the circulation that sets how much sun and rain a place gets across the years; the resistance is not the place's tempering, as with weather, but the sheer inertia of the regime, which no single storm or drought moves.
The climate cannot be sheltered from the way weather can: a place builds for its climate, adapts its life and its walls to the regime, because the regime is not the load of the day but the terms of every day.
And where weather sets what a place gets, climate sets what it can get: the odds within which the flood, the fire, and the drought all fall.

## Echo

The place set in its regime, adapted to it, and stranded when it moves.
Climate does not close; it is the standing frame the place lives inside, governing the weather that falls, the water that runs, the fire that burns, the wearing that works -- the odds every other force plays out within.
It hands the place its terms: the vegetation tuned to the regime, the architecture built for the cold or the heat, the whole life adapted to a climate assumed to hold -- and when the regime shifts, all of that adaptation, set to the old normal, is stranded.
The place is dated from its climate, and read against it: the drought is drier, the flood is worse, the fire is longer, all in the terms the regime sets.
