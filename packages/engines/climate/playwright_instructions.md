# Instructions: Climate

How a Playwright wires the climate engine into a play. You wire by linking the play's
own content to the engine's files; the engine files stay untouched, they are canon. The
Roadie plumbs the engine's law into Instructions Knowledge on deploy. Authoring
guidance, not runtime content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human sets which places are defined by a standing climatic regime -- the type they are set in, the seasons they keep, the extreme they are built for, or the shift moving the regime under them -- and whether that climate is the place's fixed frame or a thing changing in the play.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, a place whose climate is an actor links under Shown the facet of its regime: the standing type it is set in (regime), the reliable seasonal structure it runs (seasonality), the extreme it is built for (extremity), or the slow shift of the regime itself (shift). Play the climate as the standing regime shown -- the arid landscape, the monsoon calendar, the polar hardship, the stranding drift -- present in what the place puts before whoever enters over the long run, never narrated, and never as a mirror of a persona's own mood or temperament. Keep it the long-run regime, what the place expects, not the day's weather (that is weather), the actual water (hydrology), the actual burn (fire), or the actual wearing (erosion). You link to the engine, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- The day's weather is weather's; the actual water and its extremes are hydrology's; the actual burn is fire's; the actual wearing is erosion's. Climate is weather's long-run sibling -- what the place expects against what it gets -- and it governs the odds those forces play out within: it sets whether the place tends wet or dry, its fire season, its freeze-thaw. Whether the mode rings true to the place and its regime is the team's review.

## Knowledge

- Climate is the standing, long-run atmospheric regime a place is set in -- what it expects, against weather's what it gets on the day -- shown in the whole adapted landscape before anyone names it (the weather-climate distinction; the world's climates sorted into the types a place is one of; the regime that has a history and can itself move). It wires on a place at Shown, and it governs which of the place's other forces dominate: it sets the odds the flood, the fire, the drought, and the wearing all fall within. Four modes carry it -- regime is the place's standing type, the arid or humid or polar baseline it is built to; seasonality is the reliable seasonal structure, the monsoon or dry summer or equatorial sameness the year runs; extremity is the extreme the regime imposes, the killing cold or desert heat the place is built for; shift is the regime itself moving, the slow warming or drying that strands everything set to the old normal. Climate sets the terms; the other forces are the weather made extreme within them. Read a place by the facet of its climatic regime.

## System

- Do give a place whose climate is an actor its facet under Shown; do play the climate as the standing regime shown over the long run, present not narrated; do keep the reading the regime -- the type, the seasonal structure, the extreme, the shift -- not a feeling and not a symbol.
- Do read climate as the odds the other forces play out within: the place that is arid as a rule (regime) has its droughts (hydrology) deeper; the place with a long dry season (seasonality) has its fires (fire) longer; and when the regime shifts, everything set to the old normal is stranded.
- Do not model the day's weather (that is weather), the actual water (hydrology), the actual burn (fire), or the actual wearing (erosion). Do not make the climate mirror a persona's standing mood or temperament (that is the play's pitch). Do not edit the engine's files; wire only from the play's side.
