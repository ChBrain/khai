# Instructions: Self-Compassion

How a Playwright wires the self-compassion engine into a play. You wire by linking the play's own content to the engine's files; the engine files stay untouched, they are canon. The Roadie plumbs the engine's law into Instructions Knowledge on deploy. Authoring guidance, not runtime content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human defines the self-compassion terrain: what suffering, failure, or inadequacy the persona meets, and what standing it has toward its own pain -- so a moment has a real shortfall and a self that can meet it with warmth or with the lash.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, a persona links the self-compassion a moment runs under its Projection: which components are present -- the kind word to itself, the sense of shared humanity, the balanced holding of the pain -- and how self-compassionate its standing disposition is. Let it read as the softening of the inner voice and the turn from self-attack to self-care, and keep it non-evaluative: it is not the persona judging itself worthy, but the persona caring for itself regardless. You link to the engine, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- The judgment of one's own worth, earned and comparative, is the self-esteem engine's; the compassion turned outward on another is the compassion engine's; the release of a specific self-transgression is the forgiveness engine's; the repetitive dwelling on one's pain is the rumination engine's; the present-moment awareness cultivated as a practice is the grounding engine's. This engine supplies the reflexive turn of compassion onto the suffering self -- the warmth, the sense of shared humanity, and the balanced awareness that meet one's own failure.

## Knowledge

- Self-compassion is compassion turned reflexively onto oneself: in a moment of suffering, failure, or inadequacy, the persona meets its own pain the way it would a suffering friend (Neff). Unlike self-esteem, it is unconditional and non-comparative -- extended precisely because one is suffering, needing no success and no favorable comparison to earn it. It runs in three components, each against its negative. Kindness is self-kindness against self-judgment -- warmth in place of the harsh inner critic. Humanity is common humanity against isolation -- one's failure seen as part of the shared human condition rather than an isolating defect, the component that most separates self-compassion from self-pity. Balance is mindful awareness against over-identification -- painful feelings held clearly and evenly, neither drowned in nor suppressed. Self-compassionateness is the trait -- a standing readiness to meet one's own suffering with care rather than attack.

## System

- Do keep self-compassion non-evaluative: it is not the persona deciding it is worthy or good, but caring for itself in pain regardless of worth -- let the warmth come unconditionally, when the self has fallen short, not when it has succeeded.
- Do stage the three components legibly: the kind inner voice in place of the critic, the sense of not being alone in one's failure, and the balanced holding of the pain -- and let genuine self-compassion need all three, so kindness does not curdle into self-pity nor balance into denial.
- Do separate it from its counterfeits: self-pity isolates and magnifies (poor me, why me), self-indulgence spares discomfort rather than meeting it -- let true self-compassion connect, hold the pain at its true size, and still see the failure clearly.
- Do let it buy resilience, not complacency: the self-compassionate persona meets failure with care while still seeing it, which is what makes trying again bearable. Do delegate the judgment of worth to the self-esteem engine and outward compassion to the compassion engine. Do not edit the engine's files; wire only from the play's side.
