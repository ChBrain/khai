# Hedonic Adaptation: Reference

## Line of Work

Hedonic adaptation as **process**: the process by which a persona's feeling returns toward a stable baseline after a change in circumstance, good or bad, so that the change stops registering. The domain does not model the present affective state, which is mood's; nor the deliberate turning-toward that prolongs a good moment, which is savoring's; nor the reward-valuation system, which is reward's; nor the tolerance that drives a dependency to need ever more, which is addiction's; nor the resting temperament that sets the baseline's height, which is temperament's. It models the return to baseline itself: the fading of a change's affective punch, and the currents -- weakening response, rising standard, reclaiming setpoint -- that carry it.

The spine is the psychology of the hedonic treadmill and set-point theory. A significant change in circumstances produces a strong affective response that, over time, subsides as the person drifts back toward their prior level of well-being -- the good and the bad alike absorbed. It runs through more than one current: habituation, the automatic desensitizing of the response to a constant; shifting aspiration, the standard rising to meet improved conditions so the gain is judged against a raised bar; and the pull of a dispositional setpoint, a temperamentally anchored baseline the person keeps returning to. The three forms are those: habituation, aspiration, setpoint.

The psychology of hedonic adaptation and subjective well-being, applied directly as process constraints.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **Mood**: The engine models the return of feeling to baseline, not the present affective state. The persona's current mood -- its valence and arousal in the moment -- is owned by the mood engine; hedonic adaptation is the longer process by which a change's effect on that mood decays, and owns the reversion, not the state reverting.
- **Savoring**: The engine models the automatic fade of a good feeling, not the deliberate work to prolong it. The turning-toward that makes a positive moment fuller and last is owned by the savoring engine; savoring is precisely the counter-move to adaptation -- re-noticing what habituation would dull -- and hedonic adaptation owns the fade that savoring resists, not the resistance.
- **Reward and Addiction**: The engine models well-being's return to baseline, not the reward system or the dependency loop. The valuation of a reward is owned by the reward engine; the tolerance by which a dependency comes to need ever more for the same effect is owned by the addiction engine; hedonic adaptation shares their family of diminishing returns but owns the general drift of well-being back to baseline, not the reward computation or the addictive spiral.
- **Temperament**: The engine models the return to a baseline, not the temperament that sets the baseline's height. The persona's resting dispositional makeup is owned by the temperament engine; hedonic adaptation takes the setpoint as the level it homes to, and owns the homing, not the disposition that fixes where home is.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per file.

- **[hedonic adaptation](process_hedonic_adaptation.md)** (the root): The return of feeling to baseline -- a change's affective punch fading with time as the persona drifts back to their prior well-being. Anchored by Brickman & Campbell (the hedonic treadmill) and Brickman, Coates & Janoff-Bulman (adaptation to extreme change).
- **[habituation](process_habituation.md)** (form): The response route -- the affective reaction to a constant or repeated circumstance weakening with exposure, desensitized to what no longer surprises. Anchored by Frederick & Loewenstein (desensitization).
- **[aspiration](process_aspiration.md)** (form): The standard route -- the reference point rising to meet an improved circumstance, so each gain is judged against a raised bar and swallowed, the treadmill. Anchored by Brickman & Campbell (adaptation-level theory) and Frederick & Loewenstein (shifting standards).
- **[setpoint](process_setpoint.md)** (form): The baseline route -- the pull of a stable, dispositional level of well-being the persona keeps returning to, set largely by temperament. Anchored by Lykken & Tellegen (the happiness set-point) and Diener, Lucas & Scollon (the revised, movable setpoint).
