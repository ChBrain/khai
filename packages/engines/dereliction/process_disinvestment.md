# Process: Disinvestment

## Taxonomy

[Dereliction](process_dereliction.md), the form this withdrawal takes.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: dereliction

## Initiated by

Capital and authority withdraw from a whole area at once, as a class.
Disinvestment starts not with one owner's neglect but with a coordinated withdrawal -- the bank that redlines the district, the city that thins its services, the policy of planned shrinkage -- so that many places decline together, not by their own fault but by their address.
It is dereliction made systematic: the withdrawal of care from a zone rather than a building.

## Direction

Onto the Offers of every place in the zone at once.
The mortgage that will not be written, the shop that closes, the bus route cut, the school underfunded, the insurance withdrawn -- the whole district's offers thin together, and each place is dragged down by the decline of the places around it as much as by any lapse of its own.
Disinvestment moves at the scale of the map, not the lot.

## Lever

The return the place offers capital against the return capital can find elsewhere.
The carrying force is capital's flight to better yield -- money leaving a place it has decided will not pay; the resistance is whatever would hold or return investment, a subsidy, a mandate, a belief in the place.
As capital flees, the gap widens between what the place earns now, depressed, and what it could earn if reinvested -- the rent-gap -- and disinvestment is the decline side of that gap, digging it deeper the longer it runs.

## Echo

The declined district, and the rent-gap widening under it.
It shows the boarded strip, the vacant lots, the thinned services of a whole area, and carries beneath it the coiled potential that the disinvestment itself creates: the wider the gap, the greater the profit waiting for whoever re-enters.
Disinvestment is dated from the withdrawal of the class of capital, and it is the seam that hands the widened rent-gap to gentrification -- which re-enters, and displaces, precisely when the gap has grown wide enough to pay.
