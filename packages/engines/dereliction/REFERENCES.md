# Dereliction: Reference

## Line of Work

Dereliction as **process**: the decline a place undergoes when the authority
responsible for it withdraws its care, borne as the place's own offers lapse. The
domain does not model the material entropy the withdrawal unleashes, which is the
decay engine's; nor the biological reclaiming that follows once human use
withdraws, which is the succession engine's; nor the capital re-entry that later
profits from the depressed value, which is the gentrification engine's; nor the
deliberate arrest, which is the restoration engine's. It models the human act at
the root of decline -- the keeping that stops -- and the forms that withdrawal
takes, from a deferred repair to a district written off to a place given fully
over.

The spine is the recognition that decline is done, not merely suffered: a place
falls because someone with the power to keep it chooses not to, and the ruin that
results is a produced condition, not a natural fate. Beauregard read urban decline
as a discourse and an outcome of decisions -- disinvestment and abandonment as
things enacted upon a place. And where the decline runs to its end, Edensor found
that the ruin is not absence but excess: the given-over place, freed from its
ordering, comes into a second and disordered life, thick with the memory of its
work and open to alternative use. So the engine gives the withdrawal four forms --
disrepair still held, abandonment departed, disinvestment of the whole zone -- and
keeps a fourth, ruin, that is dereliction's own age-value, the sibling of decay's
patina: the given-over read as excess and not only loss.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The material entropy (decay)**: Dereliction is the social withdrawal; decay is
  the material process it unleashes. The rust, the rot, the crumble in the fabric
  are the decay engine's; dereliction is the keeper's ceasing to hold them off. A
  maintained place still decays, slowly; a derelict one decays fast because the
  care that arrested it is gone. Dereliction is a choice; decay is what matter does
  once the choice is made.
- **The biological reclaiming (succession)**: The withdrawal opens the place; the
  green that then takes it -- the weeds in the lot, the trees in the floor, the
  wild returning once human use ends -- is the succession engine's. Dereliction is
  the human leaving; succession is the non-human arriving in the space it leaves.
- **The capital re-entry (gentrification)**: Dereliction is the decline side of the
  rent-gap; the reinvestment that later closes it, re-entering the disinvested place
  and displacing whoever remained, is the gentrification engine's. Disinvestment
  digs the gap; gentrification profits from it. The two are the down and up of the
  same curve, and dereliction hands the widened gap across.
- **The intervention that arrests it (restoration)**: The deliberate act against
  the decline -- the repair, the reoccupation, the conservation of the ruin -- is
  the restoration engine's; dereliction is the fall restoration reverses or keeps.
  Ruin is where they meet: whether the second life of the given-over place is a
  thing to clear or a thing to preserve is restoration's choice.
- **A persona's feeling, and the ruin as authored symbol**: How the decline makes
  a persona feel -- the desolation, the unease, the romance of the ruin -- is the
  persona's own engines'; dereliction delivers the withdrawal, not the feeling. And
  the derelict place made to **stand for** a character's ruin, the abandoned house
  that mirrors the fallen man, is refused: that is authored key, the play's pitch,
  not the place's force.
- **The form itself is not engine content**: The engine declares the four forms
  exist; which one a given place undergoes lives on the place, linked under its
  Offers chapter. The engine refuses to fix a place's dereliction for it.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per file.

- **[dereliction](process_dereliction.md)** (the root): the withdrawal of care by
  the authority responsible for a place, the keeping that stops and lets the offers
  lapse. Anchored by Beauregard (decline as produced, not suffered) and Ginsberg
  (the ruin as a transformed form).
- **[disrepair](process_disrepair.md)**: care reduced while the place is still
  held, the deferred repair and the offers degrading on a slope. The reversible
  early phase.
- **[abandonment](process_abandonment.md)**: care ended and the place left, the
  offers withdrawn wholesale, the vacancy that is a cliff and not a slope.
- **[disinvestment](process_disinvestment.md)**: care pulled from a whole area at
  once, redlining and planned shrinkage, the district declined together and the
  rent-gap opened. The seam to gentrification.
- **[ruin](process_ruin.md)**: the place given fully over, the sanctioned offers
  gone and unsanctioned ones opened, the second disordered life Edensor prized as
  excess -- dereliction's own age-value. Anchored by Edensor; the seam to succession
  and restoration.
