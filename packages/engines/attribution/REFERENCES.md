# Attribution: Reference

## Line of Work

Attribution as **process**: how a persona assigns a cause to an outcome -- the answer to "why did that happen?" -- and how the placement of that cause sets the emotion and the expectation that follow. The domain does not model the systematic errors in the reading -- the tilt to over-blame the person, to spare the self, to see hostility in the ambiguous -- which are the bias engine's; nor the persona's generalized sense of where control lies, which is the locus-of-control engine's. It models the causal-assignment process itself: the reach behind an outcome for its cause, placed along locus and stability, and what that placement does.

The spine is the finding that a cause is read on two axes -- its locus (in the actor or in the world) and its stability (lasting or passing), with a third, controllability, cutting across -- and that the corner it lands in drives the sequel. A failure read as a stable inner lack sinks toward shame and hopelessness; the same failure read as too-little-effort turns to guilt with room to try again; a loss read as bad luck keeps hope. The four readings are the corners of locus by stability -- disposition, effort, circumstance, chance -- each carrying its own feeling and its own forecast.

## Origin

The social psychology of causal explanation, applied directly as process constraints.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **Bias**: The engine models the causal-assignment process, not the systematic slant on it. The fundamental attribution error, the self-serving bias, the actor-observer and defensive, hostile, group, and ultimate attribution errors are owned by the bias engine as resting tilts; this engine governs the placement itself, and routes the overreach to bias -- exactly as the decision and memory engines sit beside their own biases.
- **Locus of control**: The engine models the cause assigned to a particular outcome, not the generalized expectancy about where control lies. Whether a persona broadly believes outcomes are theirs to determine or the world's to hand out is owned by the locus-of-control engine; this engine governs the reading of a single event.
- **Pride, Shame, Guilt, Gratitude**: The engine models the causal reading that triggers a feeling, not the feeling itself. The pride of a success owned, the shame of a fixed lack exposed, the guilt of the undone, the gratitude for a good turn are owned by their own engines; this engine governs where the cause is placed, which is what sets them off.
- **Mindset and Self-efficacy**: The engine models the reading of a specific outcome's cause, not the held theory of ability behind it. Whether ability is believed fixed or growable is the mindset engine's, and the task-specific belief in one's own capability is the self-efficacy engine's; this engine governs the post-hoc account, not the standing belief that colours it.
- **Decision**: The engine models the explanation of an outcome already arrived, not the resolution of a choice still open. How a persona selects between future options is the decision engine's; this engine looks backward at cause, not forward at choice.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per file.

- **[attribution](process_attribution.md)** (the root): A cause is assigned to an outcome along locus and stability, and where it lands sets the emotion and the expectancy. Anchored by Heider (the naive causal analysis), Kelley (covariation as the method), and Weiner (the dimensions and their consequences).
- **[disposition](process_disposition.md)** (reading): The cause placed in the actor's enduring nature -- internal and stable; the leap from an act to a standing trait, and the closed future a fixed inner lack forecasts. Anchored by Jones & Davis (correspondent inference) and Heider.
- **[effort](process_effort.md)** (reading): The cause placed in the actor's trying -- internal, unstable, controllable; the corner that carries responsibility and keeps the future open. Anchored by Weiner (controllability and its emotions) and Abramson, Seligman & Teasdale (the unstable-internal escape from helplessness).
- **[circumstance](process_circumstance.md)** (reading): The cause placed in the standing situation -- external and stable; the wall that clears the actor without crediting them and breeds resignation. Anchored by Heider (impersonal causality) and Weiner (task difficulty as external-stable).
- **[chance](process_chance.md)** (reading): The cause placed in passing luck -- external and unstable; the wind that neither credits nor condemns and leaves the odds resettable. Anchored by Weiner (luck as external-unstable) and Abramson, Seligman & Teasdale (the external reading that spares helplessness).
