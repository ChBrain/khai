# Process: The Repetition

## Taxonomy

[Warning](process_warning.md), what a run of signals does to the signal.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: warning

## Initiated by

The alarm goes off again, and this is the ninth time this week.
The Repetition starts once warnings become routine: [habituation](process_the_habituation.md), where the signal stops being heard; [muting](process_the_muting.md), where competent people switch it off for good reasons; and [recalibration](process_the_recalibration.md), where the receiver revises the odds downward and is correct to.

## Direction

Toward a signal that has been priced by its own history.
Nobody decides to stop taking warnings seriously. What happens is that each alert is evaluated against the ones before it, most of those came to nothing, and the estimate moves accordingly -- which is learning rather than negligence.
The system is then in the state it was built to prevent, and every step to it was reasonable.

## Lever

The correctness of discounting against what discounting costs on the one occasion.
The carrying force is that the receivers are right: if ninety-five percent of the alarms on this ward are artefacts then treating an alarm as ninety-five percent likely to be an artefact is accurate, and a nurse who responded to every one at full urgency would be unable to do their job at all; the resistance is that the discount is applied uniformly and the events are not, so the correct general policy is exactly wrong on the case it exists for, and no amount of individual diligence fixes a base rate.
The Repetition converts a safety system into a background noise, accurately.

## Echo

A signal that everybody hears and nobody acts on.
It is the movement that explains why adding more alerts makes systems less safe.
The Repetition is dated from the second false alarm and compounds from there.
