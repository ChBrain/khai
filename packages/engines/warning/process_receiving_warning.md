# Process: The Receiving

## Taxonomy

[Warning](process_warning.md), what a warned persona actually does.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: warning

## Initiated by

The signal arrives, and almost nobody moves.
The Receiving starts at the gap between what warning systems are designed for and what people do: [milling](process_the_milling.md) about, checking with others; [reading the room](process_the_normalcy_read.md) for confirmation that this is real; and [personalising](process_the_personalising.md), which is the step everything actually turns on.

## Direction

Toward a delay that looks like denial and is something else.
The reliable finding across decades of field research is the opposite of the popular picture: warned populations do not stampede. They seek confirmation, they call somebody, they look outside, they finish what they were doing, and they act late.
Panic is rare enough that the warning literature treats it as a myth, and the actual problem is under-response.

## Lever

The rationality of checking against the cost of the time it takes.
The carrying force is that confirmation-seeking is sensible and largely correct behaviour: most alerts a persona receives are not for them, checking is cheap, acting on every signal would be unliveable, and the people who check are usually right that nothing is happening; the resistance is that the checking takes exactly the interval the warning was issued to buy, so a system designed to give people an hour delivers about ten minutes of it, and the shortfall is invisible until the one occasion it matters.
The Receiving spends the warning's lead time on deciding whether the warning is real.

## Echo

A population informed and not yet moving.
It is the movement most warning systems are designed as though it did not exist.
The Receiving is dated from the alert and runs until somebody nearby moves first.
