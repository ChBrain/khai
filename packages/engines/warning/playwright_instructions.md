# Instructions: Warning

How a Playwright wires the warning engine into a play. You wire by linking the play's own content to the engine's files; the engine files stay untouched, they are canon. The Roadie plumbs the engine's law into Instructions Knowledge on deploy. Authoring guidance, not runtime content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human decides whether the harm was actually coming, keeps it off the page, and never resolves it on stage, because the engine runs on the fact that nobody in it can tell. Give both sides -- the warner is a position in the engine, not a bystander to it. And keep everybody reasoning well: the threshold was set responsibly, the receivers discounted on good evidence, and the system degrades anyway.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, a persona links under Projection where they stand to the signal: whether they issue or receive it, what the threshold cost them, whether they are still checking, whether they have placed themselves inside the alert, and what the last one left behind. Read the engine on what happened next, which is the one thing a warning makes unreadable. Keep it the signal: the hazard and its size are risk's, the response to a threat in the room is fear's, the design of the instrument is usability's. You link to the engine, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- The hazard, its size, and how it is estimated are the risk engine's; the response to a present threat and the error-management asymmetry are fear's; dread with no object is apprehension's; whether a source is believed at all is trust's; the instrument's design is usability's and ergonomics's; the forecast as a number is measure's; the mass of bodies and the spread through a population are crowd's, contagion's, and tipping-point's; non-response to an emergency already under way is bystander-effect's. Response, rescue, and recovery are operational and are not modelled here. Whether a production's alert was discounted correctly, and whether the harm was averted or was never coming, are the team's review -- and the second is a judgement nobody can actually make, which is the engine's point.

## Knowledge

- Warning is a harm signalled before it arrives, to somebody who would have to act on it. It is not risk's hazard and its estimation, not fear's response to a present threat, and not usability's design of the instrument. It wires on a persona at Projection and runs in four movements. The issuing is a decision about which error to make: a threshold set low catches nearly everything and is wrong most times it fires, set high it is right when it fires and misses the early ones, and the trade cannot be engineered away -- so the care that makes a system safe is what makes it cry wolf; the confidence stated is honest when sent and binary when it lands, because the receiver is deciding whether to move rather than running a calculation; and the liability is asymmetric, since a miss produces an inquiry with a name and a false alarm produces an invoice spread across thousands, which makes over-warning structural rather than nervous. The receiving is the movement systems are designed as though it did not exist: warned populations do not stampede, they mill -- ringing somebody, checking a second source, watching the street -- so everybody waits on evidence the others are also waiting to produce; the normalcy read supplies continuous high-resolution evidence against the warning and is correct on almost every occasion and worthless on the ones that matter; and the personalising decides the outcome, predicting action better than belief, comprehension, or trust. The repetition prices the signal by its own history and nobody in it is behaving badly: habituation works on frequency rather than meaning, muting is done by competent people for defensible reasons and is indefensible only in aggregate, and recalibration is a correct update that guarantees the wrong action once. The aftermath settles credibility on evidence the warning destroyed: the averted harm is the best outcome and the weakest exhibit, the blame corrects one error by amplifying the other, and the next warning is heard at a volume set by events that had nothing to do with it. The engine's twist is at the root: a warning that works looks exactly like one that was wrong, so the only warnings that unambiguously earn credibility are the ones nobody acted on.

## System

- Do decide whether the harm was coming, keep it secret, and never settle it. If the play answers the question, the engine stops.
- Do make the threshold a choice somebody defends. It was set carefully, the reasoning was sound, and it accepted a rate of being wrong as arithmetic.
- Do stage the milling. People ring somebody, check a second source, and look out of the window, and the lead time goes on that -- which is the finding, not a failing.
- Do not stage a warned crowd as panicking. Decades of field research say they do not, and it is the best-evidenced claim in the domain.
- Do put the outcome in the personalising. A persona can accept every word of the alert and do nothing, because they have not placed themselves inside it -- and a named street moves people that a named region does not.
- Do let a competent person mute the alarm. They have good reasons, they are usually the most experienced one there, and the decision is defensible on the day and damning in a list.
- Do make the discounting correct. Forty alerts and no incidents genuinely does mean an alert is worth less, and a persona who did not update would be failing to learn.
- Do close on the averted rather than the disaster. A cost was paid, nothing happened, nobody can prove why, and somebody who was right is being asked to justify themselves.
- Do not stage the warner as vindicated or the doubters as fools. The first replaces the mechanism with a prophecy and the second with a morality.
- Do not model the hazard (risk), the threat response (fear, apprehension), belief in the source (trust), the interface (usability, ergonomics), the forecast as a figure (measure), the crowd (crowd, contagion), or the emergency under way (bystander-effect). Do not make the warning stand for a theme; that is authored key, the play's pitch. Do not edit the engine's files; wire only from the play's side.
