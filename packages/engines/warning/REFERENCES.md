# Warning: Reference

## Line of Work

Warning as **process**: a harm signalled before it arrives, to somebody who would have to act on it.

The engine's subject is a signal that cannot be evaluated. Every warning is issued before the fact is
settled, so it is always possible nothing was coming -- and the better the system, the more often that
will be exactly how it looks. Breznitz's work is the spine: a warning followed by no harm degrades the
response to the next one, and it does so whether the first warning was mistaken or whether it worked.
That is the whole difficulty compressed into a sentence, and everything here follows from it.

The boundary with what khai already carries is clean. The hazard itself -- its size, how it is
estimated, and why the estimate cannot correct itself -- is **risk**'s. The persona's response to a
threat in the room, including the error-management asymmetry that makes a smoke detector worth its
false alarms, is **fear**'s; that engine reads the organism, this one reads the signal and the
arrangement around it. Anticipatory dread with no object is **apprehension**'s. Whether a source is
believed at all is **trust**'s. The design of the instrument -- where the light goes, how loud, what
it says -- is **usability**'s and **ergonomics**'s.

**The issuing** is a decision about which error to make. A **threshold set** low catches nearly
everything and is wrong most times it fires; set high it is right when it fires and misses the early
ones, and the trade is a property of deciding under uncertainty rather than a defect anybody can
engineer out. So the care that makes a system safe is precisely what makes it cry wolf. The
**confidence stated** is honest when sent and binary when it lands, because the receiver is not running
a probability calculation, they are deciding whether to move. And the **liability** falls unevenly: a
miss produces an inquiry with a name attached, a false alarm produces an invoice spread across
thousands, so over-warning is structural rather than a failure of nerve.

**The receiving** is the movement most warning systems are designed as though it did not exist, and
the field research on it is the strongest evidence the engine has. Warned populations do not stampede.
They **mill** -- ring somebody, check a second source, look at what the street is doing -- and because
everybody is checking at once, each is waiting on evidence the others are also waiting to produce. The
**normalcy read** supplies continuous, high-resolution evidence against the warning, and it is correct
on almost every occasion and worthless on the ones that matter. And the **personalising** is what the
outcome actually turns on: across decades of studies it predicts protective action better than belief
in the warning, comprehension of it, or trust in its source. A persona can accept every word and do
nothing, because they have not placed themselves inside it.

**The repetition** prices the signal by its own history, and nobody in it is behaving badly.
**Habituation** operates on frequency rather than meaning, so it cannot tell the ninth false alarm from
the tenth true one. **Muting** is done by competent people for defensible reasons -- the alarms really
are mostly artefacts and the noise really does degrade attention -- and it is defensible one at a time
and indefensible in a list. **Recalibration** is the sharpest form: a receiver who has seen forty
alerts and no incidents now holds that an alert means little, which is a correct update on the
evidence, in the right direction, of roughly the right size -- and it is the belief that will produce
inaction on the occasion the system exists for.

**The aftermath** settles credibility using evidence the warning destroyed. If the warning failed, the
disaster is there to be examined and everybody agrees it should have been louder. If it worked, there
is a cost, a lost day, and nothing to examine. The **averted** harm is the engine's best outcome and
its weakest exhibit, since the case for it rests on a model of what would otherwise have happened and
a model is exactly what the sceptic disputes. The **blame** corrects one error by amplifying the other,
because only misses get inquiries and fatigue has no victim with a name. And the **next warning** goes
out at a volume set by events that had nothing to do with it.

Hence the **twist**, at the root. **A warning that works looks exactly like one that was wrong.** The
averted disaster and the false alarm produce the same visible world -- a disruption, a cost, no
catastrophe -- so a system that warns well accumulates a record indistinguishable from one that cries
wolf, and the receivers discount it correctly on the evidence available to them. The only warnings
that unambiguously earn credibility are the ones nobody acted on.

The engine is built so that nobody has to be foolish or negligent for any of it to run, and a play
should keep it that way. The warner set the threshold responsibly. The receivers discounted on good
evidence. The system degrades anyway.

**On the evidence, stated plainly.** The legs here are unusually mismatched and the reader should know
which is which. **The warning-response field research is the strongest thing on this page, and its
main finding contradicts the popular picture.** Decades of studies of real evacuations, sirens, and
public alerts -- Mileti and Sorensen's synthesis above all -- find that people do not panic, that
milling and confirmation-seeking are near-universal, and that the operative problem is delay and
under-response rather than stampede. Panic is treated in that literature as close to a myth, and a play
that stages a warned crowd as hysterical is contradicting the best-evidenced claim in the domain.
Personalisation's primacy is likewise a robust and repeatedly replicated result. **The alarm-fatigue
evidence is real and quantitative**: clinical studies consistently find that the large majority of
physiological monitor alarms are non-actionable, with reported figures running from the high seventies
into the nineties depending on unit and definition, and disabling and delayed response are documented
rather than inferred. The exact percentages vary enough between studies that the engine states the
pattern and not a number. **Breznitz's own experimental work is the weak leg.** _Cry Wolf_ is a set of
small laboratory studies from the 1980s, the false-alarm effect it reports is intuitive and
under-replicated at that level, and the engine leans on the field and clinical evidence rather than on
his experiments -- while taking his framing, which no later work has bettered. **The twist is an
argument, not a measurement, and it cannot be measured for exactly the reason it states.** Nobody has
cleanly compared credibility lost to a successful warning against credibility lost to a false alarm,
because from the outside the two are the same event; the preparedness paradox is widely observed and
structurally unfalsifiable, and it should be read as the same kind of claim as its neighbours' twists
rather than as a finding. **Geoffrey Rose's prevention paradox is a different argument** -- that a
measure bringing large benefit to a population offers little to each individual -- and is used here
only by analogy, not as support. What should not be taken from this engine is that warnings do not
work, that people are irrational, or that alarm fatigue is a discipline problem. The claim is narrow:
the threshold decides which error, the lead time is spent deciding whether the alert is real, the
discount is correct, and success leaves no record.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The hazard itself (risk)**: What the danger is, how large, how estimated, and why an estimate
  cannot correct itself are the risk engine's. This engine takes the hazard as given and reads the
  signal about it.
- **The response to a threat in the room (fear, apprehension)**: The organism's reaction to present
  danger, including the error-management asymmetry that makes a jumpy detector adaptive, is fear's;
  anticipatory dread with no object is apprehension's. The warning engine reads a message about a
  future state and the arrangement that produced it.
- **Whether the source is believed (trust, credibility)**: Why a persona extends or withholds
  confidence in a source is the trust engine's. This engine reads what a run of warnings does to a
  credibility that already exists.
- **The design of the instrument (usability, ergonomics, attention)**: Where the light goes, how loud,
  what the label says, and what recruits attention are those engines'. The engine reads the threshold
  and the reception, not the interface.
- **The forecast as a number (measure, legibility)**: Turning a judgement into a figure, and the
  gaming of an indicator, are those engines'. The confidence stated member reads only what happens to a
  probability between sending and arriving.
- **The crowd as an environment (crowd, contagion, tipping-point)**: The mass of bodies, the spread of
  a state through a population, and the cascade past a threshold are those engines'. The milling member
  reads confirmation-seeking among individuals, which is the opposite mechanism from contagion.
- **Nobody moving when a stranger collapses (bystander-effect)**: Diffusion of responsibility in an
  emergency already under way is that engine's. The receiving movement reads a population deciding
  whether an emergency exists at all.
- **The disaster once it arrives (the world)**: Response, rescue, and recovery are operational matters
  with their own literature. The engine ends where the harm begins.
- **The warning as authored symbol (the play's pitch)**: An alert made to **stand for** a theme -- the
  unheeded prophet who mirrors a motif of blindness -- is refused: that is authored key, the play's
  pitch. Warning sets what the signal cost and bought; the meaning read into it is the play's.

## Encoding

Source to constraint. A process tree: one root, four movements, twelve forms.

- **[warning](process_warning.md)** (the root): a harm signalled before it arrives, read on what
  happened next, carrying the twist -- a warning that works looks exactly like one that was wrong.
  Anchored by Breznitz (1984).
- **[the issuing](process_issuing_warning.md)**: where the signal is decided. Anchored by Perrow (1984)
  and the threshold literature. [the threshold set](process_the_threshold_set.md),
  [the confidence stated](process_the_confidence_stated.md), [the liability](process_the_liability.md).
- **[the receiving](process_receiving_warning.md)**: what a warned persona actually does. Anchored by
  Mileti & Sorensen (1990) and Lindell & Perry (2012). [the milling](process_the_milling.md),
  [the normalcy read](process_the_normalcy_read.md), [the personalising](process_the_personalising.md).
- **[the repetition](process_repetition_warning.md)**: what a run of signals does to the signal.
  Anchored by Breznitz (1984) and Sendelbach & Funk (2013).
  [the habituation](process_the_habituation.md), [the muting](process_the_muting.md),
  [the recalibration](process_the_recalibration.md).
- **[the aftermath](process_aftermath_warning.md)**: what happens once it is over, and where the engine
  is seen for one thing. Anchored by Weick & Sutcliffe (2001) and Rose (1985).
  [the averted](process_the_averted.md), [the blame](process_the_blame.md),
  [the next warning](process_the_next_warning.md).
