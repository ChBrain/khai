# Process: The Aftermath

## Taxonomy

[Warning](process_warning.md), what happens once it is over, and where the engine is seen for one thing.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: warning

## Initiated by

The day passes, and either something happened or it did not.
The Aftermath starts at the accounting: [the averted](process_the_averted.md), a harm that did not occur and therefore cannot be counted; [the blame](process_the_blame.md), which falls on one of the two errors and not the other; and [the next warning](process_the_next_warning.md), which is issued into whatever this one left behind.

## Direction

Toward a verdict reached on evidence the warning destroyed.
If the warning failed, the disaster is there to be examined in detail and everybody agrees the warning should have been louder. If the warning worked, there is nothing to examine: a cost was incurred, a day was lost, and the thing it bought never appeared.
So the two outcomes are not judged by the same procedure, and only one of them leaves anything behind.

## Lever

The reality of what was prevented against the impossibility of showing it.
The carrying force is that prevention is real and enormous, and the people doing it know it: the evacuation did save the town, the shutdown did stop the outbreak, the scan did catch it early, and none of that becomes false for being unprovable; the resistance is that the proof would have to be a counterfactual, so the successful warner is asked to justify a cost against a benefit that by construction has no evidence, and the person who did nothing and was lucky is holding a stronger-looking hand.
The Aftermath rewards being lucky and punishes being early.

## Echo

A credibility settled by an outcome that cannot say what caused it.
It shows the three earlier movements complete -- a threshold that accepted an error, a population that spent the lead time checking, a signal already discounted by its history -- and adds the thing that makes the whole arrangement degrade over time, which is that success is unrecordable.
The engine's twist is here: **a warning that works looks exactly like one that was wrong.** The averted disaster and the false alarm produce the same visible world -- a disruption, a cost, and no catastrophe -- so a system that warns well accumulates a record indistinguishable from a system that cries wolf, and the receivers discount it correctly on the evidence they have. The only warnings that unambiguously earn credibility are the ones nobody acted on.
This is why the Aftermath is the hardest of the four to stage and the only one worth closing on: the warner must have been right, the cost must have been real, nobody must be able to prove anything, and the audience must be able to see a correct decision being punished by people who are also reasoning correctly.
A play that stages the warner as vindicated has replaced the mechanism with a prophecy, and one that stages the doubters as fools has replaced it with a morality.
The Aftermath is dated from the all-clear and is priced into the next alert.
