# Position: Regime

## Taxonomy

Polity (no khai file of its own yet).

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: regime

## Has

The standing arrangement by which a territory is ruled.
Not what any persona wants, and not what any persona does: the settled answer to who may decide, on what warrant, and under what constraint -- the thing that is already true when the first scene opens and that no single move within it changes.
It is read on six axes, each a different question asked of the same arrangement: by the [count](position_count_regime.md), how many rule and for whom; by the [claim](position_claim_regime.md), what makes the command legitimate; by the [constraint](position_constraint_regime.md), how much the ruler can be told no; by the [holder](position_holder_regime.md), what kind of body actually holds an autocracy; by the [architecture](position_architecture_regime.md), how the offices are wired to each other; and by the [substitute](position_substitute_regime.md), what rules where none of the named forms does.
A polity has a reading on every axis at once, and the axes disagree on purpose -- a regime can be a republic by its count, legal-rational by its claim, electoral-authoritarian by its constraint, and personalist by its holder, and each of those is telling the truth about something different.

## Orders

Place the board before reading the play.
Every persona in a scene is acting inside an arrangement that decides what a move is worth, and the same act -- a petition, a resignation, a leak, a strike -- costs differently and buys differently under each type.
So the regime is fixed first, on all six axes, and the conduct is then read against it; a persona's courage or caution is not a fact about their character until it is priced by the thing they are risking it against.

## Loses

The ability to know what its own subjects think.
Every regime buys compliance by making some things unsayable, and the more completely it succeeds the less its public speech carries information -- support and prudence become indistinguishable from outside and, after long enough, from inside.
So the regimes with the most power over what may be said have the worst knowledge of what is true: they cannot tell a loyal official from a frightened one, cannot tell whether an announced policy is working, and cannot see the collapse coming, because the same apparatus that keeps opposition quiet keeps the news of it quiet too.
This is the twist the whole catalogue turns on -- the loss is not a failure of a bad regime but a cost of the instrument itself, and it scales with how well the instrument works.

## Drives

On the loss of knowing what is thought:
buys back a channel -- a tolerated press, a real election, a complaints bureau, a survey it does not control -- and accepts that a channel which reports honestly can also be used against it,
or closes the last of them, and rules with confidence over a population whose actual position it will not learn until the day it is shown.
