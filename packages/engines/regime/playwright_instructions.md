# Instructions: Regime

How a Playwright wires the regime engine into a play. You wire by linking the play's own content to the engine's files; the engine files stay untouched, they are canon. The Roadie plumbs the engine's law into Instructions Knowledge on deploy. Authoring guidance, not runtime content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human sets the board: what kind of polity the play happens in, on the axes the play actually turns on. Start with the constraint axis, since that decides which political moves exist at all -- whether the rulers can be removed, whether an election is real, whether opposition is legal. Add the holder axis if the play turns on how an autocracy ends, and the architecture axis if it turns on a procedural fight. Remember that the regime is a fact about the situation, not about any persona in it, and that no persona in the play can change it by themselves.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, a persona links under Projection the regime they live under: ordinarily one type from the constraint axis, plus whichever other axes the play needs. Read the axes as crossing, not competing -- a federal presidential electoral democracy is one polity described three ways, not three regimes, and the disagreement between two axes is usually the most informative thing on the board. Keep it the arrangement, not the conduct: agenda control, coalition-building, stalling, and the veto point are the politics engine's. You link to the engine, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- Political conduct is the politics engine's -- regime is the board, politics is the play on it. Where a persona's ability to move another comes from is power's; exit, voice, and loyalty under a declining state are loyalty's; a chain of command inside an organisation is hierarchy's and org's; a total environment run on its inmates is total-institution's; a common-pool resource and its keeping are commons' and upkeep's; what being ruled makes of a persona is subjection's. Whether a production's polity is genuinely an electoral democracy or a competitive authoritarianism is a judgement the real literature makes with difficulty and disagreement, and the team makes it too -- the suite does not.

## Knowledge

- Regime is the standing arrangement by which a persona's polity is ruled: who may decide, on what warrant, under what constraint. It wires on a persona at Projection, and it is the board rather than the play. Six axes ask different questions of the same arrangement. The count is Aristotle's -- rule by one, few, or many, each in a true form serving the ruled and a deviant form serving the rulers, whose weakness is that the pairs cannot be told apart from outside. The claim is Weber's -- traditional, charismatic, legal-rational, sorting by why the command is obeyed. The constraint is the modern spine -- liberal democracy, electoral democracy, competitive authoritarian, electoral autocracy, closed autocracy, with totalitarianism as a distinct kind rather than an extreme degree. The holder says which body actually holds an autocracy -- a person, an army, a party, a family -- which predicts how it ends better than how repressive it is. The architecture wires the offices -- parliamentary or presidential, unitary or federal, majoritarian or consensual. The substitute collects what rules where none of the named forms does. Each type is read for what it has, what it orders, and the one thing it structurally loses, which is where a play's pressure usually belongs. The engine's twist is at the root: every regime buys compliance by making some things unsayable, so the more power it has over what may be said the less it can learn what is true, and the loss scales with how well the instrument works.

## System

- Do give every polity in the play a type on the constraint axis first, since that sets which political moves exist and what each one costs; add the other axes where they matter and leave them off where they do not.
- Do read the axes as crossing rather than competing: a polity has a reading on several at once, and a play that treats them as rival answers to one question has misread the engine.
- Do put the pressure on the type's Loses: the speed a liberal democracy cannot have, the safe exit a personalist ruler cannot arrange, the stop a totalitarian project cannot declare, the certainty a competitive authoritarian cannot buy, the decision a consensus government cannot take against anybody.
- Do read the root's twist as a property of the instrument and not a flaw in a badly run regime -- the better the control of speech works, the worse the regime's own information, and it is blind in proportion to its success.
- Do reserve the substitute types for the case where the named basis really is what decides; kleptocracy, ochlocracy, and plutocracy are used as verdicts more often than as descriptions, and a play that spends them as insults gets no information from them.
- Do not model political conduct (politics), the bases of power (power), exit and voice (loyalty), organisational rank (hierarchy, org), the total environment (total-institution), the common-pool resource (commons, upkeep), or what being ruled makes of a persona (subjection). Do not make the regime stand for a theme; that is authored key, the play's pitch. Do not edit the engine's files; wire only from the play's side.
