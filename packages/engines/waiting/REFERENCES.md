# Waiting: Reference

## Line of Work

Waiting as **process**: a persona's time placed at somebody else's disposal.

The engine's founding move is to treat waiting as a **relation** rather than a feeling. Schwartz's
argument is the spine: waiting is a transfer, since the time given up by the party who waits is time
saved by the party waited upon, and the distribution of waiting across a society tracks the
distribution of power closely enough to be read as an index of it. The powerful are not kept waiting
and can keep others waiting. Nobody announces this. The arrangement states itself by operating, which
is what makes it worth an engine: there is no claim in it to disagree with.

That boundary also explains why the engine sits apart from what khai already carries. The anticipatory
hold on a possible bad outcome is suspense's; the felt passage of duration is time-perception's; the
unengaged mind seeking a target is boredom's; the opportune moment is kairos's; the threshold between
identities is liminality's. Waiting is none of those. It is the question of whose schedule accommodates
whose.

**The summons** decides most of the cost before any waiting is done, and its distinction is the one
the engine cares about most: whether the wait has an announced edge. An **appointed hour** returns the
interval before it -- the persona can work, travel, commit -- and it creates an obligation running both
ways, which is why a broken appointment is felt as a small breach. An **open call** takes that interval
too, because availability cannot be part-held: the persona is not waiting for an hour, they are holding
every hour until it comes. And **standing by** is availability as a standing condition, neither working
nor free, which is the service being provided and the part nobody counts -- a night on call in which
nothing happened is recorded as a night in which nothing happened.

**The hold** is the queueing-psychology literature, and its finding is that felt duration tracks the
conditions of the wait far more than its length. Unoccupied time stretches. An **unexplained** delay
stretches further and changes the kind of wait, because the persona supplies a reason and the supplied
reason is worse than the real one almost every time. An **unfair** wait -- somebody who came later
going first -- stretches furthest and is the only condition that produces anger rather than fatigue,
because the complaint has reorganised: the persona now wants to be seen in the right order rather than
sooner. The awkward consequence, which the engine states rather than buries, is that all three remedies
improve the wait **without shortening it**, so the most effective thing an institution can do about
waiting is make it feel shorter, and the incentive that creates does not point toward serving people
faster.

**The order** is where the engine's sharpest finding sits. The queue norm forms without instruction,
holds among strangers, needs no adjudicator, and is honoured overwhelmingly -- one of the few genuinely
universal solutions to a scarce good. And its enforcement is **local**: Milgram's intrusion studies
found objection comes from the one or two people directly behind the point of entry and almost never
from further back. The defence is of a position, not of a principle. Which means the norm holds
robustly against the casual jumper and is **completely silent about anybody admitted at the front by
the institution itself** -- the side door, the call-ahead, the arrangement made at the desk. A queue
polices its own line and cannot see its own door. Precedence compounds this: priority rules are usually
correct and publicly defensible, and are almost never explained at the moment they operate, so a sound
rule and an act of favouritism produce the same observation from the chairs.

**The schooling** is Auyero's, and it is the engine's centre. In the waiting rooms of welfare offices
he found waiting operating as a technique of government -- not by cruelty, since the officials are
mostly reasonable and the delays mostly genuine, but by repetition. A persona who has learned that
outcomes arrive when they arrive, that pressing does not help, and that being available is the only
reliable contribution they can make has been taught a political lesson by an administrative process,
and taught it more thoroughly than any argument could. The product is **learned patience**, which is
indistinguishable from a virtue and which the institution reads as cooperation.

Hence the **twist**, at the root. The instrument is **uncertainty, not duration**. A persona told to
come back at four owns the intervening hours; a persona told they will be called owns none of them, so
an unbounded wait consumes the time it did not use. And it is unanswerable in a way a refusal is not,
because **nothing has been refused**: there is no decision to appeal, nobody has said no, and a persona
who complains is complaining that something has not yet happened. An outcome deferred indefinitely is
functionally an outcome denied, delivered in a form that cannot be contested and that nobody has to
defend.

The engine is careful about intent throughout, and a play should be too. The open call is frequently
the honest answer. The unexplained delay is usually nobody's decision but the absence of anybody whose
job it is to say. The precedence is usually right. None of the mechanism requires anybody to be acting
badly, and that is precisely what makes it worth modelling.

**On the evidence, stated plainly.** The engine's legs are of very different kinds and should not be
read at one confidence. **Schwartz's account is a theoretical and observational sociology of 1974-5**,
enormously influential and not a measured result -- the correlation between power and the distribution
of waiting is compelling, widely instanced, and has never been quantified in the way the claim invites.
**The queueing-psychology findings are the best supported**: occupied-versus-unoccupied duration,
explained-versus-unexplained delay, and the salience of queue-order violations replicate reliably across
service settings, though the canonical statement of them, Maister's, is a practitioner framework rather
than a research programme and the eight propositions vary a lot in how well each is evidenced.
**Milgram's intrusion studies are real experiments** with a clear result, and they are small,
single-city, and from 1986; the local-defence finding has held up in later work but the queue norm's
strength varies substantially by culture, and an engine built on Anglo-American queueing should not be
assumed to travel. **Auyero's** _Patients of the State_ is ethnographic fieldwork in Argentine welfare
offices -- a persuasive account of a mechanism, not a demonstration of its universality, and it is the
place where the engine most invites over-reading: it is a short step from "waiting subordinates" to
"waiting is designed to subordinate", and the evidence does not support the second. What should not be
taken from this engine is that institutions make people wait on purpose, that every delay is a
technique, or that patience is false consciousness. The claim is narrow: waiting transfers time, the
transfer ranks, uncertainty costs more than duration, and repetition teaches.

## Origin

The sociology of waiting, queueing psychology, and the ethnography of administrative delay, wired as
one process on a persona. Each movement is separately sourced; the twist is Auyero's, resting on
Schwartz's founding observation.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The hold on a possible bad outcome (suspense)**: The anticipatory apprehension of what might
  still happen, sustained by uncertainty short of certainty, is the suspense engine's. A persona can
  wait for hours with no apprehension at all, and can be in suspense with nothing to wait for. This
  engine reads the arrangement, not the affect.
- **The felt passage of duration (time-perception)**: How real the future is, how available the past
  remains, and how duration registers as it passes are the time-perception engine's. The hold movement
  reads what an institution's conduct does to the felt length of an interval, and delegates the
  faculty doing the feeling.
- **The unengaged mind (boredom)**: The aversive state of mobilised attention with no target the
  situation will supply is the boredom engine's. Empty time frequently produces it, and the engine's
  claim is about what the emptiness does to the wait rather than about the state.
- **The opportune moment (kairos)**: The window that opens and shuts, and the skill of timing the
  seizing, are kairos's. Waiting is the opposite condition: a moment the persona does not choose and
  cannot bring forward.
- **The threshold between identities (liminality)**: The state between a prior identity and what comes
  next is the liminality engine's. A long wait may be liminal and most are not.
- **Rank itself (status, hierarchy)**: Position in a deference order and formal rank are those
  engines'. Waiting is an index of rank and does not model it -- it reads the transfer, and the
  arrangement's silence about what it is demonstrating.
- **The street-level official's discretion (law, employment)**: The latitude of whoever is at the
  counter, and the coping routines that become the policy, are the law engine's gap axis and the
  employment engine's. Waiting reads what the resulting delay does to the person in the chair.
- **What being governed makes of a persona (subjection, discipline)**: The world handed ready-made,
  the conduct trained by watching, and the self surrendered are the subjection composite's and the
  discipline engine's. The schooling is narrower: a disposition produced specifically by repeated
  administrative uncertainty, and it should not be read as a general account of how institutions form
  people.
- **The wait as authored symbol (the play's pitch)**: A delay made to **stand for** a theme -- the
  endless corridor that mirrors a motif of futility -- is refused: that is authored key, the play's
  pitch. Waiting sets what the interval cost and what it demonstrated; the meaning read into it is the
  play's.

## Encoding

Source to constraint. A process tree: one root, four movements, twelve forms.

- **[waiting](process_waiting.md)** (the root): time placed at somebody else's disposal, read on who is
  available to whom, carrying the twist -- the instrument is uncertainty rather than duration, and it
  is unanswerable because nothing has been refused. Anchored by Schwartz (1974) and Auyero (2012).
- **[the summons](process_summons.md)**: how the wait is set, and whether it has an edge. Anchored by
  Schwartz (1975). [the appointed hour](process_appointed_hour.md),
  [the open call](process_open_call.md), [standing by](process_standing_by.md).
- **[the hold](process_hold_waiting.md)**: what makes the interval feel as long as it does. Anchored by
  Maister (1985). [empty time](process_empty_time.md),
  [the unexplained delay](process_unexplained_delay.md), [the unfair wait](process_unfair_wait.md).
- **[the order](process_order_waiting.md)**: who goes before whom. Anchored by Mann (1969), Schwartz
  (1975), and Milgram et al. (1986). [the queue norm](process_queue_norm.md),
  [precedence](process_precedence.md), [the intrusion](process_queue_intrusion.md).
- **[the schooling](process_schooling.md)**: what a repeated habit of waiting makes of the persona who
  does it. Anchored by Auyero (2012) and Lipsky (1980). [remaining available](process_remaining_available.md),
  [nothing refused](process_no_refusal.md), [learned patience](process_learned_patience.md).
