# Process: Precedence

## Taxonomy

[The Order](process_order_waiting.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: waiting

## Initiated by

Somebody is entitled to go first.
Precedence starts wherever a rule overrides arrival order: triage by severity, a booked appointment against walk-ins, a class of ticket, a statutory priority, an emergency.

## Direction

Toward a sequence that answers to something other than arrival.
Precedence is not queue-jumping and is frequently the more defensible allocation: the person bleeding should not wait behind the person with a form, and a system that could not say so would be indefensible.
Its whole difficulty is legibility -- the entitlement is known to the institution and invisible from the chairs.

## Lever

The rightness of the priority against its invisibility to everybody else.
The carrying force is that priority rules are usually correct, publicly defensible, and would be endorsed by the very people they displace if those people knew what they were; the resistance is that they are almost never explained at the moment they operate, so a sound rule and an act of favouritism produce the same observation -- and the persona in the chair must decide between them with no information.
Precedence is right and unreadable, which is the worst combination available.

## Echo

A correct decision that looks like a wrong one.
It shows why the movement's conflicts are so reliably about legitimacy rather than about time.
Precedence is dated from the rule and enacted without reference to it.
