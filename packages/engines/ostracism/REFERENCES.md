# Ostracism: Reference

## Line of Work

Ostracism as a **process** engine: being ignored and excluded, and the staged response it triggers, taken across the reflexive, reflective, and resignation stages of the temporal need-threat model. The domain does not model the chronic, subjective sense of a gap between the connection a persona has and the connection it wants, which is the loneliness engine's; nor the discrediting mark that invites others to exclude one, which is the stigma engine's; nor the violation of trust by an intimate, which is the betrayal engine's. It models the event and its arc: the act of being cut off by others, the threat it lands on four fundamental needs, and the characteristic sequence of pain, coping, and -- if the exclusion holds -- resignation.

The spine is Williams's model of ostracism. Being ignored and excluded is detected by a fast, evolutionarily old system and threatens four fundamental needs at once -- belonging, control, self-esteem, and meaningful existence. The response runs a temporal arc. The reflexive stage is an immediate, source-independent social pain: it fires even when the ostracism is trivial, expected, explicitly accidental, or by a despised out-group, because the detection system reacts before it appraises, and it runs on the pain circuitry that registers social injury as physical (Eisenberger, Lieberman & Williams, on the dACC and social pain). The reflective stage is the coping that follows: the persona appraises the exclusion and moves to fortify the threatened needs, prosocially (reconnection, attention to others, conformity) when belonging seems recoverable, or antisocially (aggression, control-reassertion) when it does not. The resignation stage is the chronic pole: prolonged, inescapable ostracism depletes the coping resources and gives way to alienation, helplessness, depression, and unworthiness. The paradigm evidence is Cyberball, the virtual ball-toss that reliably threatens the four needs even under minimal conditions. The altitudes are those: the event, and its three stages.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **Loneliness**: The engine models the act of being excluded, not the chronic subjective gap in connection. Loneliness -- the persona's own felt discrepancy between the connection it has and the connection it wants, whatever its cause -- is owned by the loneliness engine; ostracism is the excluding event and its acute four-need threat, and where prolonged ostracism settles into a standing sense of disconnection, it hands off to loneliness. The two compose: ostracism is done to the persona by others, loneliness is felt by the persona about its situation.
- **Stigma**: The engine models the exclusion, not the mark that invites it. A discrediting attribute that spoils identity and draws exclusion is owned by the stigma engine; ostracism is what happens when others act on such a mark (or on nothing at all), the shutting-out itself, not the attribute that occasions it.
- **Betrayal**: The engine models exclusion by a group or others generally, not the violation of a trusted bond. The wound of an intimate breaking faith -- being turned on by one who was supposed to be on one's side -- is owned by the betrayal engine; ostracism can be committed by strangers, machines, or the despised, and its sting is source-independent, where betrayal's depends entirely on the violated trust.
- **Sadness / depression**: The engine models the ostracism response up to resignation, not the affective state of depression itself. The lowered, slowed, withdrawn state of sadness and its depressive pole is owned by the sadness engine; ostracism's resignation stage is the collapse of coping that feeds it, and hands the affect across.
- **Rejection sensitivity**: The engine models the ostracism episode, not the standing disposition to expect and overreact to rejection. The anxious readiness to perceive rejection where it may not exist is a trait construct (Downey & Feldman) this engine does not author; ostracism is the event and its staged response, which a rejection-sensitive persona meets with a lower threshold.

Source to constraint, per file.

- **[ostracism](process_ostracism.md)** (the root, process): Being ignored and excluded, and the threat it lands on belonging, control, self-esteem, and meaningful existence. Anchored by Williams (the need-threat model).
- **[reflexive](process_reflexive.md)** (process, stage): The immediate, source-independent social pain and the fresh threat to the four needs. Anchored by Williams (the reflexive stage) and Eisenberger et al. (social pain).
- **[reflective](process_reflective.md)** (process, stage): The coping that fortifies the threatened needs, forked prosocial (reconnect) or antisocial (reassert control). Anchored by Williams, Twenge (prosocial), and Baumeister & DeWall (antisocial).
- **[resignation](process_resignation.md)** (process, stage): The chronic pole -- coping depleted, exclusion surrendered to as alienation, helplessness, and unworthiness. Anchored by Williams & Nida (consequences and coping).
