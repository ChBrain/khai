# Process: Acquired

## Taxonomy

[Self-Handicapping](process_self_handicapping.md), a real obstacle created before the outcome.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: self-handicapping

## Initiated by

The persona actually creates the obstacle it will later be measured against.
This is acquired, or behavioral, self-handicapping (Berglas & Jones): the persona genuinely impairs its own performance ahead of the test -- withdrawing effort, procrastinating, undertraining, over-committing, or reaching for a substance -- so that a poor result has a real cause outside its ability.
It opens where the persona would rather truly hobble its chance than face a clean verdict on what it can do.

## Direction

Toward a real impediment, laid in the performance's path.
Acquired handicapping moves the persona to do something that will actually degrade the outcome: not to claim a burden but to take one on, so the obstacle is there in fact and the failure it may cause is genuinely attributable to it.
Its direction is into the performance itself -- the ability's showing really weakened, on purpose, before the result.

## Lever

What acquired handicapping spends -- the outcome itself, really sacrificed for the cover it buys.
This is the costly form: because the impediment is real, it genuinely lowers the odds of success, so the persona pays in actual performance for an excuse that will actually hold -- a strong cover bought with a worse chance.
A production holds this lever by letting the persona really hobble itself -- the unread book, the unpracticed piece, the night out before the exam -- and let the handicap both protect the ability-image and cost the result, so the self-sabotage reads as a trade, the surest excuse for the dearest price.

## Echo

The persona goes into the outcome genuinely impaired -- and the impairment is real evidence for whatever excuse the result requires.
A failure now attaches cleanly to the obstacle the persona built, and a success, won despite a real burden, augments the ability all the more; but the burden was real, and where it tips the result to failure, the persona has spent the outcome to keep the attribution.
Its cheaper cousin claims an impediment without creating one -- that is [claimed](process_claimed.md) handicapping; this form owns the real obstacle, the performance actually weakened before the test.
