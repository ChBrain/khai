# Self-Handicapping: Reference

## Line of Work

Self-handicapping as a **multi-type** engine: erecting an obstacle before an evaluative outcome so that a failure can be attributed to the obstacle rather than to ability, and a success counts double for coming despite it -- taken across the two forms the handicap takes and the disposition that tunes them. The domain does not model the after-the-fact attribution that credits success and blames failure once a result is known, which is the self-serving member of the bias engine's; nor the post-hoc account offered for a completed act, which is the repair engine's excuse; nor the belief in one's own capability, which is the self-efficacy engine's; nor the level of self-worth being defended, which is the self-esteem engine's. It models the anticipatory, behavioral protection of the ability-image: the obstacle raised before the outcome to control what its result will mean.

The spine is the self-handicapping literature. Berglas and Jones's founding demonstration showed people, faced with a test of ability whose success they could not count on repeating, choosing a performance-inhibiting drug over a performance-enhancing one -- proactively acquiring a handicap so that a poor showing could be blamed on the drug rather than on their competence. The mechanism is attributional and works both ways: a handicap discounts a failure (Kelley's discounting principle -- the failure has a plausible external cause, so it says less about ability) and augments a success (the augmentation principle -- a win despite a burden implies more ability, not less). Leary and Shepperd distinguish the two forms: acquired or behavioral handicaps, where a real impediment is created and the performance genuinely suffers, and claimed or self-reported handicaps, where an impediment is merely asserted without being created. Rhodewalt establishes self-handicapping as a disposition measurable by the Self-Handicapping Scale, more the resort of those whose sense of their ability is uncertain and self-worth is staked on it. The altitudes are those: the anticipatory strategy, its acquired and claimed forms, and the handicapper's standing disposition.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **Self-serving attribution (bias)**: The engine models the anticipatory obstacle, not the after-the-fact attribution. The self-serving member of the bias engine owns the post-outcome move -- crediting a success to oneself and blaming a failure on circumstance once the result is known; self-handicapping is prior and behavioral, the impediment put in place before the outcome, often at the real cost of the performance. The bias reinterprets a result already in; the handicap arranges the result's meaning ahead of time. The two are the after and before of protecting the ability-image.
- **Repair (excuse)**: The engine models the pre-emptive handicap, not the post-hoc account. The repair engine owns the excuse offered for a completed act -- the account that explains or mitigates a wrong or a failure after it has happened; self-handicapping lays its cover before the outcome, so that the excuse is built into the situation rather than offered once the damage is done. The excuse accounts for what occurred; the handicap pre-arranges what will.
- **Self-efficacy**: The engine models the defense of the ability-image, not the belief in ability itself. Self-efficacy -- a persona's belief in its capacity to perform -- is owned by the self-efficacy engine; self-handicapping is what a persona does to protect that belief from a disconfirming test, most where the efficacy is prized but uncertain. The efficacy is the belief; the handicap guards it.
- **Self-esteem**: The engine models the strategy, not the worth it defends. Self-esteem -- the level of a persona's sense of its own value -- is owned by the self-esteem engine; self-handicapping is one way a persona whose self-worth is staked on ability shields that worth from a clean verdict. The esteem is what is protected; the handicap is the protecting.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per file.

- **[self-handicapping](process_self_handicapping.md)** (the root, process): The erecting of an obstacle before an outcome to control its attribution -- discounting a failure, augmenting a success. Anchored by Berglas & Jones (1978) and Jones & Berglas (the attributional account).
- **[acquired](process_acquired.md)** (process, form): A real obstacle created -- effort withdrawn, practice skipped, a substance taken -- genuinely impairing the performance, the costly and stronger cover. Anchored by Berglas & Jones (behavioral handicapping).
- **[claimed](process_claimed.md)** (process, form): An impediment merely asserted -- illness, anxiety, stress reported in advance -- a verbal excuse laid down without degrading the performance, the cheaper and weaker cover. Anchored by Leary & Shepperd (self-reported handicaps).
- **[handicapper](position_handicapper.md)** (position, trait): A standing readiness to reach for a handicap under evaluative threat, highest where ability is prized and uncertain. Anchored by Rhodewalt (the Self-Handicapping Scale).
