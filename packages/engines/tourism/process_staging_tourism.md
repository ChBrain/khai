# Process: The Staging

## Taxonomy

[Tourism](process_tourism.md), what the place does in answer, and where the engine is seen for one thing.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: tourism

## Initiated by

The place hears what is being asked for and provides it.
The Staging starts where a demand for unarranged life meets people willing to meet it: [a staged back](process_the_staged_back.md) built to be discovered, [a host performing](process_the_host_performing.md) their own ordinary life, and [an authenticity sold](process_the_authenticity_sold.md), which is the point at which everybody can see it.

## Direction

Toward a place that has produced a version of itself for people who did not want a version.
The response is not cynical and rarely begins as a plan. Somebody is asked repeatedly where the locals eat, and starts saying; a family is asked what a wedding here is like, and offers to show one; a workshop leaves its door open because visitors kept knocking.
Each step is hospitable, each is small, and the result is a set of arrangements that exist because visitors wanted things that do not normally have arrangements.

## Lever

The generosity of the answer against what answering constructs.
The carrying force is that the hosts are not deceiving anybody and are frequently the most generous party in the transaction: the family really does live there, the boat really does fish, the dance is genuinely theirs, and refusing every visitor who asked would be both unkind and expensive in a place that needs the money; the resistance is that a back region prepared for discovery is a front region with a different label, so a visitor who reaches it has reached exactly as far as somebody decided they could -- and neither party experiences it that way.
The Staging rides the engine's keystone knife-edge: the demand for the unstaged is what builds the stage.

## Echo

A place with a real life and an accessible one, and no clear line between them.
It shows the marking and the seeking arriving at their result -- somewhere that has answered honestly and is now partly an answer.
This is why the Staging is the hardest of the four to stage and the only one worth closing on: the hosts must be sincere, the visitors must be sincere, nothing must have been faked, and the audience must still be able to see that what was reached had been prepared.
A play that stages the hosts as cynical has replaced the mechanism with a swindle, and one that stages the visitors as fools has replaced it with a satire.
The Staging is dated from the first time somebody asked, and deepens with every honest answer.
