# Tourism: Reference

## Line of Work

Tourism as **process**: the arrangement between a place and the people who come to look at it.

The engine's subject is a demand that cannot be met and is met anyway. MacCannell's argument is the
spine: the tourist is not a fool looking for pleasant fakes but a modern pilgrim looking for the real
life of somewhere else -- and the sincerity of that search is exactly what produces staged
authenticity, because a place asked repeatedly for its unarranged life will arrange one. The visitor
gets in, the hosts are not lying, and what was reached had been made reachable.

The boundary with what khai already carries is clean. The conversion of a stranger into a guest, the
offering that seals it, and the term nobody can name are **hospitality**'s -- that engine reads a
household admitting one person; this one reads a place receiving many, continuously, as a condition of
its economy. The front region given to an audience and the back region where the performance is
prepared are **backstage**'s, whole; MacCannell borrows Goffman's regions and this engine uses them
without owning them, reading only the tourist-specific case of a back region built to be found. Living
from one's own values rather than a false front is **authenticity**'s, which is a psychology of the
self and shares nothing with this beyond the word. The displacement of residents by incomers is
**gentrification**'s, and the passing of a place's past into its present is **heritage**'s and
**palimpsest**'s.

**The marking** happens before anybody sets out, and Urry's point is the operative one: nothing is
intrinsically worth seeing. A hillside becomes a viewpoint when somebody puts a name and a rail on it,
and the identical hillside a mile away is not a sight. The **marker** therefore creates the
significance it reports -- funding follows it, restoration follows it, crowds follow it -- and after a
generation its claim is true because it was made. The **prior image** pre-scripts the arrival, so the
visit becomes a comparison and the visitor's task on the day is to stand where the photograph was
taken. The **itinerary** makes the trip accountable to a list assembled by strangers, and the part
that would have been remembered has no entry in it.

**The seeking** is never for the marked thing. What the visitor came for is how people live here, and
the cathedral is what got them onto the plane -- which is why improving the attraction satisfies
nobody. The **real sought** cannot be handed over without ceasing to be it, since an unperformed room
with a stranger in it who came to see it is a room that now has an audience. The **tourist disowned**
is the one attitude every tourist shares, and the disowning does work: somebody who is not a tourist
is entitled to the parts tourists do not get. The **off path** is a position rather than a place, and
it moves each year at roughly the speed of the people looking for it.

**The staging** is the place answering, and it rarely begins as a plan. Somebody is asked repeatedly
where the locals eat and starts saying; a workshop leaves its door open because visitors kept
knocking. The **staged back** is genuine and is not the back: the room is real, the work in it is
real, and it has also been made passable, because a room people come through cannot be a room nobody
comes through. The **host performing** does the thing they would be doing, a little more slowly and at
the hour when people are around -- and over time the adjusted version becomes the version, so what is
preserved is whatever shows well. The **authenticity sold** is the point at which everybody can see
the arrangement, and the price is the disclosure rather than the corruption: it is the least dishonest
step in the sequence and the one that reads worst.

**The residue** splits. The visitor keeps a **souvenir** that stores nothing and reminds well, and an
**account home** compressed into four or five tellable episodes, which selects on tellability rather
than on significance and then replaces the fortnight. The **place after** keeps a structure: the
bakery that became a cafe does not become a bakery again in November, and what stopped being provided
for residents stays gone.

Hence the **twist**, at the root. **The demand for the unstaged is what builds the stage.** The
visitor does not want the show, says so, and means it; the place hears that sincerely and often
gratefully and produces a back region for them to find. Nothing about it is a deception. But any back
region a visitor can reach has been made reachable, so the search cannot terminate, and the harder it
is pressed the more thoroughly the place is converted into a performance of itself.

The engine is built so that nobody has to be cynical or foolish for any of it to run, and a play
should keep it that way. The visitor genuinely wants the real thing. The host genuinely offers it.
The boundary moves anyway.

**On the evidence, stated plainly.** This is a theoretical and ethnographic engine, and one of its
central questions is openly unresolved in the field. **MacCannell's _The Tourist_ is an argument, not
a study**: a theoretical monograph built from observation, reading, and reasoning, enormously
influential and contested from publication. **It directly contradicts Boorstin, and the engine takes a
side and says so.** Boorstin held that tourists want contrivance and are content with pseudo-events;
MacCannell held that they want the real and are given staged versions of it. Both accounts fit much of
the same behaviour, neither has been settled empirically, and this engine is built on MacCannell's
because it is the more charitable reading and the more productive one -- which is a choice rather than
a finding, and a reader who prefers Boorstin's should know the engine has not refuted it. **Cohen's
emergent authenticity cuts against the twist and is kept for that reason**: a performance introduced
for visitors can, within a generation or two, become genuinely the community's own, so staging is not
a one-way loss and the engine's mechanism describes a process rather than a verdict. **Wang's
existential authenticity relocates the question entirely** -- if what the visitor seeks is a state of
themselves rather than a property of the object, the engine's whole apparatus is reading the wrong
side of the transaction, and that possibility is live. **Doxey's irridex**, the sequence from
euphoria to antagonism in host communities, is a model with very little systematic support and is used
here as a shape rather than as evidence. **The economics are outside this engine and are large.**
Whether tourism enriches or hollows a place is an empirical question with mixed answers that vary by
scale, ownership, and season, and the place-after member reads the structural residue without settling
it. What should not be taken from this engine is that tourists are fools, that hosts are deceiving
anybody, or that tourism ruins places. The claim is narrow: the sight is made by its marker, the want
is for what cannot be handed over, the answer to that want is an arrangement, and the two sides are
left holding different kinds of thing.

separately sourced; the twist is MacCannell's, and the counterweights are Cohen's and Wang's.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The conversion of a stranger into a guest (hospitality)**: The stranger resolved as guest or
  threat, the offering that seals it, and the term nobody can name are the hospitality engine's. That
  engine reads a household admitting one person; this one reads a place receiving many, continuously,
  as a condition of its economy.
- **Front and back regions (backstage, presentation, face)**: The performance given to an audience,
  the region where it is prepared, and the management of the public self are those engines'. The staged
  back member reads one tourist-specific case and hands the theory back.
- **Living from one's own values (authenticity)**: Congruence between a true self and its expression
  is the authenticity engine's, a psychology of the self that shares nothing with this beyond the word.
  The engine models a property claimed of places and practices, not of persons.
- **Incomers displacing residents (gentrification, dereliction, neighborhood-cycle)**: Who is priced
  out, how a place declines when care is withdrawn, and the cycle of a neighbourhood are those
  packages'. The place-after member reads what visiting leaves and delegates the rest.
- **The past carried into the present (heritage, palimpsest, restoration, nostalgia)**: What a place
  keeps of its history, the layers it shows, the mending of its fabric, and the longing it provokes are
  those engines'. The marker member reads only the designation.
- **Being held by a place (place-attachment, dwelling, third-place)**: Rootedness, the person held by
  somewhere, and the informal ground where nobody is host are those packages'. A visitor is precisely
  somebody not held by the place.
- **The journey itself (journey-roles, transportation, liminality, elsewhere)**: The roles a traveller
  passes through, the story world entered, the threshold state, and the mind in a place it is not are
  those packages'. This engine begins on arrival.
- **The industry (the world)**: Airlines, operators, platforms, regulation, and carrying capacity are
  a trade with its own economics and politics. They set the volume and are not the operation.
- **The trip as authored symbol (the play's pitch)**: A journey made to **stand for** a theme -- the
  pilgrimage that mirrors a motif of searching -- is refused: that is authored key, the play's pitch.
  Tourism sets what the visit demanded and produced; the meaning read into it is the play's.
