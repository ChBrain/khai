# Instructions: Tourism

How a Playwright wires the tourism engine into a play. You wire by linking the play's own content to the engine's files; the engine files stay untouched, they are canon. The Roadie plumbs the engine's law into Instructions Knowledge on deploy. Authoring guidance, not runtime content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human decides where the current boundary sits between what visitors reach and what they do not, and keeps it off the page, because both parties experience it as no boundary at all. Give both sides -- the host is a position in the engine, not scenery in it. And keep everybody sincere: the visitor genuinely wants the real thing, the host genuinely offers it, and the engine runs at full strength with nobody dissembling.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, a persona links under Projection where they stand in the arrangement: whether they are visiting or being visited, what they came for, what they have been shown, what they were asked for, and what the season left. Read the engine on what the place had to become, never on what the visitor took away. Keep it the arrangement: the conversion of a stranger into a guest is hospitality's, front and back regions are backstage's, congruence between a self and its expression is authenticity's. You link to the engine, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- The conversion of a stranger into a guest is the hospitality engine's; front and back regions and the managed public self are backstage's, presentation's, and face's; living from one's own values is authenticity's; incomers displacing residents is gentrification's and the neighborhood-cycle composite's; the past carried into the present is heritage's, palimpsest's, and restoration's; being held by a place is place-attachment's, the dwelling composite's, and third-place's; the journey itself is journey-roles's, transportation's, liminality's, and the elsewhere composite's. Airlines, operators, platforms, and carrying capacity are the trade and set the volume rather than the operation. Whether a production's back room was opened or arranged, and whether a host's performance is theirs or the visitors', are the team's review.

## Knowledge

- Tourism is the arrangement between a place and the people who come to look at it: somewhere visited on purpose and at scale, which knows they are coming. It is not hospitality's conversion of a stranger into a guest, not backstage's front and back regions, and not authenticity's alignment of a self with its expression. It wires on a persona at Projection and runs in four movements. The marking happens before anybody sets out: nothing is intrinsically worth seeing, so a marker creates the significance it reports and the marked thing becomes the important thing, funding and restoration and crowds following it; the prior image pre-scripts the arrival, so the visit is a comparison and the task on the day is to stand where the photograph was taken; the itinerary makes the trip accountable to a list assembled by strangers, and the afternoon that would have been remembered has no entry in it. The seeking is never for the marked thing -- the visitor wants how people live here, which is why improving the attraction satisfies nobody; the real sought cannot be handed over without ceasing to be it, since an unperformed room with a stranger in it is a room that now has an audience; every tourist disowns the word, and the disowning licenses deeper access; the off path is a position rather than a place and moves at the speed of the people looking for it. The staging is the place answering, and it rarely begins as a plan: a back region opened for discovery is genuine and is not the back, a host performing their own work drifts toward the version that shows well, and the price is the disclosure rather than the corruption -- the least dishonest step and the one that reads worst. The residue splits: the visitor keeps a souvenir that stores nothing, and an account compressed into five tellable episodes that then replaces the fortnight; the place keeps a structure that outlasts every individual visitor. The engine's twist is at the root: the demand for the unstaged is what builds the stage, because any back region a visitor can reach has been made reachable.

## System

- Do set the boundary before writing anything, and never let anybody name it. Where visitors currently reach is the engine, and nobody in the scene can see it.
- Do keep the visitor sincere. They want the real thing and say so, and staging them as a fool replaces the mechanism with a satire.
- Do keep the host sincere. The family does live there, the boat does fish, and staging them as cynical replaces the mechanism with a swindle.
- Do let a marker do the seeing. Two identical hillsides, one with a rail and a name on it, and everybody stops at the same one.
- Do write the disowning. The visitor explains that they are not a tourist, and then asks for what tourists do not get.
- Do open a back room honestly. It is the real kitchen, the work in it is real, and it has been tidied because people come through -- which is the whole of the twist in one setting.
- Do put a price on it at some point. That is the least dishonest step in the sequence and the one everybody objects to, and the embarrassment belongs to the visitor rather than the seller.
- Do close on the place after rather than the departure. A square that empties, an economy with a shape, and residents who are not confused about the trade they made.
- Do not model the guest conversion (hospitality), the front and back regions themselves (backstage, presentation), congruence (authenticity), displacement (gentrification), the past (heritage, palimpsest), rootedness (place-attachment, dwelling), or the journey (journey-roles, transportation, liminality). Do not make the trip stand for a theme; that is authored key, the play's pitch. Do not edit the engine's files; wire only from the play's side.
