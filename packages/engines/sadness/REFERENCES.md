# Sadness: Reference

## Line of Work

Sadness as a **multi-type** engine: the low-approach emotion through which a persona sinks at a loss, a failure, or an unmet goal -- energy withdrawn, the gaze turned inward and down, a call for care -- taken across its forms and the trait that tunes it. The domain does not model the appraisal taxonomy of the basic emotions, which is the emotion engine's; nor the sustained adaptation to an irreversible bereavement, which is the grief engine's; nor the persisting affective background beneath the moment, which is the mood engine's; nor the ache of social isolation, which is the loneliness engine's; nor the counterfactual sting of a bad choice, which is the regret engine's. It models the emotion itself: the appraised loss, the demobilizing sink, and the withdrawal-and-call it drives.

The spine is the psychology of sadness, from its function to its trait. Sadness is the low-arousal negative emotion, the response to loss and to goals appraised as beyond reach -- the counterpart, in motivational direction, to the mobilizing emotions: where anger approaches a wrong and fear flees a danger, sadness demobilizes, withdrawing effort and turning inward. Its functions are two (Nesse; Bowlby): it disengages the persona from unattainable goals, conserving energy and freeing it to turn elsewhere, and it signals need -- the sad face and posture are among the clearest solicitations of care and support. It runs from the mild everyday letdown of disappointment, through the deep ache of sorrow at a genuine loss, to despair, where sadness fuses with the hopelessness of learned helplessness (Seligman) and effort ceases, and it appears as melancholy, the diffuse, objectless, reflective low the old humoral medicine named. Individual differences in dysphoria -- a low affective set-point and a ready tilt toward sadness -- are stable, shade the read of events through mood-congruent memory, and are a standing path toward depression. The altitudes are those: the emotion, its four forms, and its trait.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **Emotion**: The engine models sadness in its own depth -- the low-approach emotion, its forms, its trait -- not its place in the taxonomy of the basic emotions. The appraisal structure by which an event becomes joy, sadness, anger, fear, or disgust is owned by the emotion engine; this engine unfolds the one node the emotion taxonomy names sadness. The two compose: emotion keeps sadness as one basic affect, this engine unfolds it.
- **Grief**: The engine models the emotion of loss, not the sustained adaptation to an irreversible one. The long process of remaking a world around a bereavement -- the oscillation between confronting the absence and rebuilding -- is owned by the grief engine; sorrow is the deep sad emotion at the border of grief, but the extended mourning is grief's to model. This engine keeps the feeling; grief keeps the adaptation.
- **Mood**: The engine models the sadness episode, not the persisting affective background. The durable low tone a persona carries beneath the moment -- the weather rather than the lightning -- is owned by the mood engine; melancholy sustained as a resting background shades into mood's province, but the sad emotion answering a loss is this engine's.
- **Loneliness**: The engine models sadness at loss in general, not the specific ache of social isolation. The distress of unmet needs for connection and belonging is owned by the loneliness engine; sadness may accompany it, but the isolation-specific emotion is loneliness's.
- **Regret**: The engine models the sadness of a loss, not the counterfactual sting of a choice. The self-blaming comparison of what is against what might have been had one chosen otherwise is owned by the regret engine; disappointment borders it where the shortfall was one's own doing, but the counterfactual reckoning is regret's.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per file.

- **[sadness](process_sadness.md)** (the root, process): The low-approach emotion at a loss -- energy withdrawn, the inward and downward turn, the disengagement from the unreachable, and the call for care. Anchored by Nesse (disengagement) and Bowlby/Averill (the social signal).
- **[disappointment](process_disappointment.md)** (process, form): Goal-failure -- the mild, common sadness of the gap between the wanted and the got, quickly metabolized and instructive. Anchored by the appraisal literature on expectation and outcome.
- **[sorrow](process_sorrow.md)** (process, form): Deep loss -- the heavy, aching sadness of something genuinely valued gone, felt in full and worked through, at the border of grief. Anchored by the basic-emotion accounts of sadness (Ekman; Izard) and the grief literature.
- **[despair](process_despair.md)** (process, form): Hopelessness -- sadness fused with the conviction that nothing can be done, learned helplessness hardened into the collapse of effort. Anchored by Seligman (helplessness) and Beck (the cognitive model of depression).
- **[melancholy](process_melancholy.md)** (process, form): The diffuse, objectless low -- a pervasive, reflective, bittersweet greyness with no named cause, the sadness of reverie and art. Anchored by Burton and the humoral tradition.
- **[dysphoria](position_dysphoria.md)** (position, trait): Trait sadness -- a low resting affective set-point, a ready tilt toward the low, felt keenly and lifted slowly, and a dispositional path toward depression. Anchored by Cloninger and the neuroticism / depressive-temperament literature.
