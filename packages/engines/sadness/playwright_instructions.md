# Instructions: Sadness

How a Playwright wires the sadness engine into a play. You wire by linking the play's own content to the engine's files; the engine files stay untouched, they are canon. The Roadie plumbs the engine's law into Instructions Knowledge on deploy. Authoring guidance, not runtime content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human defines the sadness terrain: what loss, failure, or unmet goal the persona meets, how deep and how recoverable it is, and whether hope survives it -- so there is something of value gone, a sinking it warrants, and someone the sadness might call to.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, a persona links the sadness the moment runs under its Projection: which form (disappointment, sorrow, despair, or melancholy), what loss is appraised, and how dysphoric the persona's standing disposition is. Let it read as a low-approach sinking at a loss -- the withdrawal of energy, the inward and downward turn, the call for comfort -- not as mere displeasure. You link to the engine, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- The appraisal taxonomy of the basic emotions is the emotion engine's; the sustained adaptation to an irreversible bereavement is the grief engine's; the persisting affective background beneath the moment is the mood engine's; the ache of social isolation is the loneliness engine's; the counterfactual sting of a bad choice is the regret engine's. This engine supplies the emotion of loss itself -- the sink, the withdrawal of effort, the signal for care.

## Knowledge

- Sadness is the low-approach emotion at a loss: a persona appraises something of value gone or beyond reach, with no clear way to restore it, and sinks -- energy drains, the gaze turns inward and down -- saying not this is wrong (anger) or get me away (fear) but something is gone. Where anger and fear mobilize, sadness demobilizes; and it points two ways at once -- away from the lost, and toward those who might comfort, its posture a call for care. It runs in forms. Disappointment is the goal-failure form -- the mild, common sadness of the gap between the wanted and the got, quickly metabolized. Sorrow is the deep-loss form -- the heavy, aching sadness of something genuinely valued gone, felt in full and worked through. Despair is the hopeless form -- sadness fused with the conviction that nothing can be done, effort ceasing, the dangerous bottom. Melancholy is the diffuse form -- a pervasive, objectless, reflective low that colours the world grey without naming its cause. Dysphoria is the trait -- a low resting affective set-point, a ready tilt toward sadness, felt keenly and lifted slowly.

## System

- Do make sadness demobilize at a loss, not mobilize against a wrong: where anger advances and fear flees, sadness withdraws and turns in -- so let the persona sink and slow, its energy drawn down, not roused.
- Do let sadness call: the sad face and posture solicit care, so let the emotion read as a signal others are drawn to answer, and let the disengagement from the unreachable free the persona to turn, in time, toward what it can still reach.
- Do keep the form legible: disappointment sags at a letdown, sorrow aches at a real loss, despair gives up when hope fails, melancholy muses greyly at nothing in particular -- and let despair be the dangerous one, the sadness that can fall past its own call for help.
- Do let dysphoria set the set-point: the dysphoric persona reads outcomes as losses and lifts slowly; the buoyant meets the same day brighter. Do delegate the sustained mourning of a bereavement to the grief engine and the persisting low background to the mood engine. Do not edit the engine's files; wire only from the play's side.
