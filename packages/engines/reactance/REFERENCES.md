# Reactance: Reference

## Line of Work

Reactance as **process**: the motivational pushback through which a persona whose behavioral freedom is threatened or removed is aroused to restore it -- and moves against the very force that pressed. The domain does not model the uptake of a persuasive message, which is persuasion's, nor standing unmoved by group pressure, which is conformity's independence; reactance is neither yielding nor merely failing to yield -- it is being pushed the opposite way by the push itself. Nor does it model the raw hostility the source-directed form recruits, which is aggression's, nor the static valuation tilt the whole look leaves on the read of an option's worth, which the bias engine catalogues among the cognitive biases. It models the pushback itself: a freedom threatened, the arousal to reclaim it, and the routes that reclaiming takes.

The spine is the social psychology of psychological reactance. When a person believes they hold a set of free behaviors and one of them is threatened or eliminated, they experience reactance -- a motivational state directed at restoring the threatened freedom. The state scales with the importance of the freedom, the proportion of the person's freedoms the threat implicates, and the magnitude and high-handedness of the threat; it is why controlling influence rebounds, the boomerang that grows sharper the harder the freedom is pressed. It is the resistance counterpart to persuasion, which moves a person toward a position, and to conformity, which moves them toward the group: reactance moves them against the push. The three forms are the routes restoration takes: defiance, allure, derogation.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **Persuasion**: The engine models the pushback against a threatened freedom, not the uptake of a persuasive message. The routes to attitude change -- how a message is received, processed, and yielded to -- are owned by the persuasion engine; reactance is what that engine's controlling excess produces, the boomerang against the hard sell, and owns the resistance, not the reception.
- **Conformity (independence)**: The engine models being pushed against the push, not standing unmoved by it. Holding one's own line under group pressure without being moved either way is the conformity engine's independence; reactance is the opposite motion -- pressed, the persona moves the other way, restoring a freedom rather than merely declining to yield.
- **Aggression**: The engine models the freedom-restoring turn against the source, not the hostility it recruits. The raw anger, its arousal, and its expression are owned by the aggression engine; derogation draws on that hostility but functions to disarm the threat by discrediting its source, and owns that function, not the aggression itself.
- **Bias**: The engine models the live pushback under threat, not the static tilt it leaves. The catalogued cognitive tilt -- the standing tendency to overvalue a forbidden option -- is owned by the bias engine as one node in its codex; reactance is the motivational process of which that tilt is the residue, and owns the arousal and the restoration, not the entry in the list.
- **Contempt**: The engine models resenting and discrediting the source to restore a freedom, not the cold devaluation of another as beneath one. The downward look that holds another lesser is owned by the contempt engine; derogation lowers the source's credibility to break its grip, a means to defend freedom rather than a standing verdict on the other's worth.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per file.

- **[reactance](process_reactance.md)** (the root): The motivational pushback -- a freedom threatened, the arousal to reclaim it, the move against the push rather than toward a position, growing sharper the harder the freedom is pressed. Anchored by Brehm (the theory of reactance) and Brehm & Brehm (freedom and control).
- **[defiance](process_defiance.md)** (form): Direct restoration -- the threatened act performed, visibly, so the freedom is proven by its use. Anchored by Brehm & Brehm (re-establishment by performing the act) and Miron & Brehm (direct restoration).
- **[allure](process_allure.md)** (form): Indirect restoration -- the blocked option's pull rising with the ban rather than its worth, the forbidden gilded by the forbidding. Anchored by Clee & Wicklund (commodity theory, scarcity, and desirability) and Brehm & Brehm (increased attractiveness of the eliminated option).
- **[derogation](process_derogation.md)** (form): The source-directed route -- turning on the agent that pressed, discrediting it so the threat loses its grip. Anchored by Dillard & Shen (the anger-cognition boomerang) and Miron & Brehm (source derogation and hostility).
