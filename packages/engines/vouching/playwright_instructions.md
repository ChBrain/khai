# Instructions: Vouching

How a Playwright wires the vouching engine into a play. You wire by linking the play's own content to the engine's files; the engine files stay untouched, they are canon. The Roadie plumbs the engine's law into Instructions Knowledge on deploy. Authoring guidance, not runtime content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human keeps the referees sincere and the letters warm and true, because the engine needs no saboteur and stops working the moment it gets one. Decide who is missing from the list and why, since that gap is where the whole decision will be made and the reason for it will be innocent. And keep the reader experienced and fair, because a lazy reader replaces the mechanism with a shrug.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, a persona links under Projection where they stand to it: who would write for them, who they have written for, what they left out, whose name is missing from their list, and what they think a warm letter is worth. Read the engine on who agreed to give it, never on what it said. Keep it the third-party claim: the request for discretionary help is asking's, the second judgement on a decision is advice's, and the hour in a room is interview's. You link to the engine, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- The request for discretionary help, the transfer that names no price, and the obligation it creates are asking's, gift's, debt's, and the owing composite's; the second judgement on a decision and whether the giver knows anything are advice's and expertise's; the selection encounter and the managed front are interview's, presentation's, and face's; the credit given to a first-person account and what a fair hearing buys are credibility's, the heeding composite's, and recognition's; the informal talk that circulates and the mark it leaves are gossip's, the grapevine composite's, and stigma's. Defamation, qualified privilege, data protection, and the right to see one's own file are the law and set the format. Whether a production's referee was enthusiastic or merely unable to refuse, and whether an absence was innocent, are the team's review.

## Knowledge

- Vouching is standing lent to somebody else's claim: one persona puts their own reputation behind another's, to a third party who will decide something. It is not asking's request for discretionary help, not advice's second judgement on a decision, and not interview's hour in a room. It wires on a persona at Projection and runs in four movements. The request draws on a list nobody assembled deliberately, since being vouchable requires that somebody senior watched closely, formed a view, and stayed in post -- distributed by seating plans and staff turnover rather than by merit; declining to act as referee is itself a verdict, so reservations become letters, and a missing obvious name is read as a substitution whatever produced it. The writing produces warmth that cannot be graded: almost every reference is positive, by law in places and by decency everywhere, so the range has had its bottom removed, the protective floor disables the instrument, and the signal moves into a code of selected praise and into length, which is the only uncompellable component and is confounded by how busy the writer was. The stake is a loan of reputation: the candidate is valued at the referee's rate, the referee's standing is genuine collateral that is occasionally called, and the persona leaves holding an obligation the referee would deny being owed. The engine's twist is at the root: nothing in a reference is informative except the fact that somebody agreed to give one -- so the reader who discounts the words, weighs the source, and looks for the name that is not there is behaving correctly, and the decision ends up resting on the shape of the candidate's acquaintance.

## System

- Do keep every letter warm. All of them, for everybody, including the candidate the referee has reservations about.
- Do stage a reluctant yes that is indistinguishable from a willing one. The referee agrees because refusing would be a sentence, and nobody can tell afterwards.
- Do put the code in one adjective. Diligent, conscientious, a pleasure to supervise -- warm on the page and legible as a reservation to anybody in the trade.
- Do let the length matter. A short letter from a generous referee who was busy, and a long one from an indifferent referee who writes long letters about everybody.
- Do make the stake visible to the referee only. They know their name is on it, they never mention it, and they press hard only for the ones they would defend afterwards.
- Do leave an obvious name off the list for an innocent reason. Retired, ill, unreachable, estranged over something unrelated -- and unexplainable without making it worse.
- Do have the reader be right. They scan, note the letterhead, register the gap, and take ninety seconds, and their method is the correct response to the channel.
- Do close on the decision resting on the absence. Everything in the folder was positive, and the thing that decided it was not in the folder.
- Do not model the request for help (asking, gift, debt, owing), the second judgement (advice, expertise), the selection encounter (interview, presentation, face), the credit given to an account (credibility, heeding, recognition), or the informal talk (gossip, grapevine, stigma). Do not make the letter stand for a theme; that is authored key, the play's pitch. Do not edit the engine's files; wire only from the play's side.
