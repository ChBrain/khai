# Instructions: Uniform

How a Playwright wires the uniform engine into a play. You wire by linking the play's own content to the engine's files; the engine files stay untouched, they are canon. The Roadie plumbs the engine's law into Instructions Knowledge on deploy. Authoring guidance, not runtime content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human lets the licence be real and useful before it is costly: the garment is what makes the hard part of the work possible, and a wearer who resented it throughout would be the wrong persona for this engine. Show the object being issued rather than acquired, since almost everything here follows from it not being theirs. And decide where they get recognised out of it, because that is the scene the engine is aimed at.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, a persona links under Projection where they stand to it: whether they wear one, whether they are addressed through one, what it licenses them to do, what it stops them doing, and where they have been recognised without it. Read the engine on what the garment licensed and what it cost, never on what the wearer is like. Keep it the object: the stripping at the door of a closed institution is total-institution's, the front a persona manages is presentation's, the insider's speech is register's. You link to the engine, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- The admission sequence that strips a self is total-institution's; the managed front and what it costs are presentation's, face's, backstage's, and self-monitoring's; the object a self has extended into, the article withheld to carry a lineage, and the thing a collective lodges its we in are extended-self's, heirloom's, totem's, and the investiture composite's; the specialised speech that binds and gates is register's; what happens to restraint in a mass is crowd's and conformity's; where the office's authority comes from and why people comply are power's, hierarchy's, obedience's, and status's. Procurement, safety specification, dress codes, and the law on impersonation are the trade and set the format. Whether a production's wearer is sheltering behind the office or doing the job it exists for, and whether a standard applied off duty was proportionate, are the team's review.

## Knowledge

- A uniform is the garment that replaces a person with an office: issued rather than owned, sized and signed for, worn for the hours the office is held, and read by strangers as a category before the person arrives. It is not total-institution's stripping at the door, not presentation's managed front, and not register's specialised speech. It wires on a persona at Projection. What it holds up is that this one may act -- the nurse may touch a stranger, the officer may stop a car, the inspector may enter a kitchen, and nobody asks any of them for a reason -- and it tells anybody in trouble which of forty people in a corridor to approach. One position and three processes hang beneath it. The wearer holds an authority nobody granted them personally and a wardrobe they did not buy; they may order strangers in the narrow matters the office covers, and they lose the option of being an individual in public, since a rudeness is the service being rude and a kindness is the service being kind. The issuing dresses them in something that is not theirs, so the garment they wear most is the one they have no discretion over. The wearing supplies an authority and withdraws a person: the public wants a function rather than an acquaintance, the wearer is spared having their own character be the subject of every encounter, and praise and abuse both land on the category. The taking off removes the garment and none of the standard -- the rules against wearing it in a bar are an admission that the office does not switch off when the clothing does. The engine's twist is in the anchor: a uniform lets its wearer do what they could not and stops them doing what they could, and both run on the same substitution.

## System

- Do show the issuing. Measured, two of these and three of those, a size that is nearly right, and a form to sign.
- Do let the licence work first. An instruction obeyed by a stranger without argument, and everybody in the scene treating that as ordinary, because it is.
- Do address the office rather than the person. Members of the public asking the garment questions, complaining to it, occasionally shouting at it, with no information about who is inside.
- Do let the wearer be relieved by it. Acting as the office is easier than acting as themselves, and that is most of why the substitution is welcome.
- Do put praise and abuse on the same surface. Neither is recorded against or credited to the person, and a whole shift can pass with nobody meeting them.
- Do make the off-duty rule explicit somewhere. Not to be worn in a bar, not in that photograph -- a small institutional sentence that concedes the whole mechanism.
- Do stage a recognition out of uniform. A supermarket, a Sunday, somebody who has dealt with the office and now has an opinion about the person.
- Do not model the stripping at the door (total-institution), the managed front (presentation, face, backstage), the object carrying an identity (extended-self, heirloom, totem, investiture), the insider's speech (register), restraint in a mass (crowd, conformity), or the source of the authority (power, hierarchy, obedience). Do not make the clothing stand for a theme; that is authored key, the play's pitch. Do not edit the engine's files; wire only from the play's side.
