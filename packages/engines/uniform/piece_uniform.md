# Piece: Uniform

## Taxonomy

The garment that replaces a person with an office (no khai file of its own yet).

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: uniform

## Place

On the body, during the hours the office is held, and in a locker or a wardrobe the rest of the time.
It is almost never owned. It is issued, sized, signed for, laundered under somebody else's arrangements, replaced on a schedule, and surrendered on leaving -- so a persona spends a third of their life in clothing that belongs to an employer, a service, or a school.
Its place is the boundary between a person and the public that has to deal with them.

## Load Bearing

What it holds up is that this one may act. The nurse may touch a stranger, the officer may stop a car, the inspector may enter a kitchen, the child may be in the town at eleven on a Tuesday -- and nobody asks any of them for a reason, because the garment has already answered.
It also holds up the other side of that: it tells anybody in trouble which of the forty people in a corridor to approach, which is a service the wearer performs simply by being visible.
Remove it and every one of those acts needs an explanation, and most of them do not survive one.

## Apparent

Read as a description of the wearer and worn as a claim about the office.
What shows is a category: rank, service, trade, school, and everything the observer already believes about that category, arriving before the person does.
What actually shows is that somebody has been vouched for by an institution and is currently answerable to it, which is a narrower and more reliable statement than the one people take from it.

## Yearbook

The arc runs from the fitting to the daily wearing to the return of the kit. It is worn out rather than kept, and the few that are kept -- the dress uniform in a wardrobe, the blazer in a loft -- are kept as evidence of a period rather than as clothing.
The engine's twist is here. **A uniform lets its wearer do what they could not and stops them doing what they could.** Both directions run on the same fact, which is that the garment substitutes an office for a person: the office can give orders, enter rooms, and be obeyed by strangers, none of which the persona could do in their own coat; and the office can also be recognised in a supermarket, reported for what it said in a pub, and held to a standard at two in the morning by somebody who has never met them. Neither the licence nor the constraint is negotiable, and they arrive together in one bag.
It is not the stripping of a self at the door of a closed institution, which is total-institution's, nor the front a persona manages for an audience, which is presentation's, nor the specialised speech that marks an insider, which is register's; and a costume worn to **stand for** a theme is authored key, the play's pitch.
It fails by being worn where it is not owed, by being recognised off duty, and by outlasting the wearer's belief in the office.
