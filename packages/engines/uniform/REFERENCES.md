# Uniform: Reference

## Line of Work

Uniform as **piece**: the garment that replaces a person with an office, and what it does in both
directions.

The engine's subject is an object that is worn more hours than anything the wearer owns and is not
theirs. Joseph and Alex's account is the spine, and it identifies the mechanism precisely: a uniform is
a certificate of legitimacy issued by a group, worn on the body, and readable at a distance -- so it
suppresses the individuality of the person wearing it and confers on them the standing of the
organisation behind it, in one movement. Everything in this engine follows from those two being the
same movement rather than two effects that happen to co-occur.

The boundary with what khai already carries is clean. The stripping of possessions, name, and clothing
at the door of a closed institution is **total-institution**'s, in its mortification expression; this
engine reads a garment worn by people who go home at six. The front a persona manages for an audience,
and what it costs to maintain, are **presentation**'s and **face**'s. The specialised speech that binds
insiders and gates outsiders is **register**'s, whose badge mode is the linguistic version of what this
object does visually. The object into which an identity has extended, the article withheld to carry a
lineage, and the thing a collective lodges its we in are **extended-self**'s, **heirloom**'s, and
**totem**'s, gathered by the **investiture** composite -- and a uniform is none of them, because it is
issued rather than held and returned rather than passed on.

**What it holds up** is that this one may act: the nurse may touch a stranger, the officer may stop a
car, the inspector may enter a kitchen, and nobody asks any of them for a reason. It also tells anybody
in trouble which of forty people in a corridor to approach, which is a service performed by being
visible. **What is apparent** is a category and everything the observer already believes about it,
arriving before the person does; what actually shows is that somebody has been vouched for by an
institution and is currently answerable to it.

**The wearer** holds an authority nobody granted them personally and a wardrobe they did not buy. They
may order strangers in the narrow matters the office covers -- move along, wait here, roll up your
sleeve -- most of which would be impertinent from a person in ordinary clothes. They lose the option of
being an individual in public, since a rudeness is the service being rude and a kindness is the service
being kind. **The issuing** dresses them in something that is not theirs. **The wearing** supplies an
authority and withdraws a person. **The taking off** removes the garment and none of the standard.

Hence the **twist**, in the anchor's Yearbook. **A uniform lets its wearer do what they could not and
stops them doing what they could.** Both directions run on the same fact, which is that the garment
substitutes an office for a person. The office can give orders, enter rooms, and be obeyed by strangers,
none of which the persona could do in their own coat. The office can also be recognised in a
supermarket, reported for what it said in a pub, and held to a standard at two in the morning by
somebody who has never met the person inside it. Neither the licence nor the constraint is negotiable,
and they arrive together in one bag.

The engine is built so that nobody has to be abusing anything for it to run, and a play should keep it
that way. The wearer wanted the office. The licence is what makes the work possible. The standard is
one most wearers hold themselves to anyway.

**On the evidence, stated plainly.** This engine's spine is sociological argument rather than
measurement, and the reader should have that first. **Joseph and Alex is an essay, not a study.** The
1972 account of the uniform as a certificate of legitimacy that suppresses individuality is a
theoretical synthesis with illustrations, of the kind that persuades by showing rather than by testing,
and it is doing most of the load-bearing here. **Craik's history is cultural and comparative**, which
gives the object a very long record and no controlled comparison anywhere in it. **The engine declines
the most famous evidence in this area on purpose.** The best-known demonstration that a uniform changes
its wearer's conduct is the Stanford prison study, and its evidentiary status has collapsed -- the
demand characteristics, the coaching of guards, and the selection of participants are now documented
well enough that it cannot support a claim about anything; no member here depends on it, and a reader
who arrives expecting it should treat its absence as deliberate. The adjacent deindividuation
literature is genuinely contested and belongs to **crowd** and **conformity** rather than here. **The
off-duty constraint is attested and unmeasured.** That the standard travels beyond the shift is visible
in occupational codes, in disciplinary cases, and in what police officers and teachers in small towns
say about their private lives, and nobody has put a figure on it. **The engine flattens enormous
variation.** A surgeon's scrubs, a judge's robes, a school blazer, a chef's whites, a soldier's dress
uniform, and a supermarket tabard differ in almost everything -- how much authority they carry, whether
the wearer chose the office, whether they are worn with pride or resentment -- and the common core this
engine reads is thinner than any one of those cases. What should not be taken from this engine is that
uniforms are instruments of control, that wearers are diminished by them, or that the licence is
illegitimate: most wearers value the garment, many wear it with pride, the protection it gives against
being personally at stake in a hard encounter is real, and the work it makes possible is work somebody
has to do. The claim is narrow: it is issued rather than owned, it licenses what the person could not
do, and it constrains what the person could.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The stripping at the door (total-institution, socialization, liminality)**: The admission sequence
  in which possessions, name, and clothing are taken and a self is processed out is total-institution's
  mortification. This engine reads a garment worn by people who go home, and stops at that boundary.
- **The managed front (presentation, face, backstage, self-monitoring)**: How a persona stages a self
  for an audience, what a face costs to maintain, and where the performance is dropped are those
  engines'. A uniform is issued rather than assembled, and its wearer did not choose what it says.
- **The object carrying an identity (extended-self, heirloom, totem, investiture)**: The possession a
  self has extended into, the article withheld to carry a lineage, and the thing a collective lodges
  its we in are those packages'. A uniform is lent for the duration of a post and handed back.
- **The insider's speech (register, membership, social-identity)**: The specialised variety of language
  that binds and gates, what belonging costs, and what a group membership does to a self-concept are
  those engines'. This object does visually what register does audibly, and the mechanism is theirs.
- **The loosening of restraint in a mass (crowd, conformity, moral-disengagement)**: What happens to
  restraint in a group, and how conduct gets released from a persona's own standards, are those
  engines'. The engine deliberately makes no deindividuation claim.
- **The authority itself (power, hierarchy, obedience, status)**: Where the office's authority comes
  from, how it is arranged, and why people comply are those engines'. The uniform is read as the thing
  that makes the authority visible and portable, not as its source.
- **The trade (the world)**: Procurement, specification, safety standards, laundering contracts, dress
  codes, and the law on impersonating an officer are matters of practice and statute. They set the
  format; this engine reads what the garment does to the person in it.
- **A costume as authored symbol (the play's pitch)**: Clothing put on a persona in order to **stand
  for** a theme -- the coat that mirrors a motif of duty -- is refused: that is authored key, the play's
  pitch. Uniform sets what the garment licensed and what it cost; the meaning is the play's.

## Encoding

Source to constraint. A piece engine: one anchor, one position, three processes, all flat beneath the
object.

- **[uniform](piece_uniform.md)** (the anchor): clothing issued rather than owned, worn for the hours an
  office is held, carrying the twist -- a uniform lets its wearer do what they could not and stops them
  doing what they could. Anchored by Joseph & Alex (1972).
- **[the wearer](position_the_wearer.md)** (the position): the holder of an authority nobody granted
  them personally, who may order strangers in narrow matters and loses the option of being an
  individual in public. Anchored by Joseph (1986).
- **[the issuing](process_the_issuing.md)**: how it arrives and on whose terms, dressing the persona in
  something that is not theirs. Anchored by Craik (2005).
- **[the wearing](process_the_wearing.md)**: what it does while it is on, supplying an authority and
  withdrawing a person. Anchored by Entwistle (2000) and Joseph & Alex (1972).
- **[the taking off](process_the_taking_off.md)**: what does not come off with it, since the standard has
  no boundary in time or place. Anchored by Joseph & Alex (1972) and Craik (2005).
