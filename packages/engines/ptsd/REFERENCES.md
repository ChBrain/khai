# PTSD: Reference

## Line of Work

PTSD as a **process** engine: post-traumatic stress disorder, the condition that can follow exposure to actual or threatened death, serious injury, or violation, when the memory of the event is not processed into ordinary past-tense autobiography but stored raw and fires as though the danger were present -- taken across the four symptom clusters DSM-5 names. The domain does not model the acute stress response to a present demand, which is the stress engine's; nor adaptation to irreversible loss, which is the grief engine's; nor future-directed dread of what has not happened, which is the anxiety engine's; nor the present-threat fear response, which is the fear engine's; nor the general machinery of encoding and retrieval, which is the memory engine's; nor the general physiology of arousal, which is the body engine's. It models the disorder itself: a trauma that will not become past, and the self-maintaining loop that keeps it so. It is authored persona-architecture -- the structure of a traumatized character, for a play -- and never a diagnosis of a real person.

The through-line is a mechanic, not a mood, and that is the point of the engine. PTSD is not simply feeling bad after something bad; it is a loop that feeds on the attempt to escape it. A cue resembling the trauma fires the re-experiencing; the body answers with hyperarousal; the pain drives avoidance; and the avoidance, by bringing relief before the memory can be processed, preserves the raw trace intact and forecloses the very updating that would end it -- so the loop turns again, and the estrangement deepens. This maintaining cycle, more than any single symptom, is what the modern science explains, and what the engine stages. The warrant is a lineage, not solo work: the disorder was first observed as the "war neurosis" or "shell shock" of combatants (Kardiner) and named in Freud's traumatic neurosis, formally codified as PTSD in DSM-III (1980) under the weight of veterans' and rape-trauma research, and refined into the four-cluster criteria of DSM-5; and its mechanism was grounded by the cognitive and emotional-processing models -- Foa's fear structure and the extinction that avoidance blocks, Ehlers and Clark's maintaining processes, Brewin's dual representation of why the memory returns as present, and van der Kolk's account of the body's stuck alarm. The engine takes the disorder as its anchor and the four DSM-5 clusters -- reexperiencing, evasion, estrangement, hyperarousal -- as its members. The altitudes are those: the disorder and its loop, and its four clusters. (Complex PTSD, the disturbances in self-organization that follow prolonged, repeated trauma, is read as a composite over this engine and others -- Herman; Cloitre; ICD-11 -- not here.)

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The acute stress response (stress)**: The engine models the disorder, not the immediate reaction to a present demand. The fight-flight-freeze-fawn routing of a demand that exceeds resources is the stress engine's; PTSD is what can follow when that response does not stand down and the memory of an overwhelming event is left unprocessed. Stress is the response to a danger that is here; PTSD is the response to a danger that is over but treated as here. Most who meet a trauma have a stress response and recover; the disorder is the minority in whom the loop sets.
- **Loss and its adaptation (grief), low mood (sadness)**: The engine models trauma, not loss. Adaptation to an irreversible loss -- the oscillating work of mourning -- is the grief engine's, and persistent low mood is the sadness engine's; the estrangement cluster's corroded belief and numbing are specifically trauma-wrought, keyed to threat and horror rather than to bereavement or to depression as such. Grief is the reckoning with what is gone; PTSD is the reckoning with what will not become past.
- **Future dread (anxiety), present threat (fear)**: The engine models a past event re-firing, not the anticipation of a future one or the response to a current one. Future-directed dread of what has not happened is the anxiety engine's, and the fear response to a present threat is the fear engine's; PTSD's fear is neither -- it is a past danger returning as though present, triggered by resemblance. The tense is the tell: anxiety faces forward, fear faces the now, PTSD is dragged backward.
- **The general machinery (memory, body)**: The engine models the traumatic memory and the stuck alarm, not the ordinary faculties they distort. The encoding, consolidation, and deliberate retrieval of memories is the memory engine's; the general physiology of bodily arousal is the body engine's; PTSD owns only the specifically traumatic case -- the memory that returns involuntarily as present, and the arousal that will not stand down. It borrows the faculties; it does not model them.
- **Clinical assessment (out of domain)**: The engine is character architecture for authoring a persona, not a diagnostic instrument. It does not set thresholds, durations, or differential diagnoses for a real person, and nothing in it is a clinical judgment; the DSM criteria are used as the structure of a truthful character, not as a test to apply to anyone. Diagnosis and treatment belong to clinicians, not to a persona engine.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per file.

- **[ptsd](process_ptsd.md)** (the root, process): the disorder as a self-maintaining loop -- an unprocessed traumatic memory that fires as present, clamped down by an avoidance that keeps it unprocessed. Anchored by DSM-5 (the disorder), Ehlers & Clark and Foa (the maintaining loop), and Brewin (the memory that returns as present).
- **[reexperiencing](process_reexperiencing.md)** (process, cluster B): the past forced into the present -- intrusion, nightmare, flashback, and bodily distress at cues. Anchored by DSM-5 and Brewin (the cue-triggered sensory return).
- **[evasion](process_evasion.md)** (process, cluster C): the avoidance of trauma reminders, inner and outer -- the relief-reinforced manoeuvre that maintains the disorder. Anchored by DSM-5, Foa (extinction foreclosed), and Ehlers & Clark (maintaining behaviors).
- **[estrangement](position_estrangement.md)** (position, cluster D): the persistent negative alterations in cognition and mood -- corroded belief, distorted blame, numbing, detachment. Anchored by DSM-5 and Ehlers & Clark (the negative appraisals).
- **[hyperarousal](process_hyperarousal.md)** (process, cluster E): the body kept on a war footing -- hypervigilance, startle, irritability, broken sleep. Anchored by DSM-5 and van der Kolk (the recalibrated alarm).
