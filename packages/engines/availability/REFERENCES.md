# Availability: Reference

## Line of Work

Availability as **process**: the estimation through which a persona judges how frequent or likely a class of events is by how easily instances of it come to mind. The domain does not model the storage and retrieval machinery the ease rides on, which is memory's; nor the pull of a first figure on a numeric estimate, which is anchoring's; nor the mode by which a choice among options is reached, which is decision's; nor the standing catalogued tilt the whole look leaves on the read of a rate, which the bias engine holds among the cognitive biases. It models the reading of ease as frequency itself: the fluency of bringing instances to mind, and the substitution of that fluency for the true rate.

The spine is the psychology of judgment under uncertainty. Asked to estimate the frequency of a class or the probability of an event, a person often answers by the ease with which instances of it are brought to mind -- a heuristic that serves when ease tracks frequency, and misleads when it does not. It is one of the original three heuristics of judgment, the sister of anchoring and representativeness, and it runs through more than one route: the retrievability of stored instances, the effectiveness of a search set, and the imaginability of instances that must be constructed. The three forms are those routes: retrievability, searchability, imaginability.

## Origin

The psychology of the availability heuristic in judgment under uncertainty, applied directly as process constraints.

| :----------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Amos Tversky & Daniel Kahneman**         | _"Availability: A Heuristic for Judging Frequency and Probability"_ (1973) | The root: frequency and probability judged by the ease with which instances come to mind, and the enumeration of its biases -- retrievability of instances, effectiveness of a search set, and imaginability. The origin of the whole engine and its forms. |
| **Amos Tversky & Daniel Kahneman**         | _"Judgment under Uncertainty: Heuristics and Biases"_ (1974)               | Availability set among the three heuristics of judgment under uncertainty, and its systematic errors -- the vivid, recent, and easily searched judged more frequent than they are. The root.                                                                |
| **Norbert Schwarz et al.**                 | _"Ease of Retrieval as Information"_ (1991)                                | The metacognitive refinement: it is the felt ease of retrieval, not the number of instances retrieved, that drives the judgment -- recalling many examples, if effortful, can lower the estimate below recalling few. The retrievability form.              |
| **Sarah Lichtenstein, Paul Slovic et al.** | _"Judged Frequency of Lethal Events"_ (1978)                               | Availability in risk perception: causes of death that are dramatic, vivid, or heavily reported are overestimated and quiet, common ones underestimated, tracking memorability rather than the actual rates. The retrievability and imaginability forms.     |
| **John S. Carroll**                        | _"The Effect of Imagining an Event on Expectations for the Event"_ (1978)  | Imaginability made causal: merely imagining an outcome in concrete detail raises its judged likelihood, so the ease of constructing a scenario stands in for the probability of the event. The imaginability form.                                          |

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **Memory**: The engine models the reading of ease as frequency, not the storage and retrieval that produces the ease. How instances are encoded, indexed, and recalled is owned by the memory engine; availability rides on that machinery but owns the inference -- the felt fluency taken for the true rate -- not the recall itself.
- **Anchoring**: The engine models the estimate read from ease of recall, not the estimate pulled toward a reference value. The drawing of a numeric judgment toward a first figure is owned by the anchoring engine; the two are sister heuristics of judgment under uncertainty with distinct levers -- ease of retrieval here, a starting number there.
- **Decision**: The engine models a frequency or likelihood estimate, not the mode by which a choice among options is reached. How a persona decides -- intuitively, deliberately, by heuristic or satisficing -- is owned by the decision engine; availability supplies a judgment such choosing may use, and owns the estimation, not the choice.
- **Bias**: The engine models the live estimation from ease, not the static tilt it leaves. The catalogued cognitive tendency -- the standing overweighting of what comes easily to mind -- is owned by the bias engine as nodes in its codex; availability is the process of which those tilts are the residue, and owns the reading of ease as frequency, not the entries in the list.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per file.

- **[availability](process_availability.md)** (the root): The estimate read from ease of recall -- how frequent or likely a class is judged by how readily its instances come to mind, the memorable taken for the common. Anchored by Tversky & Kahneman (availability as a heuristic for judging frequency and probability).
- **[retrievability](process_retrievability.md)** (form): The recall route -- the recent, vivid, and charged surfacing first, and their easy retrieval inflating the judged rate; it is the felt ease, not the count, that is read. Anchored by Tversky & Kahneman (retrievability of instances) and Schwarz et al. (ease of retrieval as information).
- **[searchability](process_searchability.md)** (form): The search-set route -- the estimate depending on how memory is searched, a class easy to search along one dimension judged more frequent than one hard to search. Anchored by Tversky & Kahneman (the effectiveness of a search set).
- **[imaginability](process_imaginability.md)** (form): The construction route -- for events not stored, the ease of constructing or picturing an instance standing in for its probability. Anchored by Tversky & Kahneman (biases of imaginability) and Carroll (imagining an event raising its judged likelihood).
