# Tightness-Looseness: Reference

## Line of Work

Tightness-looseness as a **position** engine: the strength of the social norms a persona lives under and the latitude its world grants for deviance -- taken across the two normative regimes a world can hold. The domain does not model the persona's yielding to a norm, which is the conformity engine's; nor its yielding to an authority, which is the obedience engine's; nor the formation of a particular group's norm, which is the group engine's; nor the persona's own self-monitoring and impulse control, which are its own engines'; nor the individual self-boundary of independence or interdependence, which is the self-construal engine's. It models the strength of the norm field itself -- how clear and strongly enforced the rules are, and how much a world tolerates a step out of them -- the constraint the situation exerts before anyone conforms or refuses.

The spine is Gelfand's cross-cultural work on cultural tightness-looseness. Her 33-nation study establishes tightness -- the strength of social norms and the severity of sanctioning of deviance -- as a coherent cultural dimension distinct from individualism-collectivism, measured by how many strong norms a society has and how little deviant behavior it tolerates. Tight societies have pervasive monitoring, clear and strongly enforced rules, and more self-regulation and impulse control; loose societies have weaker norms, more tolerance for deviance, and more openness and disorder. The account is ecological and functional: tightness rises with a society's history of ecological and human threat -- natural disaster, disease, resource scarcity, population density, and the threat of invasion -- because strong norms coordinate a group under threat, and relaxes in threat's absence, where the constraint costs more than it buys. Her later synthesis extends the logic across levels -- nations, states, organizations, households, and the strong-versus-weak situations any person moves between -- and traces what each regime trades: order and coordination against openness and creativity. The altitudes are those: the strength of the norm field, and its tight and loose poles.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **Conformity**: The engine models the strength of the norm field, not the persona's yielding to it. Conformity -- a persona bending its judgment or behavior toward a group's norm or majority (Asch; Cialdini) -- is owned by the conformity engine; tightness-looseness is upstream of it, the severity of the field that sets how much pressure there is to conform in the first place. A tight regime makes conformity more pressed and deviance costlier; whether a given persona yields is conformity's, the strength of the field is this engine's.
- **Obedience**: The engine models the norm field, not compliance with an authority. Obedience -- a persona yielding to the command of a legitimate authority (Milgram) -- is owned by the obedience engine; tightness-looseness is the diffuse strength of the norms themselves, not the vertical demand of a particular authority. A tight world tends to back its authorities, but the norm field and the authority's command are distinct sources of pressure.
- **Group**: The engine models the strength of norms in the abstract, not the formation of a specific group's norm. The group engine owns how a particular group forms, holds, and polices its own norms; tightness-looseness is the standing severity of the norm field a persona's world runs on, the regime within which any particular group's norming happens. The regime is the climate; the group's norm is one weather system in it.
- **Self-construal**: The engine models the culture's norm strength, not the individual's self-boundary. Self-construal -- whether a persona defines itself as an independent individual or an interdependent, connected self -- is owned by the self-construal engine; tightness-looseness is the orthogonal cultural dimension of how strong the norms are, distinct from how the self is bounded. The two form a culture cluster -- a world has both a norm strength and a modal self-construal -- but they are separate axes: tight and loose cut across independent and interdependent.
