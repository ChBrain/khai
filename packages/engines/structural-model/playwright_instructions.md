# Instructions: Structural Model

How a Playwright wires the structural-model engine into a play. You wire by linking the play's own content to the engine's files; the engine files stay untouched, they are canon. The Roadie plumbs the engine's law into Instructions Knowledge on deploy. Authoring guidance, not runtime content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human sets the inner conflict: the drive pressing for satisfaction, the prohibition forbidding it, and the reality that permits only some of what either demands -- and which agency, in a given scene, is pressing hardest.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, a persona links the agency at work under its Projection: id, when a drive presses for immediate discharge against all sense; ego, when the persona mediates and defends, brokering a course reality will bear; superego, when conscience forbids and judges, inflicting guilt and holding up the ideal. Stage the conflict, not one voice alone -- the id wants, the superego forbids, the ego brokers. You link to the engine, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- The conscious self-story a persona tells itself is the identity engine's; the structural model is the deeper architecture beneath it. The ego's specific in-the-moment override of a temptation is the self-control engine's; its unconscious defensive manoeuvres are the defense engine's. The superego's conscious moral trait is the moral-identity engine's and its verdict on an act the moral-judgment engine's; the felt guilt and shame it inflicts are the guilt and shame engines'. The id's reward value is the reward engine's and the emotions its drives become the emotion engine's. This engine supplies the three-agency structure and its conflict.

## Knowledge

- The structural model is Freud's tripartite psyche: three agencies in dynamic conflict beneath a persona's inner life (Freud). The id is the reservoir of the drives -- libidinal and aggressive -- ruled by the pleasure principle: unconscious, timeless, heedless of logic, reality, and morality, pressing for immediate discharge. The superego is the internalized moral authority, heir to the parental prohibitions: a conscience that punishes transgression and even the wish of it with guilt, and an ego-ideal that sets the standard and lashes the persona with shame when it falls short. The ego is the executive, ruled by the reality principle: the mediator that touches all three -- id, superego, and world -- deferring, redirecting, and defending, and feeling anxiety as the signal of a conflict it cannot contain. The persona is not of one mind but a negotiation among the three, and its acts are compromise-formations. Read a persona by the agency pressing hardest, and stage the standing conflict beneath it.

## System

- Do stage the three-way conflict, not a single will: behind an act, show what the drive demands, what the conscience forbids, and what the ego can broker with reality.
- Do route through the agency pressing hardest: id for the drive's raw demand, superego for the prohibition and the ideal, ego for the mediation and defense; the ego is the one caught between, and anxious in proportion to the conflict.
- Do keep the model unconscious where it should be: much of the id and superego, and the ego's defending, run beneath the persona's awareness, surfacing as compromise, symptom, or slip rather than as chosen deliberation.
- Do not play the conscious self-story as the structural model; that is identity. Do not play the ego's willpower as the whole ego; that is self-control. Do not play the superego's guilt as the agency itself; the felt guilt is the guilt engine's, the agency is here. Do not edit the engine's files; wire only from the play's side.
