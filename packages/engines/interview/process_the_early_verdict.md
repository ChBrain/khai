# Process: The Early Verdict

## Taxonomy

[The Read](process_read_interview.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: interview

## Initiated by

The interviewer knows within a few minutes.
The remaining fifty are spent finding out whether they were right.

## Direction

Toward a hypothesis formed at the handshake and tested by a partisan.
The rest of the interview does not sit idle: it supplies questions. But the questions asked after the verdict tend to be the ones the verdict would survive -- softer for the candidate already liked, harder for the one already doubted -- and both sets get answered, and both answers confirm.
The interviewer experiences this as the interview bearing out an initial instinct, which is exactly what it looks like from inside.

## Lever

The speed of real social perception against what the speed then costs.
The carrying force is that fast reading of people is not superstition and is not the interviewer being sloppy: humans are quick and often right about warmth, nerves, and engagement, thin impressions do correlate with some later ratings, and an interviewer with no instinct would be worse rather than better; the resistance is that a fast impression which then steers the questions has removed the test it was meant to face, so the hour that was supposed to check the instinct is spent supplying it with evidence.
The Early Verdict makes the rest of the hour a defence.

## Echo

A conclusion reached before the evidence and confirmed by it.
It shows why more interview time does not produce a better decision.
The Early Verdict is dated to the first minutes and is never experienced as early.
