# Process: The Keeper

## Taxonomy

[Interview](process_interview.md), why the practice survives every demonstration against it.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: interview

## Initiated by

The evidence has been in for decades and nothing has changed.
The Keeper is what holds it in place: [a missing half](process_the_missing_half.md) nobody ever sees, [a shared ceremony](process_the_shared_ceremony.md) that both sides want, and [a next panel](process_the_next_panel.md) that loosens what the last reform tightened.

## Direction

Toward a practice defended for something other than what it is measured on.
Structured interviewing is cheap, published, and taught. Institutions adopt it, and within a few hiring rounds most of them are running loose interviews again, with no decision to that effect ever taken.
The engine reads that reversion as evidence about what the interview is for. It is not held because anybody believes the evidence is wrong; it is held because it is doing something the evidence does not measure.

## Lever

The genuine functions of the encounter against the one it is credited with.
The carrying force is that the occasion does real work nobody wants to lose: the candidate gets to see who they would report to and turns jobs down on it, the panel commits to a person rather than to a file and then supports them, and an appointment nobody met feels arbitrary to everybody it lands on; the resistance is that all of those are functions of meeting, none of them requires the meeting to be the thing that decides, and it decides anyway.
The Keeper rides the engine's keystone knife-edge: the interview contributes least to the decision and supplies all of the confidence in it.

## Echo

A practice that is kept for the certainty and defended for the accuracy.
It shows the three earlier movements complete -- an improvised instrument, a read taken from the wrong material, a picture that could not have failed to form -- and adds the reason none of it gets corrected.
This is why the Keeper is the hardest of the four to stage and the only one worth closing on: the panel must be conscientious, the questions must be reasonable, the candidate must be treated well, and the audience must be able to watch a careful process produce a certainty it did not earn.
A play that stages the panel as prejudiced has replaced the mechanism with a villain, and one that stages the interviewer as a fool has replaced it with a fable.
The Keeper is dated from every previous round and is renewed at the next vacancy.
