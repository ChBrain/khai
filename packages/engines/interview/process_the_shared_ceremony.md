# Process: The Shared Ceremony

## Taxonomy

[The Keeper](process_keeper_interview.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: interview

## Initiated by

Both sides want the meeting to happen.
An appointment made without one offends the candidate who was not seen and the panel who did not see.

## Direction

Toward an occasion that legitimates whatever it produces.
The candidate wants their hour: a chance to be considered as a person rather than as a form, to ask their own questions, to have been met. The institution wants it too, since a decision taken from paper alone is hard to defend to the team, to the unsuccessful, and sometimes to a tribunal.
Everybody is right about this. The hour is the form in which a person is taken seriously, and taking people seriously is not optional.

## Lever

The dignity of being seen against the authority the seeing then claims.
The carrying force is that the ceremony is owed and abolishing it would be a real loss: refusing to meet somebody before deciding their livelihood treats them as a record rather than a person, candidates rate an unmet decision as unfair even when it goes their way, and the meeting is where an employer earns the right to be turned down; the resistance is that having granted the hour, nobody can then treat it as ceremonial -- a meeting held to honour the candidate is scored as though it were held to assess them, and the honour becomes the evidence.
The Shared Ceremony is owed to the candidate and is then used against the file.
What a fair hearing buys is heeding's and recognition's; this reads only the appointment.

## Echo

A courtesy that hardened into a measurement.
It shows why the practice cannot simply be dropped, whatever the studies say.
The Shared Ceremony is dated from the invitation and is valued by both parties.
