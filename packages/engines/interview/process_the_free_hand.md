# Process: The Free Hand

## Taxonomy

[The Room](process_room_interview.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: interview

## Initiated by

The interviewer decides what to ask, in what order, and what a good answer is.
Usually while the candidate is talking.

## Direction

Toward discretion at every point where a comparison needs a constant.
Structure is the one thing repeatedly shown to raise an interview's accuracy: fixed questions asked in a fixed order, anchored rating scales, scoring each answer as it comes rather than forming a view at the end. Each of those constraints is available, cheap, and unpopular.
They are unpopular because each one removes something the interviewer experiences as the useful part.

## Lever

The interviewer's competence against the constraint that would improve it.
The carrying force is that the resistance to structure is not laziness and comes from experienced people: the fixed script cannot follow the interesting thread, it makes the encounter stilted for a nervous candidate, and treating a skilled interviewer as an interchangeable reader of cards is both insulting and wasteful of what they can actually do; the resistance is that the accuracy gain from structure is one of the larger and better replicated findings in the field, and the practice keeps drifting back to the loose form anyway -- not by decision, but by each panel quietly reverting.
The Free Hand is defended as skill and behaves as noise.

## Echo

A different examination for every candidate, run by the person marking it.
It shows where the accuracy is lost, and that the loss is chosen without ever being decided.
The Free Hand is dated from the first question and is reconstructed afterwards as a method.
