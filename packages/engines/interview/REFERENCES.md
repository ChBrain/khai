# Interview: Reference

## Line of Work

Interview as **process**: a stranger judged in a meeting arranged for the purpose, and what an hour does
to the person who has to decide.

The engine's subject is an instrument that everybody uses, nobody rates highly, and no institution will
give up. Highhouse's account is the spine, and it names the thing precisely: the persistence of
subjective selection is not an information problem, because practitioners who are shown the evidence
keep the practice anyway. What they are unwilling to surrender is not accuracy but the experience of
having judged -- and the loose interview is the only part of a hiring process that supplies it.
Everything the engine reads follows from taking that seriously rather than treating the practice as a
mistake somebody has yet to correct.

The boundary with what khai already carries is clean. The disagreement between a standardised
instrument and a practitioner's own judgement, and the tally of departures from it, is **overriding**'s;
this engine reads the occasion rather than the instrument dispute. The candidate's management of their
own front is **presentation**'s and **face**'s. The halo, the overconfidence, and the sample selected on
its outcome are **bias**'s positions, and one of them arrives here structurally. The general appetite
for a coherent picture is **bias**'s and **surprise**'s; the general form of accounting for a decision
is the **moral-account** composite's and **attribution**'s. What the job turns out to demand once it is
won is **employment**'s, the credit given to an account is **credibility**'s, and the documented case
built by an institution's examinations is **discipline**'s.

**The room** improvises the instrument and then trusts the readings. **The short hour** is the right
length for the institution and the wrong length for the question, since what it samples well is how
somebody handles being questioned by a stranger who holds something they want. **The two performances**
are honest advertisements read as disclosures: the candidate has rehearsed, the employer is describing a
role more coherent than the role, and each scores the other's version as evidence. **The free hand** is
the point where accuracy is lost, and the loss is never decided -- structure is available, cheap, and
unpopular, because every constraint it imposes removes something the interviewer experiences as the
useful part.

**The read** is a true account of the wrong hour's worth of evidence. **The early verdict** makes the
remainder of the hour a defence rather than a test, since the questions asked after a view has formed
are the ones the view survives. **The fit test** finds resemblance and reports merit: what evaluators
reach for when explaining a decision is whether the candidate was somebody they could be stuck in an
airport with, and the material of that judgement is largely class-marked, so an intake selected on fit
reproduces the room that selected it without anybody intending it. **The told story** converts the same
history into different evidence, and the return on it goes to whoever has told it before.

**The sense made** is why the room never feels wasted. **The coherent stranger** is a full portrait
assembled from forty minutes and held with the confidence of a colleague's judgement. **The absorbed
answer** is the finding this movement rests on: interviewers questioning candidates who had been
instructed to answer at random still formed a picture, still rated the exchange informative, and still
preferred it to the record -- so the instrument has no reading for absence. **The reasons after** are
composed downstream of the verdict, from an hour that would have supported several, and are then filed
as the institution's account of why.

Hence the **twist**, at the root. **The interview contributes least to the decision and supplies all of
the confidence in it.** The file, the record, the work sample, and the test each carry more of what can
be known about a candidate, and none of them produces the sensation of having met somebody. The hour
does, and the decision is hung on the sensation, which is why the loose interview cannot be argued out
of an institution: what is being defended is not its accuracy, it is the only part of the process that
feels like judging rather than filing.

The engine is built so that nobody has to be prejudiced for any of it to run, and a play should keep it
that way. The panel was conscientious. The questions were reasonable. The candidate was treated well.

**On the evidence, stated plainly.** This engine sits on a large empirical literature and on one small
memorable experiment, and the difference matters. **The structured-versus-loose gap is one of the better
replicated findings in personnel selection**, and it is the engine's firmest leg: adding fixed questions,
a fixed order, and answer-by-answer scoring raises predictive validity, and the practice drifts back
anyway. **The headline validity numbers everybody quotes were re-estimated in 2022 and most of them came
down**, some substantially, and the ordering of methods changed with them -- so any account that leans on
a particular coefficient is leaning on a figure the field has already revised. This engine leans on the
relative claim and on the reversion behaviour instead, which survives the correction; that is a choice,
and it is worth saying that a reader who wants a number will not find a stable one. **The absorbed
answer is the engine's weakest joint.** Dana, Dawes and Peterson's random-answer study is a small
laboratory design with student participants, it is the single most load-bearing citation here, and it
has not been reproduced at scale in field hiring -- the movement it anchors should be read as a
compelling demonstration rather than as a settled quantity. **Rivera's fit mechanism is established in
one sector of one country**: elite investment banking, consulting, and law firms, where cultural
matching is unusually consequential, and the engine states it as though it were general when the
evidence for a hospital ward or a warehouse is thinner. **The early verdict is stated more carefully
here than it usually is.** The popular version -- that interviewers decide in the first few seconds --
is not well supported; what is supported is that an impression formed early changes the questions asked
afterwards, which is a smaller claim and the one the member makes. **The next panel's reversion is an
observation, not a measurement**: that structure is adopted after a bad appointment and relaxed within a
few rounds is consistent with adoption surveys and with practitioner accounts, and nobody has tracked
the decay curve. What should not be taken from this engine is that interviews should be abolished, that
interviewers are fools or bigots, or that structure is a cure -- structured panels show fit effects too.
The claim is narrow: the instrument is improvised, the read comes from the wrong material, the picture
cannot fail to form, and the practice is kept for the certainty.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The instrument-versus-judgement dispute (overriding, expertise, measure)**: When a standardised
  rule and a practitioner disagree, what the departure is made of, and what the tally of departures
  shows are that composite's and those engines'. This one reads the encounter, including the case where
  no instrument exists to be departed from.
- **The candidate's front (presentation, face, impression, deception)**: How a persona manages what is
  shown of them, what a face costs to maintain, and what a concealed record does are those engines'.
  The two-performances member reads only that both accounts in the room are prepared.
- **The halo, the overconfidence, and the selected sample (bias)**: Those are positions the bias engine
  owns. The read and the missing half name their arrival and hand the mechanisms back.
- **The appetite for a coherent picture (bias, surprise)**: Why people assemble a whole from fragments,
  and what happens when the assembly fails, are those engines'. This one reads the specific consequence
  that an interview has no null result.
- **Accounting for a decision afterwards (moral-account, attribution, repair)**: How an account is
  offered and received, how causes get assigned, and how a wrong is repaired are those packages'. The
  reasons-after member reads the selection file and nothing wider.
- **The job once it is won (employment, hierarchy, socialisation)**: The effort bargain, the reporting
  line, and how a newcomer is made into a member are those engines'. The interview ends at the offer.
- **Who is allowed to apply at all (capital, heritage, credentialism, gentrification)**: The
  distribution of qualifications, contacts, and cultural inheritance that determines who reaches the
  room is those engines'. The fit test reads what happens once two candidates are already in it.
- **The design of the process (the world)**: Job specifications, scoring rubrics, employment law,
  competency frameworks, and the practicalities of shortlisting are matters of trade and statute. They
  set the format; this engine reads what an hour with a stranger does to the person deciding.
- **The panel as authored symbol (the play's pitch)**: An interview staged in order to **stand for** a
  theme -- the board that mirrors a motif of judgement -- is refused: that is authored key, the play's
  pitch. Interview sets what the hour supplied and what was built from it; the meaning is the play's.
