# Process: Distress

## Taxonomy

[Compassion](process_compassion.md), the self-focused shadow.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: compassion

## Initiated by

Another's suffering is taken in too closely, and instead of warm concern the persona is flooded with its own aversive arousal -- anxiety, upset, the urge to escape the discomfort.
This is empathic, or personal, distress (Batson; Singer & Klimecki): the self-focused mirror of compassion, where the other's pain becomes the persona's own overwhelming feeling.
It wears the face of caring but runs on self-protection.

## Direction

Away -- toward the relief of the persona's _own_ distress, not the other's.
Where compassion approaches to help, distress withdraws to escape: it turns from the sufferer, avoids the reminder, and seeks its own calm, because the pain has become unbearable to the self.
The direction is the self relieved, and the sufferer, in the turning-away, left.

## Lever

Over-identification without regulation -- the empathic channel open too wide, the other's state absorbed as one's own without the buffer that keeps it theirs.
What distress spends is the capacity to stay and help: flooded, the persona must attend to itself, and the sufferer goes unaided.
It is the near-enemy of compassion, close enough to be mistaken for it, opposite in where it turns.

## Echo

The sufferer is avoided or abandoned, and the persona, unrelieved, drifts toward the exhaustion of chronic exposure -- distress is the road to withdrawal and, over time, to the caregiving engine's burnout.
It is why compassion must be regulated to endure: the difference between distress and compassion is the difference between drowning with the other and reaching to pull them out (Singer & Klimecki).
The behavioral exhaustion it leads to is the caregiving engine's burnout; the regulation that converts distress back into compassion is the regulation engine's.
