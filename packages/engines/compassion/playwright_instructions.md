# Instructions: Compassion

How a Playwright wires the compassion engine into a play. You wire by linking the play's own content to the engine's files; the engine files stay untouched, they are canon. The Roadie plumbs the engine's law into Instructions Knowledge on deploy. Authoring guidance, not runtime content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human defines the compassion terrain: whose suffering the persona witnesses, how serious and how deserved it reads, and whether the persona meets it as a fellow or from above -- so there is a real hardship to answer, a warmth to feel, and a relief to reach for.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, a persona links the compassion the moment runs under its Projection: which face (tenderness or succor) or which shadow (distress or pity), whose suffering is appraised, and how tenderhearted the persona's standing disposition is. Let it read as a warm turn toward another's suffering with the wish to relieve it -- and mark when it curdles into the distress that flees or the pity that looks down. You link to the engine, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- The capacity to take in another's inner state is the empathy engine's; the behavioral system that protects and provides, and its burnout, is the caregiving engine's; the response to one's own loss is the sadness and grief engines'; the uplift at another's virtue is the elevation engine's; the cold downward look that dismisses is the contempt engine's. This engine supplies the warm response to another's suffering -- the tenderness, the reaching to relieve, and the shadows that counterfeit them.

## Knowledge

- Compassion is the warm response to another's suffering: a persona witnesses another's serious, undeserved hardship and is moved -- a warm, other-focused concern with the impulse to relieve it -- saying not I have lost (sadness) but you suffer, and I would ease it. Where empathy shares the state, compassion adds the wish to help; where sadness answers one's own loss, compassion answers another's. It has two faces and two shadows. Tenderness is the warm affective core -- the soft, protective fondness toward the vulnerable. Succor is the action tendency -- the reaching to soothe and relieve, what makes it more than sympathy. Distress is the self-focused shadow -- the other's pain flooding the self so the persona withdraws to relieve its own arousal, the road to burnout. Pity is the distanced shadow -- the feeling-for from above that holds the sufferer as lesser, care with the fellow-feeling missing. Tenderhearted is the trait -- a standing readiness to be moved by suffering and to reach to relieve it.

## System

- Do make compassion turn toward the sufferer and their relief, not away toward the self's own comfort: it approaches where distress flees -- so let the persona move to help, warmed rather than overwhelmed.
- Do keep the shadows legible and distinct from the real thing: distress wears caring but runs on self-protection and withdrawal; pity wears care but keeps the sufferer below -- let each be mistaken for compassion by those who feel it, and read right by the production.
- Do let the felt core be tenderness and the motive be succor: the warmth that makes help feel like care, and the reaching that makes it help at all -- and let over-reaching succor tip toward the caregiving engine's overprotection.
- Do let tenderheartedness set the threshold: the tenderhearted persona is moved by suffering others miss, and is the more exposed to distress and burnout; the guarded meet the same suffering unmoved. Do delegate the empathic capacity to the empathy engine and the behavioral providing and its burnout to the caregiving engine. Do not edit the engine's files; wire only from the play's side.
