# Compassion: Reference

## Line of Work

Compassion as a **multi-type** engine: the warm, other-focused emotion through which a persona meets another's suffering and is moved to relieve it, taken across its faces, its shadows, and the trait that tunes it. The domain does not model the capacity to take in another's inner state, which is the empathy engine's; nor the behavioral system that protects and provides for a vulnerable other, which is the caregiving engine's; nor the response to one's own loss, which is the sadness and grief engines'; nor the uplift at another's virtue, which is the elevation engine's. It models the emotion itself: the appraisal of another's undeserved suffering, the warm concern it draws, and the motivation to relieve it.

The spine is the affective science of compassion. Compassion is, in Goetz, Keltner and Simon-Thomas's account, a distinct emotion elicited by the perception of another's suffering and functioning to motivate caregiving -- an appraisal of serious, undeserved hardship, a felt warmth and concern, and an approach-and-help motivation. It is set apart most sharply from empathic (personal) distress: where compassion is other-focused, warm, and approach-oriented, personal distress is self-focused, aversive, and withdrawal-oriented, and it is the road to avoidance and burnout (Batson's empathic concern vs personal distress; Singer and Klimecki's compassion vs empathic distress, with its distinct neural signatures and trainability). It is set apart, too, from pity -- the feeling-for from above that holds the sufferer as lesser or apart, lacking the fellow-feeling of compassion. Its felt core is tenderness, its motivation is succor, and individual differences in dispositional compassion tune the whole. The altitudes are those: the emotion, its two faces and two shadows, and its trait.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **Empathy**: The engine models the warm response to suffering, not the capacity that takes the suffering in. The baseline channel for apprehending another's inner state -- how open it is and in what form -- is owned by the empathy engine (a position); compassion runs through that channel but is a distinct emotion, the caring answer to what empathy apprehends. This engine keeps the response; empathy keeps the capacity.
- **Caregiving**: The engine models the emotion, not the behavioral system it motivates. The system through which a persona is moved to protect and provide for a vulnerable other -- with its sensitivity, overprotection, and burnout -- is owned by the caregiving engine; compassion is the feeling that drives caregiving, and caregiving is the doing. The exhaustion that empathic distress leads to is caregiving's burnout, not this engine's.
- **Sadness and Grief**: The engine models the response to another's suffering, not to one's own loss. The low-approach emotion at one's own loss is the sadness engine's, and the sustained adaptation to a bereavement is the grief engine's; compassion is turned outward, toward a sufferer who is not oneself, though a shared humanity may connect the two.
- **Elevation and the other-praising emotions**: The engine models the response to another's suffering, not to another's good. The uplift at witnessed virtue is the elevation engine's, and the warm regard for another's benefit or skill is the gratitude and admiration engines'; compassion answers a hardship, the praising emotions answer a good.
- **Contempt**: The engine models pity as care held at a superior distance, not the cold downward look that dismisses. The placing of another beneath the level at which they merit consideration is owned by the contempt engine; pity is the softer look-down -- concern that keeps the sufferer below -- and where it curdles into dismissal it hands off to contempt.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per file.

- **[compassion](process_compassion.md)** (the root, process): The warm, other-focused response to another's undeserved suffering, and the motivation to relieve it. Anchored by Goetz, Keltner & Simon-Thomas (the emotion) and Batson (other-focused concern).
- **[tenderness](process_tenderness.md)** (process, face): The warm affective core -- the soft, protective fondness toward the vulnerable and the hurt. Anchored by Goetz et al. (the tender appraisal) and Gilbert (the affiliative-soothing system).
- **[succor](process_succor.md)** (process, face): The action tendency -- the reaching to soothe, aid, and relieve, what separates compassion from a sympathy that only feels. Anchored by Goetz et al. (the caregiving motivation).
- **[distress](process_distress.md)** (process, shadow): The self-focused shadow -- empathic distress, where the other's pain floods the self and the persona withdraws to relieve its own arousal, the road to burnout. Anchored by Batson (personal distress) and Singer & Klimecki (empathic distress).
- **[pity](process_pity.md)** (process, shadow): The distanced shadow -- the feeling-for from above that holds the sufferer as lesser or apart, care with the fellow-feeling missing. Anchored by Nussbaum and Solomon (the compassion/pity distinction).
- **[tenderhearted](position_tenderhearted.md)** (position, trait): Dispositional compassion -- a standing readiness to be moved by suffering and to reach to relieve it, tracking agreeableness and the caregiving temperament. Anchored by Sprecher & Fehr (the Compassionate Love Scale) and Shiota et al.
