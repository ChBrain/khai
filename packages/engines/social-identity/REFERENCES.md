# Social Identity: Reference

## Line of Work

Social identity as **position**: the part of the self a persona draws from the groups they belong to -- the group-belonging "we" that the identity engine's "I" is the complement of. The domain does not model the personal self-concept, the individual life-story of who one is -- that is the identity engine's; nor the systematic in-group biases on perception -- the in-group favouritism, out-group homogeneity, and system justification that the bias engine owns as tilts. It models the self drawn from category membership: which groups the persona falls in, what those groups are worth in the order they read, and how they hold a group whose standing is in question.

The spine is the social-identity account: a person categorises the world into in-group and out-group, identifies with the in-group, compares it against out-groups, and is driven toward positive distinctiveness because the self is lifted when the group is. When the comparison comes out unfavourable, the response is not fixed but chosen along the read of the status structure -- whether boundaries are permeable, whether the order is legitimate, whether it is stable. The four stances are those responses: identification (hold and merge), mobility (leave), creativity (reframe), competition (contest).

## Origin

The social psychology of intergroup relations, applied directly as position constraints.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **Identity**: The engine models the group-belonging "we", not the personal "I". The individual self-story -- who the persona is as one person, and how firmly and on what basis they hold it -- is owned by the identity engine; this engine is the group component the identity engine delegates, not the personal one.
- **Bias**: The engine models the self drawn from group membership, not the systematic slant on intergroup perception. In-group favouritism as a bias, out-group homogeneity, the ultimate attribution error, system justification, and groupthink are owned by the bias engine as resting tilts; this engine governs the self-categorisation and the drive for positive distinctiveness, and leaves the perceptual bias to bias.
- **Group**: The engine models the self a persona draws from belonging, not the group's development over time. How a set of people forms, storms, norms, and performs into a working whole is owned by the group engine; this engine governs the membership as a source of self, not the group's own life.
- **Loyalty**: The engine models the categorisation of the self into the group, not the standing allegiance that raises the cost of leaving it. The commitment that binds a persona to a person, group, or cause is owned by the loyalty engine; this engine governs the "we" the persona reads themselves into, which loyalty may then hold them to.
- **Stigma** and **Status**: The engine models the group's standing as a source of the persona's self, not the discrediting mark or the rank itself. The spoiled identity of a discredited attribute is the stigma engine's, and the place in a deference order is the status engine's; this engine governs how the group's standing is drawn into the self and held when it is low.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per file.

- **[social identity](position_social_identity.md)** (the root): The self drawn from group membership -- the world sorted into in-group and out-group, the in-group's standing taken as the persona's own, the drive toward positive distinctiveness. Anchored by Tajfel & Turner (the theory) and Turner et al. (self-categorisation).
- **[identification](position_identification.md)** (stance): The secure merger of self with the in-group -- solidarity, favouritism, the depersonalised self standing with its own; the baseline the others depart from. Anchored by Tajfel (the minimal group) and Brewer (optimal distinctiveness).
- **[mobility](position_mobility.md)** (stance): The individual exit -- pass into a higher-standing group, boundaries read as permeable, standing changed by changing sides. Anchored by Tajfel & Turner (social mobility) and Ellemers (permeability).
- **[creativity](position_creativity.md)** (stance): The reframe -- hold the group but change the terms of comparison to recover positive distinctiveness without changing the order. Anchored by Tajfel & Turner (social creativity) and Ellemers (impermeable but legitimate order).
- **[competition](position_competition.md)** (stance): The contest -- mobilise with the group to change the actual status relations, where the order is read as illegitimate and unstable; the turn into intergroup conflict. Anchored by Tajfel & Turner (social competition) and Ellemers (illegitimacy and instability).
