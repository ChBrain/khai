# Process: Misfire

## Taxonomy

[Detonation](process_detonation.md), the mode of the shot that did not go, the silence that is the hazard.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: detonation

## Initiated by

A misfire is a detonation that was supposed to occur but did not -- a broken wire in the detonator circuit, a device that failed, a power supply that did not deliver, or a charge that did not initiate for a reason that cannot be known until it is approached and examined.
The misfire transforms a controlled charge into an unpredictable one: the explosive is still there, still armed, still primed to detonate at the next opportunity, and that opportunity might be vibration from nearby work, the shock from nearby blasting, or someone walking near the site who knows nothing of what lies beneath the surface.
The misfire is the detonation that waits in the ground, a threat that time does not diminish but only extends.

## Direction

Into the ground or into the building where the charge lies, then waiting -- potentially forever until disturbed.
The misfire's direction is backwards, into the past: the site is secured, the exclusion zone expanded, the countdown is halted, and now all the confidence that governed the controlled shot is replaced by caution and the discipline of the wait.
The direction is inward, into investigation: every misfire must be approached with extreme care, with procedures designed to trigger it safely or render it safe, or with the doctrine of leaving it alone and quarantining the site.

## Lever

The unknown against every assumption of control.
The carrying force is the explosive that remains armed in the ground; the resistance is the doctrine that forbids approach until the misfire has been addressed -- the thirty-minute wait in standard blasting practice before anyone enters the exclusion zone, and the master's inspection of the circuit and devices to find the reason.
The paradox is that silence is the hazard: a successful detonation proves the charge worked, but failure proves nothing, teaches nothing, and the charge that did not detonate the first time remains equally ready to detonate the second, uncontrolled.

## Echo

The sudden silence where the blast should have been, then the alarm of discovery and the changed rules.
A misfire is read by its signature -- the moment when the shot timer counts down to zero and nothing happens, the audio alarm that the firing was attempted and failed, the absence of the detonation that was supposed to come, the shockwave that does not arrive.
A misfire is dated from the failed firing moment, the instant when the confidence in control is revealed as incomplete, when the site turns from a planned detonation to an emergency -- no longer a proceeding but a problem to be solved before anything else can happen.
