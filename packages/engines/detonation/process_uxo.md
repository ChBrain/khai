# Process: Unexploded Ordnance

## Taxonomy

[Detonation](process_detonation.md), the mode of the unexploded that waits, the war that keeps firing after it ended.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: detonation

## Initiated by

Unexploded ordnance is a wartime detonation that never occurred -- the bomb dropped and buried in mud, the shell fired and lodged in earth or water or a wall, the mine laid and never found, all containing the explosive charge and the fuze or initiator that would have detonated them at impact, or on a time delay, or triggered by contact or vibration.
The war is over but the ordnance remains, inert and waiting, a buried consequence that persistence and time do not erase -- shells and bombs surfacing in the spring when the fields thaw, in construction sites when the earth is disturbed, in rivers when the water level drops, even decades after the war that fired them.
The UXO is a detonation held in abeyance, a shot that was fired but never completed, waiting for the circumstance that will finish what time interrupted.

## Direction

Lying dormant in the ground, then upward into the life and work of the place when discovered or disturbed.
The unexploded ordnance has no direction of its own until it is triggered: it has waited in darkness for a decade or a century, and the direction it then takes is entirely determined by the type of ordnance, the fuze, the soil, and the force that set it off -- an impact fuze may fire when a plow strikes it, a time-delay fuze may fire when the conditions change, and the direction of the blast is whatever the buried shell or bomb decides in its own moment.
The direction of mine action is inward: the clearance specialist approaches the site, searches for the ordnance, identifies it, and handles it with procedures designed to render it safe or remove it or, in the most dangerous cases, to detonate it in a controlled way at a safe distance.

## Lever

The abundance of the ordnance against the capacity to clear it.
The carrying force is the legacy of the war: in Flanders, the iron harvest continues to yield shells from the fields a century after the trenches fell; in German cities, WWII bombs are discovered during construction and evacuations of tens of thousands of people are ordered while the bomb is safely removed or detonated; in the seas and soils of every combatant nation, the ordnance waits.
The resistance is the work of clearance: the training, the equipment, the international standards (IMAS) that govern how UXO is searched for, identified, and handled, and the coordination that mine action demands -- a generational effort to make a place safe from a war that ended before anyone now living was born.

## Echo

The discovered shell or bomb, the evacuation zone, the controlled detonation at a remote site.
UXO is read by its signature -- the artifact from a past war, recognized by its shape and size and often its markings, sometimes corroded beyond recognition and identified only by X-ray or careful excavation, sometimes perfectly preserved in the cold wet ground; the moment of discovery in a field or a construction site, the alarm that spreads, the evacuation that follows.
UXO is dated from the war that fired it, but understood by the day it is found -- the moment the past erupts into the present, the instant that clearance becomes urgent and the place is quarantined until the ordnance is handled or destroyed.
