# Instructions: Detonation

How a Playwright wires the detonation engine into a play. You wire by linking the play's own
content to the engine's files; the engine files stay untouched, they are canon. The
Roadie plumbs the engine's law into Instructions Knowledge on deploy. Authoring
guidance, not runtime content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human sets which places carry a detonation that acts -- the controlled shot at a quarry or demolition site, the sympathetic chain that reveals siting failure, the misfire that waits in the ground, the unexploded ordnance from a past war, or the precision breach of a structure -- and whether that detonation is the place's standing condition, a live event in the play, or a historical burden the place carries.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, a place whose detonation is an actor links under Shown the phenomenon of its blast or blast-risk: the shot that controls it, the sympathetic chain it risks, the misfire that waits, the UXO it carries, or the breach it shows. Play the detonation as the force itself shown -- the crater, the blast-damaged buildings, the exclusion markers, the ordnance on the ground -- present in what the place puts before whoever enters, never narrated, and never as a mirror of a persona's own explosion or shattering. Keep it the detonation itself, never the making -- the substance, the hardware, and the engineering are refused outright, not delegated -- and hand the fire the blast starts to fire and combustion, and the decision to detonate to the play. You link to the engine, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- The substance, the hardware, and the engineering are refused outright -- licensed practice outside the canon's altitude; the fire a blast ignites is fire's and combustion's; and the decision to demolish or detonate is the play's own -- detonation reads the force itself, the shock and the crater and the doctrine of standoff. The precision demolition must be true to how buildings actually fall, and the unexploded ordnance must be grounded in the history of the wars that fired it. Whether the phenomenon rings true and at the right scale is the team's review.

## Knowledge

- Detonation is the blast as its own force: the supersonic pressure wave that outruns combustion, the shock that writes itself into the place as craters and overpressure rings and exclusion doctrine (Cooper's detonation wave). It wires on a place at Shown, and it is the force itself -- typed by the particular blast (the shot, sympathetic, misfire, UXO, or breach), read at doctrine and investigation altitude, never at the altitude of formulation or hardware. Five phenomena carry it -- the shot is the controlled firing, the countdown and the siren and the moment nothing can be adjusted; sympathetic is the chain where one detonation forces the next, and the siting and spacing doctrine that prevents it; misfire is the shot that did not fire, the wait imposed before approach, the silence that is hazard not success; UXO is the unexploded ordnance from a past war surfacing in fields and construction sites, mine action and clearance as a generational task; and breach is the precision demolition, the building's fall designed as carefully as its standing. None of these includes the making -- substance, hardware, engineering, all refused -- and the fire the blast starts is fire's, the decision to blast the play's. Read a place by the phenomenon its detonation shows.

## System

- Do give a place whose detonation is an actor its phenomenon under Shown; do play the detonation as the force itself shown, present not narrated; do keep the reading the blast -- the shot, the sympathetic chain, the misfire, the UXO, the breach -- not a feeling and not a symbol.
- Do read UXO honestly: the war is over but the ordnance remains, and the place is a site of clearance, not of closure. The unexploded bomb does not sit innocently; it is waited for, searched for, and handled with disciplines honed by decades of casualty. Read the ordnance at the scale of the place's actual risk and history.
- Do not model the substance, the hardware, or the engineering (refused outright -- never in any member or play), the fire the blast starts (that is fire and combustion), or the decision to detonate (the play). Do not make the blast mirror a persona's own shattering or explosion (that is the play's pitch). Do not edit the engine's files; wire only from the play's side.
