# Process: Flexure

## Taxonomy

[Wear](process_wear.md), the mode this use spends.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: wear

## Initiated by

A part is worked back and forth, and the repeated stress accumulates until it fails.
Flexure starts wherever the object is flexed, bent, loaded, and released again and again -- the hinge opened a thousand times, the cable run over the pulley, the wire bent to and fro, the spring compressed and let go -- and each cycle leaves a little damage the material cannot undo.
It is the quiet wear: nothing is rubbed away, and yet the part is being used up from within.

## Direction

Into the material, weakening it toward the break.
The metal fatigues, the plastic crazes, the fibre frays, the spring takes a set and goes soft; the damage gathers unseen at the flex point until a crack starts, spreads, and the part lets go -- often all at once, and often when it is trusted most.
Flexure works where the stress concentrates -- the fold, the notch, the root of the tooth -- so the object fails at the place it bends, not the place it looks worn.

## Lever

The material's endurance against the cycles worked into it.
The carrying force is the repetition -- each bend a small withdrawal from a finite account of cycles; the resistance is the material's toughness and the design that eases the stress, spreading the bend so no one point takes it all.
Stay under the material's limit and it endures indefinitely; work it past, cycle after cycle, and the account is spent and the part cracks -- the wear that gives no warning on the surface.

## Echo

The part failed from within, its surface still whole.
It shows a break where nothing looked worn -- the snapped cable, the cracked bracket, the hinge sheared at the pin, the spring gone slack -- and carries beneath it the history the surface hid: a thousand uses each taking a little, and the last one only the one that finished it.
Flexure is dated from the count of cycles, and it is wear's hidden mode: the object used up not where it rubbed but where it bent.
