# Process: Seasoning

## Taxonomy

[Wear](process_wear.md), the mode this use spends.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: wear

## Initiated by

The same use that ruins one object improves another, and the wear becomes worth.
Seasoning starts wherever using a thing makes it better and more its owner's -- the boot broken in, the glove molded to the hand, the pan cured by cooking, the tool handle worn to fit the grip, the jeans faded to the body, the instrument opened up by playing.
It is wear read the other way: not the loss of the object's function but the settling of it into a fit that only use could give.

## Direction

Into the object, tuning it to its user and its task.
Where abrasion takes the surface and flexure the strength, seasoning lays down a fit -- the stiffness worked out, the shape learned, the surface brought to the sheen that a thousand handlings give and no factory can; the object grows into its use rather than out of it.
Seasoning moves toward the individual object, the one worn by these hands to this shape, no longer interchangeable with its unused twin.

## Lever

The improvement use confers against the wear it also spends.
The carrying force is the same use that wears the other modes -- but here the material takes the use as a gift, the leather suppling, the metal burnishing, the wood mellowing; the resistance is the point past which even seasoning tips into loss, the broken-in boot worn finally through.
Every seasoned object rides the line: the use that made it yours is the same use that is, slowly, still spending it.

## Echo

The object worn in, not out -- better for the use, and unmistakably its owner's.
It shows the burnished handle, the molded glove, the faded and fitted cloth, and carries beneath it wear's twist -- that use is not only subtraction, that a thing can be improved and individualized by the very wear that ruins its neighbour, and that the mark of a hand is worth more than the factory finish it replaced.
Seasoning is dated from the hours of use it took to earn, and it is wear's answer to decay's patina: not time settling on a neglected thing, but use settling into a loved one.
