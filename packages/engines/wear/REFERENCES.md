# Wear: Reference

## Line of Work

Wear as **process**: the degradation an object undergoes through use rather than
through time alone -- the object spent by the doing of its work. The domain does
not model the use-independent entropy that takes a thing whether it is used or not,
which is the decay family's; nor the mending that answers the wear, which is the
restoration engine's; nor the standard-shift that dates a still-working object,
which is the obsolescence engine's. It models degradation by use, and reads it two
ways: the object worn out, and the object worn in.

The spine is that use is a force with its own physics and its own record. Wear is
decay's complement -- decay is what happens to a thing left alone (rust, rot,
crumble), wear is what happens to a thing worked (abrasion, flexure, depletion) --
and where the two families meet is the used-and-neglected object that takes both.
Its facts come from the engineering of wear (tribology's friction and abrasion,
the fatigue of a part cycled to failure) and from the anthropology of the used
object (the tool that carries the legible record of its own use, the possession
individualized by handling). So the engine gives use four modes: three of loss --
abrasion, flexure, depletion -- and one twist, seasoning, where the same use that
spends one object improves another.

## Origin

The engineering of wear and the anthropology of the used object, applied directly
as engine constraints across process.

| Source                                               | Key Work                                              | Scope                                                                                                                                                     |
| :--------------------------------------------------- | :---------------------------------------------------- | :-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **David Pye**                                        | _The Nature and Art of Workmanship_ (1968)            | How a made thing bears use and shows it -- the workmanship that meets, or fails to meet, the wear its use will bring. The root.                           |
| **F. P. Bowden & David Tabor**                       | _The Friction and Lubrication of Solids_ (1950, 1964) | Tribology: the physics of friction and the removal of material where surfaces move against each other. The abrasion mode.                                 |
| **August Wohler**                                    | the fatigue (S-N) curve of metals (1860s)             | Material fatigue: a part cycled below its static strength still fails after enough cycles, the damage gathering unseen until the crack. The flexure mode. |
| **Sergei A. Semenov**                                | _Prehistoric Technology_ (1964)                       | Use-wear analysis: the worn object as the legible record of its own use, the traces of work read back from the tool. The root, and each mode as evidence. |
| **Mihaly Csikszentmihalyi & Eugene Rochberg-Halton** | _The Meaning of Things_ (1981)                        | The used object individualized and made meaningful by handling -- the possession worn into a fit no unused twin has. The seasoning mode.                  |

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The use-independent entropy (the decay family)**: Wear is what use spends;
  decay is what time and the elements take whether the object is used or not. The
  rust on the unused chain, the rot in the stored timber, the crumble of the
  untouched stone are the decay family's; the bald tread, the fatigued spring, the
  spent cell are wear's. The unused thing decays where it sits; the used thing wears
  in the hand -- and a thing both used and neglected takes both, each family reading
  its own share. (The decay engine today is place-typed; an object-decay is charted
  as a sibling element of the object-lifecycle, and wear pairs with it there.)
- **The mending that answers it (restoration)**: Wear is the degradation;
  restoration is the hand that mends it. The re-soling, the re-blading, the
  re-charging, the re-building are restoration's (its continuation stance is the
  standing answer to wear); wear is the spending restoration reaches in to reverse.
- **The standard-shift that outdates it (obsolescence)**: Wear is physical
  degradation by use; obsolescence is a working object rendered out of date by a
  shifted standard, not by any loss of its own. The worn shoe no longer functions;
  the obsolete phone functions and is merely outmoded. Different forces, often
  confused: one is spent, the other superseded.
- **The static fit to the body (ergonomics)**: The object's designed fit to the
  hand and the body is the ergonomics engine's; wear is what the using of that fit,
  over time, does to the object. Ergonomics is the handle sized to the hand; wear
  is the handle worn by the hand.
- **A persona's own exhaustion, and the object as symbol**: The tiredness of a
  person is the body engine's (fatigue there is the persona's, not the object's);
  wear delivers the object's degradation, not a feeling. And the worn object made to
  **stand for** a character's own wearing-down is refused: that is authored key, the
  play's pitch, not the object's force.
- **The mode itself is not engine content**: The engine declares the four modes
  exist; which one a given object undergoes lives on the piece, linked under its
  Load Bearing chapter. The engine refuses to fix an object's wear for it.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per file.

- **[wear](process_wear.md)** (the root): the degradation an object takes from use
  rather than time, spending its capacity to do its work. Anchored by Pye (the
  workmanship that meets its use) and Semenov (the worn object as the record of use).
- **[abrasion](process_abrasion.md)**: the surface rubbed away by friction, the
  object made grain by grain smaller and its working surface lost. Anchored by
  Bowden & Tabor (tribology).
- **[flexure](process_flexure.md)**: the part weakened and cracked by repeated
  stress, the hidden wear that fails from within. Anchored by Wohler (fatigue).
- **[depletion](process_depletion.md)**: the consumable spent, the store drawn down
  to nothing, use that leaves nothing to mend.
- **[seasoning](process_seasoning.md)**: the twist -- the same use improving and
  individualizing the object, worn in rather than out. Anchored by Csikszentmihalyi
  & Rochberg-Halton (the meaning of the used thing); wear's answer to decay's patina.
