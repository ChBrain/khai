# Non-Place: Reference

## Line of Work

Non-Place as **place**: the space built for passing through, and what it stops requiring of the people
inside it.

The engine's subject is a category of space that almost everybody uses daily and almost nobody counts as
somewhere they have been. Augé's account is the spine, and it identifies the mechanism rather than the
mood: what distinguishes these spaces is not that they are ugly or new but that the relation to them is
contractual. A persona is verified at the boundary -- ticket, passport, card, membership number -- and
having been established as entitled, they are thereafter a passenger, a customer, or a driver and
nothing else. Augé's phrase for what the space produces is solitude and similitude: a crowd of people
unrelated to each other, in a room nearly identical to the equivalent room in forty other cities.

The boundary with what khai already carries is clean, and one neighbour is the exact opposite number.
The informal public gathering place with regulars, neutral ground, and standing earned by returning is
**third-place**'s; this engine reads the space that cannot recognise anybody on return, because it has
no concept of one. The setting that refills depleted attention is **restorative-environment**'s. The
threshold state between a prior identity and what comes next is **liminality**'s -- a condition of a
persona rather than a property of a room. The enclosing institution that runs a whole life under one
authority is **total-institution**'s, which is this engine's other opposite: one place demands
everything and the other demands nothing.

**Transit** is the route that cannot be arrived at. Every version of a good outcome for everybody
present involves leaving, so nothing that happens inside can be a purpose -- which is also why it grants
the persona a licence available almost nowhere else, to be legitimately unoccupied and visibly waiting
for something outside their control. **The contract** offers entitlement without acquaintance: a seat
owed by a system rather than by anybody's goodwill, which is precisely what makes the space usable by
strangers, and which leaves a persona outside its terms with nobody to appeal to. **Solitude** is
proximity without contact, held stably for hours, and it rests a persona who is exhausted by being
somebody.

Hence the **twist**, in the anchor's Withheld. **A non-place checks who you are in order to have no
further use for it.** The identity is verified exactly once and then discarded. That is what makes the
space restful, since being nobody in particular is a holiday from every determination a persona carries
everywhere else; and it is what makes it empty, since a space that cannot know anybody cannot hold
anything for them and will not have registered that they were ever in it.

The engine is built so that nobody has to have designed anything badly for it to run, and a play should
keep it that way. Nobody built it to alienate. Every feature solves a real problem. The indifference is
a consequence rather than an intention.

**On the evidence, stated plainly.** This is a theoretical engine with a well-known concept at its
centre and a serious objection standing against it, and the objection is kept. **Augé is an essay in
anthropology, not a study.** _Non-Places_ is a short theoretical work that proposes a category and
illustrates it; there is no measurement in it and none has been done since that would settle whether
the category carves anything at a joint. **The strongest criticism is that the category describes an
intention rather than an experience, and it is probably right.** Ethnographies of motorways, airports,
supermarkets, and service stations find exactly what Augé says is absent: people who work there and
know each other, drivers with attachments to particular junctions, regulars in the concourse coffee
shop, relationships begun and ended in departure lounges, and travellers for whom a specific terminal
carries decades of memory. A space designed to produce no relations does not thereby produce none, and
this engine's category is a description of the design brief that the users routinely defeat. **This
engine departs from its own source on the central valuation, and says so.** Augé's tone about
non-places is largely elegiac, and the reading here is not: the anonymity is treated as a genuine good
that many people seek deliberately, and the engine gives the relief and the emptiness the same feature
rather than treating the second as the truth of the first. That is an extension, it is not Augé's, and
a reader should not attribute it to him. **The similitude claim has weakened since 1992.** Chain
uniformity was a sharper observation about the late twentieth century than it is now, when many such
spaces are deliberately localised, branded to their city, and full of regional produce -- which does not
change the contractual relation but does undercut the sameness. What should not be taken from this
engine is that these spaces are bad, that their designers are careless, or that a persona who likes
them is missing something. The claim is narrow: the relation is contractual, the verification happens
once, and the same indifference is both the rest and the absence.

## Origin

The anthropology of transit space, wired as one place on a persona. The twist follows from Augé's
account of the contractual relation; the objection to it is Merriman's and is carried in the honesty
note rather than buried.

| Source                | Key Work                                                                             | Scope                                                                                                                                                                               |
| :-------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Marc Augé**         | _Non-Places: An Introduction to an Anthropology of Supermodernity_ (1992; Eng. 1995) | **The twist.** Space entered by contract, where identity is verified at the boundary and discarded inside, producing solitude and similitude. The anchor and all three expressions. |
| **Peter Merriman**    | _Driving Spaces: A Cultural-Historical Geography of England's M1 Motorway_ (2007)    | The main objection: motorways and their service areas are full of relations, memory, and place-making, so the category describes a brief rather than an experience.                 |
| **Michel de Certeau** | _The Practice of Everyday Life_ (1984)                                               | Users making tactics inside spaces designed by somebody else, which is what defeats the design brief in practice. Behind the honesty note's objection.                              |
| **Ray Oldenburg**     | _The Great Good Place_ (1989)                                                        | The informal gathering place with regulars and standing. Owned by the third-place engine; named here because this engine is its opposite number.                                    |
| **Erving Goffman**    | _Behavior in Public Places_ (1963)                                                   | Civil inattention: the minimal mutual acknowledgement strangers give each other in shared space. Owned by presentation and its neighbours; the solitude expression reads its floor. |

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The gathering place with regulars (third-place, dwelling, hospitality)**: Neutral ground, the
  leveller, the home away from home, and what a host owes are those packages'. This engine is that
  engine's opposite number and should be used where the space cannot recognise a returning persona.
- **The threshold state (liminality, rite, role-exit)**: The suspension between a prior identity and
  what comes next is a condition of a persona, not a property of a room. A non-place can host one and
  does not constitute one.
- **The enclosing institution (total-institution, discipline, subjection)**: The place that runs sleep,
  work, and play under one authority is total-institution's. That place demands everything of a persona
  and this one demands nothing, which is the whole difference.
- **The setting that restores attention (restorative-environment, restoration, boredom)**: What refills
  depleted directed attention, and what an unoccupied mind does, are those engines'. The rest this
  engine describes is relief from obligation rather than recovery of capacity.
- **What a place comes to mean (place-attachment, nostalgia, memory)**: The bond to a location and what
  it carries are those engines'. The engine reads a space that is designed to accumulate none of it, and
  the honesty note records that people accumulate it anyway.
- **The wait itself (waiting, commute, queue norms)**: Delay as a technique of institutions, the daily
  crossing between home and work, and what standing in line does are those engines'. The transit
  expression reads the room, not the duration.
- **The design and its rules (the world)**: Wayfinding standards, security regulation, retail layout,
  franchise agreements, and the economics of throughput are matters of trade and statute. They set the
  format; this engine reads what the space stops asking of the people in it.
- **A concourse as authored symbol (the play's pitch)**: A transit space staged in order to **stand
  for** a theme -- the terminal that mirrors a motif of rootlessness -- is refused: that is authored key,
  the play's pitch. Non-place sets what the space requires and withholds; the meaning is the play's.

## Encoding

Source to constraint. A place engine: one anchor and three expressions.

- **[non-place](place_non_place.md)** (the anchor): the space built for passing through, entered by
  contract and asking nothing once inside, carrying the twist -- a non-place checks who you are in order
  to have no further use for it. Anchored by Augé (1992).
- **[transit](place_transit.md)** (expression): the route that cannot be arrived at, which grants a
  licence to be legitimately unoccupied and offers no way to make the time count. Anchored by Auge
  (1992).
- **[the contract](place_contract.md)** (expression): entitlement owed by a system rather than by
  anybody's goodwill, exact in what it covers and offering no margin outside it. Anchored by Auge
  (1992).
- **[solitude](place_solitude.md)** (expression): proximity without contact, resting a persona
  exhausted by being somebody and leaving nobody present for whom they are anybody. Anchored by Auge
  (1992), with Goffman's civil inattention as its floor.
