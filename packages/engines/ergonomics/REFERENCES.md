# Ergonomics: Reference

## Line of Work

Ergonomics as **piece**: the object's physical fit to the human body -- how its
dimensions, reach, clearance, and force demand meet or fail the body that must use
it. The domain does not model how the form communicates its use, which the
usability engine owns; nor what conduct the object enforces, which the agency
engine owns; nor the body's own states, which the body engine owns on a persona.
It models the object's physical accommodation of the body: the design of things to
human measure.

The spine is Dreyfuss's design to human measure -- the object dimensioned not to
one body but to the real range of bodies, so it fits the hand, falls within the
body's reach, leaves the body room to pass, and demands no more force than the body
can sustain. Anthropometrics and biomechanics supply the measure. The engine takes
the object's bodily fit four ways: the sizing (anthropometry), the reach envelope
(reach), the clearance (clearance), and the force demand (exertion) -- so a piece
can declare not only that it fits or fails the body but by which measure it does so.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The object's usability (the cognitive pole)**: The usability engine owns how the
  form communicates its use to the mind -- legible design. This engine owns how the
  form fits the body -- physical accommodation. The two are orthogonal: a knob can be
  perfectly legible (usability) and too small for the hand to turn (ergonomics); a
  signifier is _read_, a dimension is _met_.
- **The object's agency (the political pole)**: The agency engine owns what conduct the
  object enforces on whom. Ergonomics is amoral physical fit -- it has no program, only
  a measure. A bench that excludes the large by too-tight clearance is ergonomics (a
  misfit); a bench built to keep out the sleeper is agency (a program). Where a misfit
  is _weaponised_ into exclusion, it crosses to agency.
- **The body's own states (the body engine, on a persona)**: The body engine owns the
  body's condition -- arousal, fatigue, hunger, pain (interoceptive, read on a persona).
  Ergonomics owns the object's fit to the body, not the body's state. The chair fits or
  does not (ergonomics); the sitter's ache is the body's (even when the bad fit causes
  it). The object's measure is here; the body's response is there.
- **A persona's skill with the object (embodiment, mastery)**: The trained capacity to
  wield a demanding object is persona-side. Ergonomics is the object's physical demand,
  not the body's competence to meet it. The heavy tool's weight is ergonomics; the
  labourer's strength to swing it is the persona's.
- **The facet is not engine content**: The engine declares the four facets exist; which
  one a given piece turns on lives on the piece, linked under its Load Bearing chapter.
  The engine refuses to fix a piece's fit for it.

## Encoding

Source to constraint, per file.

- **[ergonomics](piece_ergonomics.md)** (the root): the object's physical fit to the
  body, felt in the body's ease or its strain. Anchored by Dreyfuss (design to human
  measure) and Tilley (the anthropometric range).
- **[anthropometry](piece_anthropometry.md)**: the object sized to the range of real
  bodies, not to one. Anchored by Dreyfuss (Joe and Josephine) and Pheasant (percentile
  accommodation).
- **[reach](piece_reach.md)**: what the body can get to on the object without strain.
  Anchored by the reach-envelope anthropometrics (Woodson, Pheasant).
- **[clearance](piece_clearance.md)**: the space the object gives the body to fit and
  pass. Anchored by the clearance dimension (Tilley, Woodson).
- **[exertion](piece_exertion.md)**: the force the object demands of the body. Anchored
  by biomechanics (Kroemer, Grandjean).
