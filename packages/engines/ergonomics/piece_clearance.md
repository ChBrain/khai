# Piece: Clearance

## Taxonomy

[Ergonomics](piece_ergonomics.md), the facet this is a part of. Clearance models the space the object gives the body to fit and pass -- the legroom under the desk, the headroom in the doorway, the gap the hand needs to get behind the panel. The clearance dimension of anthropometrics: the room a body of the larger size must be allowed. It is distinct from reach (whether the body can get to a thing) and from anthropometry (the sizing): clearance is whether the body can occupy or move through the space the object leaves, without pinching, ducking, or wedging.

## Place

In the gaps and openings the object leaves for the body -- under it, around it, through it. Clearance lives in the space between the seat and the wheel, the doorway's width against the shoulders, the socket's surround against the fingers that must reach it.

## Load Bearing

What clearance holds up is whether the body can get into place at all. Too little and the object traps or bars: the knees jammed under the low shelf, the large body wedged in the narrow seat, the hand that cannot fit the space to do the work. Remove clearance and the object excludes by geometry -- not by refusing the body, but by leaving it no room -- and the ones it fails are usually the largest, or those the design's assumed body was smallest against.

## Apparent

Clearance shows when a body meets its limit: the duck under the low beam, the turn of the shoulders to pass, the pinched reach into too tight a gap. To the body it clears, the space is unremarkable; to the body it does not, the object becomes a squeeze, a bark of the knuckle, a place it cannot fit. What is apparent is an opening; what is borne is whether the body has room in it.

## Yearbook

The history of clearance is the record of the bodies that fit and the bodies that jammed -- the knocks, the wedged escapes, the users who never came because they could not get in. Over time the object's openings are judged by whom they admitted and whom they trapped, and the tight gap is dated from the bodies it caught.
