# Piece: Exertion

## Taxonomy

[Ergonomics](piece_ergonomics.md), the facet this is a part of. Exertion models the force the object demands of the body -- the grip strength to hold it, the torque to turn it, the load to lift it, the press to work it. The biomechanical side of ergonomics: how much the body must give, how often, and whether that is within what the body can sustain. It is distinct from anthropometry, reach, and clearance (all matters of dimension and placement): exertion is the effort the object exacts once the body has reached and fits it.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: ergonomics

## Place

At every point the body must push, pull, grip, lift, or turn the object -- the stiff lid, the heavy door, the tight valve, the trigger held all day. Exertion lives where the object resists the body's motion, and in how much of the body's strength that resistance asks for.

## Load Bearing

What exertion holds up is whether the body can work the object without harm over time. A force within reach once may be beyond it repeated; the lid that opens with a grunt injures the wrist opened a hundred times a shift. Remove a fitted force -- make the object too stiff, too heavy, too frequent in its demand -- and the use still happens, on the body's tendons and joints, until the strain accumulates into the injury that ends the using.

## Apparent

Exertion shows in the effort the body visibly spends -- the braced stance to shift the heavy thing, the two hands on the stiff control, the wince at the tight turn. To the body the force fits, the action is easy and unremarked; to the body it overmatches, the object reads as a strain, a struggle, a thing fought rather than worked. What is apparent is a mechanism; what is borne is the force it asks of the muscle that drives it.

## Yearbook

The history of exertion is the record of the strain the object cost -- the injuries logged, the workarounds improvised to spare the joint, the strong who managed and the rest who could not. Over time the object's forces are judged by the bodies they wore down, and the too-stiff mechanism is dated from the wrists and backs it hurt.
