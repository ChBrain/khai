# Piece: Anthropometry

## Taxonomy

[Ergonomics](piece_ergonomics.md), the facet this is a part of. Anthropometry models the object sized to the body's measure -- dimensioned not to one body but to the range of real bodies, from the small user to the large, the 5th percentile to the 95th. Dreyfuss's design to human measure (his Joe and Josephine), the anthropometric data of Tilley and Pheasant. It is distinct from reach and clearance (which are what a given sizing then permits): anthropometry is the choice of what body the object is built for, and what range of bodies it therefore fits or fails.

## Place

In every dimension of the object a body has to match -- the grip's diameter, the seat's depth, the step's rise, the button's spacing. The sizing lives in the numbers the form was cut to, wherever a body part must meet a made part.

## Load Bearing

What the sizing holds up is which bodies the object serves. Size it to the average and it fits the middle and fails the tails; size it to the range and it fits nearly all; size it to one designer's own body and it fits them and few others. Remove a truthful sizing and the object silently sorts its users into those it was built for and those it was not -- and the sorted-out pay in strain or exclusion for a measure that never had them in mind.

## Apparent

The sizing shows only when a body meets it wrong: the small hand that cannot span the grip, the tall frame folded into the small seat, the child who cannot reach the sized-for-adults control. To the body it fits, the dimension is invisible; to the body it does not, the object announces its measure as a fight. What is apparent is a size; what is borne is whether that size had this body in its range.

## Yearbook

The history of a sizing is the record of who it was built for, read in who it served and who it turned away -- the tools sized for one gender's hands, the seats for one era's smaller bodies, the standards that dated as populations changed. Over time the object's measure is judged against the bodies that actually came to it, and the gap between the assumed body and the real range is the sizing's indictment.
