# Instructions: Role Exit

How a Playwright wires the role-exit engine into a play. You wire by linking the play's own content to the engine's files; the engine files stay untouched, they are canon. The Roadie plumbs the engine's law into Instructions Knowledge on deploy. Authoring guidance, not runtime content, and it does not go on tour.

## Human

- The human decides how long the doubts had been running before the scene opens, and keeps that decision off the page, because the whole shape of the engine depends on it and nobody in the play knows the answer. Decide separately whether the exit was right, and the answer should usually be yes: the engine runs at full strength on a good decision well made. And give the ex-role somewhere to be seen, since it is what the other three movements were for.

## Agent

- From the play's own content, a persona links under Projection where they stand in the leaving: which movement is running, what the stated reason is and how much of the truth it holds, what stakes would be forfeited, and what a previous exit left behind. A persona already carrying an ex-role links the ex-role, not the doubts. Read the engine on what the persona is called afterwards, never on whether they are glad. Keep it the leaving: the between-state is liminality's, the rite that carries somebody across is ritual's, the role as held is role's. You link to the engine, you never edit it.

## Collaboration

- The between-state and what the threshold offers are the liminality engine's; the rite that carries somebody across is ritual's and the passage composite's; the role as held, with its congruence and its strain, is role's; standing allegiance and the choice between leaving and speaking up are loyalty's; the discrediting attribute some exits carry is stigma's; the imagined alternative self is possible-selves' and the self-story is narrative's; mourning is grief's; the wage bargain is employment's and the institution that takes over a life is total-institution's. Whether a production's departure was licensed by its stated reason, and whether a persona is in the vacuum or has come out of it, are the team's review.

## Knowledge

- Role exit is the leaving of a role that supplied a large part of who somebody is, and what the leaving leaves. It is not liminality's between-state, not ritual's rite of passage, and not role's account of the role as held. It wires on a persona at Projection and runs in four movements. The doubts run for years below the level of a decision: a first doubt too small to raise is also too small to answer, so it is retained in full where a stated grievance would have been dealt with and disposed of; cueing gathers real information about a question that was never put, because the remark has to stay deniable; the reinterpreting rereads the years as a build-up, and once the past points at the door the case for staying is not outweighed but invisible. The alternatives hold most people indefinitely: a shadow life assembled out of exactly the parts of the present one that hurt is the negative of the role and always better, and stays better for as long as it is not entered; side bets -- a pension, a house, a reputation that means something here and nothing elsewhere -- were laid down for their own sake and function as a hold the persona cannot tell apart from commitment; the weighing compares a life known in detail against a description of one and produces confidence rather than an answer. The turning point licenses rather than causes: a last straw is selected by timing and not size, so everybody responds by offering to fix the meeting; the announcing is the one irreversible act and works on being heard, after which the role withdraws immediately and decently; the vacuum is an interval with no role in it, arriving unannounced in a sequence everybody called a transition, and some personas read a normal stage as evidence of a mistake. The ex-role is the product: a residual of trained perception that does not resign, an introducing the persona performs against their own interest because it is the most efficient true sentence available, and a placing done accurately and sympathetically by people who cannot be asked to stop. The engine's twist is at the root: a role can be quit and being its ex cannot, because there is nobody to resign to and no procedure for it.

## System

- Do set the length of the doubts before anything else, and keep it from everybody including the persona, who will report the decision as sudden.
- Do make the exit right. The persona is better off out, they say so, and the engine still runs -- staging a mistaken exit replaces the mechanism with a regret.
- Do keep the institution decent. Departures are mostly managed kindly and the kindness is not a performance; staging a punishing employer replaces the mechanism with a grudge.
- Do make the last straw too small. It is chosen by timing, and its triviality is why nobody can argue with it and why every offer to fix it is beside the point.
- Do let somebody offer the reasonable remedy. The persona has to refuse it without being able to explain why, which is the movement working exactly as designed.
- Do stage the vacuum. It is the part missing from every account given beforehand, including the persona's own, and it is a stage rather than a verdict.
- Do put the twist in an introduction. A persona saying I used to be, to somebody new, in a room where it is genuinely the most useful thing they could say, is the engine compressed into one sentence.
- Do close on the placing rather than the leaving. Somebody who likes the persona files them under a role they no longer hold, means well by it, and cannot be asked to stop.
- Do not model the between-state (liminality), the rite of passage (ritual, passage), the role as held (role), allegiance (loyalty), the discrediting attribute (stigma), the imagined self (possible-selves), the self-story (narrative), mourning (grief), or the wage bargain (employment). Do not make the exit stand for a theme; that is authored key, the play's pitch. Do not edit the engine's files; wire only from the play's side.
