# Process: The Ex-Role

## Taxonomy

[Role Exit](process_role_exit.md), what the persona becomes, and where the engine is seen for one thing.

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: role-exit

## Initiated by

The persona is now defined by something they no longer do.
The Ex-Role starts once the leaving is finished: [a residual](process_the_residual.md) the old role left in the body, [an introducing](process_the_introducing.md) that carries it into every new room, and [a placing](process_the_placing.md) done by everybody else, permanently and without malice.

## Direction

Toward a status made out of a former status.
Being an ex is not the absence of a role; it is a role, with expectations attached. People know what to do with an ex-officer, an ex-nun, an ex-athlete, an ex-husband, and what they do is not what they would do with somebody who had never been one.
The persona does not get to decline it, because nobody issued it.

## Lever

The reality of what the exit achieved against the fact that it did not release.
The carrying force is that the exit worked and should be read as a success: the persona is out, the thing they could not live with is over, most of them say it was the right decision, and an account that treats every ex as a casualty has replaced a liberation with a wound; the resistance is that leaving converts the role rather than ending it -- the persona now holds it permanently, in the past tense -- and no second exit is available, because there is nobody to resign to and no procedure for it.
The Ex-Role rides the engine's keystone knife-edge: a role can be quit; being its ex cannot.

## Echo

Somebody introduced by what they used to be.
It shows the three earlier movements complete -- doubts that ran for years, an alternative that was better for being untested, a turning point that supplied a cause after the fact -- and adds the thing they were all for, which turns out not to be a return.
This is why the Ex-Role is the hardest of the four to stage and the only one worth closing on: the exit must have been right, the persona must be glad of it, nothing must have gone wrong, and the audience must still be able to see the role holding the persona in the past tense.
A play that stages the exit as a mistake has replaced the mechanism with a regret, and one that stages the institution as punishing the leaver has replaced it with a grudge.
The Ex-Role is dated from the last day and does not expire.
