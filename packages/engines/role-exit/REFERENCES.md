# Role Exit: Reference

## Line of Work

Role exit as **process**: the leaving of a role that supplied a large part of who somebody is, and
what the leaving leaves.

The engine's subject is a passage with no third phase. Ebaugh's study is the spine, and its finding is
the one everything here hangs from: leaving a central role is not an event but a sequence that runs
for years, and it does not end in a return to the person the persona was before -- it ends in a new
status, the ex, which the persona then holds permanently. She named the stages and named the residue,
and the engine follows both.

The boundary with what khai already carries is clean. The between-state itself -- what the threshold
shows, holds, offers, and withholds -- is **liminality**'s, and it is a place rather than a process.
The rite that carries somebody from one status to another, and the coupling of the collective ceremony
to the individual crossing, are **ritual**'s and the **passage** composite's; this engine reads the
modern exit that mostly has no such rite, which is why it needs a member for the vacuum that a
reincorporation phase would have filled. The role as held, its congruence and its strain, are
**role**'s -- a position engine, where this is the operation of vacating one. The standing allegiance
that survives a decline is **loyalty**'s. The discrediting attribute that some exits carry, and only
some, is **stigma**'s.

**The doubts** run far longer than anybody reports. A **first doubt** is too small to raise, and
because raising it would require a case nobody has, it is never answered -- so it is retained in full
where a stated grievance would have been dealt with and disposed of. **Cueing** is the persona
gathering evidence without becoming liable for the question: a joke about quitting, a hypothetical at
dinner, a remark left hanging, each of which returns real information about a question that was never
actually put. The **reinterpreting** then rereads the years as a build-up, which is not dishonesty and
is not reversible: once the past points at the door, the case for staying is not outweighed but
invisible.

**The alternatives** are where most people stay. A **shadow life** is assembled out of exactly the
parts of the present one that hurt, so it is the negative of the role and always better, and it will
remain better for as long as it is not entered. The **side bets** are Becker's: a pension, a house, a
reputation that means something here and nothing elsewhere -- commitments laid down for their own sake
that later function as a hold, and that the persona cannot distinguish from commitment to the work.
The **weighing** compares a life known in detail against a description of one, and a self who holds
the role against a self who cannot be consulted, so it organises the reasons without producing them.

**The turning point** licenses rather than causes. The **last straw** is selected by timing and not
size, which is why exits look disproportionate from outside and exactly proportionate from inside, and
why everybody responds by offering to fix the meeting. The **announcing** is the sequence's one
irreversible act and it works on being heard: the role starts withdrawing immediately, decently, and
the persona reads the withdrawal as confirmation. The **vacuum** is the stage nobody is warned about
-- an interval with no role in it, arriving in a sequence everybody described as a transition -- and
some personas read a normal stage as evidence of a mistake and go back for that reason alone.

**The ex-role** is what the operation produces. The **residual** is trained perception, which does not
resign: the ex-officer still reads the exits, the ex-teacher still counts the room. The
**introducing** is performed by the persona themselves, because "I used to be" is the most efficient
true sentence available and it reissues the role at every meeting. The **placing** is done by
everybody else, accurately, sympathetically, and with no procedure for revoking it.

Hence the **twist**, at the root. **A role can be quit; being its ex cannot.** The exit does not
return the persona to who they were and does not release them from the role: it converts the role into
a permanent one, held by somebody who no longer does any of it. There is no second resignation
available, because there is nobody to resign to.

The engine is built so that nobody has to have done anything wrong for it to run, and a play should
keep it that way. The exit was right. The persona is glad. The institution behaved well. The ex-role
forms anyway.

**On the evidence, stated plainly.** This is a qualitative engine with one strong study at its centre,
and the reader should know what that means. **Ebaugh's _Becoming an Ex_ is interview work, not
measurement**: roughly 185 exiters across many kinds of exit, recruited by snowball, analysed by
grounded theory, and reconstructed into four stages after the fact by a researcher who was herself an
ex-nun. It is the best account of the phenomenon anybody has written and nobody has since run a
prospective sample that would show the stages occurring in that order. **The engine's central claim --
that the ex-role persists as a status -- rests on roles that were unusually total**: clergy, the
military, elite athletes, prisoners, people leaving high-demand groups. That is the literature's
sampling frame and it is also its bias, so the step from those exits to leaving an ordinary job is the
weakest move the engine makes, and it is stated here rather than smuggled. **Becker's side bets is a
conceptual paper** (1960) of great durability and no data; attempts to operationalise it in the
commitment literature have returned mixed results, and the engine uses it as a description rather than
a finding. **The quantitative leg is thin and points the other way as often as not.** The
athlete-retirement literature, which is the best-measured case, finds that most transitions go
reasonably well and that a substantial minority do not -- the reviews put serious adjustment
difficulty in the region of one in six rather than the majority -- and the dramatic case is heavily
over-represented in what gets published and written about, this engine included. **Goffman's "On
Cooling the Mark Out" is an essay**, and it is used here for the announcing member's observation that
departures are managed kindly, not as evidence that they are. What should not be taken from this
engine is that exits are traumatic, that people regret them, or that institutions punish leavers. Most
exits are the right decision and are reported as such. The claim is narrow: the sequence starts
earlier than anybody says, the stated reason is not the reason, the interval afterwards is a stage
rather than a verdict, and the role does not release the persona on the way out.

sourced; the twist and the four-stage shape are Ebaugh's.

## Restrictions

What the engine refuses to model, and to whom it delegates.

- **The between-state itself (liminality, passage, ritual)**: What the threshold shows, holds, offers,
  and withholds is the liminality engine's; the rite that carries somebody across, and the coupling of
  ceremony to crossing, are ritual's and the passage composite's. This engine reads the exit that
  mostly has no rite, which is why it carries a vacuum member and not a reincorporation.
- **The role as held (role)**: The expectations attached to a position, congruence and its penalties,
  overload and ambiguity are the role engine's -- a position, where this is the operation of vacating
  one. A persona in a role links there; a persona leaving one links here.
- **Standing allegiance and organisational exit (loyalty)**: Allegiance held before any decline is
  felt, and the choice between leaving and speaking up, are the loyalty engine's. This engine reads
  what leaving does to an identity rather than what it does to an organisation.
- **The discrediting attribute (stigma)**: An attribute that reduces a persona in others' eyes is the
  stigma engine's. Some ex-roles are stigmatised and most are not, and the placing member reads the
  ordinary sympathetic case rather than the discrediting one.
- **The imagined self and the self-story (possible-selves, narrative)**: The elaborated alternative
  self is possible-selves', and the account a persona composes of their own life is narrative's. The
  shadow life and the reinterpreting use both and hand the mechanisms back.
- **The felt absence (grief, nostalgia, place-attachment)**: Mourning a person, longing for a past, and
  being held by a place are those engines'. Leaving a role is not a bereavement, and the engine refuses
  to model it as one even where the two run together.
- **The job as an arrangement (employment, total-institution, org)**: The wage bargain, the institution
  that takes over a life, and the organisation as a structure are those engines'. A persona resigning a
  job is in both, and this engine reads only what the leaving does to who they are.
- **The exit as authored symbol (the play's pitch)**: A departure made to **stand for** a theme -- the
  resignation that mirrors a motif of release -- is refused: that is authored key, the play's pitch.
  Role exit sets what the leaving cost and produced; the meaning read into it is the play's.

## Encoding

Source to constraint. A process tree: one root, four movements, twelve forms.

- **[role exit](process_role_exit.md)** (the root): the leaving of a role that supplied an identity,
  read on what the persona is called afterwards, carrying the twist -- a role can be quit; being its ex
  cannot. Anchored by Ebaugh (1988).
- **[the doubts](process_doubts_exit.md)**: where the role stops fitting. Anchored by Ebaugh (1988) and
  Ashforth (2001). [the first doubt](process_the_first_doubt.md), [the cueing](process_the_cueing.md),
  [the reinterpreting](process_the_reinterpreting.md).
- **[the alternatives](process_alternatives_exit.md)**: the other life carried alongside this one.
  Anchored by Becker (1960) and Markus & Nurius (1986).
  [the shadow life](process_the_shadow_life.md), [the side bets](process_the_side_bets.md),
  [the weighing](process_the_weighing.md).
- **[the turning point](process_turning_point_exit.md)**: the event that makes leaving sayable.
  Anchored by Glaser & Strauss (1971) and Goffman (1952).
  [the last straw](process_the_last_straw.md), [the announcing](process_the_announcing.md),
  [the vacuum](process_the_vacuum.md).
- **[the ex-role](process_ex_role.md)**: what the persona becomes, and where the engine is seen for one
  thing. Anchored by Ebaugh (1988) and Lavallee (2013). [the residual](process_the_residual.md),
  [the introducing](process_the_introducing.md), [the placing](process_the_placing.md).
