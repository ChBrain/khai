# Process: Role Exit

## Taxonomy

Leaving (no khai file of its own yet).

## Owner

- Project: khai
- Engine: role-exit

## Initiated by

A persona stops being something they were.
Role exit starts wherever a role that supplied a large part of who somebody is comes to an end: the priest who leaves, the officer who resigns, the athlete whose body stops, the spouse who divorces, the parent whose children have gone, the doctor who retires.
It is not the between-state itself, which is liminality's, and not the rite that carries somebody from one status to another, which is ritual's and the passage composite's. It is the leaving, run as a sequence, and what the leaving leaves.

## Direction

Toward an identity assembled out of a role that is no longer held.
The engine reads four movements: [the doubts](process_doubts_exit.md), where the role stops fitting before anybody has a word for it; [the alternatives](process_alternatives_exit.md), the other life carried alongside this one; [the turning point](process_turning_point_exit.md), the event that makes leaving sayable; and [the ex-role](process_ex_role.md), the twist -- what the persona becomes, which is not what they were before.
The four are one operation and they run in this order, though the first can take years and the third an afternoon.

## Lever

The freedom of being able to leave against the fact that leaving does not undo.
The carrying force is that exit is real and often right: people leave roles that were wrong for them, the leaving is frequently the best thing they ever did, they say so afterwards, and an account that treats every exit as a loss has mistaken a liberation for a bereavement; the resistance is that the role does not hand the persona back on the way out -- what it returns is somebody shaped by having held it, introduced by it, and placed by it for the rest of their life.
Role exit is read on what the persona is called afterwards, never on whether they are glad.

## Echo

Somebody who is now an ex.
Role exit does not close; it is an identity being rebuilt, gathered into one reading -- doubts, alternatives, a turning point, an ex-role.
The engine's twist is here. **A role can be quit; being its ex cannot.** The exit does not return the persona to the person they were before, and it does not release them from the role -- it converts the role into a permanent one, held by somebody who no longer does any of it. The ex-officer, the ex-nun, the ex-athlete, the ex-husband: none of them can resign the second time, because there is nobody to resign to and no procedure for it.
It hands off what it is not: the between-state and what the threshold offers are liminality's; the rite that carries somebody across is ritual's and the passage composite's; the role as held, and its congruence, are role's; the standing allegiance is loyalty's; the discrediting attribute some exits carry is stigma's; the felt absence of a person or a place is grief's and place-attachment's; and an exit made to **stand for** a theme is authored key, the play's pitch.
The persona is dated from the day the role ended and is introduced by it thereafter.
