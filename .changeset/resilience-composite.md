Add the resilience composite (process: root + two forms) -- the maintenance of stable, healthy functioning across a potentially traumatic event. The gap analysis (Tier-3 #33) placed it as distinct from `grit` (goal stamina) and `hedonic-adaptation` (drift), and leaning composite.

- **Root (process):** `resilience` -- reads Bonanno's trajectory concept over two atoms it declares as dependencies: the `stress` engine's acute response to the adversity, and the `coping` engine's appraisal and coping through which the persona meets it. The composite adds no third atom -- only the **trajectory of functioning** across the event, and Masten's finding that stable functioning is the **common outcome** built from ordinary adaptive systems ("ordinary magic"), not a rare heroic trait.
- **Forms (position, the two healthy trajectories Bonanno distinguishes):**
  - `resilience, stable` -- resilience proper: functioning barely dips (a mild, transient perturbation) and holds near baseline; the persona shaken but not felled (hardiness -- commitment/control/challenge).
  - `resilience, recovery` -- the bounce-back: functioning drops markedly for weeks or months, then returns to baseline; the person did fall, and the story is the climb.

Both come through; neither is the chronic dysfunction or delayed reaction that are the trajectories resilience is _not_.

**Scope note.** The acute stress response stays with `stress`; the coping strategies stay with `coping`. Bounded against `grit` (perseverance toward a long-term goal, measured by attainment -- not the trauma trajectory), `hedonic-adaptation` (the drift of pleasure back to a setpoint -- a different quantity returning by different means), and `post-traumatic-growth` (the transformation _beyond_ baseline through the struggle -- resilience keeps the prior self intact; growth builds a changed one, and #34 will delegate the mere-maintenance case here). Every hard link resolves through a declared dependency.

Warranted (LORE, integrative) on Bonanno (2004, "Loss, Trauma, and Human Resilience" -- the multiple-trajectories model, resilience common not rare), Masten (2001, "Ordinary Magic"), Kobasa (1979, hardiness), and Bonanno & Burton (2013, regulatory flexibility). Each atom's own REFERENCES carries the primary science of its part. Set at patch as the free level. No whitelist required -- the `resilience` / `resilience_stable` / `resilience_recovery` stems are unique.
