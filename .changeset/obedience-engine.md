Add the obedience engine: how a persona complies with the command of an authority they take to be legitimate even against their own conscience -- Milgram's destructive obedience. The root (`process_obedience.md`) declares the vertical override: not persuasion, not the pull of peers, but a superior's directive obeyed against what the persona would otherwise do, responsibility relocated upward. Three forms carry it: `instrument` (the agentic shift -- the persona ceases to feel the author of their acts and comes to feel a tool carrying out the authority's will, so "I was only following orders" names a real state), `legitimacy` (the authority's perceived right to command, backed by role, setting, and symbol -- the persona obeys the office, and obedience loosens the moment the entitlement is doubted), and `binding` (the factors that hold the persona in once begun -- sequential creep, the awkwardness of breaking off, prior compliance -- and the proximity gradient that raises or lowers obedience as the victim nears and the authority withdraws).

Promotes obedience from a single `conformity/process_obedience.md` member to a full engine, the two altitudes cleared by the `obedience` homonym whitelist (governance PR, mirroring anchoring/reactance/representativeness). Bounded against `conformity` (horizontal peer-majority matching vs the vertical authority command -- a distinction Milgram himself drew), `power` (coercive leverage by control of resources vs obedience's reliance on perceived legitimacy), `moral-disengagement` (the self-exemption for a harm one still owns vs the prior agentic divestment of authorship), and `escalation` (sunk-cost persistence vs the binding factors that hold an obedient course). Warranted (LORE) on Milgram (behavioral study of obedience, 1963; _Obedience to Authority_, 1974; conditions of obedience and disobedience, 1965), Burger (the modern replication, 2009), and Kelman & Hamilton (_Crimes of Obedience_, 1989). Set at patch as the free level; a new engine may warrant a minor at the maintainer's `bump:minor` label.
