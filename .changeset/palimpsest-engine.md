Add the palimpsest engine -- the first engine of the `pitch` type. How a fixed tale is re-pitched into a new key by its relation to a prior telling (Genette's literature in the second degree): the source shows through the new tone the way old writing shows through a scraped-and-reused page, and the events do not change -- only how they land. The root (`pitch_palimpsest.md`) declares the doubled, second-degree key, honoured and altered at once; its situational face is Bakhtin's reaccentuation, a work re-toned by a new context rather than an author. Three transforms carry it: `parody` (the mocking route -- a serious source re-keyed to comic deflation, with affection beneath the mockery), `travesty` (the debasing route -- a noble tale re-clothed in low, vulgar register, its dignity pulled down while its grandeur is still glimpsed), and `transposition` (the serious route -- the tale transplanted to new ground and re-keyed in full earnest, the ancient pattern lending the new setting its weight, as the Odyssey carries Ulysses).

Breaks the seal on the `pitch` khai type (previously zero engines): member files carry the TO-TUNE chapters (Taxonomy/Owner/Tenor/Undertow/Nerve/Echo), and the engine wires like the whole-production engines -- the law declared in Instructions Knowledge, the play linking the chosen transform under its Arc (the novel pitch-wiring precedent, mirroring monomyth/morphology; open to the maintainer's redirection). Bounded against `tone` (the science of how an affective key is cued and sustained in an audience vs a specific transformative key), `humor` (the amusement mechanism a comic re-pitching may trigger vs the transform relation itself), `framing` (a presentation's tilt on judgment vs the retuning of a tale's felt key), and the play engines `dramatic-arc`/`monomyth`/`morphology` (the structure of the fixed events vs the key they are re-pitched to). No homonym gate: `palimpsest` and the transform stems `parody`/`travesty`/`transposition` are all free. Warranted (LORE) on Genette (_Palimpsests: Literature in the Second Degree_, 1982), Bakhtin (reaccentuation, _The Dialogic Imagination_, 1981), Hutcheon (_A Theory of Parody_, 1985), Queneau (_Exercises in Style_, 1947), and Jameson (pastiche vs parody, 1991). Set at patch as the free level; a new engine may warrant a minor at the maintainer's `bump:minor` label.
