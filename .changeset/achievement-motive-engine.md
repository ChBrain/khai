Add the achievement-motive engine (position: anchor + expressions) -- a persona's resting dispositional motive in achievement situations, the standing pull toward success or away from failure that colours how it meets a test of its competence before it frames any goal. The gap analysis (Tier-3 #35) targeted the upstream _motive_, distinct from `goal`'s achievement-goal orientation (the homonym risk flagged in the row).

- **Anchor (position):** `achievement-motive` -- the dispositional orientation, prior to any goal, that a persona brings to a situation where its competence can be tested (Atkinson; McClelland).
- **Faces (position)** -- Atkinson's two separable, orthogonal motives (a persona can hold both):
  - `hope-of-success` -- the motive to approach success (McClelland's need for achievement): drawn to challenge, energized by doing well, seeking the moderately difficult test where success is real but not certain.
  - `fear-of-failure` -- the motive to avoid failure: an anxiety about failing and its multidimensional costs (shame, devalued self-estimate, uncertain future, lost regard, upsetting important others -- Conroy), pulling the persona to avoid or hedge the test (safely-easy/impossibly-hard tasks, withheld effort, self-handicapping).

The resultant tendency is their difference -- approach where hope predominates, avoidance where fear does; high-on-both is the anxious achiever.

**Scope note -- the upstream motive, not the goal (the homonym).** Which achievement _goal_ a persona then adopts (mastery/performance × approach/avoidance) stays with `goal` -- the motive is upstream, energizing and selecting the goal (Elliot & Church's hierarchical model); distinct stems (`achievement_motive` / `hope_of_success` / `fear_of_failure`) avoid `goal`'s `approach`/`avoidance` members. Also bounded against `regulatory-focus` (promotion/prevention -- the strategic manner of pursuit), `motivation` (the SDT source of the drive), and the worth **emotions** (`anxiety`/`shame`/`pride` register the stakes; this owns the disposition).

Warranted (LORE) on Atkinson (1957, "Motivational Determinants of Risk-Taking Behavior" -- the two-motive dynamics and resultant), McClelland (1961, _The Achieving Society_ -- the need for achievement), Elliot & Church (1997, the hierarchical model placing motives upstream of goals), and Conroy (2002, the multidimensional Performance Failure Appraisal Inventory). Set at patch as the free level. No whitelist required -- the stems are unique.
