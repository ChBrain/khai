Add the compassion engine -- a multi-type engine spanning process and position. Compassion is the warm, other-focused response to another's suffering: a persona witnesses another's serious, undeserved hardship and is moved -- a warm concern with the impulse to relieve it (Goetz, Keltner & Simon-Thomas). Where sadness answers one's own loss, compassion answers another's; where empathy shares the state, compassion adds the wish to help.

- **Faces (process):** `tenderness` (the warm affective core -- the soft, protective fondness toward the vulnerable), `succor` (the action tendency -- the reaching to soothe, aid, and relieve, what separates compassion from a sympathy that only feels).
- **Shadows (process):** `distress` (the self-focused shadow -- empathic/personal distress, where the other's pain floods the self and the persona withdraws to relieve its own arousal, the road to burnout -- Batson; Singer & Klimecki), `pity` (the distanced shadow -- the feeling-for from above that holds the sufferer as lesser or apart, care with the fellow-feeling missing -- Nussbaum).
- **Trait (position):** `tenderhearted` -- dispositional compassion, a standing readiness to be moved by suffering and to reach to relieve it, tracking agreeableness and the caregiving temperament.

A process root fans into faces, shadows, and a trait; each validates against its own khai chapters. Bounded against `empathy` (the capacity that takes suffering in vs the warm response to it -- empathy is the channel, compassion the answer), `caregiving` (the behavioral system that protects and provides, and its burnout, vs the emotion that motivates it), `sadness`/`grief` (the response to one's own loss vs another's suffering), `elevation` and the other-praising emotions (the response to another's good vs another's hardship), and `contempt` (the cold downward dismissal vs pity's softer look-down). Warranted (LORE) on Goetz, Keltner & Simon-Thomas (compassion as a distinct emotion, 2010), Batson (empathic concern vs personal distress), Singer & Klimecki (compassion vs empathic distress, 2014), Nussbaum and Solomon (the compassion/pity distinction), Gilbert and Neff (the affiliative-soothing system), and Sprecher & Fehr (dispositional compassionate love). No whitelist required -- the `compassion` root and its member stems are unique. Set at patch as the free level; a new engine may warrant a minor at the maintainer's `bump:minor` label.
