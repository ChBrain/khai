Add the estimation composite, how a persona judges a quantity or likelihood it cannot compute. Wires three atoms -- the anchoring engine (the reference value that entered first and drags the estimate), the availability engine (frequency read off the ease of bringing instances to mind), and the representativeness engine (probability read off resemblance to a type) -- and reads them as one operation: the substitution of a tractable question for the one actually asked, made without the persona noticing the exchange. Staged as three bridges: the anchor (how near standing in for how much -- a planted figure, relevant or arbitrary, adjusted away from too little), the ready (how easily it comes standing in for how often it happens -- ease and frequency usually track, but vividness, recency, and reporting decouple them), and the likeness (the twist -- how much it looks the part standing in for how probable it is, with priors unweighted and the detailed story preferred to the plain one it contains). Carries the keystone: the better a story hangs together, the more probable it feels and the less probable it is -- every detail added makes an account match its type better and makes it strictly less likely, so coherence and likelihood run in opposite directions and the mind reads the first as the second. Wires at Persona/Projection (audit). Anchored in Tversky & Kahneman's 1974 heuristics paper and their 1983 conjunction fallacy, Kahneman's attribute substitution, with Gigerenzer's bounded rationality as the counterweight that keeps the heuristics honest as heuristics. Distinct from bias (which names the standing tilt and its motive families -- position, where this is operation), decision (the weighing toward a choice), framing/crosstalk (how an option is presented), and belief (the conviction held).
