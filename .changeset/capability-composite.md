Add the capability composite, a persona's belief about its own power. Wires four atoms -- the mindset engine (ability held as fixed or growable), the locus-of-control engine (outcomes expected to follow from one's own action or from forces outside), the self-efficacy engine (the task-specific confidence built by mastery, vicarious experience, and persuasion), and the self-handicapping engine (the obstacle erected before an outcome) -- and reads them as one self-belief that both guides action and defends itself. Staged as three bridges: the theory (the standing frame that decides whether needing to try is a route to growth or a confession of insufficient talent), the reach (the working confidence that decides what is attempted at all and how long effort survives difficulty), and the guard (the twist -- the obstacle acquired or merely claimed, so failure belongs to it and success counts double). Carries the keystone: a self can protect its belief in its ability only by never letting the ability be cleanly tested -- the protection and the sabotage are one act, and the belief kept safe from the test is kept safe from ever being earned. Wires at Persona/Projection (audit). Anchored in Bandura's self-efficacy, Rotter's locus of control, Dweck's mindset, and Berglas & Jones's self-handicapping. Models the belief about power, never actual competence; distinct from striving (the character of pursuit), volition (the crossing from wanting to doing), and self-esteem/self-relation (the regard for one's own worth).
