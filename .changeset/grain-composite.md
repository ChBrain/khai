Add the grain composite, the cultural settings a persona was formed along and cannot see. Wires three atoms -- the tightness-looseness engine (the strength of the norm field and the room it leaves for stepping out), the self-construal engine (where the line around the self falls, from within or through its ties), and the worth-logic engine (the rule by which worth is assigned and an affront to it priced) -- and reads the seam the three make: three settings that co-vary, each trading one pole's good for the other's, and none of them visible to the persona holding it. Staged as three bridges: the latitude (a property of the regime rather than of anyone's yielding -- tight buys coordination and predictability through constraint, loose buys openness and improvisation, and no regime gets both), the bounds (the default weighting of I against we, which decides what counts as a reason, since an obligation is a reason to one self and a constraint to the other), and the worth (the twist -- not the feeling of worth but the logic beneath it, setting the exchange rate between an insult and the self, so the same remark is nothing to a dignity-holder, a wound to an honour-holder, and a disgrace to a face-holder). Carries the keystone: a setting is invisible to whoever holds it, so a persona on different settings is read as a worse person rather than a differently made one -- and the sentences that do it are about character every time, that he has no dignity, that she has no shame, that they have no honour. Wires at Persona/Projection (audit). The joining is published on both flanks: Triandis on cultural syndromes, where collectivism and tightness-looseness bear on which aspects of the self get sampled, interdependent selves more likely in tight and collective worlds; and Leung and Cohen on cultural logics, defined almost identically and framed as alternative solutions to two problems every society must answer, a person's worth and social order -- which are this composite's bridges. What the composite adds is the consequence for a persona rather than for a culture. Bridge anchors: Gelfand on norm strength and its ecological-threat roots, Markus and Kitayama on the two construals, Singelis on their separability so the bridge reads a weighting and not a type, Nisbett and Cohen on honour, Ho on face. Distinct from heritage (the same move at the size of a household), conformity and obedience (the yielding, not the field), social-identity and membership and intergroup (identification with a group), face inside presentation (the interactional move, not the standing logic), self-esteem and pride and shame (the felt worth and its emotions), virtue (honour as personal integrity), the construal engine (psychological distance, a different word), and standing (rank and capital).
