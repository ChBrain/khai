Add the admiration engine -- a multi-type engine spanning process and position, and the missing third of the other-praising emotions (with gratitude and elevation). Admiration is the warm upward response to another's witnessed excellence: a persona meets another's skill, talent, or achievement -- a non-moral excellence -- and warms toward them, moved to rise rather than diminished. It is the benign face of upward comparison: where envy meets another's superiority with pain and the wish to pull it down, admiration meets it with regard and the wish to rise toward it.

- **Forms (process):** `inspiration` (the emulative form -- admiration firing striving toward a reachable model, the engine of social learning -- Thrash & Elliot; Onu), `affiliation` (the drawing-near form -- the pull to connect with and stand in the light of the admired -- Onu), `adoration` (the reverent form -- admiration of an unattainable excellence tipping into worship and devotion, the idol and the hero, fragile when it falls -- Schindler et al.).
- **Trait (position):** `admiring` -- a standing readiness to meet excellence with admiration rather than envy, to find models and be inspired (the assimilative pole of upward comparison -- van de Ven et al.).

Promotes admiration to a standalone engine (via the `admiration` homonym whitelist -- same-word-different-science: this is admiration _felt for_ another's excellence, distinct from `narcissism`'s `process_admiration.md`, the NARC self-promotion route that _seeks_ admiration for the self). A process root fans into process forms and a position trait; each validates against its own khai chapters. Bounded by the Algoe & Haidt cut against `elevation` (witnessed _moral_ excellence vs _non-moral_), `gratitude` (a benefit received vs excellence witnessed), `awe` (a vastness beyond the frame vs an excellent person), and `envy` (the painful, contrastive response to superiority vs the warm, assimilative one). Warranted (LORE) on Algoe & Haidt (the other-praising emotions, 2009), Onu, Kessler & Smith (admiration as social learning, 2016), Thrash & Elliot (inspiration), Schindler et al. (admiration vs adoration), and van de Ven et al. (benign vs malicious envy). This engine completes the atoms for a forthcoming other-praising composite, the positive mirror of the condemnation composite. Set at patch as the free level; a new engine may warrant a minor at the maintainer's `bump:minor` label.
