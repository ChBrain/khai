Add the bittersweet composite -- the mixed emotion of joy and sadness felt together, the two the folk mind calls opposites running at once. The composite authors no emotion of its own: it declares the `joy` and `sadness` engines as dependencies, wires their members by hard package links, and adds only the co-activation the two share and the shapes it takes.

- **Root:** `bittersweet` -- joy and sadness co-activated, held together rather than traded turn for turn, resting on the evaluative-space fact that positivity and negativity are separable systems that can fire at once (Cacioppo & Berntson; Larsen, McGraw & Cacioppo).
- **Bridges (the drama the atoms cannot stage alone):** `bittersweet, mix` (joy and sadness at full at the same instant -- the poignant compound, the tears of joy, the smile that is also a goodbye), `bittersweet, turn` (one sign crossing into the other -- relief as a dread lifts, the letdown as a good thing closes, the oscillation that will not settle), `bittersweet, meaning` (a bounded, meaningful ending drawing both signs to a joint peak -- the last of a good thing sweetest and saddest at once, Ersner-Hershfield, Mikels et al. on poignancy within Carstensen's socioemotional selectivity).

Newly wireable now that `joy` and `sadness` are standalone engines. Warranted (LORE, integrative) on the evaluative space model (Cacioppo & Berntson, 1994), the simultaneity studies (Larsen, McGraw & Cacioppo, 2001; Larsen & McGraw, 2011), and the poignancy-at-meaningful-endings work (Ersner-Hershfield, Mikels et al., 2008). Bounded against `nostalgia` (the sentimental longing for one's own past vs the mixed emotion itself), `mood` (a persisting background tone vs an object-bound poignant moment), and `emotion` (the general affective space vs the specific joy-and-sadness mixture). Set at patch as the free level. No whitelist required -- the `bittersweet` root and `bittersweet_*` bridge stems are unique.
