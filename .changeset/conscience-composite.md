Add the conscience composite, the moral self that governs a persona's conduct. Wires three atoms -- the moral-identity engine (how central being moral is to who a person is), the moral-conviction engine (the attitudes stamped as moral absolutes), and the moral-licensing engine (the running moral balance a self keeps) -- and reads them as one self-government of conduct. Staged as three bridges: the core (morality internalized as the true self or worn as a symbol, custodian or peripheral), the mandate (the attitude held as objective, universal moral truth, mobilizing action and licensing intolerance), and the ledger (the twist -- the moral balance, a cleansing after a wrong and an indulgence after a good). Carries the keystone: the conscience that keeps the score is the conscience that licenses the slip -- the moral self as guardian and alibi at once. Wires at Persona/Projection (audit). Anchored in Blasi's moral self, Aquino & Reed's moral identity, Skitka's moral conviction, and Monin/Miller/Sachdeva's moral licensing. Distinct from self-conscious (the emotions of transgression), moral-account (the reckoning of a wrong), moral-disengagement (deactivating self-sanction), and moral-judgment (the verdict on an act).
