Add the hatred engine (process) -- a durable, eliminatory sentiment toward a devalued other, the standing wish to distance from, harm, and diminish a target seen as bad to the core. The gap analysis (Tier-3 #20) found this homeless: the codebase held the incident emotion (`anger`) and the disengaged dismissal (`contempt`) but not the sustained, essentializing sentiment that outlasts any provocation and aims at elimination.

- **Root (process):** `hatred` -- the durable sentiment, sustained and aimed at the target's undoing.
- **Components (process):**
  - `repulsion` -- the negation of intimacy (distancing and revulsion that push the target away and deny it closeness).
  - `malice` -- the passion (active hostility, the wish to see the target harmed, thwarted, or destroyed).
  - `essentializing` -- the commitment (fixing the target's badness as its stable essence, licensing hate to persist and generalize from person to category).

Warranted (LORE) on Sternberg (2003, the duplex theory of hate -- negation of intimacy, passion, decision/commitment), Fischer et al. (2018, hate as a durable sentiment aimed at elimination, grounded in dispositional attribution), and Staub/Haslam (escalation and essentialism). Bounded against `anger` (the incident reaction aimed at redress vs the durable sentiment aimed at elimination), `contempt` (the cold disengaged dismissal vs the hot engaged wish for undoing), `disgust` (the visceral revulsion reflex vs the interpersonal negation of intimacy), `aggression` (the harmful deed vs the wish), and `moral-disengagement` (the licensing of harm vs the essentializing appraisal that condemns the enemy's nature). Set at patch as the free level. No whitelist required -- the `hatred`/`repulsion`/`malice`/`essentializing` stems are unique (`devaluation` was avoided as bias's reactive-devaluation member).
