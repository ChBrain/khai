Add the grounding engine: how two personas build mutual understanding turn by turn, each contribution accepted only on the evidence that it landed, against the common ground they already share. The root (`process_grounding.md`) declares the grounding theory of communication -- language use as joint action, understanding not delivered by the speaker but achieved by the two together, a contribution grounded only when the addressee returns evidence sufficient for current purposes, by least collaborative effort. Four moves are the ways understanding is reached, shown, mended, or missed: `acknowledgment` (light positive evidence -- the continuer, the token, the fitting next turn that keeps the talk moving; evidence of receipt more than comprehension), `demonstration` (strong evidence -- the readback or paraphrase that shows understanding and lets the speaker confirm it before the talk builds on it), `clarification` (repair -- trouble in understanding flagged, self- or other-initiated, and mended in a side-sequence before the talk resumes), and `misgrounding` (the failure -- evidence skipped or faked, the criterion assumed met when it is not, so the parties proceed on unshared ground and the error compounds unseen).

Fills a foundational gap in khai's communication-science coverage, surfaced while scoping a `crosstalk` composite: the "catch" that mends a talking-past loop had no engine to wire to -- `repair` is relational rupture-repair and `language` is the one-pass said-vs-understood gap, neither the collaborative construction of understanding. Bounded against `language` (the one-pass gap vs the joint closing of it), `repair` (the mending of a relationship vs of a misunderstanding in the moment), `speech-act` (the utterance-act vs the acceptance that grounds it), `empathy` (reading inner state vs constructing shared understanding of a message), and `memory` (the store of shared knowledge vs the live accretion of common ground). Warranted (LORE) on Clark & Brennan (grounding and the evidence ladder), Clark & Schaefer (the contribution model), Clark (language as joint action), Schegloff, Jefferson & Sacks and Schegloff (conversational repair and misunderstanding), and Stalnaker (common ground). Set at patch as the free level; a new engine may warrant a minor at the maintainer's `bump:minor` label.
