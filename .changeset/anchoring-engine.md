Add the anchoring engine: the estimation process through which a persona forming a numeric or magnitude judgment under uncertainty is drawn toward a reference value that entered first, and adjusts away from it too little. The root (`process_anchoring.md`) declares Tversky & Kahneman's anchoring-and-adjustment -- one of the three original heuristics of judgment under uncertainty -- the first figure fixing the scale every later guess is read against, gripping even when known to be arbitrary. Three forms carry it: `adjustment` (the self-generated route -- step away from a starting value you hold, but stop at the near edge of the plausible range), `accessibility` (the external route -- a supplied anchor primes anchor-consistent evidence, so the target is judged through a skewed sample), and `planting` (the deliberate route -- a first offer or list price set on purpose to drag another's estimate toward it).

Cleared the member-scope gate via the `anchoring` homonym whitelist (governance PR #905), mirroring `framing` and `reactance`: `bias` carries `position_anchoring.md` as one static valuation tilt in its codex, and a codex entry at the `position` altitude does not preempt the full `process` engine of the same phenomenon. Bounded against `decision` (the mode of choosing among options vs the bending of an estimate), `negotiation` (the bargaining a planted anchor serves vs the reference's pull), `framing` (a shift by numeric reference vs by description), and `bias` (the static catalogued tilt vs the live estimation). Warranted (LORE) on Tversky & Kahneman (_Judgment under Uncertainty_, 1974), Epley & Gilovich (insufficient adjustment, 2006), Strack & Mussweiler (selective accessibility, 1997), Galinsky & Mussweiler (first offers as anchors, 2001), and Northcraft & Neale (anchoring in expert real-estate pricing, 1987). Set at patch as the free level; a new engine may warrant a minor at the maintainer's `bump:minor` label.
