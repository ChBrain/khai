Add the anger engine -- a multi-type engine spanning process and position, and the completion of the CAD triad at the engine level (Contempt and Disgust already stand; anger is the Autonomy-violation emotion). Anger is the hot approach-emotion at a wrong: a persona appraises a demeaning offense against itself or what it cares about -- other-blame, a goal deliberately blocked, someone who could have done otherwise -- and flares toward the source to set it right. It is the exception among the negative emotions in moving toward, not back.

- **Forms (process):** `frustration` (goal-blockage -- anger at an aversively thwarted goal, Berkowitz's reformulated frustration-aggression), `indignation` (the moral form -- righteous anger at a violated norm, often on another's behalf, the justice emotion), `grievance` (the stored form -- the wrong nursed and kept, outlasting its occasion), `rage` (the flooded form -- arousal overtopping control, judgment flooded out).
- **Trait (position):** `hostility` -- trait anger and cynical hostility, the standing readiness to read the world as hostile and to anger readily, often, and long, driving the hostile attribution bias and tracking cardiovascular risk (Spielberger's STAXI; the Aggression Questionnaire; the Type-A literature).

Fills a missing basic emotion: of the five the `emotion` root names (joy, sadness, anger, fear, disgust), anger had no engine. Promotes it from the single `emotion/process_anger.md` node to a standalone engine (via the `anger` homonym whitelist), keeping `emotion` a balanced appraisal taxonomy while anger gets its own depth. A process root fans into process forms and a position trait; each validates against its own khai chapters. Bounded against `emotion` (anger as one basic affect vs its internal structure), `aggression` (the emotion vs the behavior -- neither necessary nor sufficient for the other), `contempt` and `disgust` (its CAD-triad siblings, the cold look-down and the recoil), `stress` (the appraisal-emotion vs the acute fight/flight/freeze/fawn routing), and `forgiveness`/`rumination` (the holding of a stored wrong vs its release or rehearsal). Warranted (LORE) on Lazarus (the appraisal, 1991), Averill (anger as an everyday emotion of perceived wrongs, 1983), Berkowitz (frustration-aggression reformulated, 1989), Rozin et al. (the CAD triad, 1999), and Spielberger (state vs trait anger, the STAXI). Set at patch as the free level; a new engine may warrant a minor at the maintainer's `bump:minor` label.
