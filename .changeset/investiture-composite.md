Add the investiture composite, how an object comes to carry an identity that is not in it. Wires three atoms -- the extended-self engine (the object taken into a single persona's own self), the heirloom engine (the object withheld from exchange and passed down a line), and the totem engine (the object set apart by an assembled collective) -- and reads the seam the three share: the same move made at three widths, an identity lodged in a thing that has no property justifying it. Staged as three bridges: the held (an object taken in through mastery, making, knowing, gift, or the sheer sediment of use, until having is a way of being and the loss is grieved past the cost of replacement, with a deliberate divestment the one exit that does not tear), the handed (an object formally set outside the circuit that moves everything else the line owns, held in custody rather than ownership, brought out at successions to renew a claim that memory alone could not make hard to dispute, and alienated the moment a price attaches), and the hallowed (the twist -- an object made sacred not by any property of its own but by the assembled group treating it so, charged by the heat of the gathering, cooling without renewal, its prohibition making an attack on a piece of cloth an attack on the collective). Carries the keystone: what the object carries is not in the object -- which is why no identical thing can substitute for it, why a scratch on the right one is a wound, and why the outsider who sees a scrap of cloth is not making a mistake about the material. Wires at Persona/Projection (audit). Anchored in Kopytoff's singularization as the one mechanism the three widths share, James's material self, Belk on possessions and the extended self, Csikszentmihalyi and Rochberg-Halton on invested psychic energy, McCracken on the divestment ritual, Weiner's paradox of keeping-while-giving, Durkheim's totemic principle and collective effervescence, and Douglas on prohibition and violation. Distinct from object-cycle (the object's material life, worn and decayed and mended, and its marks prized as worth), document (the inscription whose authority is that it says the same thing everywhere), owing (the obligation a transfer leaves between two persons), heritage (the formation a persona arrives already carrying), grief (the acute mourning of a loss), and commons (a shared resource governed by its users).
