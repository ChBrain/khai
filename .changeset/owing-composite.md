Add the owing composite, how an obligation between two persons is made, carried, and discharged. Wires three atoms -- the gift engine (the transfer that binds precisely by naming no price), the debt engine (the same bond written as a figure one party may call in), and the money engine (the general equivalent that can discharge any claim at all) -- and reads the seam the three make when set in a row: one obligation at three degrees of exactness, each step buying portability with binding. Staged as three bridges: the giving (the transfer that must appear voluntary, the receiving that cannot be refused without refusing the relationship, and the interval before the return that is what makes it a relationship rather than a transaction -- obligation at its least exact and its most binding, since a claim without a figure cannot be discharged and therefore does not end), the owed (the bond as a number: a claim on the debtor's future, two unequal stances, an arc from incurring through servicing to default -- enforceable and extendable to a stranger, and never neutral, since being owed buys leverage and spends candour), and the settling (the twist -- the general equivalent that prices the unlike, the freedom it buys the holder to wait where others must act, and the earmarking by which people dress fungible sums back in meaning because they feel what the abstraction costs). Carries the keystone: what settles the account ends the relation -- a return identical to the gift signals equivalence and closes it, so an obligation binds in proportion to how little it can be paid off, and liquidity spends the option of not counting. Wires at Persona/Projection (audit), the first composite on khai's economic surface. Anchored in Mauss's triple obligation, Bourdieu's misrecognition and the interval between gift and counter-gift, Graeber on quantified enforceable debt, Nietzsche on the creditor-debtor relation as an origin of moral feeling, Simmel on money dissolving personal ties into quantity, and Zelizer on earmarking. Distinct from moral-account (the wrong done and the reckoning after it), standing (the rank a persona holds), love (what draws two people together), commons (a shared resource governed by its users), scarcity (the mind under shortage), and worth-logic (where personal worth comes from).
