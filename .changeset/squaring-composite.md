Add the squaring composite, how a persona squares what happened with who they take themselves to be. Wires three atoms -- the attribution engine (the cause placed where it need not disturb the self-picture), the dissonance engine (the belief bent to fit a deed that cannot be undone), and the moral-disengagement engine (the standard switched off for one act alone) -- and reads the seam the three make: one repair reached for at three depths, each deeper one taken up when the shallower will not hold. Staged as three bridges: the cause (the outcome's why placed on locus and stability, the cheapest repair, since a cause put outside or put down as passing leaves the self untouched -- and the only depth an outsider holding better evidence can argue with), the revision (reached when the deed was freely the persona's own: the act is fixed and the attitude is not, so the attitude gives -- the dull task made genuinely interesting for a dollar and not for twenty, the chosen option upgraded the moment it is chosen, the group loved most by those whose initiation was worst -- and held in good faith, so there is no insincerity to expose), and the suspension (the twist -- the morals not abandoned wholesale but switched off at one of four points, the act relabelled, the authorship displaced onto an order or a crowd, the harm shrunk below notice, the victim placed outside the circle). Carries the keystone: each repair goes deeper and shows less, until the account is not a lie the persona tells but what the persona now holds -- and the deepest cannot even be located, because the persona will still say the act would be wrong for someone else and will be telling the truth. Wires at Persona/Projection (audit). Anchored in Aronson's self-consistency recasting of dissonance as a threat to the self-concept, which is what makes the three one story; Heider's naive analysis of action and Weiner's causal dimensions; Festinger's theory and the one-dollar result, Brehm's spreading of alternatives, and the severity-of-initiation result; Bandura's four loci of moral disengagement; and Steele's self-affirmation as the alternative to squaring rather than a stage of it. Distinct from bias (the standing catalogue of tilts, which the attribution engine already delegates there), conscience (the moral self that governs in advance), moral-account (the reckoning between two people), guilt (the sanction that fires when a harm is owned), self-affirmation and selfhood (restoring the self elsewhere instead of editing this account), escalation, deception, moral-judgment, and rumination.
